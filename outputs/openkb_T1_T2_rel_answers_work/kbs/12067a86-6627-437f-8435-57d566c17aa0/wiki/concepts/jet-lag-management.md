# Jet Lag Management

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Practical tactics for minimizing the impact of rapid time‑zone changes on sleep, energy, and performance.

## Core Principles

1. **Shift Sleep Window Early**
   - Begin adjusting your bedtime and wake time toward the destination schedule as soon as you board. This pre‑emptive shift helps align your internal clock before you land.
2. **Light Exposure**
   - Get natural sunlight within the first hour of arrival (ideally 10‑30 minutes). Light is the strongest cue for resetting circadian rhythms.
3. **Controlled Napping**
   - Limit any daytime naps to **under 20 minutes**. Short naps prevent deep‑sleep inertia while still providing a quick energy boost.
4. **Caffeine Timing**
   - Use caffeine strategically to stay alert during the new daytime, but set a **cut‑off around 2 p.m. destination time** to avoid interference with nighttime sleep.
5. **Hydration & Light Eating**
   - Stay well‑hydrated and choose light, protein‑rich meals (e.g., Greek yogurt, oatmeal, protein box) on travel days to avoid digestive disruption that can worsen jet lag.

## Application in the Travel Routine (see [[summaries/session-35_2025-02-14]])

- **During Flights:** Shift your sleep window by sleeping or resting when it aligns with the destination night. Use eye masks and earplugs to create a dark, quiet environment.
- **Upon Arrival:** Immediately step outside or near a window for sunlight exposure. Walk a short distance to increase circulation and help reset the body clock.
- **Mid‑day:** If needed, take a **20‑minute power nap** no later than early afternoon, then resume activities.
- **Evening:** Keep dinner light (e.g., grilled chicken, fish, or a balanced salad) and avoid caffeine after the 2 p.m. cutoff.
