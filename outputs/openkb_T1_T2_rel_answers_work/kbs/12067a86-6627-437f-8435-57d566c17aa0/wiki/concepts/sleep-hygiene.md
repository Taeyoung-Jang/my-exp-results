# Sleep Hygiene

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A set of practices that prioritize protecting a minimum sleep window and total sleep amount, rather than strict clock times, to sustain energy and recovery while traveling.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Protect a Minimum Sleep Window**
   - Aim for at least 7 hours of sleep across any 24‑hour period, even if the actual bedtime shifts across time zones.
   - For early flights (e.g., before 7 a.m.), go to bed as early as possible and plan a short 20‑30 minute nap after landing.

2. **Flexibility Over Fixed Bedtimes**
   - When crossing time zones, focus on total sleep rather than matching the previous night’s clock time.
   - Adjust the sleep window gradually on the plane: start sleeping earlier or later to align with the destination’s night schedule.

3. **Anchor Routine Elements**
   - Consistently drink water upon waking.
   - Perform the same short [[concepts/portable-workout]] each morning to signal a wake‑up cue.
   - Keep caffeine intake limited: first dose after breakfast, latest cutoff around 2 p.m. destination time.

4. **Jet Lag Mitigation**
   - Combine sleep hygiene with light exposure: get sunlight within the first hour of arrival to reset the circadian rhythm.
   - Limit naps to under 20 minutes to avoid deep‑sleep inertia.
   - These steps tie directly into the broader [[concepts/jet-lag-management]] framework.

---

## Practical Checklist (From [[summaries/session-35_2025-02-14]])

- **Morning (Early‑flight day)**: Wake up 90 min before departure, drink water, do the hotel‑room workout, limit caffeine until after security, grab a protein‑forward breakfast (e.g., Greek yogurt, oatmeal, or a protein box).
- **Midday**: Close eyes for a 20‑minute “rest” on the plane, walk after landing, keep lunch light.
- **Evening**: Choose modest alcohol or portion size at dinner, aim for 7 hours of sleep from the time you finally get into bed, avoid caffeine after the 2 p.m. cutoff.
