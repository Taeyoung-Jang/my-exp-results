# Energy Conservation Strategy

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A set of tactics for lowering effort when energy is low to keep stress and workload manageable.

---

## Overview
The **Energy Conservation Strategy** is a lightweight framework for deliberately scaling back activities when personal energy reserves dip. It focuses on three core actions:

1. **Identify and drop optional, high‑effort tasks** – deep cleaning, long workouts, elaborate cooking, or extra commitments.
2. **Shrink core obligations to their smallest viable form** – shorten work blocks, limit chores to a 2‑minute reset, and keep evening routines under ten minutes.
3. **Prioritize restorative anchors** – consistent sleep, brief daily outdoor moments, and a single tiny chore each weekday to prevent backlog.

These actions are intended to protect mental bandwidth while still maintaining a sense of progress.

---

## Key Elements from Session 12 (2024‑07‑15)

- **Drop optional/high‑effort activities first** – deep cleaning, long workouts, big cooking projects, extra commitments.
- **Scale down core tasks** – shrink work time when possible, keep chores under five minutes, limit evening routine to ≤10 minutes.
- **Maintain anchoring habits** – steady sleep schedule, brief daily outdoor exposure, one tiny chore per weekday.
- **Use a crash‑friendly evening routine** to transition quickly from work to rest, reducing decision fatigue.

---

## How to Apply the Strategy

| Situation | Action | Example |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Feeling fatigued after work | Cut the evening routine to 10 minutes | Follow the 6‑step routine from the source document (drop belongings, tiny reset, change clothes, start dinner, settle, eat). |
| Low motivation for household tasks | Perform only one “tiny reset” chore | Toss trash or clear a single countertop space (1‑2 min). |
| Weekend overload | Choose a single slightly larger task only | Grocery run or light cleaning on Saturday; keep Sunday open for rest. |
