# Low Tide Running

**Type:** event

Low Tide Running refers to the practice of scheduling runs to coincide with low tide, when wet, packed sand is exposed along the shoreline.  In the context of the *session‑40* conversation (see [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-10]]), the user runs on this surface to reduce joint impact while managing the unique demands placed on the calves and Achilles tendon.

## Key facts from the conversation
- **Surface:** wet, packed sand that is softer than pavement but firmer than loose beach sand.
- **Goal:** lower joint stress while still challenging stabilising muscles.
- **Training approach:** integrates [[concepts/progressive-sand-running]] with a mix of firmer surfaces (pavement, trails) – see [[concepts/surface-mixing]].
- **Strength & mobility:** employs [[concepts/strength-drills]] (slow calf raises, eccentric heel drops) and [[concepts/mobility-drills]] (ankle circles, calf stretch).
- **Monitoring:** uses [[concepts/warning-signs]] such as persistent Achilles stiffness to guide progression.
- **Environmental considerations:** accounts for [[concepts/shoreside-camber]] (slope of the shoreline) and deals with salt residue on eyewear via [[concepts/salt-film-management]].
