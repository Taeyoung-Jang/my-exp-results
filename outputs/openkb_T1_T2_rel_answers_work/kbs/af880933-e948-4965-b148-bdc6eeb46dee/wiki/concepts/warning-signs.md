# Warning Signs

**Definition** – Observable symptoms that indicate a runner may be exceeding safe loading limits while transitioning to or increasing time on softer surfaces such as packed sand.

## Key Indicators (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-10]])
- **Persistent Achilles stiffness** that lasts more than a few minutes after a run.
- **Tugging or pinching sensation** in the Achilles tendon during the run.
- **Calves that remain tight** and do not loosen even after an easy day.
- **Sharp or lingering pain** in the lower leg that does not improve with rest or light mobility work.

When any of these signs appear, the recommendation is to **hold the current sand percentage** or **reduce it** before progressing further in the [[concepts/progressive-sand-running]] plan.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/softer-surfaces]]** – Softer terrain reduces impact but shifts stress to stabilising muscles; warning signs help balance that trade‑off.
- **[[concepts/surface-mixing]]** – Alternating sand with pavement or trails can mitigate the onset of warning signs by varying load patterns.
- **[[concepts/strength-drills]]** and **[[concepts/mobility-drills]]** – Targeted calf and ankle work can address early warning signs and prevent them from escalating.
- **[[concepts/shoreside-camber]]** – Repeated running in the same direction on a sloped beach may exacerbate warning signs; varying direction helps distribute load.

## Practical Monitoring Tips
1. **Post‑run check‑in:** Spend a minute assessing Achilles and calf tension before and after each run.
2. **Log symptoms:** Note any stiffness, tugging, or lingering tightness in a training diary.
3. **Adjust load:** If symptoms appear, stay at the current sand proportion or step back to a lower percentage in the [[concepts/progressive-sand-running]] schedule.
4. **Integrate recovery:** Use ankle circles and gentle calf stretches (see [[concepts/mobility-drills]]) after runs.

By paying attention to these warning signs, runners can safely increase sand exposure while minimizing the risk of overuse injuries.
