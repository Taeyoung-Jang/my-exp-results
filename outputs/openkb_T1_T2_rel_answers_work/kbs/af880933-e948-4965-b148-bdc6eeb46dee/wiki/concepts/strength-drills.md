## Strength Drills

**Definition** – A focused set of lower‑leg strength exercises designed to build calf and Achilles resilience for increased time on softer surfaces such as packed sand.

**Core Exercises** (as outlined in [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-10]])

1. **Straight‑leg calf raises** – 3 sets of 10 slow repetitions. Performed on a flat surface, the heel lifts high and lowers under control to load the gastrocnemius and the Achilles tendon.
2. **Bent‑knee calf raises** – 3 sets of 10 slow repetitions. The knee is flexed ~30°, shifting emphasis to the soleus muscle while still stressing the Achilles.
3. **Eccentric heel drops** – 2‑3 sets of 8 reps per leg. From a raised position on a step, lower the heel slowly for a 3‑5 second descent, then switch legs. This eccentric loading is key for tendon adaptation and injury prevention.

*Total session time*: about 10‑15 minutes.

**Integration with Training**
- Perform **2‑3 strength sessions per week** alongside a [[concepts/progressive-sand-running]] schedule.
- Pair with [[concepts/mobility-drills]] such as ankle circles and post‑run calf stretches to maintain joint range and reduce stiffness.
- Adjust volume if any [[concepts/warning-signs]] (persistent Achilles tightness, tugging, or calves that remain tight) appear.

**Why It Matters**
- Softer surfaces lower impact forces on joints (see [[concepts/softer-surfaces]]) but increase muscular demand on stabilisers and the Achilles. Structured calf work builds the necessary strength and tendon tolerance.
- Consistent strength work complements the principle of [[concepts/surface-mixing]], where alternating sand with firmer terrain distributes load and promotes balanced adaptation.

**Practical Tips**
- Use a step or low platform for heel drops; ensure the movement is pain‑free.
- Keep the tempo controlled (≈2 seconds up, 3‑5 seconds down) to maximise eccentric benefit.
- Record session details in a training log to track progression and identify any warning signs.
