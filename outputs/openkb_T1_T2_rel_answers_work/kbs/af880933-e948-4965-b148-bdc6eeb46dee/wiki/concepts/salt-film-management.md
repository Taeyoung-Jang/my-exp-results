# Salt Film Management

**Definition** – Practical techniques for dealing with the salty film that builds up on eyewear when running on wet, packed sand at low tide.

## Why it matters
Running on [[entities/packed-sand]] during low tide often provides a softer surface for the joints, but the ocean spray leaves a fine layer of salt on lenses. This can:
- Impair visibility and increase the risk of tripping or mis‑stepping.
- Cause irritation or discomfort for the wearer.
- Lead to longer‑term corrosion if not cleaned regularly.

## Core strategies (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-10]])
1. **On‑the‑go rinse** – Carry a small, sealed water flask. A quick splash over the lenses clears the bulk of the film without rubbing.
2. **Damp cloth swipe** – Keep a shirt hem or microfiber cloth slightly dampened (e.g., in a zip‑lock bag). Swipe the lenses; the moisture lifts the salt without smearing.
3. **Anti‑fog/anti‑smudge wipes** – Apply a thin layer before heading out. These products do not stop salt deposition but make subsequent cleaning faster and reduce streaking.
4. **Pre‑run lens coating** – Some runners use a light spray of lens‑protective fluid (e.g., rain‑repellent) that repels water‑borne salts.

## Integration with training concepts
- **[[concepts/progressive-sand-running]]** – As the proportion of sand increases, exposure to salt also rises, making regular use of the above tactics more critical.
- **[[concepts/surface-mixing]]** – Alternating sand runs with pavement or trail runs reduces overall salt exposure and gives a natural break for lens cleaning.
- **[[concepts/strength-drills]]** and **[[concepts/mobility-drills]]** – While not directly related, maintaining strong, stable lower‑leg mechanics ensures you stay on course and can react quickly if vision is momentarily impaired by salt.
- **[[concepts/warning-signs]]** – Reduced clarity due to persistent salt film can be considered a warning sign; if you notice frequent blurring, increase cleaning frequency or adjust run timing.

## Practical checklist
- [ ] Pack a small water flask (≈100 ml).
- [ ] Pre‑wet a portion of your shirt or a microfiber cloth.
- [ ] Bring a pack of anti‑fog/anti‑smudge wipes.
- [ ] Apply a protective spray to lenses the night before.
- [ ] After each run, rinse lenses thoroughly and store glasses in a dry case.

By incorporating these simple steps, runners can enjoy the joint‑friendly benefits of low‑tide sand without compromising visual safety.
