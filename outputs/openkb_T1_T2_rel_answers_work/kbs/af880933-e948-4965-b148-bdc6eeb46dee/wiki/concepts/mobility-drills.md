# Mobility Drills

**Definition**: Simple, low‑impact movements aimed at preserving and improving ankle range of motion and calf flexibility, especially before and after runs on softer surfaces such as packed sand.

## Core Elements from [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-10]]
- **Ankle circles** – performed slowly in both directions to lubricate the joint and maintain proprioception.
- **Gentle calf stretch** – held briefly after each run to release tension accumulated from the uneven or yielding terrain.
- **Timing** – integrated as a brief warm‑up before the sand portion of a run and as a cool‑down afterward.

These drills complement the strength work outlined in [[concepts/strength-drills]] and help mitigate the [[concepts/warning-signs]] such as persistent Achilles stiffness.

## Why They Matter for Sand Running
- Softer surfaces like [[entities/packed-sand]] lower impact but increase demand on stabilising muscles and ankle mechanics.
- Regular mobility work keeps the ankle joint adaptable, reducing the risk of over‑use injuries when the foot lands on an uneven or slightly sloped beach surface (see [[concepts/shoreside-camber]]).
- They support the overall strategy of [[concepts/surface-mixing]] and [[concepts/progressive-sand-running]] by ensuring the body can handle shifting loads.

## Practical Routine
1. **Ankle circles** – 10 rotations clockwise, 10 counter‑clockwise per foot.
2. **Dynamic calf stretch** – gently rock forward on the ball of the foot for 15‑20 seconds.
3. **Static calf stretch** – press the heel down against a low wall or rock, hold for 20‑30 seconds per side.

Perform the sequence before each run that includes sand and repeat immediately after the run.
