# Surface Mixing

**Definition**: Surface mixing is the practice of alternating running on softer terrains (e.g., sand, trails) with firmer surfaces (e.g., pavement) to distribute impact forces and stabilize muscle load.

## Why Mix Surfaces?
- **Joint Protection**: Softer surfaces reduce repetitive impact on knees and hips.  
- **Muscle Conditioning**: Firmer surfaces provide predictable footing, allowing the stabilising muscles and tendons to develop strength without excessive strain.
- **Injury Prevention**: By varying the load, runners avoid over‑use injuries that can arise from repeatedly stressing the same tissues.

## Guidance from [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-10]]
- **Progressive Sand Introduction**: The plan recommends starting with ~25 % of a run on packed sand, then gradually increasing to 60 % over four weeks while keeping other runs on pavement or trails.
- **Alternating Days**: Alternate sand sessions with firm‑surface runs to give calves and the Achilles tendon time to adapt.
- **Strength & Mobility Support**: Incorporate [[concepts/strength-drills]] (calf raises, eccentric heel drops) and [[concepts/mobility-drills]] (ankle circles, calf stretches) to handle the differing demands of each surface.

## Practical Tips
1. **Plan Weekly Runs**: Designate specific days for sand‑heavy runs and others for pavement/trails.
2. **Monitor Load**: Use the [[concepts/warning-signs]] (Achilles stiffness, calf tightness) to decide whether to maintain or reduce sand time.
3. **Camber Awareness**: When running on shoreline sand, be aware of [[concepts/shoreside-camber]] and switch direction periodically.
