## Progressive Sand Running

**Definition** – A systematic approach that raises the percentage of a runner’s weekly mileage on packed sand over time, integrating complementary strength and mobility work, and monitoring for injury signals.

### Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-10]])

1. **Weekly Sand Percentage**
   - **Week 1:** ~25 % of each run on [[entities/packed-sand]] (typically the early‑week run).
   - **Week 2:** ~33 % on sand (mid‑week run).
   - **Week 3:** ~50 % on sand (early‑week and weekend runs).
   - **Week 4:** ~60 % on sand (weekend run, if no warning signs).

2. **Strength & Mobility Sessions**
   - 2‑3 sessions per week focusing on [[concepts/strength-drills]] (slow straight‑leg and bent‑knee calf raises, eccentric heel drops) and [[concepts/mobility-drills]] (ankle circles, post‑run calf stretch).

3. **Surface Mixing**
   - Alternate sand days with firmer surfaces (pavement or trails) to satisfy [[concepts/surface-mixing]] and keep ankle/achilles loads balanced.

4. **Warning Signs**
   - Persistent Achilles stiffness, tugging/pinching sensations, or calves that stay tight after easy days trigger a hold or step‑back, as described in [[concepts/warning-signs]].

5. **Shoreline Camber Management**
   - Be aware of subtle slope or camber on the wet sand; switch running direction halfway through a session or vary the run location to distribute load evenly – see [[concepts/shoreside-camber]].

6. **Practical Adjuncts**
   - Salt‑film on glasses can be mitigated with quick‑rinse water, a damp shirt wipe, or anti‑fog/anti‑smudge wipes – see [[concepts/salt-film-management]].

### Benefits
- Reduces repetitive joint impact by using softer [[concepts/softer-surfaces]].
- Enhances stabilising muscle strength through uneven terrain.
- Provides a clear, measurable progression that can be tailored to individual recovery rates.

### Implementation Tips
- Start each sand segment as a small portion of the run and increase gradually.
- Keep strength work concise (≈10‑15 min) and pain‑free.
- Log any warning signs and adjust the progression accordingly.
- If low‑tide conditions allow, schedule runs when the sand is wet and packed for optimal joint comfort.
