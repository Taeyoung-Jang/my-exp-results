# Commitment Evaluation

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A systematic way to decide whether to accept a new invitation or commitment by weighing personal energy, priorities, values, and realistic impact over time.

## Core Elements
1. **Energy Check** – Ask yourself if the invitation feels energizing or draining. A positive, light feeling signals potential alignment; fatigue or guilt suggests a mismatch.
2. **Priority Alignment** – Evaluate whether the activity supports goals you truly care about rather than something you feel you *should* care about.
3. **Weekly Reality Projection** – Imagine a typical week with the new commitment added. Does the schedule feel manageable or overloaded?
4. **Emotional Indicator** – Notice the immediate emotional response (excited vs. obligated). This quick check helps surface hidden resistance.
5. **Value Fit** – Ensure the task aligns with longer‑term life vision or personal values.
6. **Trial Period Check** – After trying a new activity (e.g., a beginner class) for **3–4 weeks**, reassess whether it still feels energizing, fits your schedule, and meets your social goals. This time‑boxed test helps confirm the initial evaluation.

## How It Connects
- Use the insights from this evaluation to craft respectful boundaries as described in [[concepts/boundary-communication]].
- If the full commitment feels too heavy but you still want to contribute, consider a reduced role, which ties into [[concepts/scaled-involvement]].
- When evaluating potential after‑work hobbies, the framework works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/after-work-hobby-selection]] and [[concepts/beginner-friendly-classes]] to ensure the activity is both enjoyable and sustainable.
- Social fit can be examined through [[concepts/social-integration]] tactics such as low‑stakes conversation scripts and deciding whether to attend solo or with a friend.
