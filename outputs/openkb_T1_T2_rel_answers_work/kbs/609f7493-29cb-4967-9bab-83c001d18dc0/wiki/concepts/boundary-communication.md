# Boundary Communication

**Definition**: A communication style that combines genuine appreciation, an honest reason for declining or postponing, and an open‑door gesture to keep the relationship warm.

## Core Elements
1. **Appreciation** – Start by thanking the person for the invitation or opportunity.
2. **Honest Reason** – Briefly explain the concrete limitation (time, energy, workload) without over‑justifying.
3. **Boundary Statement** – Clearly state the decision (e.g., "I can’t commit right now").
4. **Open‑Door / Positive Closing** – Offer goodwill, future interest, or a smaller way to help if appropriate.

## Why It Matters
- Maintains trust and respect in personal, professional, and community contexts.
- Prevents over‑commitment and the stress that comes from stretching beyond capacity.
- Encourages transparency, which research shows leads to stronger long‑term collaborations.

## Practical Scripts (from [[summaries/session-02_2024-06-15]])
| Context | Script |
|---------|--------|
| Close friend (direct "no") | "I really appreciate you inviting me. I’d love to join, but my routine leaves me with very little spare time, so I can’t give it the attention it deserves. I’m cheering you on!" |
| Coworker (direct "no") | "Thanks for thinking of me. My schedule is packed and my regular workflow doesn’t leave much extra room, so I can’t take this on without stretching myself too thin. I appreciate the offer." |
| Community organizer (direct "no") | "Thank you for reaching out—and for the work you’re doing. My day‑to‑day routine is tight, so I don’t have much extra time to commit right now. I’m grateful to be included, though, and I hope the project goes really well." |
| Any context ("not right now") | "I’m interested, but my plate is full for the moment. If you’re still doing this in a couple of months, check back with me—I might be in a better place to join." |
| Any context ("yes, but small") | "I can’t commit to the full thing, but I can help in a tiny, manageable way. If you need someone to handle one simple task or show up occasionally, I can do that." |

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/commitment-evaluation]]** – Before using boundary communication, assess the invitation against your energy, priorities, and values.
- **[[concepts/scaled-involvement]]** – When you have some capacity, you can pair boundary communication with a scaled‑down offer instead of a full commitment.

## Implementation Steps
1. **Pause & Evaluate** – Use the commitment‑evaluation checklist to determine if the request aligns with your goals.
2. **Select Script Type** – Choose "no", "not right now", or "yes, but small" based on capacity and desire.
3. **Deliver with Warmth** – Follow the four‑element structure to keep the tone friendly and respectful.
4. **Follow Up** – If you offered a smaller role, clarify the scope; if you deferred, note a future check‑in date.
