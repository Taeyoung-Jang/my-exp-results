# After‑Work Hobby Selection

**Definition** – Choosing a consistent, low‑intensity, social activity to pursue after work, based on personal energy, schedule, and social preferences.

## Core Criteria (from [[summaries/session-35_2024-12-15]])
- **Energy level** – Assess how you feel after work (tired, chatty, quiet) to match the activity’s demand.
- **Time commitment** – Start with a single evening per week; consider a 3‑ to 4‑week trial period (see [[concepts/commitment-evaluation]]).
- **Social structure** – Decide between a structured class, a relaxed club, volunteering, or a recreational sport.
- **Intensity** – Prioritize non‑competitive, low‑pressure options (e.g., beginner yoga, dance, art, language, cooking).

## Decision Framework
1. **Identify desired social vibe** – Do you want a guided environment (class) or a more informal gathering (club)?
2. **Match activity to energy** – Choose low‑intensity options if you prefer minimal physical exertion.
3. **Evaluate scalability** – Plan for possible increased involvement later (see [[concepts/scaled-involvement]]).
4. **Set a trial window** – Commit to 3–4 weeks to gauge fit before deciding (linked to [[concepts/commitment-evaluation]]).

## Typical Starter Options (see [[concepts/beginner-friendly-classes]])
- Beginner yoga or gentle yoga
- Introductory dance (salsa, swing, bachata)
- Casual art or ceramics classes
- Beginner language courses
- Community cooking workshops

## Social Integration Tips
- Use simple conversation starters before/after class to meet people (see [[concepts/social-integration]]).
- Practice **boundary communication** to exit politely if interest wanes (see [[concepts/boundary-communication]]).

## Going Solo vs. With a Friend
- **Solo** – Increases openness to new connections, aligns with low‑pressure social integration.
- **With a friend** – Reduces initial anxiety but may limit interaction with others.

## Follow‑Up Strategy
- After a few sessions, suggest a low‑key meet‑up (e.g., coffee) framed as part of establishing a routine.
- Keep invitations light to avoid pressure, reinforcing the low‑intensity nature of the hobby.
