# Scaled Involvement

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A strategy for committing to a smaller, manageable portion of a larger invitation, used when you want to stay involved without overextending.

## Overview
Scaled involvement is a way to say *yes* to an invitation while setting clear limits on the amount of time, effort, or responsibility you will take on. It is most appropriate when:
- You have genuine capacity for a minor role.
- You feel a positive emotional response (excitement, relief) rather than guilt or obligation.
- The contribution you can make still adds value to the project or relationship.

## Why Use Scaled Involvement?
- **Preserves relationships** by showing willingness to help, even in a limited way.
- **Protects personal bandwidth** by avoiding full‑scale commitment that could lead to burnout.
- **Maintains alignment** with your priorities as outlined in [[concepts/commitment-evaluation]].

## How to Determine If Scaled Involvement Is Right
1. **Check your energy level** – Does the idea feel light and manageable? (see [[concepts/boundary-communication]] for emotional checks).
2. **Identify a concrete micro‑task** – Choose a specific, low‑effort task you can realistically complete.
3. **Set clear expectations** – Communicate that you are offering a limited contribution and cannot take on additional responsibilities.

## Example Scripts from [[summaries/session-02_2024-06-15]]
- **Friend:** "I can’t commit to the full thing, but I can help in a tiny, manageable way. If you need someone to handle one simple task or show up occasionally, I can do that."
- **Coworker:** "I can’t commit to the full project, but I can contribute in a limited way, like reviewing a draft or offering feedback at key points if that helps."
- **Community organizer:** "I can’t be part of the full effort, but I can help in a very limited way—for example, showing up for a single event or lending a hand with something short‑term."

## Steps to Communicate Scaled Involvement
1. **Express appreciation** – Thank the inviter for the opportunity.
2. **State your limitation** – Clearly indicate the scope of what you can do.
3. **Offer the specific contribution** – Mention the exact task or time you can commit.
4. **Leave the door open** – Indicate willingness to help further if circumstances change.
