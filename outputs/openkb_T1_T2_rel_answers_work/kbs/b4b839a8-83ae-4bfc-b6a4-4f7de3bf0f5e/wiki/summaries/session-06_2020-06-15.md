# Summary of session-06_2020-06-15

**Main guidance**
- Align your answer exactly with the wording the form requests; use the legal status shown on your passport, birth certificate, or immigration paperwork. [[concepts/form-consistency]]
- Avoid volunteering additional details beyond the options provided. [[concepts/citizenship-status-reporting]]
- Provide supporting documents (passport, naturalization certificate) only when the agency explicitly asks for verification.

**Specific questions addressed**
1. **Choosing “[[entities/united-states]] citizen”** – If you are a [[entities/united-states]] citizen (including naturalized), select “[[entities/united-states]] citizen” on any checkbox or dropdown. No extra clarification is needed unless the form asks for the method of acquisition.
2. **“[[entities/united-states]] citizen or national?”** – Respond “Yes” when the question groups the two categories together. [[entities/united-states]] nationals are a very small group; most respondents are citizens, so a simple “Yes” suffices. [[concepts/us-citizen-vs-national]]
3. **Naturalized vs. native‑born wording** – When only “[[entities/united-states]] citizen” appears, that covers both naturalized and birth‑right citizens. Mention “naturalized citizen” only if a separate field requests how citizenship was obtained.

**Practical tip**
- Keep a copy of your most recent official document handy to verify the exact terminology used by the issuing agency. This ensures consistent answers across job applications, school records, and government forms.
