# Green Card

The **Green Card**, officially the United States Permanent Resident Card, is the primary evidence of a non‑citizen’s lawful permanent resident status in the United States. It contains personal data such as the holder’s legal name, photograph, and a unique **Alien Registration Number (A‑Number)**.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]]
- The document is cited as the source for copying the A‑Number accurately in the background‑check section of new‑hire onboarding forms.
- Emphasized that the number must be transcribed **exactly as shown**, without altering formatting, adding/removing leading zeros, or confusing it with other USCIS identifiers.
- Mentioned alongside other **government‑issued identifiers** such as passport number, driver’s‑license number, and Social Security Number.

## Practical Usage in Onboarding
1. **Copy the A‑Number** directly from the front of the Green Card when a form asks for an “Alien Registration Number.”
2. **Verify that the legal name** on the Green Card matches the name used on all other forms (see [[concepts/legal-name-usage]]).
3. **Include the Green Card number** only if explicitly requested; otherwise, avoid providing unnecessary immigration details.
4. **Ask HR** (see [[entities/human-resources]]) for clarification if a form’s request is ambiguous.
