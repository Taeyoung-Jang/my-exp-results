# United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS)

United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) is the federal agency within the Department of Homeland Security responsible for processing immigration and naturalization applications, issuing work and travel authorizations, and maintaining records of immigration identifiers such as the Alien Registration Number (A‑Number).

## Role in Onboarding Paperwork
In the context of the onboarding session **[[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]]**, USCIS is referenced as the source of official immigration identifiers that new hires must copy exactly when completing background‑check sections. The agency issues:
- Alien Registration Numbers (A‑Numbers) on green cards
- USCIS Online Account Numbers
- I‑94 arrival/departure records
- Other USCIS‑issued numbers that may appear on employment documents

Accurate transcription of these identifiers is critical to avoid common mix‑ups such as confusing the A‑Number with other USCIS numbers or transposing digits. The guidance in the session highlights the importance of copying numbers **exactly as shown** on the USCIS‑issued document.

## Practical Tips from the Session
- Use the physical or official digital copy of the green card to obtain the A‑Number.
- Verify which specific USCIS identifier a form requests; if unclear, contact **[[entities/human-resources]]** for clarification.
- Avoid formatting changes: retain leading zeros and exact spacing as printed on the USCIS document.

USCIS thus serves as the authoritative source for the immigration‑related data required during new‑hire onboarding, ensuring legal compliance and data consistency across forms.
