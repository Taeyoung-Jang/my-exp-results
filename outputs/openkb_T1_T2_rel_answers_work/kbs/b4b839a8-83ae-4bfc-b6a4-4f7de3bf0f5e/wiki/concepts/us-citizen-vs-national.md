# U.S. Citizen vs National

**Definition**

The term *U.S. citizen* refers to individuals who hold citizenship by birth or naturalization, while *U.S. national* includes citizens and a very small subset of people (e.g., residents of American Samoa) who owe allegiance to the United States but are not citizens.

**Key points from [[summaries/session-06_2020-06-15]]**

- When a form asks "Are you a U.S. citizen or national?" the safest response is simply **Yes** if you are a citizen, because the question groups the two categories together.
- U.S. nationals who are not citizens are rare, and their official documents (e.g., passport) will explicitly state "U.S. national" instead of "U.S. citizen."
- For most applicants who are citizens—whether naturalized or by birth—no further distinction is required unless a separate field asks for the method of citizenship.

**Practical guidance**

- Align your answer to the exact wording of the form (see [[concepts/form-consistency]]). 
- Use the status shown on your official documents; if the document says *U.S. citizen*, select that option.
- Only specify "naturalized citizen" if the form explicitly requests how citizenship was obtained.
