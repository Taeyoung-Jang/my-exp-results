# Citizenship Status Reporting

**Definition**: The practice of providing truthful, consistent, and appropriately scoped information about a person's legal citizenship or immigration status when completing job applications, school records, and government forms.

## Core Principles
1. **Match the Form's Terminology**
   - Use the exact status wording that appears on your official documents (passport, birth certificate, naturalization certificate, immigration paperwork). This aligns with [[concepts/form-consistency]].
   - When a form requests an immigration identifier (e.g., Alien Registration Number, USCIS Online Account Number, I‑94, passport number, driver’s‑license number), copy the value **exactly** as printed on the source document such as a green card or passport. See [[concepts/immigration-identifiers]] and consider the [[entities/green-card]] as a reference.
2. **Avoid Volunteering Extra Details**
   - Select the option that best fits your legal status and do not add explanations unless a free‑text field explicitly asks for them.
3. **Provide Documentation Only When Requested**
   - Share proof (e.g., passport, naturalization certificate) only if the agency or employer specifically asks for verification.
4. **Consistent Answers Across Contexts**
   - Keep a master copy of your key identifiers (legal name, address, date of birth, Social Security Number, immigration numbers) handy and ensure they appear identically on every form. Use the consistency checklist that includes name formatting [[concepts/legal-name-usage]], address formatting [[concepts/address-formatting]], and other identifiers.
5. **Double‑Check for Common Mix‑ups**
   - Be wary of confusing the Alien Registration Number with other USCIS numbers, transposing digits, or altering leading zeros. If unsure which identifier a form means, ask the relevant department (e.g., [[entities/human-resources]]).

## Specific Situations
- **Checkboxes for "U.S. citizen"**
  - If you are a U.S. citizen—whether by birth or naturalization—select the "U.S. citizen" option. No need to specify "naturalized" unless a separate field asks for the method of citizenship.
- **Question "Are you a U.S. citizen or national?"**
  - Answer "Yes" when the question groups the two categories together. Distinguish between citizen and national only if a form separately asks for that detail. See [[concepts/us-citizen-vs-national]].
- **Multiple‑Choice vs. Open Text**
  - For multiple‑choice sections, pick the choice that directly matches your legal status. For open‑text boxes, keep the response concise and use the terminology from the issuing agency.
- **Immigration‑Related Fields**
  - When a form asks for an "Alien Registration Number," "A‑Number," or any other USCIS‑issued identifier, copy it verbatim from your green card or official notice. Do not substitute a different USCIS number (e.g., card number) or re‑format the identifier.

## Practical Tip
- **Verification**: When uncertain about the exact term or number, consult the most recent official document or contact the issuing agency for clarification. Keep a printed or digital copy of these documents near you while completing forms, and if any requirement remains unclear, reach out to [[entities/human-resources]] for guidance.
