# Legal Name Usage

## Definition
Legal name usage refers to the practice of entering **the exact name as it appears on an official government‑issued identification document** (e.g., driver’s license, passport, or green card) when completing onboarding or other official forms.

## Why It Matters
- Consistency prevents processing delays by HR and government agencies.
- Mismatched names can trigger identity‑verification failures or tax reporting errors.
- In the context of new‑hire paperwork, the concept is highlighted in [[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]] where HR guidance stresses using the same legal name across all sections, especially those labeled “legal name.”

## Practical Guidance (from session‑31)
1. **Copy the name verbatim** from the ID, including middle names or initials if they appear.
2. Apply this exact version to any field labeled “legal name” **and** to fields labeled “full name” to maintain uniformity.
3. Verify the spelling, ordering, and punctuation (e.g., hyphens or apostrophes) against the source document.
4. Keep a master copy of the ID nearby while filling out forms to reduce transcription errors.

## Common Pitfalls
- Dropping a middle name or initial when a form only asks for “full name.”
- Re‑ordering first and last names to match personal preference rather than the ID layout.
- Adding titles (Ms., Dr.) or suffixes (Jr., III) that are not on the ID.
- Confusing the legal name with a preferred or nickname, which can cause mismatches in background‑check sections.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Consistent naming supports overall [[concepts/form-consistency]].
- Minor address variations (e.g., “Apt” vs. “Unit”) are permissible, as described in [[concepts/address-formatting]], but **name** formatting should remain exact.
- When immigration‑related identifiers are required, the same rule applies: copy the number exactly as shown (see [[concepts/immigration-identifiers]]).

## Entities Involved
- **[[entities/human-resources]]** departments enforce legal name usage during onboarding.
- **[[entities/us-government]]** agencies rely on exact matches for immigration and tax records.
- **[[entities/uscis]]** documentation (e.g., green card) provides the authoritative legal name for non‑citizens.
