# Form Consistency

**Definition**: Form consistency is the practice of providing the exact terminology, identifiers, and formatting requested on applications, records, and government forms, without adding extra detail or deviating from official documentation.

---

## Why it matters
- **Accuracy**: Matching the form's language and data fields reduces the risk of misreporting or providing contradictory information.
- **Efficiency**: Reusing the same phrasing and data across multiple submissions saves time and eliminates the need to rewrite answers for each new form.
- **Compliance**: Many agencies only accept responses that align with their defined categories; deviations can delay processing or trigger follow‑up queries.
- **Onboarding relevance**: New‑hire paperwork often includes identity verification, tax, and background‑check sections where inconsistent entries are a common source of errors.

---

## Guidance from [[summaries/session-06_2020-06-15]]
- **Use official documents**: Refer to your passport, birth certificate, or immigration paperwork to determine the exact status wording.
- **Select the closest option**: If a form offers a checkbox for “U S. citizen,” that choice covers both natural‑born and naturalized citizens.
- **Avoid extra detail**: Do not volunteer information about how citizenship was obtained unless the form specifically asks for it.
- **Keep a reference**: Have a recent official document handy so you can quickly verify the correct term.

## Additional guidance from [[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]]
- **Core data checklist**: Ensure the same representation of your full legal name, current home address, date of birth, Social Security Number, employee ID (if assigned), and start/availability dates on every form.
- **Legal name vs. full name**: When a field says “legal name,” copy the exact version from your government ID, including middle names or initials. Use the same version for “full name” fields to keep everything aligned.
- **Address formatting**: Minor differences such as “Apt” vs. “Unit” or “St” vs. “Street” are acceptable, provided the address clearly points to the same location.
- **Immigration identifiers**: For background‑check sections, copy government‑issued numbers (Alien Registration Number, USCIS Online Account Number, I‑94, passport, driver’s‑license, professional license numbers) exactly as shown. Common mix‑ups include confusing the A‑Number with other USCIS numbers, transposing digits, or omitting leading zeros.
- **Verification process**: Fill each form once, then review it with fresh eyes or a second pass to catch contradictions. When uncertain which identifier a form requests, ask HR for clarification.

## Practical tip
When a form asks for “citizenship or immigration status,” answer with the exact phrase found on your official paperwork (e.g., “U S. citizen”) and provide supporting documentation only if explicitly requested. Apply the core data checklist—name, address, DOB, SSN, and relevant ID numbers—to every section, and perform a quick second‑read to ensure consistency across the entire onboarding packet.
