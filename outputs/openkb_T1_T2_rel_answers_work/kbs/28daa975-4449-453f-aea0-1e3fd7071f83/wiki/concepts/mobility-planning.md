# Mobility Planning

**Mobility Planning** is the practice of treating travel time as an intentional part of a weekly schedule, combining fixed appointments, errands, work blocks, and dedicated buffers with layered backup options such as public transit, rideshare, or trusted friends.

---

## Overview

People often rely on a single mode of transport (e.g., a car) and feel stranded when that mode is unavailable. Mobility Planning reframes travel as a **system** rather than a constraint, allowing individuals to maintain productivity and reduce stress during periods of limited vehicle access.

The approach was illustrated in [[summaries/session-36_2025-02-13]], where a user created a two‑window daily template that captures:
- **Time windows** (morning and afternoon/evening) with address and transit‑friendliness.
- **Ride status** checkboxes for confirmed vs. tentative rides.
- A **backup plan** line for an alternate transit route, rideshare app, or a designated friend.
- Low‑pressure text scripts to request rides without imposing.

---

## Key Components

1. **Time‑Block Scheduling**
   - Divide each day into distinct windows (e.g., morning, afternoon/evening).
   - Record the destination address and note if the location is *transit‑friendly*.
   - Add a small buffer (5‑15 min) before and after each window to accommodate schedule variance.

2. **Backup Planning**
   - Identify at least one fallback option per day: a public‑transit route, a rideshare service, or a specific friend who can provide a lift.
   - Keep the backup details concise to enable quick decision‑making.

3. **Communication Strategy**
   - Use low‑pressure language when asking friends for rides. See [[concepts/low-pressure-communication]] for wording tips.
   - Include pickup and drop‑off times to make the request clear while allowing an easy “no.”

4. **Buffer Management**
   - Treat travel time as a **buffered block** rather than an afterthought. This aligns with concepts from [[concepts/commute-time-estimation]] about accounting for variability in transit schedules.

## Benefits

- **Reduced cognitive load**: All travel information is captured in one place.
- **Flexibility**: Multiple fallback options prevent a single point of failure.
- **Social comfort**: Low‑pressure messaging respects friends' time, maintaining healthy relationships.
- **Improved punctuality**: Built‑in buffers and clear travel blocks increase on‑time arrival rates.
