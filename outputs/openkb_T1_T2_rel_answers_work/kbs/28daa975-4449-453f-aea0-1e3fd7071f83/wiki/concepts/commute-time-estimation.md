# Commute Time Estimation

**Definition**: A systematic approach to predict how long a car trip will take during peak traffic periods, based on factors such as distance, typical congestion on major arteries, and time‑of‑day variations.

---

## Core Elements
1. **Baseline Distance** – Start with the straight‑line or road‑network mileage between origin and destination.
2. **Peak‑Hour Traffic Patterns** – Identify the primary freeways or surface streets that experience the most congestion during the morning commute. In the Chicago metro area, the **[[entities/eisenhower-expressway]]** and **[[entities/kennedy-expressway]]** are common bottlenecks.
3. **Historical Travel Times** – Use recent trip‑time data (e.g., from Google Maps, Waze, or local traffic reports) to establish a “normal rush‑hour” range and a “worst‑case” range.
4. **Buffer Factor** – Add a safety margin (typically 10‑20 % of the normal estimate) to accommodate unexpected incidents such as accidents or construction.
5. **Mode‑Specific Adjustments** – For drivers who may also rely on public transit, consider proximity to **[[entities/blue-line]]** or **[[entities/green-line]]**; while not directly affecting drive time, they can influence route choice (e.g., avoiding congested corridors).

## Practical Steps for Users
1. **Map the Route** – Input the exact address into a navigation app and select “depart at 8 am” (or your typical start time) to capture the predicted travel time.
2. **Record Multiple Samples** – Check the estimate on several days (e.g., Monday, Wednesday, Friday) to capture variability.
3. **Apply the Buffer** – Multiply the average normal estimate by 1.15 – 1.20 to set a realistic expectation for daily planning.
4. **Cross‑Check with Real‑World Experience** – After a week of commuting, compare actual times to the predicted range and adjust the buffer accordingly.
