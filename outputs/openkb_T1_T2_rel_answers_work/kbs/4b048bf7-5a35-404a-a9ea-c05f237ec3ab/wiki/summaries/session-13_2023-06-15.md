# Summary of session-13_2023-06-15

**Topic:** Managing a social calendar without overwhelm; polite scripts for declining last‑minute invites and proposing alternatives with a 2‑day‑notice preference.

## Key Ideas
- **Baseline capacity** – Determine how much social time feels sustainable each week; use this to guide acceptance or decline of invites. [[concepts/social-calendar-baseline]]
- **Pause & check** – Before replying, review your calendar and mood; give yourself a moment before committing.
- **Flex space** – Schedule dedicated downtime as an appointment to avoid over‑booking.
- **Micro‑connections** – Quick messages or short calls keep relationships warm without large time commitments.
- **Polite decline scripts** – Ready‑to‑copy short responses for both casual and formal contexts. [[concepts/polite-decline-scripts]]
- **Notice preference** – Emphasize a personal rule of needing at least two days’ notice, phrased without sounding rigid. [[concepts/notice-preference]]

## Sample Scripts
### General polite declines
- “I’d love to, but I’m not able to swing it today. Thank you for thinking of me.”
- “I’m tied up and can’t make it on such short notice, but I really appreciate the invite.”

### Offering alternative times
- “I can’t do today, but I’d love to catch up next week if you’re free.”
- “I need a bit more notice this time, but I’m up for something later—how does sometime next weekend look for you?”

### Tailored to a 2‑day‑notice rule
- **Casual (friends)**
  - “Ahh I wish, but I can’t do last‑minute stuff. Let’s plan something in a couple days?”
  - “Love you for asking, but I need a little more heads‑up. Want to pick a day later this week?”
- **Formal (coworkers/acquaintances)**
  - “Thank you for inviting me. I usually need a bit more notice, so I won’t be able to join today.”
  - “I appreciate you including me. Same‑day plans don’t work well for me, but I’d be glad to set something up later this week if that helps.”
