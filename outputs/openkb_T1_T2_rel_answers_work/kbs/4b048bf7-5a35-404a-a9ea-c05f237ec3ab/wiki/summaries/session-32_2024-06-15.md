# Summary of session-32_2024-06-15

**Date:** 2024-06-15

## Core Ideas
- **Commitment factors:** Plans are more likely to stick when they align with participants' genuine interest, fit naturally into existing schedules, and involve clear communication. See [[concepts/plan-commitment]].
- **Balancing flexibility vs. over‑commitment:** Be honest about personal bandwidth, set intentional "no‑plan" days (related to [[concepts/social-calendar-baseline]]), and treat future plans as if they were happening today to gauge willingness. This reflects [[concepts/flexibility-vs-overcommitment]].
- **Communicating a spontaneous style:** Frame short‑notice availability as a personal preference rather than unreliability, offering to check in closer to the date for longer‑term plans. This aligns with [[concepts/communication-of-preference]] and [[concepts/notice-preference]].

## Practical Rules
1. **"Tonight‑test" rule:** If you wouldn’t want to do something tonight, you probably won’t want it weeks later.
2. **Reserve downtime:** Keep 1‑2 days per week free of any plans to protect personal downtime.
3. **Check‑in habit:** For plans made weeks ahead, schedule a brief check‑in closer to the event.

## Text‑Message Scripts
- **Soft maybe:**  
  > "Sounds fun, but I can’t commit that far out yet. Can we touch base a few days beforehand and see where things are at?"
- **Clear no:** (example of a [[concepts/polite-decline-scripts]] style)  
  > "I’m keeping my schedule pretty light right now, so I can’t lock that in. Thanks for thinking of me though—hope it’s a great time."
- **Yes with check‑in:**  
  > "I’m in for now! My schedule tends to shift, so can we do a quick check‑in the week of just to make sure everything still works?"
- **Tentative yes (energy‑dependent):**  
  > "I’d really like to come, and I’m planning on it. I just want to be honest that it depends a bit on my energy that week. Let’s plan for yes, and I’ll let you know ahead of time if anything shifts."
