# Polite Decline Scripts

**Definition**: A collection of short, courteous message formats that let you say no to an invitation while optionally suggesting a later time, tailored to tone and personal notice preferences.

## Core Purpose
Polite decline scripts help manage a **social calendar baseline** (see [[concepts/social-calendar-baseline]]) by providing ready‑to‑copy language that protects personal capacity without damaging relationships. They also support a **notice preference** (see [[concepts/notice-preference]]) of needing at least two days' lead time.

## Categories of Scripts
### 1. General Declines
- “I’d love to, but I’m not able to swing it today. Thank you for thinking of me.”
- “I’m tied up and can’t make it on such short notice, but I really appreciate the invite.”

### 2. Declines with Alternative Suggestion
- “I can’t do today, but I’d love to catch up next week if you’re free.”
- “I need a bit more notice this time, but I’m up for something later—how does sometime next weekend look for you?”

### 3. Scripts Honouring a 2‑Day Notice Rule
#### Casual (friends)
- “Ahh I wish, but I can’t do last‑minute stuff. Let’s plan something in a couple days?”
- “Love you for asking, but I need a little more heads‑up. Want to pick a day later this week?”

#### Formal (coworkers / acquaintances)
- “Thank you for inviting me. I usually need a bit more notice, so I won’t be able to join today.”
- “I appreciate you including me. Same‑day plans don’t work well for me, but I’d be glad to set something up later this week if that helps.”

## New Additions from Session 32 (2024‑06‑15)
### 4. Soft‑Maybe Scripts (when you’re not ready to commit)
- “Sounds fun, but I can’t commit that far out yet. Can we touch base a few days beforehand and see where things are at?”
- “I’d really like to come, and I’m planning on it. I just want to be honest that it depends a bit on my energy that week. Let’s plan for yes, and I’ll let you know ahead of time if anything shifts.”

### 5. Clear‑No Scripts (more direct declines)
- “I’m keeping my schedule pretty light right now, so I can’t lock that in. Thanks for thinking of me though—hope it’s a great time.”
- “I’m tied up and can’t make it on such short notice, but I really appreciate the invite.” (re‑used from General Declines for emphasis)

### 6. Yes‑With‑Check‑In Scripts (commit, but request a later confirmation)
- “I’m in for now! My schedule tends to shift, so can we do a quick check‑in the week of just to make sure everything still works?”
- “I’m in for now! My schedule tends to shift, so can we do a quick check‑in the week of just to make sure everything still works.” (re‑used for consistency)

## How to Use Them
1. **Identify the relationship tone** (casual vs. formal).
2. **Match the script to your notice preference** – insert a reference to needing a couple of days’ notice where appropriate.
3. **Choose the appropriate level of commitment** – soft‑maybe, clear‑no, or yes‑with‑check‑in, depending on how certain you feel.
4. **Customize the timing** – replace vague periods (“next week”) with a concrete date range if you wish.
5. **Send promptly** – after the brief pause to check your calendar, copy‑paste the chosen script.

## Benefits
- Reduces decision fatigue by having pre‑written options.
- Keeps communication clear and respectful, preserving social connections.
- Aligns with personal capacity planning, reinforcing the social‑calendar baseline.
- Provides nuanced scripts for different commitment levels, reflecting the “soft maybe,” “clear no,” and “yes with check‑in” approaches discussed in recent conversations.
