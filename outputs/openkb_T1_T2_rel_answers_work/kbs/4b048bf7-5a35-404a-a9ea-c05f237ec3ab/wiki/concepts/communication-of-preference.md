# Communication of Preference

**Definition**

Communication of preference refers to the practice of explicitly stating one’s personal scheduling style or availability preferences in social interactions. By presenting these preferences as a choice rather than a limitation, the speaker maintains a perception of reliability while setting realistic expectations.

**Key Points from [[summaries/session-32_2024-06-15]]**
- Emphasize the *preference* (e.g., “I’m usually best with short‑notice plans”) instead of framing it as a shortcoming.
- Offer a *check‑in* option for longer‑term plans, signaling willingness to participate while keeping flexibility.
- Pair the statement with a brief explanation of the underlying reason (e.g., shifting schedule) to provide context without sounding flaky.

**Practical Guidelines**
1. **State the preference clearly** – Use first‑person language to own the schedule style.
2. **Offer a mitigation** – Propose a future check‑in or a fallback plan.
3. **Keep tone positive** – Highlight enthusiasm for the activity while noting the need for flexibility.
4. **Align with other concepts** – This approach supports [[concepts/plan-commitment]] by ensuring all parties are aware of constraints, and it balances with [[concepts/flexibility-vs-overcommitment]] to avoid burnout.
