# Notice Preference

**Definition**: A personal rule that specifies how many days' notice are needed before committing to a social invitation, expressed in a way that feels courteous rather than rigid.

## Core Elements
- **Notice window** – Typically a minimum of two days, but can be framed as "a couple of days" or "a few days in advance."
- **Tone** – Language should balance clarity with friendliness, adjusting formality based on the relationship (e.g., casual for close friends, more polished for coworkers).
- **Consistency** – Repeating the preference helps others learn and respect the boundary, reducing last‑minute pressure.

## Why It Matters
Implementing a notice preference protects personal downtime and prevents overwhelm, complementing the broader practice of establishing a [[concepts/social-calendar-baseline]]. It also supports the use of [[concepts/polite-decline-scripts]] by providing a clear reason for declining and a basis for suggesting alternative dates.

## Practical Application (from [[summaries/session-13_2023-06-15]])
- **Casual scripts**: "Ahh I wish, but I can’t do last‑minute stuff. Let’s plan something in a couple days?"
- **Formal scripts**: "Thank you for inviting me. I usually need a bit more notice, so I won’t be able to join today."
- **Alternative suggestion**: Offer a specific future window (e.g., "next week" or "the next few days") to keep the conversation moving.

## Tips for Using Notice Preference
1. **State the preference early** – Mention it when you first discuss plans.
2. **Provide a fallback** – Suggest a concrete alternative time frame.
3. **Match the phrasing to the audience** – Use relaxed language with friends and a more professional tone with acquaintances.
4. **Be consistent** – Reiterate the rule when needed; consistency builds expectation.
