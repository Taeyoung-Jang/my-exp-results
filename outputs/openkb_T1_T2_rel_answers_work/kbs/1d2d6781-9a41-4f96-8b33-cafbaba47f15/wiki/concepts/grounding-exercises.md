# Grounding Exercises

Grounding Exercises are short, intentional activities designed to bring immediate calm, focus, and a sense of safety without the use of a phone. They are typically performed in the first 5–15 minutes of a morning routine and can be especially helpful when waking up with anxiety or a racing mind.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]])
- **Breathing patterns** – e.g., inhale for a count of 4, exhale for a count of 6, paired with a calming self‑statement such as “I don’t have to fix anything right now.”
- **Sensory anchoring** – noticing one thing you can feel, hear, and see to root attention in the present moment.
- **Mantra focus** – simple phrases like “Here. Now.” repeated on exhale/inhale to gently redirect wandering thoughts.
- **Duration** – a 5‑ to 15‑minute block that fits neatly into a broader [[concepts/morning-routine]].

## Example Scripts
1. **Script One** – Stand or sit, slow breathing (4 in / 6 out), repeat the affirmation, then scan for a tactile, auditory, and visual cue.
2. **Script Two** – Sit with hands on legs, think “Here” on each exhale and “Now” on each inhale, returning to the words whenever the mind drifts.

## How Grounding Supports Phone‑Free Productivity
By stabilizing the nervous system before any screen interaction, grounding creates a calm baseline that improves focus and reduces the impulse to check notifications. This aligns with the broader goal of [[concepts/phone-free-productivity]] in a structured [[concepts/morning-routine]].

## When to Use
- On weekdays when anxiety spikes upon waking.
- As part of the “reset” version of a morning routine.
- Anytime a quick mental reset is needed before moving on to tasks like planning, journaling, or breakfast.
