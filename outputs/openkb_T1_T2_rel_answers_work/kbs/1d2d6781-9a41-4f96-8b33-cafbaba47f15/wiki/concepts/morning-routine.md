# Morning Routine

A **morning routine** is a purposeful, largely phone‑free sequence of actions performed within the first hour after waking, aimed at establishing focus, reducing anxiety, and maintaining momentum into the day. It combines night‑before preparation, grounding, planning, and, if desired, a concise “quick check” of essential messages using a tailored iPhone Focus mode.

## Core Elements

1. **Night‑before checklist** – lay out clothes, prep breakfast ingredients, fill a water bottle, place a notebook and pen, and note a top priority for the morning. (See [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]])
2. **First‑hour schedule** – divided into 5‑10‑minute blocks covering hydration, gentle movement, planning or grounding, personal‑care, breakfast, and tidy‑up before phone use.
3. **Grounding block** (5–15 min) – uses simple breathing or mantra scripts to calm a racing mind. See [[concepts/grounding-exercises]].
4. **Micro‑journaling** (15–25 min) – a single prompt: *“What’s the one thing on my mind that would feel lighter if I wrote it down?”* to release mental clutter without overanalysis.
5. **Morning Quick Check** – a brief, defined phone interaction (1–2 min) that scans only truly time‑sensitive items (texts, missed calls, calendar alerts). This step is optional but useful for those who need immediate awareness. It is implemented via a custom iPhone Focus named **Morning Quick Check** (see [[concepts/morning-quick-check]] and [[concepts/focus-mode]]).
   - **Focus set‑up**: Settings → Focus → + → *Morning Quick Check* → People (add essential contacts) → Apps (allow *Messages* and optionally *Calendar*) → Options: hide notification previews, disable lock‑screen widgets, enable Dim Lock Screen.
   - **Friction techniques**: place the phone on a dresser, kitchen counter, or hallway shelf out of arm’s reach, and use a rule such as “if I reach for it, I must stand up.” This adds a small obstacle that discourages extended scrolling. See [[concepts/attention-friction]].
6. **Variations**
   - **Weekday** – streamlined for a quick start to work.
   - **Weekend** – slower, with extended reflective activity.
   - **Anxiety‑reset weekday** – incorporates an extended grounding block and the quick check.
   - **Phone‑integrated weekday** – adds the Morning Quick Check while keeping the rest of the routine phone‑free.

## Benefits
- **Phone‑free productivity** – postpones digital distractions, preserving mental bandwidth for intentional tasks ([[concepts/phone-free-productivity]]).
- **Reduced decision fatigue** – night‑before preparation removes early‑morning choices.
- **Anxiety management** – grounding scripts and a limited phone check provide a reset mechanism.
- **Consistent momentum** – a clear plan guides the transition from wake‑up to work or leisure.
- **Controlled connectivity** – the Focus mode and friction placement let you stay reachable for emergencies (e.g., calls from essential contacts via *Messages* on an [[entities/iphone]]) without falling into scrolling traps.

## Implementation Tips
- Keep the night‑before checklist short and visible (e.g., on the nightstand).
- Use a single notebook for planning and the journaling prompt.
- Adjust block lengths to fit personal wake‑up times, but stay within the 60‑minute window.
- Set up the **Morning Quick Check** Focus in advance and schedule it to activate automatically at your wake‑up time.
- Choose a phone placement that requires you to stand up (dresser, kitchen counter, hallway shelf) to add gentle friction.
- Practice the grounding scripts regularly to make them feel automatic.
- If you use an iPhone, you can also customize the lock‑screen reminder (e.g., “Morning first, phone later”) to reinforce the habit.
