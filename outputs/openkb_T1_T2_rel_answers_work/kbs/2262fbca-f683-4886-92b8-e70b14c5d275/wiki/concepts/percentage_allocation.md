# Percentage Allocation

**Definition:**
A budgeting method that assigns a predetermined percentage of each income receipt to different financial buckets—typically taxes, emergency savings, and discretionary spending.

## Core Elements
- **Tax bucket** – In the gig‑economy context, a common recommendation is **≈30 %** of every payment, covering federal, self‑employment, and state taxes.
- **Emergency/ savings bucket** – Usually **5‑10 %**, moved to a separate account for unexpected expenses.
- **Essentials bucket** – The remaining income funds rent, food, transportation, and other weekly necessities.

## How It Works in Practice
1. Identify your **bare‑minimum weekly costs** (rent, food, gas, phone, etc.).
2. When a gig shift pays, immediately allocate:
   - **30 %** → tax savings account (see [[concepts/percentage_allocation]] related to [[concepts/tax_safe_harbor]])
   - **5‑10 %** → emergency savings account (see [[concepts/separate_accounts]])
   - Remaining → cover weekly essentials and any discretionary spending.
3. Automation can be achieved via [[concepts/automatic_transfers]] or manual transfers; the key is consistency.

## Benefits
- **Scalability:** Percentages scale automatically with fluctuating gig income.
- **Simplicity:** Reduces decision fatigue—each payment follows the same rule.
- **Tax compliance:** Aligns with safe‑harbor strategies such as paying a fixed portion of earnings each quarter, helping avoid IRS penalties.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- [[concepts/estimated_taxes]] – Percentage allocation supplies the cash flow needed for quarterly estimated tax payments.
- [[concepts/budgeting_rhythm]] – Setting a regular cadence (weekly) for applying percentages creates a budgeting rhythm.
- [[concepts/automatic_transfers]] – Automating the allocation reinforces discipline.
- [[concepts/separate_accounts]] – Using distinct accounts for each bucket prevents commingling of funds.
