# Estimated Taxes

**Definition**: Estimated taxes are periodic advance payments of federal (and often state) income tax that self‑employed individuals, such as gig‑economy workers, send to the tax authority throughout the year rather than waiting until the annual filing deadline.

## Why gig workers need them
- Gig income is not subject to employer withholding, so workers must set aside money themselves.
- Paying regularly helps avoid a large, unexpected bill and penalties for underpayment.

## Common safe‑harbor methods (from [[summaries/session-40_2025-03-14]])
1. **30 % of each payment** – a simple percentage allocation that covers federal, self‑employment, and state tax.
2. **Pay‑what‑I‑owed‑last‑year ÷ 4** – locate the *total tax* line on last year’s [[entities/form_1040]], divide by four, and make equal quarterly payments.  This works even if the prior year’s tax was covered mainly by W‑2 withholding.

## How to calculate quarterly payments
- **Step 1**: Find last year’s total tax on Form 1040 (line near the bottom).
- **Step 2**: Divide that amount by four.
- **Step 3**: Pay the resulting figure each quarter using the IRS online portal (or mail [[entities/form_1040_es]]).
- If you start gig work mid‑year, pay only the remaining quarters.

## Where to submit payments
- **Federal**: Use the *Pay IRS* online portal or submit a paper [[entities/form_1040_es]] check.
- **State**: Most states provide a similar online portal; check your state revenue department.

## Practical tip
Set up an automatic transfer (see [[concepts/automatic_transfers]]) that moves the chosen tax percentage from each gig payment into a dedicated tax‑savings account. This makes the estimated‑tax workflow frictionless and ensures you meet quarterly deadlines.
