# Neighborly Rapport

**Definition**: A set of low‑pressure, friendly actions and communication strategies that foster familiarity and goodwill among neighbors who share a common living environment.

## Core Elements

1. **Low‑stakes favors** – Offering to pick up a small grocery item, sharing extra food, or holding a gate. These gestures are framed as "tiny" or "extra" to keep expectations light.
2. **Drop‑in invitations** – Phrasing invites so they can be accepted or declined easily, e.g., "I'm going to sit outside for a bit if you feel like joining. No worries if not."
3. **Brief, warm greetings** – Simple hellos, weather comments, or a smile that signal friendliness without demanding a conversation.
4. **Cue awareness** – Observing body language and response length to gauge how much interaction a neighbor wants at that moment.
5. **Shared‑space respect** – Keeping noise low, cleaning up after yourself, and being mindful of communal areas.

## Application in Session 14

The advice in [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-12]] outlines specific scripts that embody neighborly rapport:
- Offering a quick grocery run: *"I’m heading to the store—need anything tiny while I’m out?"*
- Sharing extra food: *"I ended up with extra—want a bit? Totally fine if you’re good."*
- Inviting a brief garden visit: *"Hey, I’m going to be out in the garden for a bit if you feel like popping out. No worries if not."

These examples illustrate how small, optional gestures can strengthen social bonds without creating obligations.
