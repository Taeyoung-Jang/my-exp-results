# Shared Outdoor Space Etiquette

**Definition**: A set of practices that help residents interact politely and comfortably in communal outdoor areas, balancing friendliness with respect for personal boundaries.

## Core Principles
1. **Warm, brief greetings** – A simple "hi" or comment about the weather signals friendliness without demanding a conversation.
2. **Read cues** – Short answers or hurried movements indicate a preference for minimal interaction; respond with a smile or wave instead of lingering.
3. **Low‑pressure communication** – Keep offers and invitations optional and low‑stakes. Use phrasing that makes declining easy.
4. **Small, helpful gestures** – Offer to pick up a grocery item, share extra food, or hold a gate, framing them as "tiny" favors.
5. **Respect shared space** – Be mindful of noise levels, clean up after yourself, and maintain the area for everyone’s use.

## Practical Scripts (from [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-12]])
- **Garden sit‑down**: "Hey, I’m heading out to the garden for a bit if you feel like popping out. No worries if not."
- **Running errands**: "I’m heading to the store—need anything tiny while I’m out?"
- **Extra food**: "I ended up with extra—want a bit? Totally fine if you’re good."

These scripts embody the principles of [[concepts/low-pressure-communication]] and help build [[concepts/neighborly-rapport]] without creating obligations.

## Benefits
- Fosters a pleasant community atmosphere.
- Reduces social friction and misunderstandings.
- Encourages mutual respect and cooperation in the shared outdoor environment.
