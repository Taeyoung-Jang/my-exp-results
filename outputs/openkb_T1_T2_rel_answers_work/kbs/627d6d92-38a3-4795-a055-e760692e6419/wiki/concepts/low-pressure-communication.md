# Low‑Pressure Communication

**Definition**: A communication approach that conveys warmth and openness while keeping exchanges brief, optional, and free of expectations for further commitment.

## Core Characteristics
- **Brief greetings** – simple "hi" or comment on weather, often accompanied by a smile or wave.
- **Cue sensitivity** – notice short answers, hurried movements, or non‑verbal signals and adjust to a shorter interaction.
- **Low‑stakes offers** – propose small favors (e.g., picking up an item, sharing extra food) framed as *tiny* or *optional*.
- **Drop‑in invites** – phrasing that allows an easy "yes" or "no" without pressure, such as "I’m going to sit outside for a bit if you feel like joining. No worries if not."

## Application in Shared Outdoor Spaces
The concept is especially relevant in settings like the garden shared by residents in [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-12]]. Here, low‑pressure communication helps maintain a friendly atmosphere while respecting personal boundaries.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- Builds a foundation for [[concepts/neighborly-rapport]] by allowing trust to develop gradually.
- Complements [[concepts/shared-outdoor-space-etiquette]] by providing a communication style that supports mutual respect and minimal intrusion.

## Practical Tips
1. **Start with a warm, brief opener** – "Hey, nice day today!"
2. **Observe the neighbor’s response** – if they give a quick answer or turn away, keep the interaction short.
3. **Offer a small, specific favor** – "I’m heading to the store, need anything tiny?"
4. **Use easy‑out phrasing** – "Feel free to join me outside for a bit, no pressure."

By consistently applying low‑pressure communication, residents can nurture a pleasant, cooperative community without feeling forced into extended conversations.
