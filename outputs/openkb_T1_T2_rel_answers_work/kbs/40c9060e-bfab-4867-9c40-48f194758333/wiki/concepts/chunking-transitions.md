# Chunking Transitions

**Definition**: Chunking transitions is the practice of breaking a major life change—such as a move, downsizing, or neighborhood shift—into a series of small, concrete tasks. By focusing on one bite‑size piece at a time, the overall process feels less overwhelming and more actionable.

---

## Why It Matters

- **Reduces cognitive load**: Instead of visualising the whole move as a single massive event, you work on discrete steps, which lowers anxiety.
- **Creates momentum**: Completing a small task provides immediate feedback and motivation to continue.
- **Facilitates emotional processing**: Each chunk can include a brief ritual (e.g., placing a comforting object) that acknowledges grief while moving forward.

---

## Application in a Move (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-13]])

The assistant in the session demonstrated chunking by:
1. **Day 1** – Verify utilities, set up the bed, place a comforting item.
2. **Day 2** – Unpack toiletries and basic kitchen items, take a short walk.
3. **Day 3** – Update address with the post office, bank, and services.
4. **Day 4** – Unpack daily‑use items and assign them permanent spots.
5. **Day 5** – Explore the neighborhood: grocery store, café, longer walk.
6. **Day 6** – Re‑introduce a familiar routine and tidy clutter.
7. **Day 7** – Light tidy and a reflective pause.

Each day contains **3–5** focused actions, illustrating how a complex transition can be expressed as a manageable weekly plan.

---

## Key Practices

- **Identify core categories** (utilities, admin, unpacking, neighborhood exploration, routines).
- **Set 1–3 priority tasks per day** to avoid overload.
- **Pair practical steps with emotional rituals** (e.g., placing a cherished object, a short walk, or a brief reflection).
- **Review and adjust** daily based on what feels most pressing.
