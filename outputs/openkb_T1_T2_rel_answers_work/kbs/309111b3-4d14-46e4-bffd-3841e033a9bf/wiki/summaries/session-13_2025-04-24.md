# Summary of session-13_2025-04-24

- **Core Idea**: Small, flexible adjustments to sleep, exercise, meals, and clothing help maintain comfort and energy when weather shifts unexpectedly. [[concepts/flexible_routine_adjustments]]

- **Routine Tweaks**:
  - Warm up before bed (hot shower, heavier blanket) to improve sleep.
  - Wake up a few minutes earlier to accommodate slower morning movement in cold air.
  - Extend indoor warm‑up before exercising or replace outdoor workouts with indoor activity.
  - Choose warm, substantial meals (soups, oatmeal, roasted vegetables) to offset increased energy expenditure.

- **Layering Strategy**: Use three light layers – a thin base, a breathable mid‑layer, and a wind‑resistant outer layer – to add or remove clothing as temperature changes throughout the day. [[concepts/temperature_layering_strategy]]

- **Fabric Recommendations**:
  - **Base layer**: [[entities/merino_wool]] or [[entities/synthetic_blend]] (warm, breathable, quick‑dry).
  - **Mid layer**: light fleece, polyester grid fleece, or thin synthetic sweater (instead of cotton to avoid clamminess). [[entities/grid_fleece]]
  - **Outer layer**: [[entities/softshell]] or thin windbreaker (blocks wind, remains breathable). [[entities/windbreaker]]
  - Overall guidance aligns with [[concepts/optimal_fabric_choices]].

- **Managing Clammy Feelings**: Replace cotton mid‑layers with synthetic or fleece options; shed layers promptly; keep the outer layer breathable so moisture doesn’t get trapped.

- **Commute‑Specific Mid‑Layer Choices**:
  - **Grid fleece**: best for short chilly walks + indoor sitting – offers warmth and breathability. [[entities/grid_fleece]]
  - **Thin puffer**: suitable for very cold mornings but can feel bulky indoors.
  - **Wool sweater**: good for consistently cool indoor environments but less ventilated during movement.
