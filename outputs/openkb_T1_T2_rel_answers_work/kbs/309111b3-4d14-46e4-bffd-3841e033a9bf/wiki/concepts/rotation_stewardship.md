# Rotating Stewardship

**Definition**: A governance pattern for community gardens in which responsibility for day‑to‑day coordination (e.g., rapid decisions on watering, shade, pest removal) rotates among members on a weekly basis.

## Core Elements
- **Week‑long steward**: One or two gardeners are designated each week to act as the primary contact for time‑sensitive actions.
- **Clear boundaries**: The steward knows which tasks can be performed immediately (e.g., adding temporary shade, emergency watering) and which require group notification or a vote.
- **Documentation**: Any action taken by the steward is recorded in the garden’s on‑site log (whiteboard, binder, or laminated bed tag) to keep others informed.

## How It Works (as described in [[summaries/session-31_2025-06-05]])
1. **Assign rotation**: At the start of the season, volunteers sign up for weekly slots, often coordinated via a [[concepts/shared_calendar]].
2. **Communicate role**: The current steward’s name is posted on the garden’s whiteboard and in any group chat.
3. **Execute immediate actions**: The steward can add temporary shade cloth, provide emergency water, or harvest bolting herbs without waiting for a group response.
4. **Notify for non‑immediate changes**: Adjustments to the regular watering schedule or lightweight supports are logged and broadcast to the group.
5. **Escalate when needed**: For permanent structural changes or budget items, the steward initiates a brief discussion that may lead to a vote at the next weekly check‑in.

## Benefits
- **Speed**: Reduces bottlenecks for urgent garden needs.
- **Shared load**: Distributes the coordination workload evenly across members.
- **Accountability**: A rotating log makes it easy to track who made which decision.
