# Summary of session-08_2025-06-15

## Main ideas
- **Checking buffer**: Keep enough cash to cover normal monthly expenses plus a modest cushion for unexpected bills. A common target is the highest balance needed between paychecks plus a comfort margin.
- **Buffer sizing rule**: Review the past 2‑3 months, note the lowest balance between paychecks, then add a few hundred dollars (or a chosen margin). This becomes the target buffer.
- **Typical size**: For the user, $3,000 was discussed as a reasonable buffer if it comfortably exceeds the low‑point balance and covers the month’s spending.
- **Automation approaches**:
  - **Conditional sweep**: Transfer excess to savings only when the checking balance stays above the buffer (ideal but not always supported).
  - **Scheduled sweep**: Use fixed‑date transfers (e.g., the Wednesday/Thursday after payday) and calculate a fixed amount based on typical post‑payday balances minus the buffer.
- **Timing considerations**: Schedule transfers after major bill dates (rent, credit‑card) to avoid overdrawing. For a bi‑weekly payday, a transfer a few days after each payday works well.
- **Adjustment loop**: Monitor the balance for a month or two, then tweak the transfer amount or buffer if the account consistently runs too high or too low.

## Practical steps outlined
1. **Collect data**: Pull the last 2–3 months of checking balances and identify the low point between paychecks.
2. **Add margin**: Choose a comfort amount (e.g., $200‑$500) and add it to the low point → target buffer.
3. **Set floor**: Keep the target buffer as a minimum balance (e.g., $3,000).
4. **Determine transfer amount**: After each payday, subtract the floor from the typical post‑payday balance; use that difference as the scheduled transfer.
5. **Schedule transfer**: Choose a day after payday that is also after big bill due dates (e.g., Wednesday/Thursday).
6. **Monitor & iterate**: Review balance trends for 30‑60 days, adjust transfer size or buffer as needed.
