# Checking Buffer

A **checking buffer** is a deliberately sized cash reserve held in a checking account to cover day‑to‑day expenses, bill timing, and small financial surprises without needing to dip into overdraft or liquidate investments.

## Why it matters
- Provides a safety net for timing mismatches between income (paychecks) and outflows (rent, utilities, credit‑card payments).
- Prevents idle cash from sitting in a low‑interest checking account longer than necessary.
- Serves as the trigger point for automated moves of excess funds to savings or investment accounts.

## How to calculate a checking buffer
1. **Cash‑flow analysis** – Review the past 2‑3 months of checking balances and identify the lowest balance reached between paychecks. [[concepts/cash-flow-analysis]]
2. **Add a comfort margin** – Add a small, fixed amount (e.g., $200–$500) to that low point to guard against unexpected expenses.
3. **Resulting figure** – The sum becomes your target buffer.  
   *Example from [[summaries/session-08_2025-06-15]]: the user keeps a $3,000 floor because it comfortably exceeds the low‑point balance and covers the month’s spending.*

## Implementing the buffer in practice
- **Set a floor**: Treat the target buffer as the minimum balance your checking account should never fall below.
- **Automated savings sweep**: Use the buffer as a trigger for moving excess cash to a savings or investment account.  
  - If your bank supports conditional transfers, configure a sweep that only occurs when the balance stays above the floor. [[concepts/automated-savings-sweep]]
  - Where conditional logic isn’t available, schedule a fixed‑date transfer after payday, ensuring the amount transferred leaves at least the buffer amount in checking.
- **Timing considerations**: Schedule transfers after major bill due dates (e.g., rent, credit‑card payments) to avoid accidental overdrafts.
- **Adjustment cycle**: Monitor the balance for a month or two; if the buffer is consistently higher than needed, reduce it, or raise it if you frequently approach the floor. This ongoing refinement keeps the buffer aligned with actual cash‑flow patterns.
