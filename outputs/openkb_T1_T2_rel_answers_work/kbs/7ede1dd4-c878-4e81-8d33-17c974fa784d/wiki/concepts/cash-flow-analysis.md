# Cash‑Flow Analysis for Buffer Sizing

**Definition**: A method of reviewing recent checking‑account inflows and outflows to determine the appropriate size of a [[concepts/checking-buffer]] that covers regular expenses and a safety margin.

## Why it matters
- Ensures you keep enough liquid cash to meet month‑to‑month obligations without over‑capitalizing idle funds.
- Provides a data‑driven basis for automating transfers to savings (see [[concepts/automated-savings-sweep]]).

## Step‑by‑step approach (as described in [[summaries/session-08_2025-06-15]])
1. **Collect recent balance data** – Pull the last 2–3 months of checking‑account balances.
2. **Identify the low point** – Find the smallest balance that occurred between paychecks; this reflects the worst‑case cash‑on‑hand.
3. **Add a comfort margin** – Add a fixed amount (e.g., $200‑$500) to the low point. The sum becomes your target [[concepts/checking-buffer]].
4. **Validate against monthly spend** – Compare the target buffer to your average monthly outflows to ensure it covers regular bills plus the margin.
5. **Set automation parameters** – Use the calculated buffer to inform the amount and timing of any scheduled transfers to savings, aligning with the [[concepts/automated-savings-sweep]] strategy.

## Practical tips
- **Look for patterns**: Pay attention to recurring large expenses (rent, credit‑card payments) when selecting the analysis window.
- **Adjust periodically**: Re‑run the analysis quarterly or after any major change in income or expense patterns.
- **Keep it simple**: The calculation is essentially `lowest balance + comfort margin = target buffer`.

## Benefits
- **Confidence**: You know the buffer is grounded in real cash‑flow behaviour.
- **Efficiency**: Excess cash can be automatically swept into higher‑yield accounts without risking overdrafts.
- **Flexibility**: The method adapts to both stable and irregular income streams.
