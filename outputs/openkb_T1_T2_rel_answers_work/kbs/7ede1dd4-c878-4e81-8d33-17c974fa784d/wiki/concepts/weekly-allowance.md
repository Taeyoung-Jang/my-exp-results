# Weekly Allowance

**Definition**: A weekly allowance is a predetermined, single discretionary spending amount set aside for a given week, used to simplify budgeting and minimize constant financial decision‑making.

## Core Elements
- **Fixed discretionary amount** – One clear number you can spend from without tracking each individual expense.
- **Flexibility** – Allows you to adjust the amount week‑by‑week based on cash flow, but keeps the structure consistent.
- **Psychological ease** – Reduces decision fatigue and the feeling of being “policed” on every purchase.

## How It Is Applied in Session‑37 (2025‑12‑13)
In the conversation, the assistant recommended a three‑part weekly structure for a user with $1,200 available and no incoming paycheck:

1. **Savings move** – $100‑$150 (≈8‑12 % of the pot) set aside first.
2. **Checking buffer** – $800‑$900 left untouched for peace of mind.
3. **Weekly allowance** – $150‑$250 left as the discretionary amount for the week.

The weekly allowance is the amount the user can spend freely, keeping the budgeting process low‑stress while still preserving most of the funds.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader **[[concepts/pay-yourself-first]]** strategy, where savings are prioritized before discretionary spend.
- Works alongside a **[[concepts/checking-buffer]]** to ensure financial security.
- Supports **[[concepts/low-stress-budgeting]]** by providing a simple, single‑number spending target.
- Can be implemented using **percentage budgeting** (though that concept is not a separate page here, the idea is implicit in the source).

## Practical Steps to Create a Weekly Allowance
1. **Determine total cash available** for the week (e.g., paycheck, savings, current balance).
2. **Set a savings percentage** (10‑20 %) and move that amount to a savings vehicle.
3. **Establish a checking buffer** (≈65‑75 % of the remaining cash) to keep untouched.
4. **Assign the remainder** as the weekly allowance.
5. **Track only the allowance** for discretionary purchases; avoid dipping into the buffer.

## Benefits
- **Simplicity** – One number to monitor.
- **Control** – Clear limits prevent overspending.
- **Progress** – Regular savings contributions are built in.
- **Mental comfort** – Large buffer reduces anxiety about cash flow.
