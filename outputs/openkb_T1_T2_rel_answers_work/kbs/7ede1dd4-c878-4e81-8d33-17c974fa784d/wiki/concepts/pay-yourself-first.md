# Pay‑Yourself‑First

**Definition**: A budgeting principle that directs you to allocate money to savings (or debt repayment) first, then cover essential bills, and finally use the remainder for discretionary spending. This approach reduces decision fatigue and builds financial habits without feeling restrictive.

## Core Elements
- **Automatic Savings Move** – Transfer a fixed percentage (typically 10‑20 % of incoming cash) into a separate savings account as soon as funds are available. This can be set up as an [[concepts/automated-savings-sweep]] for hands‑free execution.
- **Checking Buffer** – Keep a larger, untouched portion of your checking balance as a safety net, often 65‑75 % of the total cash on hand. This buffer prevents anxiety about low balances and supports [[concepts/low-stress-budgeting]].
- **Weekly Allowance** – The remaining amount becomes a single, clear discretionary spending limit for the week, simplifying daily decisions. See [[concepts/weekly-allowance]] for the mechanics.

## How It Was Applied in Session‑37
In the conversation on 2025‑12‑13, the user had $1,200 available with no incoming paycheck. The assistant suggested:

This example demonstrates the three‑part structure: **pay‑yourself‑first**, **checking‑buffer**, and **weekly‑allowance**, all framed to support a [[concepts/low-stress-budgeting]] mindset.

## Benefits
- **Progressive Savings**: Even small, regular contributions grow over time.
- **Reduced Cognitive Load**: One clear spending number eliminates constant budgeting decisions.
- **Financial Security**: The buffer protects against unexpected expenses.
