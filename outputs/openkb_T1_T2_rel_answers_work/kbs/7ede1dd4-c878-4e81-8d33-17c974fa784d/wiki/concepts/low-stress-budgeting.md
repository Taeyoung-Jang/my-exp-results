# Low‑Stress Budgeting

**Definition**: Low‑stress budgeting is a method that emphasizes minimal friction, using simple rules and clear categories to keep financial management easy and mentally comfortable.

## Core Principles
- **Simplicity** – Use a handful of high‑level buckets instead of tracking every expense.
- **Flexibility** – Apply percentage‑based allocations that can be adjusted without breaking the system.
- **Psychological Ease** – Provide a clear spending allowance while preserving a large buffer, reducing anxiety about running out of money.

## How It Works (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-12-13]])
1. **Pay‑yourself‑first** – Allocate a modest portion of available funds (≈ 10‑20 %) to savings right away. [[concepts/pay-yourself-first]]
2. **Checking Buffer** – Keep a sizable, untouched amount in the checking account to act as a safety net. [[concepts/checking-buffer]]
3. **Weekly Allowance** – Define a single discretionary spending amount for the week, derived from what remains after savings and buffer. [[concepts/weekly-allowance]]
4. **Adjustable Percentages** – Rather than fixed dollar amounts, use rough percentages to keep the system adaptable to varying income or cash levels.

## Example Layout
- **Total cash on hand**: $1,200 (no paycheck this week)
- **Savings move**: $100‑$150 (≈ 8‑12 % of total) – a small, intentional kick‑start.
- **Checking buffer**: $800‑$900 – a large cushion that prevents feeling financially constrained.
- **Weekly allowance**: $150‑$250 – the amount the user can spend freely without guilt.

## Benefits
- **Reduces decision fatigue** – Only one discretionary number to monitor.
- **Maintains financial safety** – The buffer ensures unexpected expenses don’t force panic.
- **Encourages consistent savings** – Even a modest, regular transfer builds habit.
