## Triage Rule

The **triage rule** is a concise guideline for deciding whether a segment of a transcript warrants a brief video check or can be comprehended fully from the text. It helps learners allocate time efficiently while preserving the benefits of visual and tonal cues.

### Core Criteria
1. **Visual reference** – If the line mentions a diagram, chart, demonstration, or any visual element (e.g., “as you can see…”, “this graph shows…”), jump to the video at that timestamp.
2. **Strong emphasis or tone‑dependent meaning** – When the speaker uses heightened emphasis, excitement, or a phrase like “the crucial part is…”, a quick video glimpse can capture tone and intent.
3. **Demo or contextual gesture** – Any mention of a live demo, hand gesture, or physical action that adds meaning beyond the words.

If none of these signals are present, treat the passage as **purely verbal explanation** and stay within the transcript.

### How It Fits Into the Workflow
The rule is applied during **Step 3** of the transcript workflow described in [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]]. After the focused read‑through (Step 2), the learner scans each highlighted line for the three signals above and decides whether to:
- **Jump** – watch the few seconds surrounding the timestamp, then return to the transcript, or
- **Stay** – continue reading without video interruption.

### Related Concepts
- [[concepts/purposeful-preview]] – setting a clear learning intention before starting.
- [[concepts/selective-note-taking]] – focusing notes on high‑value content, which complements the triage rule by limiting unnecessary video checks.

By consistently applying the **triage rule**, learners can capture essential visual context while keeping their study sessions brief and focused.
