# Selective Note Taking

**Definition**: A disciplined approach to capturing only the most valuable information—clear claims, definitions, processes, and examples—while deliberately skipping filler, greetings, and off‑topic digressions.

## Why it matters
Selective note‑taking reduces cognitive overload, speeds up review, and improves retention. By narrowing focus to high‑impact content, learners can create concise notes that are easier to revisit and integrate with existing knowledge.

## Core practices (from [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]])
1. **Identify high‑value sentences** – Look for statements that present a claim, define a term, describe a process, or give a concrete example.
2. **Ignore filler** – Skip small talk, greetings, and tangential anecdotes that do not add substantive meaning.
3. **Highlight sparingly** – Use a single color or marker to denote the selected sentences; avoid over‑highlighting which defeats the purpose.
4. **Pair with purpose** – Before reading or watching, set a clear learning goal (see [[concepts/purposeful-preview]]) to guide what counts as “core” information.
5. **Apply triage** – When a highlighted line references a visual cue or strong emphasis, use the [[concepts/triage-rule]] to decide whether a brief video check is needed; otherwise keep the note transcript‑only.

## Benefits
- **Speed**: Faster initial note‑taking and later review.
- **Clarity**: Notes stay focused on actionable insights.
- **Memory**: Concise, meaningful notes reinforce recall when revisited.
