# iPhone (product)

The **iPhone** is the hardware platform referenced throughout [[summaries/session-34_2024-12-16]] as the core device for an audio‑first knowledge acquisition system.

## Key facts from the session
- Serves as the host for all recommended apps: [[entities/voice-dream-reader]] for text‑to‑speech, [[entities/overcast]] and [[entities/airr]] for podcast clipping, and [[entities/noted]] / [[entities/audio-pen]] for voice‑note capture.
- Uses **Apple Notes** (via [[entities/apple-notes]]) as a single “Audio Inbox” where clips, TTS highlights, and transcribed voice notes are appended.
- Relies on **Apple Shortcuts** ( [[entities/apple-shortcuts]] ) to automate a **[[concepts/shortcuts-workflow]]** that receives shared content from any app and appends it to the hub note.
- Leverages the built‑in **Speak Screen** feature (part of [[entities/apple]] accessibility) to read text‑based highlights aloud, supporting the **[[concepts/text-to-speech]]** approach.
- Encourages **[[concepts/active-listening]]** by prompting users to pause, recap, or record brief voice reflections while listening.

## Typical workflow on iPhone
1. **Create an “Audio Inbox”** note in [[entities/apple-notes]].
2. **Share** podcast clips from [[entities/overcast]] or [[entities/airr]] → Shortcut → append to the note.
3. **Highlight** article or PDF text in [[entities/voice-dream-reader]] → share → Shortcut → append as text.
4. **Record** thoughts in [[entities/noted]] or [[entities/audio-pen]] → auto‑export or share → Shortcut → append audio + transcript.
5. **Review** by opening the single note, turning on **Speak Screen** for text, and tapping any embedded audio files.

This workflow demonstrates how the iPhone can act as a lightweight, eye‑friendly hub for consuming and revisiting information primarily through sound.
