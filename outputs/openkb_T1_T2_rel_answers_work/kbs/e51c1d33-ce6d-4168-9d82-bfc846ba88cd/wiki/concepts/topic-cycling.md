# Topic Cycling

**Definition**: Topic cycling is the systematic rotation of language‑learning themes—especially grammar points—across successive study sessions so that learners continually revisit and expand on core concepts.

## Why Cycle Topics?
- **Retention**: Revisiting a topic after a short interval reinforces memory and moves information from short‑term to long‑term storage.
- **Balanced Skill Growth**: By alternating focus (e.g., ser/estar → preterite → subjunctive), learners maintain progress in speaking, listening, and reading without over‑emphasizing a single area.
- **Motivation**: Changing the focus each day keeps study sessions fresh and reduces monotony.

## How It Works in the Session‑11 Plan
The 2‑week schedule in [[summaries/session-11_2024-06-15]] demonstrates a concrete implementation of topic cycling:

| Day | Grammar Focus (20 min) | Speaking Prompt (5 min) | Listening Type |
|-----|------------------------|--------------------------|----------------|
| 1‑2 | ser / estar (identity, location, temporary states) | Describe who you are, where you are, how you feel | Slow‑Spanish news podcast |
| 3‑4 | Irregular present‑tense verbs (tener, hacer, salir…) | Talk about your daily routine | Graded learner dialogue |
| 5‑6 | Direct & indirect object pronouns | Discuss things you buy, give, or show | Short dialogue or app exercise |
| 7‑8 | Preterite vs. imperfect (comparison on day 7) | Mini‑story mixing scene setting & actions | Beginner podcast with anecdotes |
| 9‑10| Simple future tense | Plans for next week | Beginner YouTube lesson |
| 11‑12| Conditional tense | Hypothetical wishes (more money, time) | Storytelling podcast |
| 13‑14| Present subjunctive | Wishes, hopes, simple recommendations | Beginner dialogue |

Each two‑day block revisits a core grammar theme, providing a focused study window while the surrounding speaking and listening activities keep the language active.

## Practical Tips for Implementing Topic Cycling
1. **Plan a Cycle Calendar** – Outline a 2‑week (or longer) map of topics before you start.
2. **Keep Each Slot Small** – Aim for 15‑20 minutes of new material, then use the following speaking and listening blocks to apply it.
3. **Use Consistent Prompts** – Pair each grammar point with a specific, repeatable speaking prompt (as shown in the schedule) to create a clear production goal.
4. **Track Progress** – Mark which topics you’ve covered and note any lingering difficulties; revisit problematic topics in the next cycle.
5. **Adjust Lengths as Needed** – If a topic feels dense, extend its block to 2‑3 days while shortening others.

By repeatedly cycling through essential Spanish grammar points, learners maintain steady momentum across all language domains while preventing skill plateaus.
