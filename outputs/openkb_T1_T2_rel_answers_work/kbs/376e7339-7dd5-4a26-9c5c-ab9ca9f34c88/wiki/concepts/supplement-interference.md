## Supplement Interference with Medication Absorption

**Definition**: The impact that certain dietary supplements, foods, and medications have on the gastrointestinal absorption of oral thyroid hormones, especially levothyroxine, and on the accuracy of thyroid function laboratory tests.

---

### Key Interfering Substances
| Substance / Category | Typical Effect on Levothyroxine | Timing Recommendation |
|----------------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------|
| **Calcium** (e.g., calcium carbonate, calcium citrate) | Binds levothyroxine in the gut, reducing absorption by up to 40% | Take **≥4 hours** after levothyroxine |
| **Iron** (ferrous sulfate, ferrous gluconate) | Forms insoluble complexes with levothyroxine, lowering bioavailability | Take **≥4 hours** after levothyroxine |
| **Multivitamins** containing calcium, iron, or magnesium | Same binding effect as the individual minerals | Take **≥4 hours** after levothyroxine |
| **High‑dose fiber** (psyllium, methylcellulose) | Can adsorb levothyroxine and speed intestinal transit | Take **≥4 hours** after levothyroxine |
| **Coffee** (especially brewed coffee) | Decreases levothyroxine absorption by ~20% when taken immediately after the pill | Wait **45–60 min** before coffee |
| **Breakfast / food** (particularly soy, high‑fat meals) | Food can delay gastric emptying and compete for transport mechanisms | Wait **45–60 min** before eating |
| **Proton‑pump inhibitors (PPIs)** (omeprazole, esomeprazole) | Raise gastric pH, which can modestly reduce levothyroxine dissolution | Keep a **few‑hour gap**; inform clinician |
| **Antacids** (aluminum, magnesium hydroxide) | Directly bind levothyroxine, similar to calcium/iron | Keep a **2–4 hour gap** |
| **Biotin** (high‑dose supplements) | Does **not** affect absorption but can cause falsely high/low thyroid assay results | Record use; avoid during lab draw if possible |

---

### Practical Scheduling Guidelines
1. **Morning regimen (most common)**
   - Take levothyroxine with plain water on an empty stomach.
   - Wait **45–60 minutes** before coffee or any food.
   - Ensure calcium, iron, multivitamins, fiber supplements are taken **≥4 hours** later (e.g., mid‑morning or lunch).
2. **Bedtime regimen (alternative)**
   - Take levothyroxine **2–3 hours after dinner**, ensuring the stomach is relatively empty.
   - Maintain the same schedule daily for consistency.
3. **Consistency is critical** – stick to the chosen timing for **4–6 weeks** before drawing thyroid labs to allow steady‑state hormone levels.

---

### Why Timing Matters for Lab Tests
- Levothyroxine has a **half‑life of ~7 days**; steady‑state concentrations take 5–6 weeks after dose or timing changes.
- Interfering substances can cause **artificially low free T4** or **elevated TSH**, leading clinicians to adjust the dose unnecessarily.
- Biotin can **distort assay readings** for TSH, free T4, and free T3, potentially prompting incorrect dose changes.
