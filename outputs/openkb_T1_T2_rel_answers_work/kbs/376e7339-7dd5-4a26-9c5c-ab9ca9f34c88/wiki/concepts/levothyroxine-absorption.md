# Levothyroxine Absorption

Levothyroxine (synthetic T4) is best absorbed when the stomach is empty.  The drug’s bioavailability can be reduced dramatically by simultaneous intake of certain foods, drinks, and supplements, leading to misleading laboratory results and persistent symptoms.

## Core Principles
| Guideline | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Take on an empty stomach** – usually first thing in the morning with plain water. | Stomach acidity and lack of competing minerals maximize absorption. |
| **Wait 45–60 minutes before coffee or breakfast**. | Caffeine and food can lower absorption by up to 30 %. |
| **Avoid calcium, iron, multivitamins, and high‑dose fiber for at least 4 hours** after the pill. | These minerals bind levothyroxine and prevent it from entering the bloodstream. |
| **If taken at bedtime**, wait **2–3 hours after dinner** before swallowing the tablet. | Ensures the stomach is relatively empty and reduces interaction with dinner nutrients. |
| **Maintain a consistent schedule** (same time each day, same interval to meals). | Hormone levels (TSH, free T4, free T3) need a stable dosing pattern to reach equilibrium. |

## Common Interfering Substances
- **Calcium supplements** (including calcium‑containing antacids)
- **Iron supplements** (including ferrous sulfate)
- **Multivitamins** containing calcium or iron
- **High‑dose fiber** (e.g., psyllium)
- **Coffee** (especially within an hour of dosing)
- **PPIs/antacids** (require a few‑hour gap)
- **Biotin** – does not affect absorption but can **distort lab assays** (e.g., TSH, free T4).

These interactions are summarized in the related [[concepts/supplement-interference]] page.

## Practical Routine Example
1. **Morning**: Take [[entities/levothyroxine]] with a glass of water at 07:00.
2. **Wait** 45 minutes → have coffee or breakfast at 07:45+. 
3. **Log** timing, heart rate, and any symptoms (see [[concepts/symptom-tracking-template]]). 
4. **Take calcium/iron/fiber** after 11:00 (≥4 h later).
5. **If preferred bedtime dosing**: take the pill at 22:00, ensuring dinner finished by 19:00‑20:00.

## Impact on Laboratory Testing
- **TSH** and **free T4** are the primary tests used to adjust dose.  After any change in timing or dose, allow **4–6 weeks** for levels to stabilize before drawing [[entities/tsh-test]], [[entities/free-t4-test]], and optionally [[entities/free-t3-test]].
- Inconsistent timing can produce fluctuating results that mask the true therapeutic state, leading to unnecessary dose adjustments.

## Tracking for Clinicians
Consistent documentation of:
- Levothyroxine dose time
- Meal and coffee timing
- Supplement intake
- Resting heart rate and symptom notes

helps clinicians interpret lab values accurately.  A concise daily log template is described in [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-16]].
