## TSH Test (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone Test)

The **TSH test** measures the level of thyroid‑stimulating hormone in the blood and is a primary tool for evaluating thyroid function, especially after **[[entities/thyroid-surgery]]** or during **[[entities/levothyroxine]]** dose adjustments.

### Role in Clinical Management (as discussed in [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-16]])
- Ordered routinely alongside **[[entities/free-t4-test]]**; **[[entities/free-t3-test]]** may be added if symptoms do not align with TSH/Free‑T4 results.
- Used to determine whether levothyroxine dosing is adequate; a high TSH suggests under‑replacement, while a low TSH indicates possible overtreatment.
- Clinicians advise patients to keep medication timing and supplement intake consistent for **4–6 weeks** before drawing the test, ensuring hormone levels have stabilized.

### Practical Patient Guidance
- Track symptoms with a structured daily log (see **[[concepts/symptom-tracking-template]]**) and bring the log to the appointment.
- Note any **[[concepts/supplement-interference]]** (e.g., calcium, iron, coffee) that could affect levothyroxine absorption, as these can also influence TSH results.
- Maintain the same **levothyroxine** intake schedule (morning on an empty stomach or bedtime with a proper gap after dinner) during the tracking period.
