# Free T4 Test (Work)

The **Free T4 Test** quantifies the concentration of unbound thyroxine (T4) circulating in the blood. Unlike total T4, it reflects the hormone that is biologically active and available for tissue uptake, making it a crucial marker for assessing thyroid function, especially in patients on levothyroxine therapy.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-16]]
- **Primary Use**: Used alongside the [[entities/tsh-test]] to monitor dosage adjustments after [[entities/thyroid-surgery]].
- **When Ordered**: Recommended in the session‑39 guidance when patient symptoms (energy, temperature, mood) do not align with TSH results, or when clinicians consider adding a [[concepts/levothyroxine-absorption]] check.
- **Relation to Other Tests**: May be paired with the [[entities/free-t3-test]] if free T3 is needed to explain persistent symptoms.
- **Timing for Reliable Results**: The document advises keeping a consistent levothyroxine dosing schedule for **4–6 weeks** before drawing labs, ensuring the Free T4 measurement reflects steady‑state hormone levels.

## Clinical Context
- **Monitoring Levothyroxine**: Free T4 helps determine whether the replacement dose is sufficient, especially when TSH is borderline or when patients experience fluctuating symptoms.
- **Interpretation**: A low free T4 with a normal or mildly elevated TSH may suggest under‑replacement, whereas a high free T4 with suppressed TSH can indicate overtreatment.
- **Interaction with Supplements**: The test can be affected indirectly by supplement interference (e.g., biotin) that may skew assay results; thus clinicians are warned to note biotin intake.
