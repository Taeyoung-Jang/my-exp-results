## Free T3 Test

**What it is**
The *Free T3 Test* quantifies the amount of unbound triiodothyronine (T3) hormone in serum. Free T3 is the biologically active fraction of thyroid hormone that can enter cells and exert metabolic effects.

**Why it matters**
In the context of post‑thyroidectomy levothyroxine management (see [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-16]]), Free T3 can be requested when patient symptoms (energy swings, temperature sensitivity, mood changes) do not align with the more common labs—TSH and free T4. It helps clinicians assess whether peripheral conversion of T4 to T3 is adequate or if a combination therapy may be needed.

**When it is ordered**
- Persistent symptoms despite target TSH and free T4 levels.
- Evaluation of potential over‑ or under‑replacement of levothyroxine.
- Investigation of rare disorders of thyroid hormone metabolism.

**Interpretation basics**
- **Low free T3** may indicate insufficient conversion, malabsorption, or overtreatment with levothyroxine alone.
- **High free T3** can suggest excess dosing, supplemental T3 usage, or rare hyperthyroid states.

**Relation to other tests**
- Often ordered alongside [[entities/tsh-test]] and [[entities/free-t4-test]] to provide a full thyroid panel.
- Results can be influenced by **supplement interference** (e.g., biotin) – see [[concepts/supplement-interference]].

**Clinical workflow example**
1. Patient logs daily symptoms using a [[concepts/symptom-tracking-template]] (energy, temperature, mood, heart rate, medication timing).
2. After 2–3 weeks of consistent logging, the clinician reviews the log and orders TSH, free T4, and optionally free T3.
3. Lab values are interpreted in conjunction with the symptom log to fine‑tune levothyroxine dosing.
