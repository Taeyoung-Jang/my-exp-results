# Levothyroxine

Levothyroxine is a synthetic form of the thyroid hormone thyroxine (T4) prescribed to treat hypothyroidism and to replace thyroid hormone after [[entities/thyroid-surgery]]. It is taken orally, typically once daily.

## Clinical use
- Primary treatment for patients without a functioning thyroid gland.
- Dosage is individualized; adjustments are guided by serum [[entities/tsh-test]] and [[entities/free-t4-test]] levels, with occasional [[entities/free-t3-test]] checks when symptoms do not match lab results.

## Absorption considerations
- Levothyroxine absorption can be significantly reduced by concurrent intake of certain supplements and foods. See [[concepts/supplement-interference]] for details.
- Recommended timing: take with water on an empty stomach, wait **45–60 minutes** before breakfast or coffee. A **4‑hour** gap is advised before calcium, iron, multivitamins, high‑dose fiber, or antacids/PPIs.
- An alternative bedtime regimen is acceptable if the pill is taken **2–3 hours after dinner**, ensuring the stomach is relatively empty.

## Monitoring & tracking
- Patients are encouraged to keep a daily symptom log to help clinicians adjust dosage. A practical template is described in [[concepts/symptom-tracking-template]].
- Key daily variables to record include energy, temperature sensitivity, mood, sleep quality, resting heart rate, levothyroxine timing, and any interfering supplements.
- Consistency in dosing schedule for at least **4–6 weeks** before drawing labs improves reliability of [[entities/tsh-test]] and hormone measurements.
