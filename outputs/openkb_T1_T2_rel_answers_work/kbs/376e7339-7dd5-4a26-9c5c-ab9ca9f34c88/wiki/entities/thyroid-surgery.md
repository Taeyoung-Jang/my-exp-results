# Thyroid Surgery (event)

Thyroid surgery refers to operative procedures that remove all or part of the thyroid gland, commonly performed for conditions such as thyroid cancer, nodular disease, or uncontrolled hyperthyroidism. In the context of [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-16]], the patient underwent thyroid surgery in the previous fall and is now managing hormone replacement with [[entities/levothyroxine]].

## Key Facts from the Session
- **Post‑surgical hormone management**: After total thyroidectomy, the body no longer produces endogenous thyroid hormone, requiring lifelong levothyroxine therapy.
- **Dose titration**: The endocrinologist adjusts levothyroxine dose every few weeks to stabilize energy, temperature, and mood.
- **Monitoring**: Regular tracking of symptoms and consistency in medication timing are crucial for effective dose adjustments.
- **Laboratory testing**: Follow‑up labs typically include [[entities/tsh-test]] and [[entities/free-t4-test]]; [[entities/free-t3-test]] may be added if symptoms do not align with TSH/free‑T4 results.

## Practical Takeaways
1. **Consistent dosing schedule** – take levothyroxine on an empty stomach, wait 45–60 minutes before food or coffee, or follow a bedtime protocol with a 2–3 hour gap after dinner.
2. **Track symptoms** – use the symptom‑tracking template for 2–3 weeks before appointments and maintain the same schedule for 4–6 weeks before labs to ensure reliable results.
3. **Communicate supplement use** – log calcium, iron, multivitamins, fiber, PPIs, and biotin, as these can affect both absorption and lab assay accuracy.
