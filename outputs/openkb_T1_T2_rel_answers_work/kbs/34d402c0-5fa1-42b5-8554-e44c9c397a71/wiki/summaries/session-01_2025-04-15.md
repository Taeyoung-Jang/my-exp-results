## Summary of session-01_2025-04-15

- **Weather’s impact on daily routines** – The conversation highlights how temperature and daylight fluctuations affect sleep, mood, appetite, and overall energy.

- **Core sleep‑hygiene recommendations** – Keep wake‑time consistent year‑round, consider going to bed slightly earlier in winter, and use gentle “ramp‑up” cues rather than abrupt alarms. See [[concepts/sleep_hygiene]].

- **Light therapy as a primary cue** – Immediately upon waking, turn on a bright lamp or sunrise‑style light; if possible, step outside for a minute of natural light. When outdoors is too cold, use a window with the brightest daylight plus a strong indoor lamp. This is covered by [[concepts/light_therapy]].

- **Morning movement** – A brief (2‑3 min) sequence of easy stretches, shoulder rolls, and slow squats warms the body and signals wakefulness. This fits within the broader idea of a structured [[concepts/morning_routine]].

- **Hydration first** – Drink water right after the light and movement phase before consuming caffeine. Proper hydration supports energy levels.

- **Caffeine timing** – Wait roughly 10 minutes after water intake (or after the movement phase) before having coffee or tea, allowing natural wake‑up hormones to act and making caffeine effects cleaner.

- **Adaptations for very cold mornings** – If stepping outside is uncomfortable, stand near a bright window, or briefly open the door to catch a flash of outdoor light. The rest of the routine remains unchanged, emphasizing the importance of preserving light and movement cues.

- **Overall 15‑20 minute wake‑up plan**  
  1. Turn on bright indoor light (or sunrise lamp).  
  2. Perform 2‑3 min of gentle stretches/movements.  
  3. Get 1‑2 min of outdoor (or bright window) light if feasible.  
  4. Drink water.  
  5. Wait ~10 min, then have coffee/tea.  

This low‑effort routine counters lethargy caused by cold, dark mornings and can be tweaked for extreme weather conditions.
