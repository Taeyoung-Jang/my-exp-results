## Summary of Session 38 (2025-10-15)

**Main focus:** Practical strategies for keeping an apartment comfortable and energy‑efficient during prolonged high‑heat conditions, with an emphasis on sleep quality.

### Key Recommendations

- **Block heat ingress** – Close blinds/curtains, use blackout liners or temporary reflective film on sun‑facing windows. passive cooling
- **Ventilation timing** – Open windows only when outdoor temperature is lower (usually overnight/early morning). ventilation strategy
- **Target temperature settings** – Nighttime ≈ 74–76 °F; daytime setback ≈ 78–80 °F. Keep the setback under a 4–5 °F swing to avoid excessive recovery load. thermostat setback
- **Use fans wisely** – Position a fan to pull cooler night air through an open window or to circulate air over the bed. air circulation
- **Personal cooling** – Cool shower before bed, breathable bedding, cold packs or damp cloth on pulse points. personal cooling methods
- **Energy‑saving AC modes** – Set AC to “eco”/“auto” mode, raise thermostat a few degrees while out, and avoid running it flat‑out all day. energy efficient cooling
- **Micro‑climate exploitation** – Sleep in cooler zones (lower floors, shadier rooms) or on the floor to stay cooler.

### Signs of Excessive Heat Gain

- AC runs > 1 hour with little temperature drop.
- Indoor temperature continues to rise despite AC running.
- Walls, furniture, or other surfaces feel noticeably warm late in the day.

These indicate the setback is too large or passive measures are insufficient. heat soak detection

### Low‑Cost Rental Fixes

1. **Window treatments** – Thick curtains, blackout liners, or removable reflective film.
2. **Weather‑stripping** – Draft stoppers for windows/doors to limit hot air infiltration.
3. **Reduce internal heat sources** – Use lamps instead of overhead fixtures, avoid oven use on hot days.
4. **Portable fans** – Affordable way to improve perceived temperature without extra AC load.

### Additional Comfort Hacks

- Stay hydrated to aid the body’s thermoregulation.
- Use cold packs or damp cloths on wrists, neck, or feet for quick relief.
- Consider public AC‑cooled spaces (libraries, cafés) during peak heat if possible.
