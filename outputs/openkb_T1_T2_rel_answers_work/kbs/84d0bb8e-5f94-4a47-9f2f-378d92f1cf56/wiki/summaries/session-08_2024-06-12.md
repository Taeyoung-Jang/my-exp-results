# Summary of session-08_2024-06-12

**Main focus** – How to develop reliable body awareness that distinguishes normal bodily sensations from signals that need medical attention, and a compact decision‑tree to guide action.

## Key ideas
- **Baseline awareness** – Regularly check in with your body during neutral moments to establish what feels “normal” for you. This creates a personal baseline and reduces over‑analysis of every ache. See [[concepts/body_awareness]].
- **Gentle noticing** – Give mild, familiar sensations time to resolve before reacting. Observe patterns rather than isolated episodes.
- **Decision‑tree framework** – A four‑step guide (see [[concepts/symptom_decision_tree]]):
  1. **Wait** – Mild, familiar, self‑resolving (e.g., brief muscle twinge, transient headache).
  2. **Track** – Non‑severe but recurring or uncertain symptoms. Jot down:
     - when it occurred  
     - duration  
     - intensity  
     - recent activity/food  
     - factors that improve or worsen it  
  3. **Schedule a visit** – New or persistent symptoms that last days, interfere with daily life, or change noticeably (e.g., daily headache, ongoing abdominal discomfort).
  4. **Urgent/ER** – Sudden, severe, or safety‑related signs (red‑flags). Examples include:
     - **Headache**: worst ever, abrupt onset, visual changes, speech difficulty, unilateral weakness.  
     - **Abdominal pain**: intense, unrelenting, fever, vomiting, rigid abdomen.  
     - **Chest symptoms**: pressure/pain radiating to arm/jaw, breathing difficulty, dizziness, fainting.  
   See [[concepts/red_flags]].
- **Cautionary principle** – When unsure, err on the side of the more cautious option.

## Practical takeaways
- Incorporate brief, neutral body checks into daily routine.  
- Use the decision‑tree as a quick mental filter rather than a rigid checklist.  
- Keep tracking notes ultra‑simple to avoid analysis paralysis.  
- Recognize and act promptly on red‑flag symptoms to ensure safety.
