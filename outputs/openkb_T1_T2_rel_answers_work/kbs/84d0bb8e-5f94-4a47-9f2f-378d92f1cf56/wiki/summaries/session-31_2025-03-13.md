# Summary of session-31_2025-03-13

**Date:** 2025‑03‑13  

### Core Idea
The user wants gentle, quick **body awareness** checks during routine errands (shopping, commuting, chores) that avoid turning into anxiety‑driven monitoring. The assistant provides concrete cues, scripts, and *if/then* rules that keep the practice neutral, brief, and functional.

### Key Concepts & Techniques
- **Single‑cue check‑in** – pick one natural pause (e.g., reaching for an item, waiting at a crosswalk) and notice a single bodily sensation for a few seconds. [[concepts/single_cue_check_in]]
- **Neutral scripts** – short phrasing that ends with “moving on” to prevent decision‑making about health. Example: “Noticing, then moving on.” [[concepts/neutral_script]]
- **Weather metaphor** – “How’s the weather in my body?” treats sensations like a weather report, keeping observation non‑judgmental. *(plain text)*
- **Fallback acknowledgement** – “Okay, I hear you,” followed by returning to the next action, to acknowledge discomfort without spiralling. *(plain text)*
- **If/Then habit rules** – three concrete patterns for shopping:
  1. *If* a twinge is noticed → *then* pause for one slow breath, relax shoulders, continue walking.
  2. *If* the mind jumps to worry → *then* silently name the current action for ~10 s, then continue.
  3. *If* general tension is felt → *then* shift weight or adjust posture once, then continue. [[concepts/if_then_shopping_rules]]
- **Break guideline** – take a short rest or head to checkout **only** when discomfort persists enough to impede the next small step (e.g., reading a label). This ties the break to functional impact rather than fear. *(plain text)*

### Practical Takeaways
1. **Select one cue per errand** rather than scanning every aisle.  
2. **Use a 5‑10 s neutral script** that always ends with returning to the task.  
3. **Apply the three if/then rules** to keep checks brief and action‑oriented.  
4. **Reserve a break** for truly distracting discomfort, not for every sensation.  

These suggestions aim to embed **body awareness** as a background habit—like checking the weather—so it supports well‑being without becoming an obsessive health test. [[concepts/body_awareness]]
