# Single‑Cue Check‑In

**Definition**: A *single‑cue check‑in* is a micro‑practice of pausing at a naturally occurring moment in an everyday activity (e.g., reaching for an item, waiting at a crosswalk) to briefly notice one bodily sensation—such as breath, posture, or a small ache—without evaluating, fixing, or deciding anything about it.

**Key Elements**
- **Cue selection**: Choose a cue that already exists in the routine so the check‑in feels like a seamless part of the flow, not an added task.  
- **Duration**: Keep the observation to 5–10 seconds.  
- **Neutral focus**: Notice a single sensation (e.g., “how does my breath feel?”) and then return to the next action.  
- **No decision‑making**: The practice is observational only; it does not involve deciding whether you are “okay” to continue.

**Why it matters**
- Supports the broader practice of [[concepts/body_awareness]] by embedding awareness in daily life.  
- Prevents the drift into anxiety‑driven monitoring that can happen when the check‑in becomes a health test.

**Relationship to other concepts**
- Works hand‑in‑hand with a [[concepts/neutral_script]] such as “Noticing, then moving on,” which reinforces the non‑judgmental nature of the check‑in.
- Provides the foundational cue for the more elaborate [[concepts/if_then_shopping_rules]] that give concrete *if/then* actions during shopping.
- Can be combined with [[concepts/red_flags]] or a [[concepts/symptom_decision_tree]] when a sensation persists or becomes functionally impairing, prompting a different response (e.g., a short break).

**Example from practice**
> While reaching for a box of cereal, pause for a breath, feel the shoulders for a moment, note any tension, then continue to the next aisle.
