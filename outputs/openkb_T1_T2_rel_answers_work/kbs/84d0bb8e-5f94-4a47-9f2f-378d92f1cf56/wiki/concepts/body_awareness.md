# Body Awareness

**Definition** – Brief, neutral body check‑ins that build a personal baseline and help notice changes without anxiety.

## Core Elements
- **Neutral Check‑ins**: Pause during routine moments (e.g., morning, before meals, waiting at a crosswalk) to note overall bodily sensations without any specific complaint.
- **Baseline Building**: Regular observations create a reference for your unique patterns of breath, muscle tone, heart rate, and mild discomfort.
- **Gentle Noticing**: When a new or unusual sensation appears, give it a short observation window before reacting. Most minor aches fade within minutes to hours.
- **Pattern Awareness**: Focus on recurring trends rather than isolated incidents. Persistent or changing sensations are more likely to be significant.
- **Single‑Cue Check‑in**: Choose one natural cue in an activity (e.g., reaching for an item, standing in line) and use that moment for a quick body scan. [[concepts/single_cue_check_in]]
- **Neutral Script**: Use a brief phrase that ends with returning to the task, such as “Noticing, then moving on.” [[concepts/neutral_script]]
- **If/Then Rules**: Apply simple habit loops while shopping, e.g., “If I notice a twinge, then I pause for one slow breath, relax my shoulders, and keep walking.” [[concepts/if_then_shopping_rules]]

## Practical Steps
### From [[summaries/session-08_2024-06-12]]
1. **Set a Routine** – Choose 2‑3 neutral times each day to simply “scan” your body.
2. **Record Minimal Details** – If you notice something atypical, note when, duration, intensity, recent activity/food, and anything that helped or worsened it. (These notes feed into the decision‑tree "Track" stage.)
3. **Apply the Decision Tree** – Use the hierarchy Wait → Track → Schedule a visit → Urgent/ER to decide the next action. [[concepts/symptom_decision_tree]]

### Added from [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-13]]
4. **Integrate Single‑Cue Check‑ins** – During errands, pick a single cue (e.g., the moment you put an item in your cart) and briefly notice breath or foot sensation for 5‑10 seconds.
5. **Use Neutral Scripts** – Adopt a fixed phrasing like “Noticing, then moving on” or “How’s the weather in my body?” to keep the check‑in non‑evaluative.
6. **Apply Specific If/Then Rules** –
   - *If* a small twinge appears, *then* take one slow breath, relax shoulders, and continue walking.
   - *If* your mind jumps to worry, *then* silently name the current action for about ten seconds before proceeding.
   - *If* general tension is felt, *then* shift your weight or adjust posture once and keep going.
7. **Fallback Acknowledgement** – When discomfort arises, quietly say “Okay, I hear you,” then shift attention back to the next activity without analysis.
8. **Break Guideline** – If a sensation persists enough to impair the next simple step (e.g., reading a label), allow a brief rest or head to checkout early.

- Reduces anxiety and over‑analysis of everyday aches.
- Promotes timely medical attention for true warning signs.
- Encourages a calm, curious mindset toward one’s own physiology.
