# Red Flags (Medical)

**Definition** – Red‑flag symptoms are specific signs or patterns that suggest a potentially serious underlying condition and warrant prompt medical attention, often emergency care.

## Common Red‑Flag Categories (from [[summaries/session-08_2024-06-12]])

| System | Red‑Flag Examples |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Headache** | "worst headache of your life", sudden onset, visual changes, difficulty speaking, unilateral weakness |
| **Abdominal Pain** | Intense, unrelenting pain, fever with worsening pain, repeated vomiting, rigid or swollen abdomen |
| **Chest Symptoms** | Chest pressure or pain radiating to arm/jaw, trouble breathing, sudden dizziness or fainting |

## Why Red Flags Matter
- They help differentiate normal body noise from signals that could indicate serious pathology.
- Recognizing red flags enables timely escalation to urgent or emergency care, reducing risk of complications.

## Integration with a Symptom Decision Process
The red‑flag criteria fit into the broader [[concepts/symptom_decision_tree]]:
1. **Wait** – mild, familiar sensations that resolve.
2. **Track** – recurring but non‑severe symptoms.
3. **Schedule a visit** – persistent or changing issues.
4. **Urgent/ER** – presence of any red‑flag sign triggers this step.

When a symptom matches a red‑flag pattern, the decision tree jumps directly to the **Urgent/ER** branch, bypassing the "wait" or "track" stages.

## Relationship to Body Awareness
Developing personal baseline awareness ([[concepts/body_awareness]]) improves the ability to spot deviations that may constitute red flags. Knowing what feels normal makes the emergence of warning signs more apparent.
