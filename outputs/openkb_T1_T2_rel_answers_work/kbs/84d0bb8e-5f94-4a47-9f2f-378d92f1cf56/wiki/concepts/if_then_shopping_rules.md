# If/Then Shopping Rules

**Type:** Concept

**Related Summary:** [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-13]]

## Definition
The *If/Then Shopping Rules* are a trio of concise, action‑oriented guidelines that let you incorporate gentle body‑awareness into grocery shopping without slipping into anxiety‑driven monitoring. Each rule follows a simple *if* (trigger) → *then* (brief response) pattern and ends by returning you to the task at hand.

## The Three Core Rules
1. **Twinge Rule** – *If* you notice a small twinge, *then* pause for one slow breath, relax your shoulders, and continue down the aisle.
2. **Worry Rule** – *If* your mind jumps to worry, *then* silently name the current action (e.g., “I’m choosing pasta”) for about ten seconds, then carry on.
3. **General Tension Rule** – *If* you feel a vague wave of tension, *then* shift your weight or adjust your posture once, and resume shopping.

These rules are designed to be **5–10 seconds** long, keeping the check‑in brief and neutral.

## How They Fit Within a Broader Practice
- They are an application of the **[[concepts/single_cue_check_in]]** approach: each rule is triggered by a natural pause in the shopping flow.
- The response uses a **[[concepts/neutral_script]]** (“pause, notice, then move on”) to avoid turning the observation into a health decision.
- If discomfort persists beyond the brief response, the shopper can refer to a **[[concepts/red_flags]]** guideline—e.g., taking a short break when the sensation interferes with the next step.
- The overall practice supports **[[concepts/body_awareness]]** by embedding low‑stakes self‑checks into everyday errands.

## When to Escalate
If a sensation does not subside after the brief *then* step and begins to impair functional tasks (reading a label, choosing an item), the shopper may move to a more structured **[[concepts/symptom_decision_tree]]** or simply pause the trip and rest.
