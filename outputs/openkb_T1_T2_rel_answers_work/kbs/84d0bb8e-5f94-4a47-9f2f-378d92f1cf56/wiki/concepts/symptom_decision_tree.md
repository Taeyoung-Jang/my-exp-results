# Symptom Decision Tree

**Definition** – A concise, non‑diagnostic hierarchy that helps individuals evaluate bodily sensations and choose an appropriate response: **Wait**, **Track**, **Schedule a visit**, or **Urgent/ER**.

---

## Core Structure
| Step | When to use | Typical examples |
|------|-------------|------------------|
| **Wait** | Mild, familiar sensations that resolve on their own. | Brief muscle twinge after awkward sitting; mild headache that fades with rest. |
| **Track** | Non‑severe but recurring or uncertain symptoms. Keep a quick note of: • when it happened • duration • intensity • recent activity/food • factors that improve or worsen. | Dull ache that appears intermittently over several days; mild digestive upset linked to certain meals. |
| **Schedule a visit** | New or persistent issues that last days, interfere with daily life, or change noticeably. | Daily headache for a week, ongoing abdominal discomfort affecting meals, recurring chest sensations without clear trigger. |
| **Urgent/ER** | Sudden, severe, or safety‑related signs (red‑flags). | Worst headache ever, abrupt onset with vision changes; intense abdominal pain with fever or vomiting; chest pressure radiating to arm/jaw, difficulty breathing, fainting. |

## Practical Tips
1. **Start with neutral checks** – Regularly assess how your body feels when nothing is wrong; this builds the baseline needed for quick judgments.
2. **Keep tracking ultra‑simple** – Use a 5‑item bullet list (time, length, intensity, context, modifiers) to avoid analysis paralysis.
3. **When in doubt, choose the more cautious tier** – If a symptom straddles categories, err on the side of scheduling a visit or seeking urgent care.
