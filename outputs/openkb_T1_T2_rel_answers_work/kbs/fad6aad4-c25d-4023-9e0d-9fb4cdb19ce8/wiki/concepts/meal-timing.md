# Meal Timing

**Definition**: Consistent scheduling of meals to maintain steady energy levels throughout the day.

---

## Overview
Meal timing refers to the practice of eating meals at regular, predictable intervals. The goal is to provide the body with a steady supply of nutrients, preventing large blood‑sugar swings that can lead to energy crashes. In the context of busy schedules, adhering to a simple, repeatable timing pattern can be more impactful than perfect nutritional composition.

---

## Key Points from [[summaries/session-15_2024-06-15]]
- **Two‑meal routine**: When lunch is skipped, a stable breakfast and a small afternoon snack become critical to avoid an energy dip.
- **Consistency matters**: Eating breakfast at roughly the same time each day and pairing it with a predictable snack supports energy stability.
- **Bridge the gap**: A light snack in the afternoon (the "snack top‑up") serves as a bridge between breakfast and dinner, smoothing out the post‑lunch slump.

## Practical Recommendations
1. **Set a regular breakfast window** (e.g., 7‑8 am) and try to eat within it daily.
2. **Plan a small, protein‑rich snack** for mid‑afternoon (around 2‑3 pm) to maintain energy until dinner.
3. **Keep dinner at a consistent time** (e.g., 6‑7 pm) to close the daily cycle.
4. **Stay hydrated** throughout the day, as dehydration can mimic an energy dip.

---

## Benefits
- Reduces the likelihood of blood‑sugar spikes and crashes.
- Improves focus and productivity on busy workdays.
- Supports metabolic health by establishing a predictable eating rhythm.
