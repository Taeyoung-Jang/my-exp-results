# Snack Top‑Up

**Snack Top‑Up** refers to a brief, nutrient‑dense bite taken in the mid‑afternoon to prevent an energy crash, especially when a person follows a two‑meal pattern (breakfast and dinner).

## Purpose
- Provides a quick source of protein and/or healthy fats to stabilize blood‑sugar levels after the morning meal.
- Acts as a “fuel top‑up” that smooths the dip in energy before dinner, supporting sustained focus and mood.

## Typical Components
- **Nuts** (almonds, cashews, mixed nuts)
- **Fruit** (banana, apple) paired with a protein source
- **Dairy** (cheese stick, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese)
- **Protein bars** or **protein‑powder‑infused smoothies**
- **Hummus** with crackers or veggie sticks

These options are chosen because they combine **protein** and **healthy fats**, which digest more slowly than simple carbs, thereby extending satiety.

## Guidance
1. **Portion size** – Aim for a snack that delivers roughly 100‑200 kcal, enough to tide you over without feeling like a full meal.
2. **Timing** – Consume the snack about 2‑3 hours after breakfast, typically between 12 pm – 2 pm, aligning with regular [[concepts/meal-timing]].
3. **Balance** – Pair a carbohydrate (e.g., fruit) with protein/fat (e.g., nuts or cheese) for maximal energy‑stabilizing effect, complementing a [[concepts/balanced-breakfast]].

## Examples from [[summaries/session-15_2024-06-15]]
- Handful of almonds or a banana on Monday.
- Cheese stick or protein bar on Tuesday.
- Apple or cashews on Wednesday.
- Hummus with crackers on Thursday.
- Mixed nuts or a granola bar on Friday.

By integrating a consistent *Snack Top‑Up* into a daily routine, individuals who skip lunch can maintain steady energy levels throughout the day without resorting to larger, less balanced meals.
