# Gluten‑Free Cooking

Gluten‑free cooking focuses on creating meals that avoid wheat, barley, rye, and their derivatives while maintaining flavor, texture, and visual appeal. The approach emphasizes **naturally gluten‑free ingredients** rather than relying on specialty substitutes, and it integrates strict **cross‑contamination prevention** practices.

## Core Principles

1. **Ingredient selection** – Choose foods that are inherently free of gluten, such as fresh vegetables, fruits, legumes, meat, poultry, fish, dairy, nuts, seeds, and most grains like rice, quinoa, and buckwheat. The menu from [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]] showcases this by using:
   - [[entities/halloumi]]
   - [[entities/french-green-lentils]]
   - [[entities/gluten-free-crackers]]
   - [[entities/flourless-chocolate-cake]]
2. **Label vigilance** – Even naturally gluten‑free foods can be processed with hidden gluten. Common culprits include soy sauce, marinades, broth, malt vinegar, and spice blends. Swapping regular soy sauce for **tamari** (a gluten‑free alternative) is a safe practice.
3. **Cross‑contamination prevention** – Dedicated utensils, separate cutting boards, and cleaning surfaces before preparation are essential. See [[concepts/cross-contamination-prevention]] for detailed risk mitigation.
4. **Make‑ahead planning** – Preparing components ahead of time reduces last‑minute handling and exposure to shared kitchen tools. The session‑12 menu provides a clear timeline that aligns with [[concepts/make-ahead-planning]].

## Practical Applications from Session‑12

- **Main dishes** that feel “party‑worthy” without wheat: a roasted vegetable and halloumi tray bake, and a vegetarian flourless chocolate cake for dessert.
- **Side dishes** that are naturally gluten‑free: a warm lentil and herb salad using [[entities/french-green-lentils]] and roasted carrots with a honey‑cumin glaze.
- **Appetizers** that avoid hidden gluten: caprese skewers, a cheese and fruit board served with [[entities/gluten-free-crackers]], and a whipped feta dip.
- **Safety checklist** – A concise list for hosts and guests covering separate utensils, no shared oil, dedicated toasters, and labeling of gluten‑free items.

## Benefits

- **Universal appeal** – Meals look and taste like mainstream dishes, reducing the need for special explanations.
- **Flexibility** – The same principles apply to both omnivorous and vegetarian menus.
- **Health** – Emphasizing whole foods often improves nutrition beyond gluten avoidance.
