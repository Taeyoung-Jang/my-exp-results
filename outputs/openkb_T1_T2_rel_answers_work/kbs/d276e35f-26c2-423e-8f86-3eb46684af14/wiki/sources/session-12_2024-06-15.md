**User:** I’m trying to plan a couple easy weeknight dinners and maybe a small get-together menu, but I’m stuck on what to make because I have to avoid all wheat and barley because of my Celiac diagnosis, so regular pasta or breaded items aren't an option for me, especially if they're fried in the same oil. Could you help me brainstorm a few crowd-pleasing meal ideas (and maybe some snack/app options) that fit that, and any tips for avoiding cross-contamination in a shared kitchen?

**Assistant:** You’ve got plenty of options that feel “normal” to everyone else but stay safely gluten‑free for you.

For easy weeknight dinners, things like tacos or fajita bowls are great because corn tortillas are naturally gluten‑free and everything cooks in one pan. You could also do baked potatoes topped with chili or roasted veggies and cheese, or a simple stir‑fry served over rice. If you want something pasta‑like, most grocery stores have decent gluten‑free pasta now, and it works especially well in saucy dishes like pesto chicken pasta or a veggie marinara.

For a small get‑together, dishes that are naturally gluten‑free tend to be easiest. Think roasted chicken with potatoes and a big salad, a chili or stew with gluten‑free cornbread, or taco‑style build‑your‑own bowls. For snacks, things like cheese boards with gluten‑free crackers, fresh veggies with dips, caprese skewers, guacamole with corn tortilla chips, or bacon‑wrapped dates all go over well without calling attention to being gluten‑free.

As for staying safe in a shared kitchen, the main things are keeping your own cutting board, toaster, and utensils; wiping down counters before cooking; and making sure anything fried or toasted is done in equipment that never touches gluten. If others are cooking with gluten, ask them to use separate knives, spoons, and butter or condiment containers so they don’t accidentally double‑dip crumbs into anything you use. Keeping a “gluten‑free zone” on the counter can make things much easier.

**User:** Could you give me a specific menu for 6–8 people that I can prep mostly ahead of time—one main, a couple sides, and 2–3 apps—plus a simple shopping list? Also, what are the most common “hidden gluten” ingredients I should double-check on labels (like sauces/spices), and which kitchen items are the biggest cross-contamination risks?

**Assistant:** Here’s a menu that works well for 6–8 people, can be prepped mostly ahead, and doesn’t feel “gluten‑free” to anyone eating it.

You could make a main of slow‑roasted chicken thighs with lemon, garlic, and herbs. They reheat beautifully and stay juicy. For sides, roasted baby potatoes with olive oil and rosemary hold well at room temp, and a big salad with mixed greens, feta, cucumbers, and a simple vinaigrette can be assembled ahead and dressed right before serving. For apps, you might set out a charcuterie‑style board with cheeses, cured meats, fruit, and gluten‑free crackers, plus a bowl of guacamole with corn tortilla chips and some caprese skewers that keep their shape and stay fresh.

Your shopping list would just be the ingredients for those dishes: chicken thighs, lemons, garlic, fresh herbs, olive oil, baby potatoes, mixed greens, cucumbers, feta, whatever fruits and cured meats you like, an assortment of cheeses, gluten‑free crackers, ripe avocados, limes, tomatoes, basil, mozzarella, and corn tortilla chips. Pantry items like salt, pepper, and vinegar you may already have.

When checking labels, the ingredients that most often hide gluten are soy sauce, many marinades, gravy mixes, certain broths, malt vinegar, barley malt sweeteners, and some spice blends that include “flavorings” or anti‑caking agents containing wheat. Always double‑check anything that’s processed, especially sauces and seasonings.

The biggest cross‑contamination risks in a shared kitchen tend to be toasters, air fryers, shared frying oil, wooden cutting boards, scratched nonstick pans, and condiment jars where people have dipped bread knives. Keeping your own dedicated versions of the high‑risk items and wiping down surfaces before cooking usually covers most problems.

**Menu with dish names and quick make‑ahead steps**

Lemon‑Herb Roasted Chicken Thighs  
Season chicken thighs with salt, pepper, lemon zest, garlic, and herbs. Roast until browned, then chill. Reheat covered at low heat on the day of your gathering.

Rosemary Baby Potatoes  
Cut small potatoes in half, toss with olive oil, salt, pepper, and rosemary, and roast until tender. These keep well at room temp and reheat easily.

Mixed Greens Salad with Feta and Simple Vinaigrette  
Wash and dry greens, slice cucumbers, and crumble feta. Mix vinaigrette (olive oil, vinegar, mustard, honey, salt). Store separately; assemble and dress right before serving.

Charcuterie Board with Gluten‑Free Crackers  
Slice cheeses, arrange cured meats, fruit, nuts, and gluten‑free crackers. Most of this can be plated an hour ahead and refrigerated.

Caprese Skewers  
Thread cherry tomatoes, basil leaves, and mozzarella balls onto toothpicks. Drizzle lightly with olive oil. Chill until serving.

Guacamole with Corn Tortilla Chips  
Mash ripe avocados with lime, salt, cilantro, and diced tomato. Press plastic wrap directly on the surface and refrigerate to prevent browning.

**Safe swaps to remember**  
Use tamari or certified gluten‑free soy sauce instead of regular soy sauce.  
Choose broth labeled gluten‑free since some contain wheat.  
Pick mustards, vinegars, and spice blends that specify gluten‑free or list all ingredients clearly.

**Short gluten‑free safety checklist you can send to guests or hosts**

Use separate utensils for gluten‑free food and avoid double‑dipping knives or spoons.  
Keep gluten‑free dishes away from items containing bread, crackers, or flour.  
Do not use shared frying oil, toasters, air fryers, or cutting boards that have touched gluten.  
Wipe counters before prepping anything gluten‑free and serve from clean platters.  
Check ingredient labels for wheat, barley, rye, malt, or anything not clearly labeled gluten‑free.

For the appetizers, make about twenty to twenty‑four Caprese skewers using roughly one pint of cherry tomatoes, one container of mini mozzarella balls, and a bunch of basil.
