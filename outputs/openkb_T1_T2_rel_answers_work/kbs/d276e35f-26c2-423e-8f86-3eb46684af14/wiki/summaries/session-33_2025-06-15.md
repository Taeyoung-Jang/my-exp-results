# Summary of session-33_2025-06-15

**Main focus**: How clinicians choose tests for food‑related gut issues, common sources of misleading results, and practical steps for a diet‑challenge preparation.

## Decision pathway for GI testing
- Begin with a detailed clinical history to differentiate patterns of **food allergy**, **food intolerance**, or **autoimmune GI disease**.
- **Allergy testing**: targeted IgE serum assays or skin‑prick tests; high specificity when history aligns, but false‑positives are common in broad panels.
- **Intolerance testing**: functional approaches such as lactose/H₂ breath tests or structured elimination‑reintroduction; can be confounded by recent antibiotics, SIBO, or overly aggressive elimination diets.
- **Autoimmune testing**: serology for celiac disease (tTG‑IgA, EMA‑IgA) requires ongoing gluten exposure; gluten‑free periods before testing are a classic pitfall.
- Beware of unvalidated “food sensitivity” panels – they often produce long lists of irrelevant findings and may lead to unnecessary restrictive diets.

## Diet‑challenge pitfalls that can skew repeat labs/biopsy
- **Timing & dose**: insufficient exposure or inconsistent intake before labs/endoscopy may fail to provoke measurable immune changes; excessive exposure can cause early symptom‑driven cessation.
- **Dietary background**: altering other foods concurrently obscures the specific effect of the challenge food.
- **Medications & supplements**: PPIs, H2 blockers, antihistamines, steroids, NSAIDs, probiotics, vitamins/minerals can blunt inflammatory markers or alter gut flora, affecting both bloodwork and histology.
- **Recent events**: acute gastroenteritis, recent antibiotics, travel‑related diet changes, sleep disruption, or high stress can modify immune responses and should be disclosed.

## Patient‑focused checklist (to send via MyChart)
1. Exact daily serving size of the challenge food and whether it should be spaced throughout the day.
2. Number of days required before **bloodwork** and any extended lead‑in needed for **endoscopy**.
3. List of current meds/supplements (PPIs, H2 blockers, antihistamines, steroids, NSAIDs, probiotics, vitamins) with guidance on when to pause them.
4. Clarification on whether recent infections, antibiotics, travel, stress, or sleep issues would require rescheduling.
5. Preferred symptom‑tracking format.
