# Narrative Nonfiction

Narrative nonfiction is a style of factual writing that adopts the techniques of fiction—strong characters, vivid scenes, and a clear narrative arc—to make real events feel like a story.  It prioritizes **propulsive pacing** and immersive detail, allowing readers to experience history, biography, or investigative reporting with the same momentum they expect from a novel.

## Core Characteristics
- **Story‑driven structure**: Events are organized around a central conflict or mystery, often focusing on a single episode or person to maintain tension.
- **Rich, scene‑level description**: Authors recreate moments with sensory detail, making the past feel present.
- **Balanced research and readability**: While grounded in rigorous sources, the prose avoids dense exposition in favor of a flowing narrative.
- **Audiobook friendliness**: Because the prose reads like a story, these works often excel in [[concepts/audiobook-narration]] formats, where skilled narrators can amplify the drama.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/propulsive-pacing]]**: Narrative nonfiction relies on a fast‑moving tempo that keeps readers turning pages, a hallmark highlighted in the recommendations from the *session‑35* discussion.
- **[[concepts/microhistory]]**: Many narrative nonfiction titles zoom in on a single event, disaster, or expedition, providing the “one‑moment” focus that makes the genre compelling.
- **[[concepts/big-ideas]]**: While the focus is on storytelling, the works often explore larger themes—power, survival, innovation—through the lens of a specific story.
