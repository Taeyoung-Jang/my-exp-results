## Dead‑Time Learning

**Definition** – A learning strategy that turns otherwise unproductive moments (commuting, cooking, walking, etc.) into focused, low‑effort study sessions by attaching tiny, curiosity‑driven tasks to activities that are already automatic.

### Core Principles
1. **Anchor learning to existing routines** – Use the momentum of an automatic habit (e.g., a commute) as the cue for a micro‑learning bite.
2. **Keep it micro** – Limit each learning unit to a few minutes or a single concept so it feels like a mental snack rather than homework.  [[concepts/microlearning]]
3. **Follow curiosity** – Choose material that naturally piques interest in the moment, allowing intrinsic motivation to drive repetition.  [[concepts/curiosity-driven-learning]]
4. **Mental recall over notes** – After each bite, briefly restate the main idea in your own words; the act of retrieval reinforces memory without extra tools.

### Practices Illustrated in [[summaries/session-40_2025-06-17]]
- **Podcast/Audiobook pairing** – Listening to business or finance audiobooks during a long commute and using chapter boundaries as natural pause points for mental summarization.
- **Micro‑recap** – When a chapter ends, silently restate its core message in one sentence before the next chapter begins.
- **Weekly loop‑back** – At the end of the week, replay one or two standout chapters identified by instinctive “perk‑up” moments, reinforcing high‑value content without note‑taking.
- **Cue‑based recall** – After exiting the car, perform a 10‑second memory jog (e.g., “Today’s big idea was X”) to solidify the day's learning.

### Benefits
- **Zero‑friction integration** – No new apps or schedules are needed; learning lives inside already‑scheduled activities.
- **Higher retention** – Repeated, brief mental anchoring and spaced re‑listening improve long‑term recall.
- **Sustained motivation** – Curiosity‑driven selection prevents the feeling of “homework” and keeps the process enjoyable.
