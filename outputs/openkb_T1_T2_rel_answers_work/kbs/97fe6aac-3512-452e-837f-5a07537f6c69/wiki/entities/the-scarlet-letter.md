# The Scarlet Letter (work)

**Author:** Nathaniel Hawthorne

**Publication Year:** 1850

**Genre:** Historical fiction, Romanticism, Moral tragedy

**Length:** Roughly 300‑350 pages (varies by edition)

**Key Themes:** Sin, guilt, public shame, identity, moral judgment

**Relevance to session‑04_2024-10-16:**
- Recommended as a "bridge" classic because it is **plot‑driven**, concise, and tackles a strong [[concepts/moral-dilemma]] while avoiding lengthy digressions.
- Cited alongside [[entities/great-expectations]], [[entities/les-miserables]], and [[entities/the-count-of-monte-cristo]] as alternatives to more demanding works like [[entities/middlemarch]] or [[entities/the-brothers-karamazov]].

**Related Concepts:**
- [[concepts/character-arc]] – Pip’s development in *Great Expectations* is paralleled by Hester Prynne’s evolution.
- [[concepts/plot-driven-narrative]] – The novel maintains forward momentum despite its thematic depth.
- [[concepts/moral-dilemma]] – Central to Hester’s choice to wear the scarlet letter.
