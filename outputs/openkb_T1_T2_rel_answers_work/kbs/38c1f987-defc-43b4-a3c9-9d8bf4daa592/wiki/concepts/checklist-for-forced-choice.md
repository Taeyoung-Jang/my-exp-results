# Checklist for Forced‑Choice Items

**Definition**: A concise, mental protocol for interpreting and responding to forced‑choice "preference" items on personality assessments, ensuring answers reflect typical behavior rather than ideals or strategic choices.

## Core Elements (≈10 seconds per item)
1. **Timeframe** – Ask yourself: *What do I usually do on an average day?*  Focus on everyday patterns, not occasional exceptions.
2. **Frequency cues** – Pay attention to words like *usually*, *often*, *more often*.  Answer based on the most frequent occurrence.
3. **Context separation** – Briefly distinguish **work** vs **home**.  Default to the *non‑role* self (the version of you when you’re not acting under professional protocols).
4. **Ideal vs Actual** – Reject the “how I should act” lens; choose the behavior that *actually* happens.
5. **Literal wording** – Treat terms such as *prefer*, *tend to*, *more often* at face value, without re‑interpreting them through personal definitions.

## Handling “Two‑Positive” Pairs
When an item pits two desirable traits against each other (e.g., safety vs speed, empathy vs boundaries), use the **first‑impulse rule**:
- Imagine a neutral, everyday situation.
- Identify which trait surfaces *instinctively* before any professional training or self‑monitoring.
- Select that trait as the answer.

## Average Day vs. Best Self
- Answer as **“average day”**.  Personality batteries aim to capture temperament, not aspirational performance.
- Consistent stress‑related shifts are part of your real‑world pattern and should be reflected if they are typical.
