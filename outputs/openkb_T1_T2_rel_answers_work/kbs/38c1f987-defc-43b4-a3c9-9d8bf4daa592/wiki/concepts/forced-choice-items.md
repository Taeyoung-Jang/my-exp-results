# Forced‑Choice Items

**Definition**: Forced‑Choice Items are psychometric questions that present two (or more) statements and require the respondent to select the option that best describes their typical behavior, thereby eliminating socially desirable responding and highlighting relative preferences.

## Core Characteristics
- **Mutual exclusivity**: Only one statement can be chosen, compelling a direct comparison.
- **Preference focus**: Items aim to capture *preference* or *tendency* rather than ability, skill, or ideal behavior.
- **Frequency wording**: Common cues such as *usually*, *more often*, *tend to*, and *prefer* signal that the question targets habitual patterns.
- **Context‑neutral**: Respondents are instructed to answer based on their *average* day, not role‑specific or idealized behavior.

## Why Scores May "Flip"
In [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]] the user experienced a shift in scores after a psychologist clarified wording. This typically occurs when:
1. The respondent was interpreting terms through personal definitions rather than the test’s intended meaning.
2. The clarification realigns answers with the *actual* wording, revealing the true underlying preference.

## Practical Guidance for Test‑Takers
1. **Read literally** – Treat words like *prefer*, *tend to*, and *usually* at face value.
2. **Average‑day mindset** – Answer based on everyday, non‑professional behavior.
3. **First‑impulse rule** – When both options are positive, choose the trait that comes instinctively.
4. **Separate contexts** – Briefly distinguish work vs. home, then default to the baseline self.
5. Use a quick mental checklist (see [[concepts/checklist-for-forced-choice]]) to stay consistent.
