# Summary of Session 6 (2022‑06‑15)

**Main focus**: How personality, upbringing, and stress shape reactions to criticism, and practical methods for an INFP to process feedback without spiraling.

## Key Concepts
- **Factors influencing feedback response**: personality baseline, upbringing, current stress levels, and past experiences. feedback processing
- **INFP traits**: emotional processing, tendency to dwell on criticism. INFP personality
- **Emotional regulation**: creating a pause, reframing feedback as information, separating worth from behavior. emotional regulation
- **Stress management**: safe, rested state improves resilience. stress management

## Practical Strategies
1. **Create a buffer** – use a short pause (e.g., “Let me sit with that and get back to you”).
2. **Reframe the criticism** – translate it into neutral language, focus on actionable content.
3. **Use a simple script** for the moment of feedback:
   > "Thanks for telling me. I want to make sure I understand, so I’m going to take a little time to think it over. I’ll follow up once I’ve had a chance to look at it more clearly."
   This supports respectful acknowledgment while giving space. communication script

## Post‑Feedback Checklist (5‑7 questions)
- What exact behavior or outcome was mentioned?
- Which part of the message is clearly useful or actionable?
- What emotions did it trigger, and why?
- Stripping emotion, what is the simplest next step?
- Does this feedback align with my self‑knowledge or reflect the giver’s preferences/stress?
- Do I need clarification or examples?
- How will I let go of any residual emotional charge?

## Handling Vague or Unfair Criticism
- **Ground the feedback**: restate it in plain, unemotional terms.
- **Seek specificity**: plan a brief follow‑up to ask for concrete examples or expectations.
- **Maintain perspective**: remind yourself the feedback targets behavior, not personal worth.

## Takeaway
By acknowledging the role of personality and context, inserting a brief pause, reframing emotionally charged input, and following a structured checklist, an INFP (or anyone) can transform criticism into constructive guidance without mental replay.
