# Physiological Self‑Monitoring for Recovery

**Type:** Concept

## Definition
Physiological self‑monitoring for recovery is the practice of regularly recording easy‑to‑obtain bodily signals—such as resting heart rate (RHR), heart‑rate variability (HRV), sleep quantity/quality, and subjective energy or mood—to determine whether the body has genuinely returned to baseline after a stressful or high‑intensity period.

## Why it matters
During a "crunch" (e.g., finals week, product launch, heavy travel) the nervous system is flooded with stress hormones like adrenaline and cortisol.  When the event ends, individuals often *feel* recovered, but lingering physiological strain can remain hidden.  Measuring objective markers helps distinguish a true bounce‑back from a temporary masking of fatigue.

## Core metrics (as outlined in [[summaries/session-05_2022-07-01]])
- **Resting Heart Rate (RHR)** – baseline beats per minute measured first thing in the morning.  An elevation of +7 bpm for three consecutive days signals that the autonomic nervous system is still in a heightened state.
- **Heart‑Rate Variability (HRV)** – the variation between heartbeats, usually reported as a percent or millisecond value.  A drop of ≈15 % below personal baseline for two days suggests reduced vagal tone and incomplete recovery.
- **Sleep metrics** – total hours and a simple refresh rating (e.g., "refreshed" vs "groggy").  Normal duration but repeated unrefreshed mornings for two nights flags insufficient neural recovery.
- **Subjective mood/energy** – quick daily rating of how motivated, irritable, or energetic you feel.  A downward trend over several days, even if RHR/HRV appear normal, can indicate a psychological component of lingering stress.
- **Workout effort perception** – noting whether a usual easy‑zone effort feels harder than usual.  Persistent hardness for more than one day is a practical red‑flag.

## How to implement a simple 2‑week tracking template
1. **Establish baseline**: Record your typical RHR, HRV, average sleep hours, mood/energy level, and normal workout effort for at least three consecutive non‑stress days.
2. **Log daily** using the template from the source summary (see [[summaries/session-05_2022-07-01]]).  Keep entries short: `RHR ___ / HRV ___ / Sleep ___ / Mood ___ / Energy ___ / Workout ___`.
3. **Apply thresholds** (see [[concepts/recovery-red-flags]]) to decide when to back off:
   - RHR +7 bpm for ≥3 days → rest or easy Zone 2.
   - HRV –15 % for ≥2 days → avoid intensity.
   - Unrefreshed sleep for 2 nights → rest day or light activity.
   - Workout feels harder for >1 day → keep training easy.
4. **Review** after the two‑week period.  If all metrics return to baseline within a day or two, the bounce‑back is likely genuine.  Persistent deviations suggest hidden fatigue and the need for longer recovery.
