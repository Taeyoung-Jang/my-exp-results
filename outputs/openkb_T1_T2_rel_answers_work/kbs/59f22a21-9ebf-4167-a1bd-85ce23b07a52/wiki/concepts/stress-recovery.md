## Stress and Recovery Dynamics

**Definition**: The interplay between physiological stress activation (e.g., adrenaline, cortisol) and the mechanisms that restore equilibrium after demanding events such as exams, product launches, or travel.

### Core Elements
- **Stress Activation** – Acute spikes in adrenaline and cortisol signal the body to mobilize resources. The immediate boost can mask fatigue, allowing short‑term performance but does not sustain long‑term recovery.
- **Recovery Triggers** – Once the stressor ends, the body must down‑regulate these hormones. High‑quality sleep is the primary driver that facilitates hormonal reset and neural processing of stress.
- **Individual Variation** – People differ in how quickly their stress system shuts off and how resilient their sleep is under pressure. These differences explain why some bounce back after a single night of sleep while others experience lingering fatigue.

### Practical Indicators
- **Resting Heart Rate (RHR)** and **Heart‑Rate Variability (HRV)** – Elevated RHR or reduced HRV post‑crunch signal incomplete recovery.
- **Subjective Measures** – Mood, energy, and perceived workout effort reveal hidden fatigue even when objective metrics appear normal.

### Monitoring & Action
- Use simple daily logs (see [[concepts/physiological-self-monitoring]]) to track RHR, HRV, sleep quality, mood, and workout feel.
- Apply the thresholds described in [[concepts/recovery-red-flags]] to decide when to rest, stay in easy Zone 2 activity, or resume normal training.
