# Summary of session-05_2022-07-01

## Main topic
Discussion of how people recover after intense “crunch” periods (e.g., finals, product launches, travel) and practical ways to verify genuine bounce‑back.

## Key points
- Recovery speed varies due to adrenaline drop, individual **stress response** (cortisol), and sleep quality. See [[concepts/stress-recovery]] for more detail.  
- Fast recovery usually signals good sleep and efficient shutdown of the stress system; lingering issues appear as gradual mood dip, irritability, reduced focus, frequent illness, or lighter sleep. These are examples of [[concepts/recovery-red-flags]].  
- Practical self‑monitoring can use daily logs of **resting heart rate**, **heart‑rate variability**, **sleep metrics**, **mood/energy**, and **workout effort**. All of these fall under the umbrella of [[concepts/physiological-self-monitoring]].  
- A simple 2‑week tracking template is recommended, with a baseline row and five to seven fields per day, to capture the above metrics.  
- Suggested thresholds for red‑flags:  
  - RHR + 7 bpm for three consecutive days → switch to rest or easy Zone 2.  
  - HRV − 15 % for two consecutive days → avoid intensity.  
  - Unrefreshed sleep for two mornings straight → take a rest day or keep activity easy.  
  - Workout feels noticeably harder than usual for more than one day → keep activity light. These rules are consistent with [[concepts/recovery-red-flags]].

## Takeaway
A concise self‑tracking approach lets individuals differentiate genuine recovery from hidden fatigue, using simple physiological and subjective metrics and clear action thresholds.
