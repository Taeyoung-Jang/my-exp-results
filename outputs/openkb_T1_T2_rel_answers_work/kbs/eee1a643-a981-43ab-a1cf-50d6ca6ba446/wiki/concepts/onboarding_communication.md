# Onboarding Communication

Onboarding communication refers to the set of intentional messages and interactions that help a new employee integrate into a team, understand priorities, and build relationships quickly. The goal is to convey openness, curiosity, and readiness to contribute while respecting existing workflows.

## Key Elements from Session 38
- **Tone Variations**: Providing multiple drafts—casual, formal, and manager‑focused—illustrates the need to match the communication style to the audience, a core part of [[concepts/communication_strategies]].
- **Name‑Learning Disclaimer**: Explicitly stating that the newcomer is still learning names reduces social friction and promotes [[concepts/workplace_camaraderie]].
- **Quick 15‑Minute Chat Invite**: Including a concise invitation for a brief conversation (without labeling it a meeting) aligns with the [[concepts/quick_chat_invite]] pattern, encouraging informal knowledge‑sharing.
- **Platform Choice**: The drafts are designed for posting on [[entities/slack]], leveraging its informal, real‑time nature to foster rapid connections.
- **Boundary Setting**: By offering a short time slot (15 minutes) and making the request optional, the messages respect colleagues’ time and set clear expectations, linking to [[concepts/boundary_setting]].

## Why It Matters
Effective onboarding communication accelerates a new hire’s ramp‑up time, improves team cohesion, and signals a proactive attitude. It balances **clarity** (what the newcomer wants to learn) with **approachability** (friendly tone, name‑learning note), and it integrates seamlessly into everyday tools like Slack.

## Practical Tips
1. **Choose the appropriate tone** based on the audience—casual for peers, formal for broader channels, and direct for managers.
2. **Acknowledge the learning curve** (e.g., “still learning names”) to set realistic expectations.
3. **Propose a brief, optional chat** (15‑minute) to lower the barrier for engagement.
4. **Use the native platform language**; keep Slack messages concise and friendly.
5. **Iterate**: Adjust wording based on feedback and team culture.
