# Marketing Opt‑Out Strategies

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Methods for limiting post‑prequalification marketing communications and protecting credit‑inquiry privacy.

---

### Why Opt‑Out Matters
When shoppers use soft‑pull **prequalification** tools, lenders often request permission to contact them for offers.  Unchecked consent can lead to a flood of phone calls, texts, emails, and postal mail, which adds noise and can expose personal data to third‑party marketers.

### Core Strategies
1. **Use Dedicated Contact Channels**
   - Create a separate email address solely for loan shopping.
   - Use a phone number you can later discard or forward.
2. **Explicitly Decline Marketing Consent**
   - Look for optional checkboxes that allow phone, text, email, or partner outreach and leave them unchecked.
   - If the form bundles marketing consent into required terms, be prepared for some contact.
3. **Opt‑Out of Prescreened Credit Offers**
   - Visit **optoutprescreen.com** (see [[entities/optoutprescreen-com]]) to remove your name from nationwide prescreened offers.
4. **Register on the National Do‑Not‑Call List**
   - Add your phone number at the **Do‑Not‑Call List** (see [[entities/do-not-call-list]]) to curb unsolicited sales calls.
5. **Review Fine‑Print Before Submitting**
   - Confirm statements like *“this will not affect your credit score”* and *“soft inquiry only”* to ensure the request is a **soft‑pull** and not a **hard‑inquiry**.
   - Identify any language indicating a hard credit check, which could affect your **credit‑score**.

### Timing with the Loan Process
- Perform all soft‑pull **prequalification** steps first, applying the opt‑out tactics above.
- When ready for formal applications, group hard pulls within the **rate‑shopping‑window** (typically 14 days) to limit impact on the **credit‑score**.
