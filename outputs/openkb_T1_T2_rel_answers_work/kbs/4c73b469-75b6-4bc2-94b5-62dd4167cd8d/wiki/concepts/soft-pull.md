# Soft Credit Pull

A **soft credit pull** (or soft inquiry) is a type of credit check that does **not** appear on a consumer’s credit report and therefore does **not** lower the credit score. Lenders and other entities use soft pulls primarily for informational purposes, such as pre‑qualification, identity verification, or account monitoring.

---

## Key Characteristics
- **No impact on credit score** – Unlike a [[concepts/hard-inquiry]], a soft pull is invisible to credit scoring models.
- **No appearance on credit report** – It is not listed for future lenders to see.
- **Limited data** – Typically provides a snapshot of credit‑related information (e.g., credit‑score range, existing accounts) but not the full detailed report.
- **Common uses**:
  - **Pre‑qualification** for loans or credit cards, allowing borrowers to see potential offers without risk. See [[concepts/prequalification]].
  - Ongoing account monitoring by existing lenders.
  - Identity verification for non‑loan services.

---

## Process in Loan Shopping (per [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]])
1. **Locate the soft‑pull option** – Look for phrasing such as “soft credit inquiry,” “will not affect your credit score,” or “pre‑qualify.”
2. **Provide basic information** – Name, address, income, employment status, and the loan amount you are considering. No documentation is required at this stage.
3. **Receive an indicative offer** – The lender can give you a rate estimate or eligibility range without a hard pull.
4. **Avoid hidden hard‑pull clauses** – Ensure the form does not contain language like “pull your credit report” or “hard inquiry.”

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/credit-score]]** – Soft pulls may show an approximate score range but do not alter the score itself.
- **[[concepts/hard-inquiry]]** – Hard inquiries are used for formal applications and can lower the credit score temporarily.
- **[[concepts/rate-shopping-window]]** – After soft‑pull pre‑qualifications, borrowers can submit multiple hard‑pull applications within a short window (typically 14 days) to minimize score impact.
- **[[concepts/marketing-opt-out]]** – Soft‑pull forms often include optional marketing consents; users can opt out to reduce spam.

---

## Practical Tips
- **Use a dedicated email address** for loan shopping to contain marketing follow‑ups.
- **Check for soft‑pull language** before submitting any form.
- **Limit the number of soft pulls** to a reasonable amount; while they don’t affect the score, they may increase marketing contact.
