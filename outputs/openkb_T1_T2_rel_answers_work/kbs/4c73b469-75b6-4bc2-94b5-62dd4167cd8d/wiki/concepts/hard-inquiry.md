# Hard Credit Inquiry

A **hard credit inquiry** (also called a hard pull) occurs when a lender or other authorized party checks your full credit report as part of a formal loan or credit application. Unlike a [[concepts/soft-pull]], a hard inquiry is recorded on your credit report and can cause a small, temporary dip in your [[concepts/credit-score]].

---

## How It Works
- Triggered by **formal applications** for credit products such as auto loans, mortgages, credit cards, or personal loans.
- The lender requests your full credit file from a credit bureau, which then logs the inquiry.
- Each hard inquiry typically reduces a credit score by a few points for up to 12 months, though the impact lessens over time.

## Relevance to the Rate‑Shopping Window
- Credit scoring models (e.g., FICO, VantageScore) recognize that consumers compare offers. They treat multiple hard inquiries for the **same type of loan** made within a defined period as a **single inquiry**. This period is known as the [[concepts/rate-shopping-window]], usually about **14 days** (sometimes up to 30 days) for auto and mortgage loans.
- To minimise score impact, submit all formal applications for a given loan type within this window.

## Best Practices
1. **Use soft‑pull prequalification first** – Seek pre‑qualification offers that state they involve a [[concepts/soft-pull]] only; these do **not** affect your score.
2. **Plan a tight application timeline** – After gathering offers, apply to your top lenders within the rate‑shopping window so the hard pulls count as one.
3. **Monitor your credit reports** – Verify that only the intended hard inquiries appear and that any unexpected ones are investigated.
4. **Avoid recent hard pulls before credit‑card limit requests** – If you are trying to improve your standing with a credit‑card issuer (e.g., after a tier downgrade), recent hard inquiries can signal new credit seeking and may be viewed negatively. Give the account a few months of clean activity after any hard pull before requesting a limit increase or tier reconsideration, as discussed in [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-13]].

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- **Credit Score:** Hard inquiries are one of several factors influencing your [[concepts/credit-score]].
- **Prequalification:** Soft‑pull pre‑qualification helps avoid premature hard pulls.
- **Marketing Opt‑Out:** While hard inquiries themselves don't trigger marketing, completing loan applications often includes optional marketing consent; use [[concepts/marketing-opt-out]] strategies to limit follow‑up contacts.
