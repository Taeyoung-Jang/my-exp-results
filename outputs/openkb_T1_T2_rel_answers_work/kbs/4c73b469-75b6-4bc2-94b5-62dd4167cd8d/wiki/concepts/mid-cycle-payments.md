# Mid‑Cycle Payments

**Definition**: Payments made before a credit‑card statement closing date, often weekly or after each purchase, to keep the balance reported to credit bureaus low and demonstrate low‑risk behaviour.

## Why it matters
- Issuers typically report the balance **as of the statement closing date**, not the due date. By reducing the balance before this cut‑off, the reported **credit utilization** appears lower, which can improve the issuer’s perception of risk.
- Mid‑cycle payments are a normal banking practice and do not raise suspicion; they simply manage the timing of reported balances.
- Keeping utilization in the 10‑30 % range (or lower) signals stability without appearing dormant.

## Practical approach (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-13]])
1. **Identify the statement closing date** each month.
2. **Track balance** throughout the cycle; when it nears 10‑30 % of the limit, make a payment.
3. Choose a cadence that fits your habits – either a **weekly payment** routine or a **pay‑after‑each‑purchase** method.
4. Ensure payments are **on‑time** and posted before the closing date so they affect the reported balance.

## Benefits
- Maintains a low reported utilization, supporting a perception of low‑risk behaviour.
- Demonstrates consistent, responsible usage, which can aid in future limit‑increase or tier‑reconsideration requests.
- Avoids the appearance of “barely using” the card while still keeping the balance modest.

## Common concerns addressed
- **Suspicion**: Issuers recognize mid‑cycle payments as routine; they do not view them as gaming the system.
- **Impact on usage signal**: Even with payments, the issuer sees the full transaction history, so usage is not hidden.
- **Zero vs. small balance reporting**: Both are acceptable; the key is consistency and low reported utilization.
