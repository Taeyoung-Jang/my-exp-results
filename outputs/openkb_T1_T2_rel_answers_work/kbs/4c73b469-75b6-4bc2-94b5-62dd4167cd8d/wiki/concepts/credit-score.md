# Credit Score

**Definition**: A credit score is a three‑digit number that summarizes an individual's credit history and predicts their likelihood of repaying a loan. Lenders use it as a primary factor when evaluating loan applications.

## How It Is Calculated
- **Payment history** (35%): Timeliness of past debt payments.
- **Credit utilization** (30%): Ratio of current balances to total credit limits.
- **Length of credit history** (15%): Age of the oldest account and average account age.
- **New credit** (10%): Recent hard inquiries and opened accounts.
- **Credit mix** (10%): Variety of credit types (e.g., credit cards, auto loans, mortgages).

## Relevance in Session 3 (2024‑06‑15)
The assistant advises the user to:
- Review credit reports for errors and dispute them, directly influencing the **payment history** component.
- Reduce credit‑card balances to lower **utilization**, which can quickly improve the score.
- Avoid new credit applications that generate **hard inquiries**, as they temporarily lower the score.
- Use **soft‑pull** pre‑qualification checks, which do not affect the score.
- Time multiple formal applications within the **rate‑shopping‑window** (≈14 days) so they count as a single hard inquiry.

These actions aim to strengthen the credit score before applying for a loan, thereby increasing approval chances and securing a better interest rate.

## Relevance in Session 33 (2025‑02‑13)
The discussion expands the focus to how everyday card‑usage habits affect the issuer’s perception of risk, which in turn can influence the credit‑score components of **utilization** and **payment history**:
- Emphasize **low‑risk behaviour** by keeping utilization comfortably below the limit (roughly 10‑30 % is a good target) and paying on time every month. [[concepts/low-risk-behaviour]]
- Manage the **statement‑balance reporting** date: issuers usually report the balance as of the statement closing date, not the due date. Paying down the balance before this date keeps the reported utilization low. [[concepts/statement-balance-reporting]]
- Use **mid‑cycle payments** (weekly or after each purchase) to ensure the balance reported to credit bureaus stays low while still showing regular usage. This does not look suspicious and does not hide activity; it simply controls what is reported. [[concepts/mid-cycle-payments]]
- Avoid erratic spikes in spending, multiple new accounts, or late payments, all of which could raise the risk profile and indirectly affect the credit score.
- After a period of several months of steady, low‑utilization usage, proactively request a limit increase or tier reconsideration, presenting income stability and clean payment history without stressing urgency.
