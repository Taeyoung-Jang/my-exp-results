# Low‑Risk Behaviour

Low‑Risk Behaviour is a pattern of credit‑card usage that conveys stability and predictability to the card issuer. It is characterised by several observable signals that issuers monitor when evaluating tier placement, credit‑limit adjustments, and overall account health.

## Core Signals
- **Low credit utilization** – Keep the balance reported at the statement closing date well below the limit, typically in the 10‑30 % range. This demonstrates that you are not dependent on the available credit. [[concepts/statement-balance-reporting]]
- **On‑time payments** – Pay at least the minimum by the due date every month; full‑payment is ideal. Consistent punctuality reduces perceived risk.
- **Stable spending patterns** – Use the card regularly but avoid large, erratic spikes in spending that deviate from your normal behaviour.
- **Limited new credit activity** – Refrain from opening multiple new accounts or making many hard inquiries in a short period, as this can signal financial stress.
- **Mid‑cycle payments** – Making payments during the billing cycle (weekly or after each purchase) helps keep the reported utilization low without appearing to game the system. [[concepts/mid-cycle-payments]]

## Why It Matters
Issuers rely on these behavioural cues more than any opaque scoring model when deciding whether to raise a tier, increase a limit, or maintain the current status. Demonstrating Low‑Risk Behaviour over several months builds trust and positions the account for favorable reviews.

## Practical Tips (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-13]])
1. **Pay before the statement closing date** to control the balance that gets reported.
2. **Aim for a reported utilization of ~10‑30 %**; the exact number is less important than staying comfortably below the limit.
3. **Maintain a clean payment history** – no late fees, no returned payments.
4. **Avoid sudden large purchases** unless they fit your typical spending pattern.
5. **Delay limit‑increase requests** until you have 2‑4 months of steady, low‑risk activity after a downgrade.

By consistently exhibiting these behaviours, you signal to the issuer that you are a low‑risk, predictable customer, which improves the likelihood of tier upgrades and credit‑limit increases.
