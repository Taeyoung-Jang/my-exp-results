# Rate‑Shopping Window

The **rate‑shopping window** is a time frame defined by credit scoring models (e.g., FICO, VantageScore) during which multiple **hard inquiries** for the same type of loan are treated as a single inquiry for scoring purposes. This allows borrowers to compare offers from different lenders without significantly harming their [[concepts/credit-score]].

## Typical Duration
- Most models use a window of **around 14 days**.
- Some models may extend the period up to **30 days**.
- The window starts with the first hard pull related to the loan type (e.g., auto loan, mortgage).

## Why It Matters
- **Credit Impact:** Without the window, each hard inquiry would lower the credit score slightly. Within the window, the score impact is limited to one inquiry.
- **Strategic Shopping:** Borrowers can submit applications to several lenders in a short, focused period, compare rates, and choose the best offer while preserving credit health.

## Practical Guidance (from [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]])
1. **Do soft‑pull prequalifications first** – these do not affect the credit score and help gauge realistic rates.
2. **Schedule all formal applications** to occur within the 14‑day window to ensure they count as a single hard inquiry.
3. **Coordinate timing** – start the window by initiating the first hard pull, then submit remaining applications promptly.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/hard-inquiry]] – the type of credit check that can affect the score unless covered by the rate‑shopping window.
- [[concepts/soft-pull]] – a non‑impactful credit check used for prequalification.
- [[concepts/prequalification]] – the initial soft‑pull step that helps borrowers estimate loan terms before formal applications.

## How to Use It Effectively
- **Plan Ahead:** Identify the lenders you want to apply to and gather required documents before the window opens.
- **Monitor Timing:** Keep a calendar reminder to ensure all applications are submitted within the 14‑day period.
- **Check Lender Policies:** Verify that each lender’s hard pull will be counted toward the same loan type and scoring model.

By understanding and leveraging the rate‑shopping window, borrowers can confidently explore multiple loan offers with minimal impact on their credit profile.
