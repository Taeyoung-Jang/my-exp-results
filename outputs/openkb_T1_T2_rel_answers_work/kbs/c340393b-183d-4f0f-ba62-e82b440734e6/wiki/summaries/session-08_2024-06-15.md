## Summary of Session 8 (2024‑06‑15)

This document provides a practical framework for auditing personal caffeine and alcohol habits to improve sleep and daytime energy. It includes a **copy‑paste tracking template**, concrete **decision‑rule thresholds**, methods for handling **confounders**, and a simple **scoring system** to flag “bad sleep” nights.

### Key Concepts
- **Habit tracking**: Recording intake amount, timing, and subjective ratings each day. [[concepts/habit-tracking]]
- **Sleep quality metrics**: Time to fall asleep, night wakings, rested rating. [[concepts/sleep-quality-metrics]]
- **Decision rules with thresholds**: E.g., >30 min sleep onset → shift caffeine cutoff earlier. [[concepts/decision-rules]]
- **Confounder handling**: Tagging late workouts, heavy dinners, stress spikes, and optionally analyzing them separately. *(no wikilink)*
- **Scoring method for bad sleep**: 1 point each for >30 min sleep onset, >2 night wakings, rested ≤5/10; ≥2 points = bad night. *(no wikilink)*


### Concrete Decision‑Rule Set
- **Caffeine timing**: If sleep onset > 30 min on days with caffeine after a given time, move cutoff 1 hr earlier for three nights.
- **Alcohol timing**: If rested rating ≤ 5 after a night of drinking, shift last drink ≥ 1 hr earlier for the next three drinking nights.
- **Energy dip**: If daytime energy ≤ 5 despite normal sleep, reduce total caffeine slightly or insert one caffeine‑free day for three days.
- **Weekend spillover**: If next‑day mood/energy is “off” after alcohol, test one fewer drink or lower‑alcohol options for three weekends of drinking occasions.

### Handling Confounders
- Tag each day with a simple **day‑type** label.  
- Primary analysis focuses on “clean” days (no confounder boxes checked).  
- Secondary analysis groups days by each confounder (e.g., all *late workout* days) to see independent effects.

### Review Process
1. Apply the **bad‑sleep score** to each night.  
2. Flag nights with ≥2 points as **bad sleep nights**.  
3. Examine common tags among flagged nights (caffeine after 2 pm, alcohol close to bedtime, specific day types).  
4. Use insights to adjust timing/quantity and repeat the cycle.

The overall aim is to move from autopilot consumption to data‑driven, incremental adjustments that support better sleep and daytime performance.
