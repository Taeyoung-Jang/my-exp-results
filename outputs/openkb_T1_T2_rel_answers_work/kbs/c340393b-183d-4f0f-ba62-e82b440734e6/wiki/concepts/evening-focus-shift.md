# Evening Focus Shift

**Definition**

Evening Focus Shift is the practice of moving cognitively demanding work to the earlier part of the evening (roughly 6 pm–7 pm) so that natural energy peaks are leveraged and the need for caffeine‑based energy chews or other stimulants is reduced.

**Why it matters**

- Natural circadian rhythms provide a small energy boost after the workday, which can be harnessed for high‑focus tasks.
- By front‑loading focus work, the later “dip” period (around 7:30 pm–8:30 pm) can be filled with lighter activities, reducing the temptation to reach for a chew.
- Aligns with a broader **[[concepts/chew-tapering-strategy]]** by creating a smoother transition off stimulants.

**Key steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-13]])**
1. **Identify the most demanding tasks** for the day (e.g., analysis, writing, coding).
2. **Schedule them for 6 pm–7 pm** – the window before the typical evening chew.
3. **Take a brief break for dinner or a snack** at 7 pm to separate focus work from the natural dip.
4. **Shift to lighter tasks** (organising, planning) from 7:30 pm onward.
5. **Replace the chew cue** with a non‑caffeinated sensory ritual (e.g., a citrus seltzer) if cravings arise.

**Interaction with other concepts**
- Works hand‑in‑hand with **[[concepts/low-stimulation-reset]]**: after the focused block, a short reset (stretching, walk) helps maintain calm without a stimulant surge.
- Supports the **[[concepts/decision-rules]]** used at 7:30 pm to decide whether to continue work or stop for the night.

**Benefits**
- Reduces caffeine intake and its impact on sleep.
- Creates a predictable evening rhythm, improving sleep hygiene.
- Enhances overall productivity by aligning tasks with natural energy cycles.
