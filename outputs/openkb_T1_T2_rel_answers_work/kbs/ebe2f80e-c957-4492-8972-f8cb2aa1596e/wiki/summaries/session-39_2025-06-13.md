## Summary
This document provides a concise, actionable guide for end‑of‑life planning. It outlines a 10‑step checklist covering legal documents, financial information, and digital legacy; supplies a one‑page fill‑in‑the‑blank template with sections for **in case of death**, **in case of incapacity**, and a new **tiny decisions** area; and offers low‑drama communication tips for asking an executor or POA. It also highlights common pitfalls and suggests delegating administrative or emotional tasks during probate and funeral arrangements.

## Key Checklist Items
1. **Will** – create and store securely will
2. **Healthcare Directive** – name a medical decision‑maker healthcare directive
3. **Durable Power of Attorney** – designate a trusted financial agent power of attorney
4. **Financial & Asset Inventory** – list accounts, insurance, property estate planning checklist
5. **Practical Instructions** – mortgages, utilities, contacts
6. **Funeral & Memorial Wishes** – burial, cremation, service preferences funeral arrangements
7. **Password Manager** – centralize logins, set emergency access digital legacy
8. **Digital Legacy Contact** – Apple, Google, Facebook settings
9. **Encrypted Digital Storage** – cloud or offline, shared with trusted people secure document storage
10. **Communication** – notify two trusted individuals of location and access

## One‑Page Template Overview
A fillable sheet with headings for:
- Personal Information
- Document Locations
- **Executor (In Case of Death)**
- **POA / Decision‑Maker (In Case of Incapacity)**
- Key Accounts & Assets
- Digital Life
- Practical Instructions
- **Tiny Decisions That Matter Later**
Each section provides prompts for names, contact details, and specific preferences.

## Tiny Decisions Section
Captures the often‑overlooked choices that surface during probate:
- Obituary wording and tone
- Service details (venue, music, readings)
- Disposition (burial, cremation, donation)
- Immediate notifications
- Handling of personal items (photos, letters, heirlooms)
- Explicit “don’t do this” wishes

## Delegation & Pitfalls
- **What not to handle alone:** extensive document gathering, routine phone calls, legal form clarification, and emotionally heavy decisions (e.g., obituary wording). Enlist a trusted friend, family member, or professional (lawyer, funeral director) for support.
- **Common pitfalls:** procrastination, scattered documents, outdated designations, relying on verbal wishes, and failing to share access information.

## Suggested Communication Approach
Frame the conversation as routine preparation: *“I’m organizing my paperwork so nothing is chaotic later; would you be comfortable serving as my executor/POA? It’s just a precaution, not an immediate burden.”* Emphasize health, trust, and the goal of reducing future stress.
