# Zero‑Based Budgeting

Zero‑Based Budgeting is a budgeting method where **every dollar of monthly income is allocated to a defined expense, savings, or buffer category**, so that the total of all allocations exactly equals net income.  The approach forces conscious decisions about spending, highlights discretionary excesses, and creates a built‑in safety net.

## Core Principles
- **Income equals outflows**: Add up all sources of net take‑home pay and then assign that exact amount to line‑items.
- **All categories are explicit**: Housing, utilities, debt payments, discretionary spending, a buffer for unexpected costs, and any extra debt repayment are listed separately.
- **Zero surplus or deficit**: The budget balances to zero, eliminating “unassigned” money that might be inadvertently spent.
- **Iterative adjustments**: If the budget overshoots income, discretionary categories are trimmed until the balance is zero.

## Example from [[summaries/session-11_2023-06-15]]
The session‑11 conversation applied a zero‑based budget to a user with a net monthly income of **$4,650**.  The allocation was:

| Category | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| **Housing & Essentials** (rent, utilities, internet, cell, car payment, insurance, gas, groceries, health insurance) | $3,430 |
| **Debt minimum payments** (Credit Card A, Credit Card B, personal loan, student loan) | $830 |
| **Dining / coffee** | $200 |
| **Miscellaneous shopping** | $100 |
| **Subscriptions** | $50 |
| **Buffer (flex category for unexpected expenses)** | $100 |
| **Extra payment to Credit Card A** (avalanche strategy) | $300 |
| **Starter emergency fund contribution** | $0 (paused) |
| **Total** | **$4,650** |

Key take‑aways from this example:
- Reducing discretionary spending from $675 to $350 freed **$300** for extra debt repayment while still preserving a **$100 buffer**.
- The buffer helps avoid new credit‑card charges, supporting the broader goal of building an **[[concepts/emergency-fund]]**.
- The extra $300 is directed toward the highest‑interest debt, aligning with the **[[concepts/debt-avalanche-method]]**.

## Benefits
- **Clarity**: Users see exactly where each dollar goes.
- **Control**: By assigning a buffer, the plan reduces reliance on credit cards.
- **Flexibility**: Once debts are paid down, the freed‑up amounts can be reallocated to savings or other goals.

## When to Combine with Other Concepts
- Pair with the **[[concepts/debt-avalanche-method]]** to prioritize high‑interest debt while maintaining a cash cushion.
- Use an **[[concepts/emergency-fund]]** as the buffer category until a starter fund (e.g., $1,000) is built, then shift surplus to debt or long‑term savings.
- Track balances of specific obligations such as **[[entities/credit-card-a]]**, **[[entities/credit-card-b]]**, **[[entities/personal-loan]]**, and **[[entities/student-loan]]** within the budget.

## Implementation Steps
1. List all sources of net income.
2. Record **fixed essential** costs.
3. Add **minimum debt payments**.
4. Allocate a modest **buffer** (e.g., $100) for irregular expenses.
5. Determine discretionary categories and set realistic limits.
6. Direct any remaining surplus to **extra debt payments** or **savings**.
7. Review monthly; adjust categories as balances change.

Zero‑Based Budgeting thus provides a disciplined, transparent framework that supports debt reduction, emergency‑fund growth, and long‑term financial stability.
