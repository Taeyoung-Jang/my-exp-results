## Retirement Contributions

**Retirement contributions** refer to the portion of income that an individual sets aside—typically in tax‑advantaged accounts such as 401(k)s, IRAs, or similar plans—to fund future financial security after leaving the workforce.

### Why Contribute Early and Consistently?
- **Compounding growth:** Money invested now has more time to compound, increasing the eventual balance.
- **Employer match:** Many workplaces offer an [[concepts/employer-match]]; contributing enough to capture the full match yields an immediate, risk‑free return.
- **Risk mitigation:** Regular contributions protect against the temptation to postpone saving and reduce the impact of market timing.

### Prioritization Guidance from Session 37
The advice in [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]] recommends the following order of financial actions:
1. **Establish a basic emergency cushion** (see [[concepts/emergency-fund]]).  
2. **Pay down high‑interest debt** (e.g., using a debt‑avalanche approach).  
3. **Start or increase retirement contributions**, especially to capture any employer match.

When the user’s debt payments are cleared and cash flow improves, the suggestion is to:
- Allocate enough of the freed‑up income to **max out the employer match** first.
- Direct the remaining amount toward growing the emergency fund until it reaches a “solid enough” level (typically 3‑6 months of expenses).
- Once the emergency fund target is met, shift the bulk of the surplus back to **retirement contributions** for accelerated long‑term growth.

### How Much to Contribute?
- **Minimum for match:** Contribute at least the percentage required to receive the full employer match (often 3‑6 % of payroll).
- **Beyond the match:** After the emergency fund is adequately funded, aim to increase contributions toward the statutory limits (e.g., $22,500 for 401(k)s in 2024) or personal targets.

### Interaction with Liquidity Management
Retirement accounts are typically **illiquid** until retirement age, so they should not be used for short‑term cash needs. This underscores the need for a well‑funded [[concepts/emergency-fund]] and proper [[concepts/liquidity-management]] via high‑yield savings or money‑market vehicles.

### Practical Steps
1. **Set up automatic payroll deductions** to ensure contributions are consistent.
2. **Review employer match formulas** (e.g., 100 % match up to 4 % of salary) and adjust contributions accordingly.
3. **Periodically rebalance** and increase contributions as income grows or debts are paid off.
4. **Monitor the balance** of the emergency fund; once it reaches the desired multi‑month coverage, redirect additional cash toward retirement.
