# Liquidity Management

**Definition**: Liquidity Management is the practice of allocating cash and near‑cash assets so that funds are readily available for short‑term needs while still earning a reasonable return.

---

## Why It Matters

Effective liquidity management supports the three‑step financial foundation described in [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]]:
1. **Emergency cushion** – immediate access for unexpected bills.
2. **Debt control** – avoiding high‑interest costs.
3. **Retirement growth** – long‑term investing once short‑term stability is secured.

Balancing these priorities requires keeping some money in ultra‑liquid accounts (e.g., a [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]]) and moving surplus into slightly less liquid but higher‑yielding vehicles such as a [[entities/money-market-fund]] or short‑term [[entities/t-bill]]s.

---

## Core Strategies (from Session 37)

1. **Tiered Storage**
   - **Tier 1 – Immediate Access**: 1‑2 months of essential expenses in a high‑yield savings account. This tier provides the fastest access with negligible risk.
   - **Tier 2 – Low‑Risk Yield**: 1‑2 months in a money‑market fund, offering a modestly higher rate while remaining highly liquid.
   - **Tier 3 – Slightly Higher Yield, Still Liquid**: Remaining funds placed in short‑term Treasury bills (typically 3‑month T‑bills) organized in a ladder so that a portion matures each month.

2. **T‑Bill Ladder**
   - Purchase three‑month [[entities/t-bill]]s staggered one month apart.
   - After the initial three purchases, one bill matures each month, allowing you to either roll it into a new three‑month bill or draw cash if needed.
   - This creates a predictable, rotating stream of liquid cash that earns a bit more than a savings account.

3. **Account Consolidation**
   - Keep the HYSA at an online bank for Tier 1.
   - Use a single taxable [[entities/brokerage-account]] to hold both the money‑market fund and the T‑bill ladder, simplifying monitoring and transaction costs.
   - While you could use [[entities/treasurydirect]] for T‑bills, a brokerage typically offers faster execution and easier early‑sale options.

---

## Practical Tips

- **Capture Employer Match First**: If you have an [[concepts/employer-match]], allocate enough of the freed‑up cash to achieve the full match before expanding liquidity tiers. The match provides an instant, risk‑free return.
- **Re‑evaluate Periodically**: As your emergency‑fund goal (often 3‑6 months of expenses) is approached, shift excess cash from Tier 1 into Tier 2 or Tier 3, and increase [[concepts/retirement-contributions]].
- **Early Sale Considerations**: Selling a [[entities/t-bill]] before maturity in a brokerage may incur a tiny market‑price variance, but the impact is minimal for short‑term bills.
- **Liquidity vs. Yield Trade‑off**: The further you move cash from HYSA to money‑market to T‑bills, the higher the expected yield, but the slightly longer the time to access funds. Align the mix with your comfort level for occasional cash needs.
