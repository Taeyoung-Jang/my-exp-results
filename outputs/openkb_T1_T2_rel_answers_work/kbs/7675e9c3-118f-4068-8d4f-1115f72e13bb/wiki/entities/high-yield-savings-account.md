# High‑Yield Savings Account

A **High‑Yield Savings Account** (HYSA) is an online deposit account that typically provides a significantly higher interest rate than a regular checking or traditional savings account while maintaining FDIC insurance and easy access to funds.

## Role in Personal Finance
- Serves as the **first layer** of an [[concepts/emergency-fund]], often holding about two months of essential expenses for immediate liquidity.
- Supports [[concepts/liquidity-management]] by allowing quick withdrawals without penalties, making it ideal for cash that may be needed on short notice.
- Can be used to capture an **employer match** indirectly by freeing up cash to contribute to retirement accounts; the HYSA holds the cash until it is redirected to [[concepts/retirement-contributions]].

## Recommended Allocation (per [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]])
- **2 months of expenses** placed in a HYSA for instant access.
- Additional emergency cash may be split into a [[entities/money-market-fund]] and short‑term [[entities/t-bill]] holdings for modest yield improvements while retaining liquidity.

## Practical Considerations
- Choose an online bank that offers competitive APY, no monthly fees, and easy electronic transfers to a [[entities/brokerage-account]] for further investment.
- Ensure the account is linked to a [[entities/checking-account]] for seamless movement of funds when needed.
- Unlike [[entities/credit-card-a]] or [[entities/credit-card-b]], a HYSA does not involve revolving credit; it is a pure savings vehicle.

## Example Usage Flow
1. Deposit freed‑up monthly cash into the HYSA.
2. Build the first two months of emergency coverage.
3. Once the HYSA layer is funded, allocate additional cash to a money‑market fund or purchase short‑term T‑bills via a brokerage.
4. Continue contributing to retirement accounts to capture any [[concepts/employer-match]].
