# Money Market Fund

A **money market fund** is a type of mutual fund that invests in short‑term, high‑quality debt securities such as commercial paper, Treasury bills, and certificates of deposit.  It aims to provide investors with **high liquidity**, modest yields, and minimal price volatility, making it a common choice for the middle layer of an [[concepts/emergency-fund]] strategy.

## Key Points from [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]]
- Recommended as the second tier of a six‑month emergency fund, holding roughly **two months of essential expenses**.
- Offers better interest than a regular high‑yield savings account while still allowing quick access to cash.
- Typically held in a **taxable brokerage account** alongside short‑term [[entities/t-bill]] holdings for easy management.
- Works well in a **liquidity‑management** approach that balances accessibility with modest returns.

## Typical Use Cases
- **Emergency fund tiering**: Pairing a high‑yield savings account (first month‑to‑month) with a money market fund for the next month‑to‑month provides a stepped liquidity profile.
- **Cash‑reserve allocation**: Investors often allocate surplus cash here while awaiting higher‑yield opportunities or while maintaining a safety net.
- **Bridge to short‑term Treasury investments**: Money market funds can sit alongside a [[entities/t-bill]] ladder, offering immediate liquidity while the T‑bills mature over time.

## Practical Implementation
1. Open a **brokerage account** (e.g., [[entities/brokerage-account]]) that offers a low‑expense, government‑money‑market fund.
2. Transfer the designated portion of the emergency fund (≈2 months of expenses) into the fund.
3. Keep the fund’s balance aligned with the target emergency‑fund amount; rebalance as needed when cash is added or withdrawn.
