## Brokerage Account

A **brokerage account** is a financial product that allows an individual to hold, buy, and sell a variety of investment assets, including stocks, bonds, money‑market funds, and Treasury securities.

### Key Facts from [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]]
- Used as the central hub for the **middle‑layer** of an emergency fund: the money‑market fund and short‑term T‑bills are recommended to be kept in a taxable brokerage account for ease of management.
- Preferred over [[entities/treasurydirect]] for buying T‑bills because it offers a more streamlined interface, quicker access, and the ability to liquidate holdings if cash is needed before maturity.
- Enables simple implementation of a **T‑bill ladder** (e.g., purchasing staggered 3‑month T‑bills) while keeping the assets in the same account as the money‑market fund.

### Relationship to Concepts
- Supports [[concepts/liquidity-management]] by providing rapid access to cash while still earning modest returns.
- Plays a role in building an [[concepts/emergency-fund]] when the fund is split across HYSA, money‑market, and T‑bills.
- Facilitates contributions to [[concepts/retirement-contributions]] when the investor chooses to hold retirement‑eligible securities in the same platform.
- Complements the strategy of capturing an [[concepts/employer-match]] by allowing easy allocation of surplus cash to retirement accounts within the brokerage.

### Related Entities
- [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]] – recommended for the ultra‑liquid first layer of the emergency fund.
- [[entities/money-market-fund]] – often held in the same brokerage account as the T‑bills.
- [[entities/t-bill]] – short‑term Treasury securities purchased via the brokerage.

### Practical Tips
1. Open a **taxable brokerage account** with a reputable firm (e.g., Vanguard, Fidelity, Charles Schwab).
2. Allocate the first 1‑2 months of expenses to a high‑yield savings account for immediate access.
3. Place the next 1‑2 months in a money‑market fund within the brokerage for modest yield and liquidity.
4. Invest the final 1‑2 months in short‑term T‑bills using a laddered approach to ensure regular maturities.
5. Review the account periodically to rebalance the emergency‑fund layers and to increase [[concepts/retirement-contributions]] as the fund reaches its target size.
