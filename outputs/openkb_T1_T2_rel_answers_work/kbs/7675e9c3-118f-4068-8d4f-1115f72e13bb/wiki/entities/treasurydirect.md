# TreasuryDirect

**Type:** organization

TreasuryDirect is the official online portal operated by the U.S. Department of the Treasury that lets individuals purchase and hold Treasury securities such as [[entities/t-bill]]s directly, without a broker.

## Role in Emergency‑Fund Strategy (Session 37)
In the discussion of a tiered emergency fund, TreasuryDirect was mentioned as an alternative venue for holding short‑term Treasury bills. While it provides direct access to low‑risk government securities, the session noted that it is **clunky** and slower to move cash compared with using a [[entities/brokerage-account]].

## Comparison with Brokerage Accounts
- **Ease of purchase:** Brokerages allow quick buying of [[entities/t-bill]]s with a few clicks; TreasuryDirect requires a separate login and longer processing times.
- **Consolidation:** Keeping both the money‑market fund and T‑bills in a single brokerage simplifies tracking and rebalancing.
- **Liquidity:** If cash is needed before a bill matures, a brokerage lets you sell on the secondary market (with minimal price risk for short‑term bills). TreasuryDirect does not offer a secondary‑market sell option; you must wait for maturity or transfer the security to a broker, which can be cumbersome.
- **Access speed:** Funds can be transferred from a brokerage to a checking or high‑yield savings account more rapidly than from TreasuryDirect.

## Practical Considerations
- Use TreasuryDirect if you prefer a **direct government relationship** and don’t need frequent buying/selling.
- Prefer a brokerage for a **liquidity‑focused emergency fund**, as it supports both a [[entities/money-market-fund]] and easy T‑bill transactions, aligning with the [[concepts/liquidity-management]] approach.
- For investors aiming to capture an [[concepts/employer-match]] and grow retirement savings, the speed and convenience of a brokerage often outweigh TreasuryDirect’s marginal benefits.
