# T‑Bill (U.S. Treasury Bill)

A **T‑Bill** (short for Treasury Bill) is a short‑term government debt security issued by the United States Treasury.  They are sold at a discount and mature at face value, typically in periods of 4, 8, 13, 26, or 52 weeks.  Because they are backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government, they are considered a very low‑risk, highly liquid investment.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]]
- **Use in emergency‑fund layering**: The session suggests allocating the final two months of a six‑month emergency fund to short‑term T‑bills, providing a modest yield while preserving liquidity.
- **T‑bill ladder**: A simple ladder can be built by purchasing three‑month T‑bills staggered one month apart, so one matures each month.  Matured bills can be rolled into a new three‑month T‑bill or used for cash needs.
- **Account placement**: The recommendation is to hold T‑bills in a **brokerage account** rather than using TreasuryDirect, because brokerages simplify buying, consolidating with other assets, and allow easier liquidation if cash is needed before maturity.
- **Liquidity considerations**: While T‑bills are highly liquid, selling them before maturity in a brokerage may incur a tiny market‑price risk; however, price fluctuations are minimal for very short‑term bills.

## Practical implementation
- **Brokerage account**: Use a taxable [[entities/brokerage-account]] to purchase and manage T‑bills alongside a [[entities/money-market-fund]].
- **Alternative platform**: TreasuryDirect ([[entities/treasurydirect]]) is an option but is less user‑friendly for frequent transactions.
- **Integration with liquidity strategy**: T‑bills complement other liquid assets such as a [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]] and a money‑market fund, supporting overall [[concepts/liquidity-management]].
