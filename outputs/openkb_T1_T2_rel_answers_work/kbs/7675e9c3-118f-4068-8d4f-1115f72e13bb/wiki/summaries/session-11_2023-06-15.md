# Summary of Session 11 (2023‑06‑15)

**Purpose**: The user seeks a realistic monthly budget and a debt‑payoff plan, comparing the avalanche and snowball methods, and wants accurate payoff timelines with interest calculations.

## Key Concepts
- [[concepts/zero-based-budgeting]] – assigning every dollar of income to a specific category, including a buffer.  
- [[concepts/debt-avalanche-method]] – prioritising debts by highest interest rate to minimise total interest.  
- emergency fund – a small cash reserve to avoid new credit‑card usage.  
- budget buffer – a flex category for unexpected expenses.  
- monthly interest rate – derived from APR ÷ 12 (nominal, compounded monthly).  
- payoff formula – `n = -log(1 - r·P / A) / log(1 + r)` for months needed to retire a balance.

## Debt Avalanche Payoff Plan (full extra $300)
- **[[entities/credit-card-a]]**: $6,800 @ 24.99% → payment $510/mo (210 min + 300 extra) → *≈ 14 months*; interest ≈ $1,000.  
- **[[entities/credit-card-b]]**: $3,200 @ 19.99% → after Card A, payment $510/mo → *≈ 6 months*; interest ≈ $150.  
- **[[entities/personal-loan]]**: $12,400 @ 11.5% → payment $915/mo (310 min + 605 rolled) → *≈ 15 months*; interest ≈ $900.  
- **[[entities/student-loan]]**: $18,900 @ 5.2% → payment $1,130/mo (215 min + 915 rolled) → *≈ 18 months*; interest ≈ $650.

Total horizon ≈ 55 months (~4.5 years) with total interest roughly $2,700.

## Alternative: Build $1,000 Emergency Fund First
- Divert $100/mo to savings for ~10 months, reducing extra payment to [[entities/credit-card-a]] to $200 (total $410).  
- **Card A payoff**: ~20 months total (10 months at $410, then 10 months at $510); interest ≈ $1,500.  
- Subsequent phases shift by ~4 months, leaving the final horizon around 59 months.

## Recommendations
1. Adopt the [[concepts/zero-based-budgeting]] with a $100 buffer.  
2. Prioritise the avalanche method; the interest savings outweigh the motivational benefit of snowball here.  
3. Pause the $100 regular savings until the $1,000 starter emergency fund is reached, then resume.  
4. Re‑evaluate discretionary limits after the emergency fund is in place to ensure sustainability.

*All calculations treat APR as nominal and compounded monthly.*
