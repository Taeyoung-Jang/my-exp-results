# Summary of Session 37 (2024‑12‑15)

## Prioritization Framework
- Build a **basic emergency cushion** first (cover unexpected bills).  
- Pay down **high‑interest debt**, as its cost usually exceeds any investment return.  
- Begin **[[concepts/retirement-contributions]]** after the above, especially to capture any employer [[concepts/employer-match]].  
- Continue expanding the emergency fund toward a **3‑to‑6‑month** expense buffer while maintaining regular retirement savings.

## Allocating Freed‑up Monthly Money
- If you have an employer match, allocate enough to capture the full match immediately – it’s an instant, risk‑free return.  
- Direct the remaining freed‑up amount to the **[[concepts/emergency-fund]]** until it reaches your target level.  
- Once the fund is “solid enough,” shift the bulk of the extra cash toward **[[concepts/retirement-contributions]]**.

## Defining a “Solid Enough” Emergency Fund
- Standard recommendation: **3‑to‑6 months** of essential expenses.  
- With **stable income** but **variable expenses**, lean toward the higher end (5‑6 months).  
- Consider the size of your worst realistic surprise (e.g., car repair, medical bill) to set the target.

## Tiered Storage Strategy for a 6‑Month Fund
1. **[[entities/high-yield-savings-account]]** –≈ 2 months of expenses for instant access.  
2. **[[entities/money-market-fund]]** –≈ 2 months; still highly liquid, slightly better yield.  
3. **Short‑Term Treasury Bills** –≈ 2 months; low risk with modest yield, organized in a ladder.

## Example Split & Simple T‑Bill Ladder
- **2 months** in a high‑yield savings account, **2 months** in a money‑market fund, **2 months** in 3‑month Treasury Bills.  
- **T‑bill ladder**: purchase three‑month Treasury Bills staggered one month apart. After the first three purchases, one bill matures each month, which can be rolled into a new three‑month bill or used for cash needs.

## Practical Implementation & Account Choices
- **[[entities/high-yield-savings-account]]**: online bank (e.g., Ally, Capital One 360).  
- **Money‑Market Fund & Treasury Bills**: taxable **[[entities/brokerage-account]]** (e.g., Vanguard, Fidelity) for ease of management.  
- **Brokerage vs. TreasuryDirect**: brokerages offer faster buying, consolidated view, and easier liquidation. TreasuryDirect works but is less user‑friendly.  
- **Early withdrawal**: selling a Treasury Bill before maturity in a brokerage incurs a tiny market‑price risk; face value is guaranteed at maturity.
