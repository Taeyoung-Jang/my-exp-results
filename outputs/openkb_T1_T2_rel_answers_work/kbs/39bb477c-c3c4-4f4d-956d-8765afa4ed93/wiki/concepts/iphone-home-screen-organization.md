## iPhone Home Screen Organization

**Definition**: A set of practices for arranging apps, folders, and the dock on an iPhone to keep the most‑used tools immediately accessible while reducing visual noise.

### Core Principles
1. **Dock Prioritization**: Place four anchor apps in the dock – typically Phone, Messages, a web browser, and the primary productivity hub (e.g., Notes or Tasks).
2. **First‑Screen Focus**: Keep 3‑4 top‑frequency apps on the first home‑screen page without folders, ensuring one‑tap access.
3. **Limited Folders**: Use only two or three broad folders on subsequent screens:
   - *Work Essentials*: professional tools.
   - *Personal / Admin*: finance, health, utilities.
   - *Occasional Utilities*: scanning, budgeting, etc.
4. **Visual Breathing Space**: Leave empty slots between folders and apps to create a calm layout.

### Practical Steps (derived from [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-15]] and updated with insights from [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-14]])
- Identify the apps you open daily; place the three or four most common on the front page.
- Consolidate web‑heavy services (social media, shopping, news, banking, streaming) into Safari home‑screen shortcuts instead of native apps. This aligns with a **browser‑first phone** approach and reduces background updates.
- Keep native apps only for system‑integrated functions: Calls, Messages, FaceTime, Maps, Camera, Photos, Wallet, Settings, App Store, a 2FA/authenticator app (or passkeys), and your primary email or music player.
- Review the dock weekly to ensure it reflects your current anchor needs and adjust folder contents as your workflow evolves.
- In **iOS Settings → General → Background App Refresh**, disable the global toggle or turn it off per non‑essential app to further limit background activity.
- Trim Siri suggestions via **Settings → Siri & Search** to reduce passive background processing.

### Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader strategy for reducing app overload, linked to [[concepts/app-clutter-management]].
- Supports the minimalist device philosophy described in [[concepts/minimal-iphone-setup]].
- Works hand‑in‑hand with the **browser‑first phone** model ([[concepts/browser-first-phone]]) that emphasizes Safari shortcuts for non‑essential services.

### Benefits
- Faster access to essential tools.
- Lower cognitive load from fewer visual elements.
- Consistent layout that adapts as your workflow evolves, especially when adopting a browser‑first strategy and tighter iOS background settings.
