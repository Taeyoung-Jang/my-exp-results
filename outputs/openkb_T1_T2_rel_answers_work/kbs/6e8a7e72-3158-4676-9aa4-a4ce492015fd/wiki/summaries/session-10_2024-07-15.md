# Summary of session-10_2024-07-15

**Main focus:** How to assess whether a [[entities/premium-subscription]] is truly valuable over time and a practical method for testing its necessity.

## Key ideas
- **Usage‑based evaluation** – Look at how often premium features are used and whether they *actually* save time, reduce friction, or improve outcomes rather than merely feeling nice.
- **Trial‑off period** – Start with a short, low‑stakes experiment (≈ 1 week for frequent users, up to a month for occasional use) to compare performance with and without the premium tools. See [[concepts/trial-off-experiments]].
- **Separating habit irritation from real impact** – Measure concrete outcomes (time taken, quality of results) instead of relying on the feeling of annoyance when habits are disrupted.
- **Long‑term users** – Even after years of reliance, a gentle “avoid‑premium” week (or light‑use week) can reveal true dependence without causing major disruption.
- **Tracking template** – Log each day:
  1. **Task** that normally involves a premium feature.  
  2. **How it went without the feature** (time estimate, quality assessment).  
  3. **Impact rating** – "no change", "slightly worse", "significantly worse" or note irritation spikes.
- **Scoring the week** – Review notes for patterns; predominance of brief irritation indicates a *nice‑to‑have* perk, while consistent slower or poorer outcomes signals genuine value.

## Suggested workflow
1. **Pick the window** – 1‑week for regular users; extend to a month if usage is sporadic.  
2. **Set a fallback rule** – Allow emergency use of premium tools for critical tasks to keep the test realistic.  
3. **Log daily** using the simple template above.  
4. **Analyze** at week‑end: tally impact ratings, note any “caught‑it‑self‑wanting‑feature” moments.  
5. **Decision** – If the majority of entries are “no change” or only minor irritation, consider canceling; otherwise keep.
