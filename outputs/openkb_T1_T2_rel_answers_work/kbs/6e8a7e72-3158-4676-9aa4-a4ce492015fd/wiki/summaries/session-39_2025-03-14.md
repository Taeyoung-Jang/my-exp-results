# Summary of session-39_2025-03-14

**Main idea:** Provides practical criteria and simple processes for evaluating whether a subscription service is worth paying for and for keeping subscriptions under control.

## Decision criteria
- Frequency of use  
- Rate of product updates / ongoing value (see [[concepts/premium-value-assessment]])  
- Availability of a comparable free or one‑time option  
- The “Would I miss it if it cancelled?” test (part of [[concepts/subscription-evaluation]])

## Typical categories
- **Usually not worth it:** single‑purpose apps, utility tools, devices that lock core features behind a fee.  
- **Usually worth it:** streaming services, cloud storage, professional software with continual improvements.  
(These distinctions are covered by [[concepts/subscription-categories]].)

## Checklist before starting a new subscription
1. Will I use it often enough to offset the monthly cost?  
2. Does the subscription add continuous value beyond unlocking static features?  
3. Is there a free or one‑time alternative that meets my needs?  
4. Would I miss it if it disappeared tomorrow?

## Light‑weight monthly review
- Choose a single day each month.  
- Look at the payment method used for subscriptions.  
- Ask the “miss‑it” question for each charge.  
- Cancel any service that feels like padding.  

Use [[concepts/subscription-tracking]] to consolidate renewal dates in a calendar with alerts and to keep all subscriptions on one payment method for easy oversight.

## Default rule & trial period
- Only try a subscription that solves an existing problem, not a hypothetical one.  
- Test it for 2–4 weeks; if you forget it or have to force use, cancel.  

The short trial guideline aligns with [[concepts/trial-off-experiments]].
