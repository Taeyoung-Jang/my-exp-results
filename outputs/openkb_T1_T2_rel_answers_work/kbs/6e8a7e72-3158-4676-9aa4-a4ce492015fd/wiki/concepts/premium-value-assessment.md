# Premium Value Assessment

**Definition**: A systematic, data‑driven approach to determine whether the benefits of a premium subscription justify its ongoing cost, based on actual usage, measurable productivity impact, the “would I miss it if cancelled?” test, and clear distinction between essential functionality and mere convenience.

## Core Components

1. **Usage Frequency**
   - Track how often premium‑only features are employed in routine tasks.
   - High frequency suggests core reliance; low frequency points to optional niceties.

2. **Productivity Impact**
   - Measure concrete outcomes such as time saved, reduction in errors, or improvement in output quality.
   - Compare these metrics against a baseline when the premium features are disabled.
   - See [[concepts/productivity-impact-metrics]] for detailed measurement ideas.

3. **Trial‑Off Experiment**
   - Conduct a short, controlled period (typically 1 week) where premium features are intentionally avoided or used minimally.
   - Record tasks, time taken, and perceived quality.
   - Analyze results to separate habit irritation from genuine loss of efficiency.
   - The experimental design is outlined in [[concepts/trial-off-experiments]].

4. **Decision Test & Default Rule**
   - Apply the “Would I miss it if it cancelled?” question to each premium feature during the trial.
   - Follow the default rule: only try a subscription that solves an existing problem, not a hypothetical one.
   - If the answer is no, the subscription likely does not add true value.
   - This aligns with broader [[concepts/subscription-evaluation]] practices.

5. **Scoring & Decision Making**
   - Use a simple rating system (e.g., "no change," "slightly worse," "significantly worse") for each logged task.
   - Aggregate scores to identify patterns: predominance of irritation indicates a "nice‑to‑have" perk, while consistent negative impact signals true value.

## Practical Workflow (from [[summaries/session-10_2024-07-15]])

1. **Define the Window** – Choose 1 week for frequent users; extend to a month for occasional usage (the 2–4 week test period suggested in [[summaries/session-39_2025-03-14]] is also acceptable).
2. **Set a Fallback Rule** – Allow emergency use of premium tools for critical tasks to keep the test realistic.
3. **Daily Log Template** – Capture task, how it went without the feature, and impact rating.
4. **Weekly Review** – Tally ratings, note any "caught‑it‑self‑wanting‑feature" moments, and decide whether to retain or cancel the subscription.
5. **Tracking & Alerts** – Add renewal dates to a calendar with reminders and consolidate all subscriptions onto a single payment method for easy monthly review.  See [[concepts/subscription-tracking]] for more guidance.

## Benefits of Premium Value Assessment
- Prevents unnecessary recurring costs.
- Highlights hidden efficiencies that may be overlooked.
- Encourages data‑driven decision making rather than relying on brand perception or habit.
- Provides a clear, repeatable framework that works across different subscription categories (see [[concepts/subscription-categories]]).
