# Subscription Categories

**Definition**: A framework for grouping subscription services based on their typical value proposition, usage patterns, and update frequency.

## Core categories

1. **High‑value, evolving subscriptions** – Services that continuously improve or provide ongoing access to a large, changing content library. Examples include streaming platforms, cloud storage, and professional software with regular feature updates. These tend to **justify recurring fees** because the user receives **continuous, incremental value**.
2. **Low‑value, static subscriptions** – Services that offer a fixed set of features with little or no change over time, such as single‑purpose utility apps or devices that lock core functionality behind a monthly charge. These often **fail the "Would I miss it if cancelled?" test** and are better suited to a one‑time purchase or a free alternative.

## Decision criteria (linked to [[concepts/subscription-evaluation]])
- **Frequency of use** – How often the service is needed in day‑to‑day activities.
- **Rate of updates / ongoing value** – Whether the provider adds meaningful improvements regularly.
- **Availability of free or one‑time alternatives** – If a comparable option exists without recurring cost.
- **Personal impact test** – "Would I miss it if it disappeared tomorrow?"

## Practical implications
- When evaluating a new service, place it in one of the two categories above to quickly gauge its likely worth.
- For **high‑value, evolving** subscriptions, consider a short trial (2‑4 weeks) to confirm natural integration into your workflow. See [[concepts/trial-off-experiments]] for guidance on trial periods.
- For **low‑value, static** subscriptions, seek a one‑time purchase or free version before committing.

## Tracking and review (see [[concepts/subscription-tracking]])
- Record the category of each subscription in a personal tracker.
- Conduct a lightweight monthly review: check charges, apply the "miss‑it" test, and re‑categorize if usage patterns have shifted.
