## Summary

This session provides a practical framework for homeowners deciding whether to **extend an existing circuit** or **install a new circuit** for a light fixture or outlet. It covers:

- **Permits & legality** – When a permit is typically required (new cable runs, circuit extensions) and how to verify local requirements. [[concepts/permit-requirements]]
- **Safety considerations** – Key red‑flags such as hidden junction boxes, mismatched wire gauges, overcrowded panels, and missing grounds. [[concepts/electrical-safety]]
- **Load estimation** – Quick rules of thumb: 12 A continuous limit for 15 A circuits and 16 A for 20 A circuits, distinguishing continuous vs. non‑continuous loads. [[concepts/load-estimation]]
- **Panel & box inspection** – Checklist for breaker size, labeling consistency, wire gauge matches, and box fill limits. Red‑flag criteria trigger a “run new circuit” decision.
- **Circuit tracing** – Low‑tech methods (breaker‑off test, visual cable direction) and optional handheld tracer to map the path before any demolition.
- **Decision tree** – A concise, on‑site flowchart that starts with identifying the breaker and ends with a clear go/no‑go for extending versus adding a new circuit, incorporating the numeric cut‑offs and stop conditions.
- **Assessment tool list** – Recommended non‑contact voltage tester, breaker finder/circuit tracer, flashlight, inspection mirror, and a notepad for mapping.

The guidance stays at the **planning level** (no step‑by‑step wiring instructions) and emphasizes stopping the project early if any safety or capacity red flags appear, favoring a new circuit and proper permitting.
