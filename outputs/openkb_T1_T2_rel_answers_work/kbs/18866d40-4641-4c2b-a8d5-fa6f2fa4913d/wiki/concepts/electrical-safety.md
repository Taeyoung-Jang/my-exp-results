# Electrical Safety

Electrical safety encompasses the precautions, checks, and best practices required to prevent hazards such as shock, fire, and equipment damage when performing residential wiring tasks. The concept is drawn from practical guidance in [[summaries/session-10_2022-06-12]], which outlines the key safety considerations for DIY homeowners and electricians.

## Core Safety Principles

1. **Identify and De‑energize** – Always locate the correct breaker for the area, turn it off, and verify loss of power with a non‑contact voltage tester before opening any box.
2. **Understand Circuit Capacity** – Evaluate load limits (e.g., 12 A continuous on 15 A circuits, 16 A on 20 A circuits) to avoid over‑loading which can cause overheating and fire. See [[concepts/load-estimation]] for the quick load‑estimation method.
3. **Inspect Panel and Boxes** –
   - Look for mismatched wire gauge and breaker size.
   - Check for crowded conductor routing, improvised splices, and missing or damaged grounding.
   - Ensure all boxes are rated for the number of conductors they contain.
4. **Detect Red‑Flag Conditions** – If any of the following are observed, stop work and consider a new circuit with a permit:
   - Overcrowded or improperly labeled panels.
   - Mixed cable types or missing neutrals/grounds in device boxes.
   - Signs of overheating, corrosion, or burned insulation.
   - Unclear or excessively long cable paths that would require many hidden junctions.
5. **Use Proper Tools** – Non‑contact voltage tester, breaker finder/circuit tracer, flashlight, inspection mirror, and a notepad for mapping are essential for safe assessment.

## Relationship to Permits

When safety checks reveal that a circuit extension would exceed capacity, involve complex routing, or require new wiring runs, a permit is typically required. This ties directly into [[concepts/permit-requirements]], which governs when a homeowner must obtain a permit before proceeding.

## Practical Workflow

1. **Identify breaker** – Label and confirm power loss.
2. **Map circuit** – List all devices on the breaker.
3. **Estimate load** – Apply the continuous‑load limits (see [[concepts/load-estimation]]).
4. **Panel inspection** – Verify proper breaker size, wire gauge, and tidy layout.
5. **Box inspection** – Check for space, grounding, and clean splices.
6. **Decision point** – If any safety red‑flags appear, opt for a new circuit and follow the appropriate permit process; otherwise, extending the circuit may be safe.

## Summary

Electrical safety is the foundational checklist that informs whether a homeowner can reliably extend an existing circuit or must resort to a new, permitted installation. By systematically applying these checks, risks are minimized and compliance with local codes is more likely.
