# Communication Strategies for Volunteers

**Definition**: A set of conversational techniques that help volunteers express their needs, set safety boundaries, and collaborate positively with coordinators.

## Core Elements
1. **Positive framing** – Start with enthusiasm (e.g., “I’m excited to help…”) before stating limitations.
2. **Clear, concise statements** – Directly mention the specific task you’re uncomfortable with (e.g., live‑power work) without over‑explaining.
3. **Offer alternatives** – Pair each boundary with a suggestion of tasks you can perform (greeting visitors, setting up chairs, sorting donations, etc.).
4. **Polite gratitude** – thank the coordinator for any offers to train or re‑assign, reinforcing a cooperative tone.
5. **Consistent tone** – Keep the conversation calm and friendly, even if push‑back occurs.

## Sample Phrases (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-06-12]])
- “I’m excited to help out here. Just a heads‑up that I’m not comfortable working with anything that involves electrical hookups or power tools, but I’m happy to take on other setup or support tasks.”
- “I understand setup is a team effort, and I’m glad to help with the parts I can do safely. I’m just not trained for electrical or tool work, so I don’t want to put myself or anyone else at risk.”
- “Thank you, I really appreciate the offer, but even with a quick walkthrough I’m not comfortable doing anything involving live power or tools. I’d rather stick to the tasks I can do safely.”
