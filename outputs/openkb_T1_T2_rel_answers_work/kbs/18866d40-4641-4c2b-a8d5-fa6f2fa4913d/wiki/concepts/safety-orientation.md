# Volunteer Safety Orientation

**Definition**: A brief, practical briefing that introduces new volunteers to the policies, procedures, and physical layout of a community center to ensure safe and effective participation.

## Core Elements
- **Check‑in process** – where and how volunteers sign in, and who they report to.
- **Emergency protocols** – basic steps for fire, medical incidents, and evacuation; location of exits, first‑aid stations, and emergency equipment.
- **Facility tour** – walkthrough of key areas such as storage rooms, activity spaces, and any zones that volunteers may use.
- **Interaction guidelines** – rules for engaging with visitors, children, and vulnerable groups, including confidentiality and conduct expectations.
- **Task boundaries** – clarification of which duties involve electrical or tool work and which are low‑risk, non‑equipment tasks.

## Preparation Tips for Volunteers
- Arrive on time and dress comfortably and appropriately.
- Review any pre‑orientation emails or handouts.
- Identify likely responsibilities and prepare a few concise questions.
- Be ready to discuss any safety concerns, especially regarding live power or power‑tool use.

## Communicating Limits
Volunteers should use clear, friendly language to state discomfort with electrical tasks and suggest alternative duties such as greeting visitors, organizing supplies, setting up chairs/tables, sorting donations, and assisting staff. Effective phrasing aligns with [[concepts/communication-strategies]] and respects [[concepts/electrical-safety]] guidelines.
