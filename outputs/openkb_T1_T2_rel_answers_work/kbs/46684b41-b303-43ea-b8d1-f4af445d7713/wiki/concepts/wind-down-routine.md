# Wind‑Down Routine

A **wind‑down routine** is a short, low‑stimulus sequence of activities performed shortly before bedtime that helps signal the brain it’s time to transition from wakefulness to sleep. Rather than a rigid schedule, the routine focuses on calming cues that reduce mental arousal and promote the natural onset of sleep.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-10]])
- **Dim lighting** – Lowering light levels about an hour before bed cues melatonin production.
- **Screen curfew** – Turning off phones, tablets, and computers at least 60 minutes prior to sleep reduces blue‑light exposure.
- **Simple, quiet activities** – Reading a few pages, gentle stretching, or mindful breathing are effective without demanding effort.
- **Consistent timing** – Performing the routine at roughly the same time each night helps anchor the circadian rhythm.

## Why It Matters
A wind‑down routine is a key component of overall [[concepts/sleep-hygiene]], providing a predictable transition that lowers stress hormones and prepares the body for a restful night. When paired with a **consistent sleep schedule** [[concepts/consistent-sleep-schedule]], it enhances sleep quality and makes it easier to fall asleep without feeling forced.

## Practical Tips
1. **Start 30–60 minutes before your intended bedtime.**
2. **Turn off bright screens** and switch to dim, warm lighting.
3. **Engage in a calming activity** such as reading a physical book, gentle yoga, or a short meditation.
4. **Avoid stimulating content** (e.g., work emails, intense movies).
5. **Keep the environment cool and quiet** – consider a white‑noise app if needed.
