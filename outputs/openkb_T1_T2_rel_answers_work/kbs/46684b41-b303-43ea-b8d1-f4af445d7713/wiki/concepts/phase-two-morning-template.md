# Phase‑Two Morning Template

**Definition**: A gentle, two‑hour morning structure (typically 7:00 – 9:00 am) that follows an initial wake‑up routine and uses two consistent *anchor habits* to cue the rest of the day, while leaving a flexible block for varied activities.

## Core Elements

1. **First Anchor – Focus Activity (7:00‑7:10)**
   - A brief, purposeful task that signals the start of the productive part of the morning.
   - Examples:
     - **Workday**: open the main project, review the task list, write a quick plan.
     - **Weekend**: read a few pages, do a short creative practice, or check personal goals.
   - Serves as a mental cue that transitions you from the dog‑walk/ breakfast routine into the day’s flow.

2. **Second Anchor – Physical/Environmental Reset (7:10‑7:25)**
   - A short stretch, tidy‑up, or small chore that creates a clean physical and mental space.
   - Helps solidify the momentum built by the first anchor.
   - Linked concept: [[concepts/anchor-habits]].

3. **Flexible Block (7:25‑9:00)**
   - An open period for deeper work, hobby time, reading, or additional chores.
   - The block is *guided* by the anchors but not rigidly scheduled, allowing adaptation to the day’s needs.
   - Related concept: [[concepts/winter-light-exposure]] (using light to stay alert during this flexible period).

## Supporting Practices

- **Night Preparation**: Lay out clothes, prep breakfast, and organize any items needed in the morning. This reduces decision‑fatigue when it’s cold and dark.
- **Light Exposure**: Turn on a lamp or use a sunrise alarm immediately after waking to boost alertness. See [[concepts/winter-light-exposure]].
- **Gentle Consistency**: Aim for consistency in the two anchors rather than a perfect minute‑by‑minute schedule.

## Why It Works

- **Cue‑Response Loop**: Repeating the same anchors creates a Pavlovian cue that signals the brain to shift into “day mode.”
- **Reduced Decision Load**: By pre‑defining the first 25 minutes, you avoid paralysis caused by the multitude of choices that winter mornings can present.
- **Flexibility**: The open block respects natural energy fluctuations, especially important during darker months.

## Example Timeline
| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| **7:00‑7:10** | First anchor – focus activity (e.g., quick planning or reading) |
| **7:10‑7:25** | Second anchor – stretch, tidy, or small chore |
| **7:25‑9:00** | Flexible zone – deeper work, hobby, reading, etc. |
