# Consistent Sleep Schedule

A **consistent sleep schedule** is the practice of maintaining roughly the same bedtime and wake‑up time each day, allowing a narrow window rather than an exact minute. This regularity reinforces the body’s internal circadian clock, leading to more stable energy, mood, and overall sleep quality.

## Why Consistency Matters
- The body responds to rhythm more than precision; small variations (±15‑30 minutes) are tolerable, but larger shifts—especially on weekends—can create a “jet‑lag” effect on Monday.
- Consistency works hand‑in‑hand with other sleep‑supporting habits such as good [[concepts/wind-down-routine]] and overall [[concepts/sleep-hygiene]].

## Practical Guidance from Session 14
- **Pick a ballpark window** for bedtime (e.g., 10:30‑11:00 pm) and aim to wake at the same time each weekday.
- **Allow limited weekend drift**: keep Saturday/Sunday wake‑up no more than 1‑1.5 hours later than weekdays to avoid severe rhythm disruption.
- **Gradual adjustment**: shift weekend wake‑times by 20‑30 minutes each weekend until the target window (7:30‑8:00 am) is reached.
- **Morning light exposure** within 30 minutes of waking helps lock the schedule.
- Pair the schedule with a calming wind‑down routine and solid sleep hygiene practices.

## Related Concepts

- [[concepts/sleep-hygiene]] – broader habits (caffeine cutoff, nap limits, screen management) that reinforce schedule stability.
