## Sleep Hygiene

**Definition**
Sleep hygiene refers to a set of behavioral and environmental practices that support the ability to fall asleep quickly, stay asleep through the night, and wake up refreshed. Good sleep hygiene helps stabilize the circadian rhythm and improves overall sleep quality.

**Key Elements**
- **Caffeine cutoff** – avoid caffeine after early afternoon (e.g., after 2 pm).
- **Screen curfew** – turn off phones, tablets, and computers at least one hour before bedtime.
- **Nap management** – keep daytime naps short (≤20 min) and finish before mid‑afternoon.
- **Morning light exposure** – spend 10–15 minutes outdoors within an hour of waking to reinforce the body’s internal clock.
- **Wind‑down routine** – adopt a calming pre‑sleep routine such as dimming lights, gentle reading, or light stretching. See [[concepts/wind-down-routine]] for details.
- **Consistent sleep schedule** – aim for a regular bedtime window rather than an exact minute, keeping weekdays and weekends within 1–1.5 hours of each other. Related guidance is found in [[concepts/consistent-sleep-schedule]].

**Relation to Session 14**
The recommendations in the *Sleep Hygiene* checklist were distilled from the advice given in [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-10]], which emphasized:
- limiting caffeine after early afternoon,
- keeping evening screens off an hour before bed,
- using brief, early naps only when needed,
- ensuring exposure to natural light soon after waking, and
- following a simple, repeatable Sunday‑night wind‑down routine.

By applying these practices consistently, the weekend‑drift described in the session can be minimized, leading to smoother Monday mornings and more stable energy and mood throughout the week.
