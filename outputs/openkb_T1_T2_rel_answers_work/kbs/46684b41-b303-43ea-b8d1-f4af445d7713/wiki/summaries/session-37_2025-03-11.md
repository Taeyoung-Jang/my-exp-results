# Summary of session‑37 (2025‑03‑11)

**Main contribution**: A concise, winter‑friendly morning schedule (7 am–9 am) built around two *anchor habits* and a flexible block, plus supporting tips for night preparation and light exposure.

## Key ideas
- **Night preparation** – set out clothes, prep breakfast, and organize any items you usually fumble with the night before. This reduces decision‑fatigue in the cold, dark morning.
- **Light exposure** – use a lamp or a sunrise alarm immediately upon waking to trick the brain into alertness. [[concepts/winter-light-exposure]]
- **Anchor habits** – pick 1‑2 small, repeatable actions that signal the start of the day. They act as *anchors* that make the transition from the dog walk to the rest of the morning automatic. [[concepts/anchor-habits]]
- **Phase‑two template (7 am–9 am)** – a gentle “second phase” after the initial breakfast/dog walk routine. [[concepts/phase-two-morning-template]]
  1. **7:00‑7:10 – Focus activity (first anchor)** – on workdays: open main project, review task list, or write a quick plan; on weekends: read, do a short creative practice, or check personal goals.
  2. **7:10‑7:25 – Physical/environmental reset (second anchor)** – stretch, tidy the space, or do a small chore to create a clean mental slate.
  3. **7:25‑9:00 – Flexible zone** – transition into deeper work, hobby time, reading, or any activity that fits the day’s needs. The block is guided but not rigid, preserving momentum without feeling boxed in.
- **Gentle consistency** – avoid aiming for a perfect schedule; allow flexibility after the two anchors so you can adapt to daily variations while maintaining overall structure.

## Practical template (time blocks)
| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| **7:00‑7:10** | First anchor – focus activity (workday vs weekend options) |
| **7:10‑7:25** | Second anchor – short stretch, tidy‑up, or small chore |
| **7:25‑9:00** | Flexible zone – deeper work, reading, hobby, or additional chores |

## Take‑away
Implementing two consistent anchors each morning, supported by night‑time prep and immediate light exposure, creates a reliable yet adaptable winter routine that sustains momentum from 7 am through the rest of the day.
