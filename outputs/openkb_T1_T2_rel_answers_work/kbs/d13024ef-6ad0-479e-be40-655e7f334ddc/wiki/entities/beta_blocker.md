# Beta Blocker

Beta Blockers are a class of medication prescribed to manage cardiovascular conditions by reducing heart rate and blood pressure. In the context of the **gentle morning routine** discussed in [[summaries/session-32_2024-11-09]], the user’s beta‑blocker regimen shapes the need for a low‑stimulation start to the day.

## Key Facts from the Document
- **Constraint:** The user must avoid any stimulating activity (e.g., caffeine, intense movement, bright light) for the first 30–60 minutes after waking.
- **Routine Adaptation:** The morning plan emphasizes slow awakening, hydration, a very easy walk, soft lighting, and quiet reflection to accommodate the medication’s effects.
- **Alternative Options:** On especially sensitive mornings, the routine can be further softened by extending seated breathing, shortening or skipping the walk, and focusing on gentle hydration and light exposure.
- **Checklist Integration:** The document provides a **no‑stimulation checklist** that aligns with the constraints imposed by beta‑blocker use.
