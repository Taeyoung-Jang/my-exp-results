# Summary of Session 14 (2024‑06‑15)

This conversation provides a practical framework for choosing the right subscription plan for a document‑editing application, especially when multiple tiers and optional storage add‑ons are involved.

## Core Ideas
- **Feature‑first assessment** – Ask what you *actually* use weekly and what you’d miss if it disappeared. This filters out nice‑to‑have features that don’t impact daily work. [[concepts/feature-evaluation-checklist]]
- **Storage habits** – Review current usage before buying extra storage “just in case.” Compare built‑in storage of higher tiers with your add‑on needs. (plain text)
- **Total cost over a year** – Convert monthly prices to yearly totals to see the real financial impact. [[concepts/cost-over-time]]
- **Upgrade path awareness** – Know whether the app allows easy add‑on purchases or tier jumps; this can justify starting with a lower tier. [[concepts/subscription-tier-comparison]]

## Checklist for Staying vs. Upgrading
1. Does the **standard plan** already cover everyday editing and your level of collaboration?
2. Does the **next tier** include enough built‑in storage to eliminate the add‑on?
3. Are higher‑tier features (e.g., advanced collaboration, deeper version history, enhanced security) used **weekly** rather than occasionally?
4. Compare **yearly cost** of *standard + add‑on* vs. *next tier* (with or without add‑on). (plain text)

## Signals That an Upgrade May Be Worth It
- Frequent hitting of limits on collaboration speed, version history depth, or advanced editing tools.
- Storage add‑on still near its limit, while the next tier offers sufficient built‑in storage.
- Regular frustration over locked‑behind‑tier features.
- Small yearly price difference after removing the add‑on.
