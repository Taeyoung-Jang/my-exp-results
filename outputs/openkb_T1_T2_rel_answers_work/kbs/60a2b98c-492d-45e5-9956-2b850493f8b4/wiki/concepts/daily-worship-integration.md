# Daily Worship Integration into Work Schedule

This concept describes how a Sunni Muslim can embed the core acts of worship—salah, dhikr, Qur’an recitation, and adab—into a busy weekday without creating guilt or burnout. The approach is drawn from the practical suggestions in [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-11]].

## Anchor Prayers as Daily Rhythm
- Treat the five daily prayers as fixed **anchors** in the day.  Fajr is performed before leaving for work; Dhuhr and Asr are slotted into lunch or brief afternoon breaks; Maghrib and Isha are done after returning home.
- Use a compact prayer mat kept in a bag or desk drawer to enable discreet worship in conference rooms, quiet corners, or a parked car.

## Tiny Dhikr & Qur’an Habits
- After each prayer, spend 1‑2 minutes on simple dhikr (e.g., *SubhanAllah*, *Al‑Hamd*).  This can be done silently while walking back to the desk.
- Keep a modest Qur’an routine: half a page or a few verses after Fajr and before sleep, or listen to a short audio recitation during the commute.

## Adab as Living Worship
- Practice the Islamic etiquette of honesty, patience, and gentleness in everyday workplace interactions.  This turns ordinary behavior into an act of worship.

## Handling Missed Prayers & Waswasa
- Follow the Sunni *qada* rule: one missed prayer is made up once, without special formulas.  Adopt a “pray and move on” attitude to prevent obsessive checking, aligning with the principles in [[concepts/waswasa-management]].

## Travel Adjustments
- When traveling, the believer may shorten (qasr) and combine (jamʿ) prayers according to the guidelines in [[concepts/madhhab-travel-prayer-rules]].  The core routine remains the same but uses the shortened two‑rak‘ah format.

## Practical Hanafi Office Tips
- For a 9‑5 schedule, the Hanafi approach emphasizes praying each prayer **within its time**, preferably at the end of lunch for Dhuhr and at the close of the afternoon for Asr.  Combining prayers is not permitted for work pressure; instead, use the “edge‑of‑window” strategy.

### Summary of Key Steps
1. **Anchor** each prayer in the day’s natural breaks.
2. Add **micro‑dhikr** after each prayer.
3. Maintain a **small Qur’an habit** tied to commute or bedtime.
4. Embed **adab** in work interactions.
5. When a prayer is missed, **make it up once** and release any guilt.
6. Apply **travel shortening/combining** only when legitimately on a journey, per [[concepts/madhhab-travel-prayer-rules]].

By viewing worship as a series of gentle pauses rather than an added workload, the routine stays sustainable, spiritually nourishing, and free from excessive self‑critique.
