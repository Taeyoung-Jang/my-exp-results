# Parish Health Flags (Green and Red)

## Overview
Parish health flags are observable signs that help adult seekers assess whether a faith‑formation setting—such as [[entities/rcia]] or other adult groups—provides a supportive, spacious environment or imposes pressure, rigidity, or exclusion.  They are grouped into **green flags** (positive indicators) and **red flags** (warning signs).  Understanding these flags assists participants in discerning a healthy community while staying aligned with personal curiosity rather than merely seeking routine.

## Green Flags (Healthy Indicators)
| Flag | What to Look For |
|------|------------------|
| **Welcoming Questions** | Leaders and members invite questions without rushing toward conclusions, reflecting a spirit of genuine inquiry. |
| **Spacious Pace** | The group allows individuals to progress at their own speed; there is no pressure to “catch up” or meet a rigid timeline. |
| **Respect for Boundaries** | Personal life and boundaries are respected; leaders do not pry into matters unrelated to the formation content. |
| **Balanced Structure** | A consistent schedule (e.g., weekly meetings) provides rhythm while still leaving room for personal reflection and community interaction. |
| **Community Breathing Space** | Attendees feel they can “breathe” in the room—able to share, listen, and sit in silence without feeling forced to perform. |
| **Leader Openness** | Facilitators demonstrate curiosity about each participant’s journey rather than trying to shape a predetermined outcome. |

These green flags align with the broader idea of **structure + community** in [[concepts/adult_faith_formation]].

## Red Flags (Unhealthy Indicators)
| Flag | What to Watch For |
|------|-------------------|
| **Question‑Fixing** | Questions are treated as problems to correct rather than invitations to explore. |
| **Doubt Equals Dis‑commitment** | Expressions of doubt or hesitation are labeled as a lack of commitment or faith. |
| **Emotional Pressure** | Participants feel they must match the group’s intensity or enthusiasm to belong. |
| **Intrusive Boundary Crossing** | Leaders or members pry into personal matters, suggesting the community has a claim over private life. |
| **Rigid Agenda** | The program follows a strict curriculum without room for adaptation to individual needs or pacing. |
| **Clingy Atmosphere** | The group appears welcoming but also overly demanding of time, attention, or emotional investment. |

Red flags often reveal a shift from **curiosity** to **routine‑driven conformity**, echoing the tension described in [[concepts/curiosity_vs_routine]].

## How to Respond
1. **Small Concerns** – If a single comment feels off, bring it up privately with a trusted facilitator; many issues are simply missteps.
2. **Patterned Issues** – Repeated red flags merit stepping back, seeking another parish, or disengaging, as continual discomfort undermines authentic exploration.
3. **Continuous Check‑In** – Maintain a personal weekly reflection (see [[summaries/session-37_2024-06-11]]) to monitor how the environment affects your emotional and spiritual state.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- **Adult Faith Formation** – Healthy flags support the dual need for structure and community highlighted in [[concepts/adult_faith_formation]].
- **Curiosity vs. Routine** – Recognizing these flags helps differentiate genuine interest from a desire for predictable routine, a central theme in [[concepts/curiosity_vs_routine]].
