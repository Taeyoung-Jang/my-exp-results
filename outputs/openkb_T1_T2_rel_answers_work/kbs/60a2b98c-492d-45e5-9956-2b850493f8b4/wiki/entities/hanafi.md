# Hanafi School of Thought

The **Hanafi** school is one of the four major Sunni Islamic legal schools (madhhabs). It is known for its methodological rigor, reliance on reasoned analogical deduction (qiyās), and relatively lenient criteria for defining a traveler in the context of shortening (qasr) and combining (jamʿ) prayers.

## Key points from the session‑14_2023‑06‑11 summary
- **Travel distance**: Requires roughly 48 miles (≈77‑80 km) one‑way for qasr to apply.
- **Maximum stay**: A traveler remains a traveler for up to **15 days**; after that the travel concessions end.
- **Combining prayers (jamʿ)**: Generally **not permitted** for ordinary travel, rain, or illness. Allowed only at **‘Arafah** and **Muzdalifah** during Hajj. Instead, Hanafi practice encourages “sorting” prayers—praying Dhuhr at the end of its time and Asr immediately after, and similarly for Maghrib/Isha.
- **Workplace application**: For a typical 9‑5 office job, the Hanafi approach advises praying each of the five daily prayers within its prescribed time, preferably at the edges of breaks (e.g., Dhuhr at the end of lunch, Asr near the end of the afternoon). If a meeting blocks prayer, one should pray **as soon as the obstacle lifts**, still within the time window.
- **Missed prayers**: One missed prayer equals one make‑up prayer (qada); no special formulas are required.
- **Waswāsa management**: Emphasises a “pray and move on” mindset to avoid obsessive checking after missed acts.
