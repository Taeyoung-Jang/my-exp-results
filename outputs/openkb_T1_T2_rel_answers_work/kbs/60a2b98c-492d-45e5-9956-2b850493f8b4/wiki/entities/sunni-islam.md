# Sunni Islam (entity)

**Type:** other

## Overview
Sunni Islam is the predominant branch of Islam worldwide, adhering to the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad as recorded in the Qur’an and the Sunnah. It recognizes the authority of the community (Ummah) in interpreting religious matters and is characterized by a diversity of legal schools (madhhabs).

## Core Beliefs
- Monotheism centered on [[entities/allah]].
- The Qur’an is the final, unaltered revelation.
- The Prophet’s sayings and actions (Hadith) guide daily life.

## Major Madhhabs
Sunni Islam includes four primary legal schools, each with its own nuances on worship, jurisprudence, and travel rulings:
- [[entities/hanafi]]
- [[entities/shafi-i]]
- [[entities/maliki]]
- [[entities/hanbali]]

These madhhabs differ on matters such as the minimum travel distance for [[concepts/madhhab-travel-prayer-rules]] and the permissibility of combining prayers during hardship.

## Worship Practices
The document **[[summaries/session-14_2023-06-11]]** outlines a practical approach to integrating core acts of worship—prayer, dhikr, Qur’an recitation, and adab—into a busy 9‑5 schedule. This reflects the broader Sunni emphasis on **daily worship integration**, linking secular activities with spiritual routines (see [[concepts/daily-worship-integration]]).

## Travel and Prayer Adjustments
During legitimate travel, Sunni jurisprudence permits shortening (qasr) and, in most madhhabs, combining (jamʿ) prayers. The nuances of these rules are captured in [[concepts/madhhab-travel-prayer-rules]], noting that the Hanafi school restricts combining to specific circumstances (e.g., at [[entities/arafah]] and [[entities/muzdalifah]] during Hajj).

## Managing Spiritual Anxiety (Waswasa)
The guidance in the session‑14 document also addresses the psychological aspect of worship, offering strategies to avoid obsessive doubt (waswasa). Sunni teachings encourage a balanced mindset, as summarized in [[concepts/waswasa-management]].
