# Maliki School of Thought

The **Maliki** madhhab is one of the four major schools of jurisprudence in [[entities/sunni-islam]]. It is known for its particular rulings on travel‑related prayer practices, as highlighted in the discussion of [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-11]].

## Key Characteristics
- **Travel distance for qasr**: Generally requires a journey of about 48–50 miles (≈77–80 km) before the shortening of prayers applies.
- **Maximum stay before losing traveler status**: Four days of intended residence will end the permissibility of qasr and jamʿ.
- **Combining prayers (jamʿ)**: Allowed during legitimate travel and, in some opinions, when there is rain or other hardship, making it more flexible than the strict Hanafi position but slightly more conservative than the Shafi‘i and Hanbali schools.
- **Comparison with other madhhabs**:
  - [[entities/hanafi]]: Requires a longer distance, a 15‑day stay limit, and permits jamʿ only in very specific contexts (e.g., Arafah and Muzdalifah during Hajj).
  - [[entities/shafi-i]] and [[entities/hanbali]]: Allow both qasr and jamʿ during travel, rain, or hardship with a shorter stay limit (≈4 days).

## Practical Implications for Daily Worship
For a Muslim following the Maliki school, the guidance in [[concepts/madhhab-travel-prayer-rules]] suggests:
- Shortening the four‑rak‘ah prayers to two rak‘ahs when on a qualifying journey.
- Combining Dhuhr with Asr and Maghrib with Isha when traveling, or when genuine hardship (e.g., rain) makes separate prayers difficult.
- Maintaining the regular prayer schedule during a typical 9‑5 workday, using the “edge‑of‑window” approach rather than formal combining, as the Maliki school does not endorse jamʿ for ordinary work pressure.

These points help integrate core acts of worship—such as the five daily prayers, dhikr, and Qur’an recitation—into a busy routine while avoiding excessive guilt or waswasa, aligning with the broader advice on [[concepts/waswasa-management]] and daily worship integration.
