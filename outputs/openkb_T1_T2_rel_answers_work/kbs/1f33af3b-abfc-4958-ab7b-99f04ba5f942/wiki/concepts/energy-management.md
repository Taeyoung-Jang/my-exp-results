# Energy Management

Energy Management is the practice of aligning tasks and activities with one's fluctuating physical and mental energy levels throughout the day. By recognizing periods of high and low vitality, a person can schedule demanding work, errands, exercise, and relaxation in a way that maximizes productivity while reducing stress.

## Core ideas from [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]]

- **Morning high‑energy window** – Use the first few hours after waking for deep‑focus work or a brief workout when energy is highest.
- **Mid‑day moderate‑energy** – Reserve this time for lighter tasks such as email, small errands, or routine chores.
- **Evening low‑energy** – Focus on wind‑down activities, light chores, or passive recreation to help the body transition to rest.

## Energy‑based schedule variations

| Energy level | Wake‑up | Key activities | Example adjustments |
|--------------|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| Low | Slightly later (e.g., 7:15 am) | Light stretch, gentle start, minimal evening chores | Reduce evening productive blocks, extend downtime |
| High | Earlier (e.g., 6:30 am) | Morning workout or personal project, back‑to‑back errands after work | Add optional hobby time before bed |

## Fallback planning

When an unexpected long errand (90 + minutes) consumes the evening, the fallback plan treats the errand as the sole productive block, simplifies dinner, skips non‑essential chores, and shortens the wind‑down routine. This prevents cascading stress and preserves overall rhythm.

## Relationship to other concepts

Energy Management works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/structured-weekday-routine]] and [[concepts/time-blocking]]:  
- **Structured weekday routine** provides the thematic blocks (morning, midday, evening).  
- **Time‑blocking** assigns fixed intervals to specific activity types, allowing the schedule to be reshaped based on energy signals.

By integrating these practices, individuals can create a flexible yet predictable daily flow that respects their natural energy cycles.
