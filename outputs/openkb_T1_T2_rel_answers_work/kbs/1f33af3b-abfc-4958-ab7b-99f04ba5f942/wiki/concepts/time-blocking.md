# Time Blocking

**Definition**: Time blocking is the practice of dividing the day into discrete, pre‑planned intervals—each dedicated to a particular type of activity (e.g., deep work, errands, exercise, wind‑down). By assigning a concrete start and end time, it reduces decision fatigue, protects focus, and creates a predictable rhythm.

## How it appears in [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]]

The session‑03 document uses time‑blocking to shape a 9 – 5 weekday routine:
- **Morning block (7:00 – 8:00)** – wake‑up, light movement, breakfast, and a quick priority review.
- **Deep‑focus work block (8:00 – 11:00)** – high‑cognitive‑load tasks before distractions accumulate.
- **Midday/light‑task block (11:00 – 12:00, 1:00 – 3:00)** – email, admin, short errands.
- **Afternoon errand/chore block (3:00 – 4:00)** – quick grocery stop or household tasks.
- **Evening block (5:00 – 6:00)** – exercise or finishing chores, followed by dinner, downtime, and a wind‑down routine.

Two energy‑based variations are provided:
- **Low‑energy day** – later wake‑up, minimal morning activity, reduced evening chores.
- **High‑energy day** – earlier start, added workout, back‑to‑back errands, and optional hobby time.

A **fallback plan** (when an errand runs 90+ minutes) treats the errand as the evening’s productive block, skips non‑essential chores, and shortens the wind‑down.

## Benefits
1. **Focus protection** – dedicated deep‑work windows limit interruptions.
2. **Task clarity** – each block has a clear purpose, reducing the need to constantly re‑prioritize.
3. **Energy alignment** – blocks can be shifted to match high‑ or low‑energy periods.
4. **Predictable rhythm** – regular start/end times create habit loops that lower decision fatigue.
5. **Contextual flexibility** – the same structure can incorporate location‑specific exploration and social goals.

## Implementation Tips
- Start with 2‑3 core blocks (morning focus, midday functional, evening unwind) and refine as you learn.
- Add a recurring **exploration window** in the afternoon for city‑specific activities; pre‑choose a few neighborhoods to rotate through.
- Use short micro‑breaks within blocks for tiny chores if working from home.
- Review the schedule weekly to adjust block lengths, neighborhood picks, and social activities based on actual experience and energy trends.
