# Energy Management

**Definition**: Energy Management is the practice of scheduling work and breaks to match one's natural energy cycles, thereby maintaining productivity and preventing fatigue.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-33_2024-04-15]])
- **Identify peak periods** – Recognize when you think most clearly (often mornings) and reserve high‑impact, focus‑intensive tasks for those windows.
- **Match task difficulty to energy level** – Place demanding work (e.g., report drafting, client calls) during peaks and allocate lighter activities (e.g., email triage, routine updates) to lower‑energy times.
- **Transition rituals** – After the workday, use a short walk, podcast, or a few minutes of doing nothing to shift out of “office mode,” helping to preserve evening energy.
- **Batch interruptions** – Limit checking Slack or other notifications to set intervals (e.g., hourly) unless truly urgent, reducing energy drains from constant context switching.

## Practical Tips
1. **Morning audit** – Spend the first 10 minutes of the day noting your current energy level and selecting 1‑3 anchor tasks that align with it.
2. **Energy‑based task buckets** – Create columns such as *High Energy*, *Medium Energy*, and *Low Energy* and place tasks accordingly.
3. **Scheduled breaks** – Insert short breaks (5‑10 minutes) after 60‑90 minutes of deep work to recharge.
4. **Evening wind‑down** – End the day with a non‑screen activity to signal the brain that work is over.

By consciously aligning work with personal energy rhythms, you can sustain productivity, reduce burnout risk, and maintain a healthier work‑life balance.
