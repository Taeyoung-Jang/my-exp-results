# Time Blocking

**Definition**: Time blocking is a planning technique where you divide your day or week into dedicated time slots, each assigned to a particular activity or type of work. This creates clear boundaries, reduces decision fatigue, and protects deep‑focus periods.

## Core Elements
- **Fixed slots**: Named blocks (e.g., "Research", "Class Prep", "TA/Admin") are placed on a calendar and treated as non‑negotiable appointments.
- **Activity alignment**: High‑energy or cognitively demanding tasks (like research or writing) are scheduled during peak focus times, while lower‑energy activities (admin, email) occupy the afternoon or evening slots.
- **Consistency**: Repeating the same type of block each week builds habit and makes planning predictable.

## How It Appears in the Source
The session‑07 discussion for graduate students proposes an **ideal week** using time blocking:
- Three deep‑research blocks (Monday 9‑12, Wednesday 9‑12, Friday 9‑12) protect uninterrupted work.
- Class sessions and prep are slotted around lectures, with prep either before or after the class.
- TA or administrative duties are placed in early‑afternoon windows when focus naturally wanes.
- A weekly reset block on Friday afternoon ensures the system stays tidy.

These blocks illustrate the principle of aligning task type with optimal energy levels and creating repeatable patterns across the week.

## Benefits
- **Focus preservation** – protects deep‑work periods from interruptions, supporting [[concepts/deep_work]].
- **Reduced overload** – visualizing commitments prevents over‑booking.
- **Clear priorities** – each block has a single purpose, making it easier to decide what to work on.
- **Facilitates review** – having consistent slots simplifies the weekly review process ([[concepts/weekly_review]]).

## Practical Steps to Implement
1. **Identify anchor activities** – list the major categories (research, classes, TA, admin, personal).
2. **Map energy cycles** – decide when you are most alert and assign high‑cognition tasks to those windows.
3. **Create calendar blocks** – use a digital calendar to create recurring events for each activity.
4. **Add buffer zones** – include short transition periods (5‑10 min) between blocks.
5. **Review and adjust** – during your weekly review, assess whether blocks are effective and tweak them as needed.
