# Notification Batching

**Definition**: A productivity technique that groups incoming alerts—such as Slack messages, emails, or app notifications—and processes them at predefined times rather than instantly, to minimize context switching and preserve deep work periods.

## Why It Matters
In the "session-33_2024-04-15" conversation, the assistant recommended checking Slack at set intervals (e.g., once an hour) unless a message is clearly urgent. This approach helps maintain momentum on priority tasks, especially when combined with a limited "Today" list of three items.

## Core Components
1. **Scheduled Checks** – Designate specific moments in the day (e.g., start of work, after a meeting, hourly) to review notifications.
2. **Urgency Filter** – Quickly scan for messages flagged as urgent; handle those immediately, otherwise defer.
3. **Canned Responses** – Use short pre‑written replies to acknowledge receipt without committing to immediate action (e.g., "Got this! I’ll circle back soon.").
4. **Batch Processing** – Group similar notifications (e.g., all Slack pings) and address them together to reduce the mental load of constant context switches.

## How to Implement
- **Set a timer** for notification windows (e.g., 10 min every hour).
- **Adjust status** in Slack or other tools to signal "Do not disturb" between batches.
- **Create templates** for quick replies, as suggested in the session summary.
- **Track interruptions** in a simple log to identify patterns and refine batch frequency.

## Benefits
- Preserves focus for deep work periods (see [[concepts/deep_work]]).
- Aligns with energy management by respecting natural concentration peaks (see [[concepts/energy-management]]).
- Supports a clean, limited daily task list (see [[concepts/task-list-structure]]).
