# Deep Work

**Definition**: Deep Work is a state of sustained, undistracted concentration on a cognitively demanding task, allowing for high‑quality output and rapid skill acquisition.

## Why it matters for graduate students
- Enables progress on research projects that require complex thinking.
- Counters the fragmentation caused by frequent class, TA, and admin duties.
- Aligns with the mindset shift of treating coursework and research like long‑distance training: small, consistent effort beats sporadic bursts.

## Practical guidance from [[summaries/session-07_2022-04-15]]
- **Anchor blocks**: Schedule 2–3 dedicated deep‑work sessions each week (e.g., Monday 9‑12, Wednesday 9‑12, Friday 9‑12).
- **Protect the time**: Treat these blocks as non‑negotiable, shielding them from meetings, emails, and low‑energy tasks.
- **Single‑goal focus**: At the start of each block, decide on one concrete research target and define a clear stopping point for the day.
- **Preparation**: Use a brief daily check‑in to identify the most important task to carry into the deep‑work slot.

## Relationship to other concepts
- **[[concepts/time_blocking]]**: Deep Work slots are a form of time‑blocking, deliberately allocating calendar space for high‑impact work.
- **[[concepts/weekly_review]]**: A weekly review helps assess whether deep‑work blocks were effective, adjust their timing, and set the next week’s primary research focus.

## Implementation checklist
1. Identify 2–3 weekly windows when mental energy is highest.
2. Mark them in your digital calendar as *Deep Work*.
3. Before each block, write a single research goal.
4. During the block, eliminate notifications and close unrelated apps.
5. After the block, note progress and any follow‑up actions.
6. Review outcomes during the weekly review to refine future blocks.
