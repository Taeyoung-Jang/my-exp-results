# Seattle

Seattle is the largest city in the state of Washington, situated on a series of bays and inlets of the Puget Sound. Its climate is characterized by frequent rain and overcast skies, making considerations of **walkability**, **transit reliability**, and a **car-light lifestyle** especially important for residents.

## Relevance to a car‑light lifestyle
The city’s extensive public‑transport network—including the [[entities/seattle-light-rail]] system and the [[entities/d-line-bus]]—provides alternatives to driving, which is attractive for people who want to stay mostly car‑light, particularly in wet weather. Neighborhoods that score high on [[concepts/walkability]] and have reliable transit are frequently highlighted in discussions about living in Seattle without a car.

## Neighborhoods mentioned in the session‑14 summary
- **[[entities/ballard]]** – flat core, dense cluster of shops, cafés, and grocery options; decent bus service.
- **[[entities/capitol-hill]]** – strongest transit hub, especially near the light‑rail station; high urban density and many “cozy third places.”
- **[[entities/fremont]]** – quirky vibe with good walkability but hillier terrain; best when staying near Fremont Ave/34th St.
- **[[entities/greenwood]]** and **[[entities/columbia-city]]** – quieter but still lively; need to watch for limited transit frequency.

## Streets & transit corridors
The commercial corridor around **[[entities/44th-street]]** in Ballard is a key area for transit riders, with several D‑Line bus stops that improve connectivity for car‑light residents.
