# Transit Reliability

**Definition**: The ability of public transportation to operate frequently, on‑time, and with adequate shelter or coverage, even under adverse weather conditions such as heavy rain.

## Why it matters for a car‑light lifestyle
In rainy coastal cities, reliance on a vehicle becomes impractical. A dependable transit system lets residents:
- Complete daily errands without long, soggy walks or waiting in the rain.
- Maintain a sense of connection to the city when streets feel empty or dark.
- Reduce exposure to flooding, splash, and wind that accompany car travel.

## Key factors highlighted in [[summaries/session-14_2021-06-15]]
1. **Frequency and punctuality** – Buses or trains that stick to schedules avoid long waits under drizzle.
2. **Rain‑proof infrastructure** – Shelters, covered platforms, and well‑maintained stops lessen the discomfort of waiting.
3. **Network coverage** – Proximity to reliable lines (e.g., [[entities/seattle-light-rail]]) reduces the need for a car, especially for weekend outings.
4. **Integration with walkable neighborhoods** – When transit hubs sit within dense, walkable districts, residents can quickly transition from a train to a coffee shop or grocery store.

## Red flags for transit reliability in rain
- Routes that appear close on a map but run infrequently or are prone to delays during storms.
- Stops lacking proper drainage, leading to standing water or icy conditions.
- Limited night service, leaving neighborhoods feeling isolated after dark.

## Related concepts
- [[concepts/walkability]] – Complements reliable transit; good sidewalks enable the first‑mile/last‑mile connection.
- [[concepts/car-light-lifestyle]] – The broader lifestyle goal that depends on transit reliability.

## Example neighborhoods (Seattle)
- **Capitol Hill** – Strong light‑rail presence ([[entities/seattle-light-rail]]) and frequent bus service make it a top choice for winter reliability.
- **Ballard** – Core areas near the 44th Street corridor benefit from the [[entities/d-line-bus]] but have fewer rail options.
- **Fremont** – Charming but less robust transit; residents must stay close to the central corridor for dependable service.
