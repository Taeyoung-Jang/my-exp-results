# Light Therapy

**Definition**: Light therapy involves exposure to artificial bright light, typically around 10,000 lux, to simulate natural daylight and help regulate the body’s internal clock.

---

## Key Features (as discussed in [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-12]])

- **Intensity**: Aim for ~10,000 lux. Most commercially available lamps, such as a [[entities/ten-thousand-lux-lamp]], meet this level.
- **Distance**: Sit about 1–2 feet from the lamp; check the manufacturer’s specifications for the exact effective range.
- **Color Temperature**: A cooler, “daylight” spectrum (≈ 5,000–6,500 K) feels most natural for morning use.
- **Session Length**: 20–30 minutes per morning, ideally within the first hour or two after waking.
- **Timing Window**: Use early in the day and stop by early afternoon to avoid delaying sleep onset.

---

## How Light Therapy Supports [[concepts/circadian-rhythm-management]]

Exposure to bright light signals the brain that it is morning, helping to advance the circadian phase. This can counteract the delayed sleep‑wake patterns that occur in high‑latitude winter months (e.g., in [[entities/toronto]] where sunrise can be after 7 a.m.).

---

## Integration with Daily Habits

- **[[concepts/routine-anchoring]]**: Pair the lamp session with another morning habit—coffee, checking email, or a brief stretch—to embed it in a stable routine.
- **[[concepts/second-wind-ritual]]**: After the lamp session, consider a short “second‑wind” activity (e.g., a protein snack or a brief walk) to maintain energy through the early afternoon.

---

## Practical Tips for Use

1. **Positioning**: Place the lamp at eye level but off to the side; you don’t need to stare directly at it.
2. **Consistency**: Use the lamp each morning for best results; occasional use yields limited benefit.
3. **Safety**: If you have eye conditions or bipolar disorder, consult a clinician before starting.
4. **Adjusting for Dark Mornings**: When waking before sunrise (common in winter), start the lamp immediately rather than waiting for daylight.
