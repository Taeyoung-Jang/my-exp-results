# Quick Check‑Ins

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Low‑effort, asynchronous messages (e.g., memes, short voice notes, brief text) that act like postcards, allowing friends to stay connected without requiring immediate replies.

## Core Idea
Quick Check‑Ins are designed to maintain a sense of presence and care while minimizing the pressure of real‑time conversation. They are especially useful when participants are in different time zones or have mismatched schedules.

## Key Practices (from [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-15]])
- **Treat messages as postcards:** Send a thought, meme, or voice note when convenient for you; the recipient can view it later.
- **No immediate response required:** Emphasize that the sender does not expect a prompt reply, reducing anxiety.
- **Personal yet lightweight:** Voice notes add a personal touch without the commitment of a full call.
- **Frequency over length:** Short, frequent check‑ins keep the connection warm more effectively than occasional long conversations.

## Benefits
- Reduces coordination overhead.
- Allows flexibility across [[concepts/overlap-windows]] such as JST vs. US Pacific or UK times.
- Supports the broader strategy of [[concepts/rotating-call-schedule]] by keeping the relationship active between scheduled calls.

## Example Templates
- *“Just thought you’d enjoy this meme – no rush to reply!”*
- *“Hey, quick voice note to say I’m thinking of you. Catch up when you can.”*

## When to Use
- When friends live in different zones (e.g., [[entities/japan-standard-time]] vs. [[entities/us-pacific-time]] or [[entities/united-kingdom]]).
- During periods of high workload or when real‑time chat isn’t feasible.
- As a bridge between scheduled calls in a rotating plan.
