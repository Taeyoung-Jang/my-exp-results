## Second‑Wind Ritual

A **second‑wind ritual** is a short, consistent practice performed in the late afternoon to raise alertness and mood when natural daylight fades. It works by providing a sensory cue—often brighter light, a small protein‑rich snack, or a brief physical activity—that signals the body the day is still ongoing, helping to mitigate the typical post‑lunch slump.

### Core Elements (as described in [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-12]])
- **Bright lighting**: Switching on a higher‑intensity lamp (similar to a [[concepts/light-therapy]] device) for a few minutes.
- **Protein snack**: Consuming a small portion of nuts, cheese, or yogurt to stabilize blood‑sugar levels.
- **Micro‑movement**: A brief walk, stretch, or 5‑minute exercise to increase circulation.
- **Consistent timing**: Performing the ritual at the same clock time each day, ideally before early evening darkness sets in.

### Relationship to Other Concepts
- Supports **[[concepts/circadian-rhythm-management]]** by reinforcing a regular daily structure.
- Complements **[[concepts/routine-anchoring]]**, acting as a secondary anchor after the primary morning routine.
- Can be paired with quick check‑ins (**[[concepts/quick-check-ins]]**) to assess energy levels and adjust the ritual as needed.

### Practical Tips
1. Set a reminder for the chosen time slot (e.g., 3:30 pm).
2. Keep a designated snack and lamp within easy reach.
3. Choose an activity you enjoy to make the ritual sustainable.
4. Track how the ritual affects your energy over a week and tweak components if necessary.

Implementing a second‑wind ritual can smooth the transition to darker evenings, especially in high‑latitude cities where daylight dwindles early in the day.
