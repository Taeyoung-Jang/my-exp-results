## Local Election Research

**Local Election Research** is a practical, time‑boxed approach for voters to collect trustworthy, non‑partisan information about down‑ballot contests and ballot measures. It emphasizes using a small set of high‑quality sources, spotting red‑flags, and focusing effort where party cues are weak.

### Core Steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]])
1. **Official Ballot Review (≈15 min)** – Visit the county or city election office website to download the sample ballot. Identify any *non‑partisan* races (e.g., school board, judges, sheriff, DA) and note them for deeper scrutiny. [[concepts/nonpartisan-races]]
2. **Non‑partisan Voter Guides (≈30 min)** – Consult local newspaper guides, public‑radio guides, or groups like the League of Women Voters (if available). Look for mixed‑coalition endorsements, cross‑endorsements, or unusually nuanced write‑ups that may signal a departure from straight‑ticket voting.
3. **Local News Explainers (≈20 min)** – Read short articles or recaps from trusted local outlets covering the flagged races. Prioritize pieces that discuss qualifications, legal constraints, or “unusual alliances” rather than purely ideological framing.
4. **Candidate Forum Clips (≈15 min)** – Watch brief moments (1‑2 min per candidate) from any town‑hall, debate, or interview. Gauge whether candidates focus on policy mechanisms or rely on generic slogans; the former often aligns better with non‑partisan evaluation.
5. **Ballot‑Measure Analysis (≈20 min)** – Read the official measure summary, the fiscal impact statement, and a neutral local‑news explainer. Identify odd coalitions (e.g., unions paired with business groups) that suggest the measure does not split cleanly along party lines. [[concepts/ballot-measure-analysis]]
6. **Final Cross‑Check (remaining 5‑15 min)** – Re‑review any items where information seemed mismatched with your usual partisan expectations. Confirm that no hidden complexities have been missed.

### Red‑Flag Indicators
- Absence of party labels on the ballot.
- Guides or articles using heavily loaded language or presenting unusually complicated dynamics.
- Candidates emphasizing professional qualifications over ideology.
- Measures with support/opposition coalitions that cross typical partisan divides.

### Why It Matters
Applying this workflow helps voters who plan to vote straight‑ticket avoid unexpected outcomes in the few down‑ballot contests where party affiliation provides little guidance. By concentrating effort on the most ambiguous races and measures, voters can maintain overall efficiency while still making informed choices.
