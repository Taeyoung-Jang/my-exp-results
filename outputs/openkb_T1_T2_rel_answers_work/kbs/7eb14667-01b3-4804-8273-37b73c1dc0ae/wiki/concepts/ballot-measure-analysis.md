# Ballot Measure Analysis

**Definition**: A focused, time‑boxed approach for voters to evaluate referendum‑style items on a ballot, identifying fiscal impacts, stakeholder coalitions, and any partisan‑alignment anomalies.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]])
1. **Official Summary & Fiscal Impact** – Start with the election office’s description and the accompanying budget analysis. These provide the neutral baseline of what the measure does and how it costs the government.
2. **Neutral Explainer** – Supplement the official text with a short, impartial article from a trusted local news outlet (e.g., public radio, community newspaper). The goal is to get a plain‑language interpretation without advocacy.
3. **Coalition Scan** – Note who is supporting and opposing the measure. When the backers include atypical pairings (e.g., labor unions *and* business groups, or civil‑rights groups *and* law‑enforcement associations), the measure likely cuts across traditional party lines.
4. **Red‑Flag Checklist**
   - Vague or missing fiscal details.
   - Heavy reliance on emotive language in campaign materials.
   - Absence of party identifiers on the ballot.
   - Conflicting statements between official summary and independent analysis.
5. **Quick Cross‑Check** – Allocate a final 5‑15 minute buffer to revisit any measure where the above steps revealed inconsistencies or unexpected alliances.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader **[[concepts/local-election-research]]** workflow that helps voters navigate down‑ballot information efficiently.
- Overlaps with **[[concepts/nonpartisan-races]]** in that both focus on identifying items where party cues are insufficient for decision‑making.

## Practical Use Cases
- Straight‑ticket voters who need to verify whether a measure aligns with their overall political goals.
- Voters seeking to avoid unintended policy consequences from measures that hide behind partisan framing.
- Community organizers creating concise voter guides that highlight non‑traditional coalitions.
