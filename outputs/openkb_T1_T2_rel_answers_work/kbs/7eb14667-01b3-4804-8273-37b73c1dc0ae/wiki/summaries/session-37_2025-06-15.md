## Summary of session‑37_2025‑06‑15

**Topic:** Practical, time‑boxed checklist for voters who plan to vote straight‑ticket but need to spot down‑ballot exceptions (non‑partisan offices, cross‑endorsements, or measures that don’t align cleanly with party lines).

### Core Recommendations
1. **Start with the official ballot (15 min)** – Pull the sample ballot from the county/city election office website and flag any *non‑partisan* races (school board, judges, sheriff, DA, etc.).
2. **Skim non‑partisan voter guides (30 min)** – Use local newspaper or civic‑group guides (e.g., the League of Women Voters) to identify races with mixed coalitions, cross‑endorsements, or unusually complex write‑ups.
3. **Check local news explainers for flagged races (20 min)** – Look for coverage that emphasizes professional qualifications, legal constraints, or mentions “unusual alliances.”
4. **Watch short candidate‑forum clips for those races (15 min)** – Spot statements that diverge from typical partisan expectations.
5. **Analyze ballot measures (20 min)** – Read the official summary, fiscal impact statement, and a neutral explainer; note any odd coalitions (e.g., unions + business groups) indicating a split‑ticket issue. See [[concepts/ballot-measure-analysis]] for more detail.
6. **Final quick cross‑check (remaining 5‑15 min)** – Review any items that still feel mismatched with your straight‑ticket assumptions.

### Red‑Flag Indicators
- Lack of party labels on the ballot.  
- Guides or articles that use loaded language or present unusually complex dynamics.  
- Candidates focusing on qualifications over ideology.  
- Measures with support/opposition coalitions that cross typical partisan lines.

### Intended Outcome
By following this focused two‑hour routine, voters can maintain their straight‑ticket strategy while avoiding surprise outcomes in the few down‑ballot contests where party cues are insufficient.
