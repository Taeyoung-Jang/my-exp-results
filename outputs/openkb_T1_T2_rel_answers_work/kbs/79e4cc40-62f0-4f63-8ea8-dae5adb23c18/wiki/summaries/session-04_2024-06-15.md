# Summary of session-04_2024-06-15

## Main Themes
- **Social setting preferences** – the vibe of a gathering depends more on people, pace, and comfort than on size.  [[concepts/social_setting_preferences]]
- **Energy & bandwidth management** – check personal stamina; use small‑talk breaks, find a “base” person, and step out for a breather to avoid drain.  [[concepts/energy_management_in_social_events]]
- **Boundary communication** – frame declines as personal preferences, offer alternatives, and stay firm if the invitation is repeatedly pushed.  [[concepts/boundary_communication]]
- **Small‑talk fatigue** – fast‑paced events can exhaust introverted or reflective individuals; strategies include selective engagement and purposeful exits.  

## Key Findings & Recommendations
1. **Assess mood and bandwidth** before accepting an invitation; match the event’s energy to what you’re craving (connection vs. low‑pressure).  
2. **Make big events manageable** by:
   - Finding one or two people to “anchor” you.
   - Taking brief breaks to reset energy.
   - Focusing on purpose (e.g., supporting a friend) rather than obligation.
3. **Communicate preferences politely**:
   - Short template: “Thanks for thinking of me! Big parties aren’t really my scene, but I hope you have a great time.”
   - Warm template: “I really appreciate the invite. I’ve learned I’m much more comfortable in smaller hangouts, so I’ll pass on the party, but I’d love to catch up another day.”
   - Firm template for pushy friends: “I totally get why you want me there, but big events don’t work for me. I’m going to sit this one out, but I’m always up for seeing you in a smaller setting.”
4. **Consistent boundary setting** leads friends to adjust expectations over time, reducing social strain.

## Actionable Steps
- Create a personal checklist: mood, bandwidth, purpose, preferred setting.
- Draft the three message templates above and keep them handy for quick replies.
- When attending a large event, pre‑select one or two familiar people as “safe zones” and schedule short breaks.
