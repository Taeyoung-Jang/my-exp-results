# Widget Utility

**Definition**: Widget Utility evaluates the practical benefit a home‑screen widget provides—typically quick, glance‑able information or actions that reduce the need to open an app.

---

## When a widget is worthwhile
- **Immediate insight**: Shows key data at a glance (e.g., upcoming events, tasks).  
- **Tap reduction**: Removes a step by displaying actionable info directly on the home screen.
- **Frequency of use**: The underlying app is accessed multiple times a day.
- **Low visual noise**: The widget is compact and does not overcrowd the screen.

## Criteria from **[[summaries/session-08_2024-06-15]]**
- The assistant recommends using **one** widget only if it “consistently saves you a tap.”
- A **Google Calendar** widget is highlighted as a prime example because it surfaces the day's schedule without opening the app.
- Widgets that do not provide quick value should be avoided to keep the home screen “calm and intentional.”

---

## Relationship to other concepts
- Part of a broader **[[concepts/app-decluttering]]** strategy: widgets replace some apps, reducing icon count.
- Integral to **[[concepts/home-screen-organization]]**, defining the “planning zone” alongside the calendar and task manager.

---

## Example widget referenced
- **[[entities/google-calendar]]** widget placed at the top of the iPhone home screen, serving as the primary planning tool.

---

## Practical takeaways
1. Identify a single app that you check frequently for time‑sensitive information.
2. Choose a minimal widget that displays that information clearly.
3. Position the widget where it aligns with related icons (e.g., calendar widget above task manager icons).
4. Periodically reassess: if you still open the app directly, the widget may not be adding sufficient utility.
