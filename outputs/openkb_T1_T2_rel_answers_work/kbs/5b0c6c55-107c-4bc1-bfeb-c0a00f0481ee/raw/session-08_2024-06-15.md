**User:** My phone’s starting to feel like a junk drawer of apps, and I’m trying to get more intentional about what I keep. How do you decide which apps are truly worth having on your home screen versus tucked away (or deleted)?

**Assistant:** I usually think about it in terms of what deserves my immediate attention versus what’s fine to access only when needed.

If an app helps you every day, saves you time, or genuinely supports a habit or goal, it earns a spot on the home screen. Things you open constantly without thinking—messages, maps, music, calendar—tend to belong there.

Apps that are useful but not essential can live a swipe or two away. If you only need them occasionally, they don’t need prime real estate.

And anything you haven’t opened in weeks, duplicates something another app already does, or exists purely out of inertia is probably safe to delete. A quick test is to imagine reinstalling it. If that doesn’t feel like a big deal, you likely don’t need it.

Mostly it comes down to whether the app makes your day easier or just adds noise. Your home screen should feel like a little control panel, not a storage closet.

**User:** Could you walk me through a practical way to do this cleanup—like a step-by-step process or checklist? And how many apps do you think is “reasonable” to keep on the home screen if I’m juggling chat, calendar/scheduling, and reading/news apps?

**Assistant:** Here’s a simple way to do the cleanup without overthinking it:

1. Start by moving every app off your home screen into a temporary folder. A blank slate makes it easier to be intentional.  
2. Add back only the apps you used today. If you opened it without thinking, it belongs up front.  
3. Look at the ones you used in the past week and bring back anything that genuinely supports daily routines, like scheduling, messaging, or navigation.  
4. Everything left in the folder gets a quick check: keep it tucked away if it’s occasionally useful, or delete it if you haven’t touched it in a long time.  
5. Revisit the home screen after a few days and adjust based on what you actually reach for.

As for how many to keep up front, most people feel comfortable with somewhere between six and twelve apps. Enough to cover your essentials—chat, calendar, reading, maybe one or two personal anchors—but not so many that you start hunting for icons again.

**Assistant:** I’d think of the home screen as a little “command center.” Start by giving the most time‑sensitive things the easiest reach: your main personal chat app, your work chat app, and your calendar or task manager. Those are the ones you’re likely to check or respond to quickly, so they naturally rise to the top.

From there, add the apps that support your slower, more intentional moments—your reading or news apps. They don’t need top‑left priority, but they deserve to be on the home screen if you use them daily and want to make the habit easier. If that puts you in your 8–12 app range, you’re good.

As for grouping, people often keep fast‑response apps together so their brain knows where to look, then cluster the calmer “consume or plan” apps elsewhere. The main goal is that you can glance at the screen and instantly know where everything lives.

Widgets are great if they genuinely replace the need to open an app. A small calendar or task widget can be worth it because it surfaces what you need without an extra tap. If a widget doesn’t give you quick value at a glance, sticking to icons tends to keep things cleaner and less busy.
