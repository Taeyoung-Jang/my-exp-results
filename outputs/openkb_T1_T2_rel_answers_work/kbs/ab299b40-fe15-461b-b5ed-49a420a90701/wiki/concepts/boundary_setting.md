# Boundary Setting

**Definition**: The practice of establishing and communicating personal limits so that caregiving responsibilities are respected while still maintaining healthy relationships.

## Core Elements
- **Framing limits as flexibility** – Present boundaries as a need for adaptable scheduling rather than an excuse.
- **Offering alternatives** – Suggest shorter hang‑outs, local meet‑ups, or partial participation to show willingness without over‑committing.
- **Using tentative language** – Phrases like “tentative yes” or “I’ll let you know closer to the date” convey openness while preserving space for care duties.
- **Setting clear confirmation deadlines** – Provide a realistic date (e.g., 2‑3 weeks before a weekend trip, 4‑6 weeks for longer trips) so others can plan without indefinite waiting.
- **Managing deposits** – Communicate either a graceful bow‑out before the deposit deadline or a willingness to pay with a disclaimer about possible non‑attendance.
- **Short on‑the‑spot responses** – Simple one‑liners such as “I’m not able to lock anything in yet, but I’ll let you know if it opens up.” help maintain boundaries under pressure.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The approach relies on **[[concepts/commitment_flexibility]]**, allowing the person to stay engaged without rigid promises.
- It is a specific application of **[[concepts/communication_strategies]]**, tailoring messages for close friends, group chats, and family members.
