## Personal Space Zoning

**Definition**: Personal Space Zoning is the practice of dividing a small living or care environment into distinct zones—typically an immediate bedside zone and a functional locker zone—to reduce clutter, create visual consistency, and provide quick, reliable access to essential items.

**Core Elements**
- **Immediate bedside zone**: Holds items needed every few minutes (water, a comfort object, phone, lip balm, meds). Placement is consistent day‑to‑day so the user can locate items by touch.
- **Functional locker zone**: Stores less‑frequent items (clothes, toiletries, chargers) organized by shelf and pocket (top shelf for next‑day clothes, middle area for toiletries, door pocket for grab‑and‑go items).
- **Consistency**: Maintaining the same layout each day reinforces a sense of control and reduces cognitive load.
- **Minimal clutter**: Only the most essential objects remain in the immediate zone, leaving open surface space to keep the area feeling calm.

**Benefits**
- Enhances sense of ownership in highly structured environments.
- Enables rapid retrieval of needed items without disrupting staff routines.
- Supports emotional grounding by keeping comforting objects always visible.
- Reduces visual and tactile overload, which can lower stress.

**Implementation Steps**
1. Identify the items you need constantly vs. occasionally.
2. Assign bedside surface spots for immediate items, keeping the layout identical daily.
3. Organize locker shelves: top‑shelf for next‑day clothes, middle for toiletries, door pocket for grab‑and‑go items.
4. Add a single soothing object (fabric, photo) to the bedside zone.
5. Review weekly: remove anything that has migrated into the immediate zone and re‑establish order.

By following these steps, individuals can create a personal micro‑environment that feels stable and calming, even when the broader schedule is controlled by external staff.
