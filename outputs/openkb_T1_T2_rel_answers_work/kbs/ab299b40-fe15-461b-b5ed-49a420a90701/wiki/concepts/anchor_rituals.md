# Anchor Rituals

**Definition**: Small, repeatable actions or sensory cues that individuals can perform independently of external schedules to anchor their sense of self and time.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-13]])
- **Micro‑breathing** – taking three slow breaths at transition points (e.g., sitting on the bed, closing a locker).
- **Tactile cue** – touching a personal object such as a soft fabric patch or smooth stone before starting any activity.
- **Verbal phrase** – silently or aloud repeating a grounding sentence like “I’m steady.”
- **Scent or light** – using a tiny personal diffuser or a small lamp that can be turned on/off quickly.

These cues share three key properties:
1. **Portability** – they require no equipment beyond what the person already carries.
2. **Briefness** – each ritual can be completed in a few seconds, fitting into gaps in staff‑driven routines.
3. **Independence** – they do not rely on staff timing, making them reliable even in highly structured settings.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Supports [[concepts/personal_space_zoning]] by providing a mental anchor that complements a physically organized environment.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/quick_reset_routine]]; after a reset, an anchor ritual can reinforce the newly‑ordered space.
- Can be viewed as a form of personal [[concepts/boundary_setting]], establishing internal boundaries that protect one’s sense of calm.

## Practical Application
1. **Identify a cue** – choose a breath, touch, phrase, scent, or light that feels soothing.
2. **Pair with a routine gap** – link the cue to a predictable moment (e.g., when you sit down, when you close the locker door).
3. **Practice consistently** – repeat the cue each time the trigger occurs to build an automatic association.
4. **Integrate with space** – place the tactile object or scent source in the bedside zone so it’s always within reach.

By embedding these micro‑rituals into daily life, individuals can create a sense of “home” and personal agency, even when external schedules are out of their control.
