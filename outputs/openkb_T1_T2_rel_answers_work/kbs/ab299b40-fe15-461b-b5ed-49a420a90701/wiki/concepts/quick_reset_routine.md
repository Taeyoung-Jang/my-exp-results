# Quick Reset Routine

**Definition**: A short, repeatable sequence of actions designed to re‑establish a sense of calm, order, and personal control when the surrounding environment feels chaotic or overly structured.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-13]])
1. **One slow breath** – close the eyes, inhale deeply, and exhale slowly to signal the nervous system to relax.
2. **Physical reset** – smooth the blanket or pillow with a gentle hand sweep, creating a visual cue of tidiness.
3. **Item placement** – return each bedside object to its pre‑defined spot (water bottle, comfort object, phone, etc.).
4. **Visual scan** – quickly glance over the surface to confirm the empty space remains, reinforcing the feeling of a cleared area.

The entire routine takes under a minute and can be performed at any moment the room feels overwhelming.

## Benefits
- **Immediate grounding**: The breath and tactile actions anchor attention to the present.
- **Reduced cognitive load**: By restoring a known layout, the brain spends less effort searching for items.
- **Emotional regulation**: The ritual signals safety, lowering stress hormones.
- **Scalable**: Works whether you are seated, lying in bed, or briefly standing.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/anchor_rituals]]: the quick reset is a concrete anchor that can be triggered after a micro‑ritual (e.g., a breathing cue).
- Supports [[concepts/personal_space_zoning]] by reinforcing the consistent zones of immediate vs. secondary items.
- Can be part of broader **boundary setting** strategies, helping you maintain personal control within a highly regulated environment.

## Practical Tips
- **Keep a mental checklist** of the four steps; practice them a few times a day until they become automatic.
- **Use the same hand** to smooth the blanket each time, creating a subtle proprioceptive cue.
- **Pair with a scent or small light** if allowed, adding an extra sensory anchor.
- **Adapt for bed‑bound situations**: perform the breath and item‑placement while lying down, using a bedside table edge for the visual scan.
