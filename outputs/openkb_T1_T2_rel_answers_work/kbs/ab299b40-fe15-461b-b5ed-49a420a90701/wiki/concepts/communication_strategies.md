## Communication Strategies

**Definition**: A set of techniques for crafting clear, audience‑specific messages that convey personal limits without sounding like excuses.

### Core Elements
- **Audience segmentation** – Adjust tone and detail for close friends, group chats, and family members.
- **Boundary framing** – Present limits as a need for flexibility rather than avoidance (see [[concepts/boundary_setting]]).
- **Commitment language** – Use "tentative yes" or "pencil‑in" phrasing to keep options open (see [[concepts/commitment_flexibility]]).
- **Alternative offers** – Suggest smaller hangouts, partial participation, or local meet‑ups as substitutes for full commitments.
- **Confirmation deadlines** – Provide a realistic "confirm by" date to help groups plan without waiting indefinitely.
- **Deposit handling** – Clearly state willingness or inability to meet deposit deadlines, including non‑refundable options.
- **On‑the‑spot one‑liners** – Short responses for pressure moments (e.g., "I’ll let you know if it opens up").

### Application in Session 04 (2023‑06‑15)
The summary of [[summaries/session-04_2023-06-15]] demonstrates these strategies:
1. **Tailored messages** for a close friend, a group chat, and a family member.
2. **Boundary framing** by emphasizing caregiving responsibilities.
3. **Commitment flexibility** through tentative language and explicit deadlines.
4. **Alternative options** offering smaller or partial participation.
5. **Deposit‑deadline texts** handling both graceful bow‑out and non‑refundable deposit scenarios.
6. **Quick one‑liners** for immediate pressure.

### Why It Matters
Effective communication strategies reduce misunderstandings, preserve relationships, and empower individuals with caregiving duties to set realistic expectations without feeling guilty.
