**User:** I’m trying to figure out how to talk to friends and family about why I’m so hesitant to commit to trips or big future plans. The hard part is that I carry the long-term burden of his care, so I don't make plans that assume that responsibility will end. **Assistant:** Here’s a message you could send that’s honest but not heavy:

“I really appreciate being included in plans, and I love having things to look forward to. I just want to be upfront that I have ongoing care responsibilities at home, and because of that I can’t always commit far in advance. It’s not that I don’t want to join in — I just need a little more flexibility than most people. If something changes and I can make it, I absolutely will.”

For setting boundaries without it sounding like an excuse, you can frame it as needing flexibility rather than avoiding commitment. For example, you can say that you’re happy to be a “tentative yes” until you know what your responsibilities will look like closer to the date, or that you’ll join if it’s possible but can’t promise in advance. This keeps the tone warm and honest, and it makes it clear that the limitation is situational, not about them.

**User:** Could you help me choose a good “confirm by” date that feels fair to the group but realistic for me? Like, how far in advance is reasonable for a weekend trip vs. a longer trip, and what should I do if they need deposits before I can know for sure?

**Assistant:** For a weekend trip, a confirmation about two to three weeks out usually feels fair to everyone and still gives you some wiggle room. Most people don’t finalize details for short trips until they’re fairly close, so that timeline won’t feel unusual.

For a longer trip, giving an answer about a month to six weeks in advance is usually enough for the group to handle logistics. Anything much earlier than that can be tough for you and isn’t always necessary for them.

If they need deposits before you can give a firm yes, the clearest approach is to say something like, “I’d love to come, but I can’t commit in time for the deposit deadline. Please plan without me, and if things line up later, I’ll join for part of it.” That way you’re not holding them up, and you’re not taking on financial risk you can’t guarantee. You can also offer to join for a shorter portion of the trip once you know your situation.
