# High‑Yield Savings Account

**Type:** product

## Overview
A High‑Yield Savings Account (HYSA) is an interest‑bearing deposit account that offers higher returns than a traditional checking account while remaining fully liquid. It is commonly used to store funds that are not needed for daily transactions but may be needed on short notice.

## Role in the Simple Budget Framework
In the [[concepts/simple_budget_framework]] discussed in [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-13]], the HYSA serves as the destination for the portion of cash that exceeds the [[concepts/buffer_checking]] amount kept in a [[entities/checking_account]].

## Bucket Structure
The account can be organized into labeled **buckets**—for example, General Savings, Travel, and Larger Purchases—as described by the [[concepts/hysa_buckets]] concept. These internal categories help users allocate money toward specific goals while all funds stay within a single, interest‑earning product.

## Liquidity & Interest
Unlike many investment vehicles, a HYSA remains fully accessible: users can transfer money back to checking instantly when a spontaneous purchase arises, ensuring that the buffer in the checking account is never compromised.

## Interaction with Checking Account
The HYSA works in tandem with the [[entities/checking_account]] buffer. When the checking balance falls below the desired buffer level, a user transfers the needed amount from the HYSA, maintaining the financial cushion while still benefiting from earned interest on the idle funds.

## Practical Guidance from Session 40
- **Initial allocation:** With $15,000 total and a $3,000 high‑month estimate, ~ $7,000 stays in checking while the remaining ~$8,000 moves to the HYSA.
- **Monthly refill:** Users top up the checking buffer once a month from the HYSA, keeping the system low‑maintenance.
- **Bucket labeling:** A single HYSA can host multiple labeled buckets, avoiding the need for separate external accounts.
