# Simple Budget Framework

**Definition:** A lightweight budgeting approach that divides money into three primary buckets—essential monthly expenses, discretionary weekly caps, and a flexible buffer fund. It can be extended with a high‑yield savings account (HYSA) for the buffer, allowing the buffer to earn interest while remaining liquid.

**Origin:** Introduced in [[summaries/session-08_2024-06-15]] as a response to a user seeking a low‑stress budgeting system for $2,000 in liquid funds. The approach was later refined in [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-13]] to incorporate a checking‑buffer model and HYSA bucket structure for larger balances.

**Structure**
1. **Essentials Bucket** – Covers fixed monthly costs such as rent, utilities, phone, transit, and minimum debt payments.
2. **Weekly Caps Bucket** – Two sub‑categories with set weekly limits:
   - Groceries (e.g., $65/week) – see weekly caps.
   - Fun / Miscellaneous (e.g., $20/week) – also part of weekly caps.
3. **Buffer Bucket** – A reserved amount kept in a checking account to cover day‑to‑day flexibility and unexpected expenses. For larger sums, the buffer can be split between a checking_account buffer and a high_yield_savings_account where the bulk sits in labeled HYSA buckets (general savings, travel, big purchases). This allows the buffer to earn interest while staying easily accessible.

**Determining the Buffer**
- Use the highest monthly expense from the past 3‑6 months (or a "high average" after removing outliers) to set the checking buffer. This ensures the buffer can comfortably cover spending spikes.
- A common rule is to keep roughly two months of the highest‑spend month in checking as the buffer, then move the remainder to the HYSA.

**Tracking Method**
- Use a single running note on a phone or a minimal app.
- Record the starting weekly amounts, subtract each purchase, and reset on a chosen day (Monday or payday).
- Once a month (or on payday), perform a "money day" checklist: verify the checking balance meets the buffer level, top up from the HYSA if needed, review HYSA bucket allocations, and briefly glance at the prior month’s spending.

**Handling Calendar Variations**
- For months with five weeks, keep weekly caps unchanged and cover the extra week with any leftover padding.
- If padding is exhausted, apply a simple rule: reduce each weekly cap by a small fixed amount for the next month.

**Benefits**
- Minimal maintenance: only a few numbers to watch each week and a monthly top‑up routine.
- Predictable spending without complex spreadsheets.
- Flexible enough to adjust gradually based on real‑world experience.
- Allows idle cash to earn interest via the HYSA while keeping a ready‑to‑use buffer for spontaneity.
