# Buffer Fund

A **buffer fund** is a modest amount of money set aside specifically to cover unforeseen costs without disrupting a regular budgeting plan. In the context of the *session‑08_2024‑06‑15* conversation, a $300 buffer was recommended as a safety net for the user who has $2,000 of liquid funds.

## Key Characteristics
- **Size**: Often a low‑hundreds figure; in the example, $300.
- **Purpose**: To absorb emergencies or unexpected expenses, preserving the integrity of weekly spending caps.
- **Access Rule**: Should remain untouched unless a genuine, unplanned need arises.
- **Integration**: Any surplus from weekly caps at the end of a period can be moved into the buffer, increasing its cushion over time.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- It fits within a **[[concepts/simple_budget_framework]]**, where the three‑bucket approach includes essentials, discretionary weekly caps, and the buffer fund.
- The buffer complements the **[[concepts/weekly_caps]]** (groceries $65/week, fun/misc $20/week) by providing a fallback when the weekly allocations are insufficient or an extra week occurs.

## Practical Guidance from the Source
1. **Allocate after fixed bills**: Subtract rent, utilities, phone, transit, and debt payments, then assign $300 to the buffer.
2. **Use leftover padding**: In months with extra weeks, any remaining unassigned money (e.g., $280) can temporarily fund the additional week before dipping into the buffer.
3. **Adjust only when necessary**: If the buffer is depleted, reduce weekly caps slightly for the next month rather than constantly recalculating.
