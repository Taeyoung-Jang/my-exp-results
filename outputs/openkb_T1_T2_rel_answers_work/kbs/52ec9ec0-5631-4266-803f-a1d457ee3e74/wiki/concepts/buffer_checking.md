# Buffer Checking Account

**Definition**: A *buffer checking account* is a portion of your liquid cash kept in a checking account specifically to act as a safety net for day‑to‑day expenses and spontaneous purchases. It is sized to cover a set number of months of your highest observed spending, ensuring you never feel cash‑constrained.

---

## Why Use a Buffer?
- Provides immediate access to funds without the friction of transfers.
- Reduces anxiety around variable monthly expenses.
- Keeps the budgeting system simple and low‑maintenance.

## Determining the Buffer Size
1. Review your last 3‑6 months of expenses.
2. Identify the **highest** monthly spend (or a “high average” after removing outliers).
3. Multiply that figure by two (or a factor that feels comfortable) and add a small comfort margin.

> *Example (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-12-13]]):* With a highest month of **$3,000**, a buffer of **$7,000** covers two months plus extra breathing room.

## Implementation Steps
1. **Set the buffer amount** in your checking account (e.g., $7,000).
2. Keep the rest of your cash in a **[[concepts/hysa_buckets]]** strategy, typically in a high‑yield savings account.
3. **Refill cadence** – Re‑top the buffer once a month (e.g., on payday) from the HYSA. Adjust more frequently only if you prefer tighter control.

## Operational Rules
- **Spending rule**: Only spend from checking if the balance stays above the buffer threshold. If a purchase would dip the balance below the buffer, transfer the needed amount from the HYSA first.
- **Monthly “money day**”: Review the buffer level, adjust HYSA bucket allocations, and glance at last month’s spending.
