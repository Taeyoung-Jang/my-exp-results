## Spacing and Negative Space

Spacing and negative space refer to the deliberate gaps left between objects, furnishings, and decorative elements to give a room visual breathing room. In small living areas, consistent spacing prevents clutter and helps each piece feel intentional.

### Key Principles from [[summaries/session-40_2025-06-15]]
- **Uniform Gaps:** Aim for roughly 2 inches of space between picture frames, shelves, and tabletop items. Larger pieces may allow up to 3 inches.
- **Negative Space Around Furniture:** Leave enough room around sofas, chairs, and tables so the eye can rest; this creates a sense of openness.
- **Floor Planning:** Arrange artworks or décor on the floor first, adjust until the layout feels balanced, then transfer to the wall.
- **Proportional Width:** For gallery walls, keep the overall width at about two‑thirds to the full width of the sofa, using the same spacing rules throughout.
