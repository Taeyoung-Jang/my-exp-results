# Decision Fatigue Reduction

**Definition**: Strategies that limit the number of daily decisions required for handling possessions, thereby conserving mental energy.

---

## Why it matters

In the "session-03_2023-06-15" discussion, the assistant highlighted that people who prefer a bare environment benefit most from a system that "reduces the number of decisions you have to make."  When items have an obvious, fixed home, the mental effort to store or retrieve them disappears, preventing the build‑up of decision fatigue and making the space self‑maintaining.

---

## Core principles

1. **One‑Touch Placement** – Keep frequently used objects in open or easily reachable spots so they can be put back without thinking.
2. **Fixed Storage Locations** – Assign a single container or shelf for each category (e.g., a single bin for seldom‑used things) and never deviate.
3. **Category Caps** – Limit the number of items in each core group (clothes, kitchen, decor, papers) so the decision set stays small.
4. **Default Reset** – A brief daily routine that simply returns items to their preset spots, reinforcing the habit.

---

## Practical steps (based on the source)

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Identify the handful of items you use daily (keys, wallet, favorite mug). place them in obvious locations. |
| 2 | Choose one container for "everything else" in each category (e.g., a closed bin for extra kitchen tools). |
| 3 | Set hard limits for each category (e.g., "no more than 10 shirts"). |
| 4 | Perform a 1‑minute "reset" each evening: put everything back to its designated spot. |
| 5 | Conduct a quick weekly scan of closed storage to ensure nothing has slipped out of place. |
