# Gallery Wall Design

**Definition**: A set of visual and spatial rules for arranging multiple pieces of wall art so the grouping reads as a single, intentional unit without creating visual clutter.

## Key Principles (from [[summaries/session-40_2025-06-15]])

1. **Piece Count** – Aim for 5‑9 items in a small living room. The exact number is flexible; cohesion matters more than count.
2. **Overall Shape** – Arrange the frames so the outer contour forms a simple rectangle or a loose oval. This unified outline helps the eye perceive the collection as one object.
3. **Anchor Piece** – Choose the largest or most dominant artwork as the visual anchor. Hang it first, then build the surrounding composition.
4. **Consistent Gaps** – Maintain even spacing between frames, typically **2 inches**; increase to 3 inches for larger pieces. Consistency ties the pieces together. [[concepts/spacing_rules]]
5. **Proportional Width** – The gallery wall should occupy roughly **two‑thirds to the full width of the sofa** it sits above. Staying within this range prevents the arrangement from overwhelming the seating area.
6. **Vertical Placement** – Position the bottom edge **6‑8 inches above the sofa back**, keeping the art visually connected to the furniture.
7. **Centering** – Center the entire grouping on the sofa rather than the wall, especially when the sofa is off‑center due to windows or doors.
8. **Frame Finish** – Use a single neutral finish (black, white, or light natural wood). If mixing finishes, repeat each at least twice to maintain intentionality.

## Texture & Color Considerations

- While the concept focuses on layout, it works best when combined with a **limited color palette** across the room (e.g., two main tones repeated in décor). [[concepts/color_palette]]
- Matching frame colors to the room’s palette reinforces cohesion without demanding exact art color matches.

## Workflow Tips

- **Floor Planning**: Lay all frames on the floor, arrange until the spacing feels even, photograph the layout, then transfer to the wall.
- **Adjustable Gaps**: Use painter’s tape or cardboard templates to visualize spacing before hanging.
