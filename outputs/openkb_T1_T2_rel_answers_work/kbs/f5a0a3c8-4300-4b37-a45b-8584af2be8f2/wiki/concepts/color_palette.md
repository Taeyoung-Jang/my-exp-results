# Limited Color Palette

A **limited color palette** is a design strategy that restricts the visual scheme to two main colors—often a warm tone (e.g., terracotta or caramel) paired with a soft neutral—and then repeats quieter variations of those hues across all elements in the space.  By anchoring décor, textiles, and accessories to this narrow set, the room gains a unified feel without feeling busy.

## Why it matters
- **Reduces clutter perception**: Fewer dominant colors prevent the eye from being overwhelmed, especially in small rooms.
- **Creates visual flow**: Repeating hues across furniture, rugs, throws, and wall art ties disparate items together.
- **Simplifies decisions**: Choosing items becomes a matter of matching the two anchor tones rather than juggling many colors.

## Practical steps (from [[summaries/session-40_2025-06-15]])
1. **Select two anchor colors** – The session suggests a warm tone like terracotta and a neutral such as a soft beige or greige.
2. **Apply the anchors broadly** – Use the warm tone for larger items (e.g., a rug or a bookshelf) and the neutral for walls, frames, or secondary accessories.
3. **Introduce quieter variants** – Lighten or mute the anchors for smaller objects (e.g., throw pillows, decorative bowls) to maintain harmony while adding subtle texture.
4. **Stay consistent** – Keep the palette consistent across all room zones, including the gallery wall, to reinforce cohesion.
