# Home Repair Fund

A **home repair fund** is a separate savings account or financial bucket used to accumulate money specifically for routine‑but‑unpredictable home expenses such as roof repairs, appliance replacements, and seasonal maintenance.  The purpose is to avoid dipping into everyday spending or an emergency fund when a repair is needed, thereby preserving cash flow and reducing financial stress.

## Core Principles
- **Treat irregular expenses as monthly**: Allocate a modest, consistent amount each month to the fund, even though the actual expense may occur only once every few years.
- **Separate from emergency fund**: The emergency fund covers life‑disrupting events (e.g., job loss, medical crises), while the home repair fund handles expected yet infrequent home‑related costs.
- **Flexible bucket structure**: Most people use a single general fund, but some find it motivating to maintain separate buckets for predictable large projects (e.g., roof) and a general “random repairs” bucket.
- **Small safety valve**: While focusing on a large goal (like a roof replacement), contribute a modest amount (e.g., $20‑$30 per month) to the general repair fund to avoid derailing the primary savings target.

## Practical Steps (from [[summaries/session-11_2024-09-15]])
1. **Estimate future costs** – Review past expenses or use a rule of thumb (e.g., 1‑2 % of home value per year) to approximate annual repair needs.
2. **Derive a monthly target** – Divide the estimated annual amount by twelve. Start with a realistic figure and adjust as you learn more about your home’s maintenance patterns.
3. **Choose a storage method** – Keep the fund in a separate savings account or a dedicated sub‑account to prevent accidental spending.
4. **Monitor growth** – Watching the balance increase reinforces the sense of security and reduces the feeling of deprivation.
