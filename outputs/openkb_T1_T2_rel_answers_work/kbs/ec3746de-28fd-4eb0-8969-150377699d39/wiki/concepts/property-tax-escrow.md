# Property Tax Escrow Verification

**Definition**: The process of determining if your property tax obligations are being handled through an escrow account managed by your lender, or if you are responsible for paying them directly to the county.

---

## Why It Matters
- Prevents missed tax payments that can lead to penalties or tax liens.
- Ensures your monthly mortgage payment reflects the correct escrow component.
- Helps you budget accurately during the first weeks of home ownership.

## Key Steps (Based on [[summaries/session-38_2025-05-17]])
1. **Check the Mortgage Servicer Portal**
   - Log into the online portal of your lender. Look for an "Escrow" section under the payment breakdown.
   - If you see line items such as "Property Taxes" or "Taxes/Insurance" listed, they are being escrowed.
   - Absence of an escrow section usually means you will receive a direct tax bill.
   - Relevant entity: [[entities/mortgage-servicer-portal]]

2. **Review the Closing Disclosure**
   - On page 1 under *Projected Payments*, locate "Estimated Taxes, Insurance & Assessments". If it notes *In Escrow*, taxes are escrowed.
   - Page 4 contains an explicit *Escrow Account* section detailing which items (taxes, insurance) will be paid from escrow.
   - If this section is blank or says "No Escrow Account," you are responsible for direct payment.
   - Relevant entity: [[entities/closing-disclosure]]

3. **Confirm via Lender Contact**
   - Call the customer‑service number listed on your closing packet and ask a representative to confirm the escrow status for property taxes.

4. **Cross‑Check with Your Note/Deed of Trust**
   - Look for a clause titled "Escrow Items" or "Funds for Escrow Items" that lists property taxes as an obligation of the lender.

## Timing
- Verify as soon as you gain access to the mortgage portal, typically within a few days of closing.
- Re‑check after the first mortgage statement to ensure the escrow component appears as expected.

## Practical Tips
- **Document the Findings**: Screenshot the escrow section in the portal and keep a copy of the relevant pages of the Closing Disclosure.
- **Set Reminders**: If taxes are not escrowed, add a calendar reminder for the county’s billing cycle (often semi‑annual).
- **Monitor Escrow Balance**: Periodically review your escrow balance to ensure sufficient funds for upcoming tax payments.

By following these steps, new homeowners can confidently confirm their property tax payment method and avoid costly oversights during the critical first weeks of ownership.
