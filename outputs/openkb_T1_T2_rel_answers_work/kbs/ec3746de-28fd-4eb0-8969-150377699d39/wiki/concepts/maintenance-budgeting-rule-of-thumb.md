# Maintenance Budgeting Rule of Thumb

**Definition**: A quick-start guideline for estimating how much to set aside each year for home maintenance, expressed as a percentage of the property's value.

## Core Idea
The rule suggests saving **1 % to 2 % of the home’s current market value per year** for routine and irregular repairs.  Divide that annual figure by 12 to get a manageable monthly contribution.  This provides a baseline amount that can be adjusted over time as homeowners gather real expense data.

## How It Works
1. **Determine Home Value** – Use a recent appraisal, online estimate, or purchase price.
2. **Calculate Annual Target** – Multiply the home value by 0.01 – 0.02.
3. **Derive Monthly Target** – Divide the annual target by 12.  For example, a $300,000 home yields $3,000‑$6,000 per year, or $250‑$500 each month.
4. **Adjust Over Time** – As actual repair costs become known, increase or decrease the percentage to better match reality.

## Practical Application (from [[summaries/session-11_2024-09-15]])
- The rule serves as a **starting point** when homeowners lack historical expense data.
- It is especially useful for budgeting **irregular, high‑cost items** like roof replacements, HVAC systems, or major appliance upgrades.
- Homeowners can begin with a modest amount (e.g., the lower end of the range) if cash flow is tight, then raise it as they become comfortable.

## Benefits
- **Simplicity** – No complex calculations; a single percentage yields a clear savings goal.
- **Scalability** – Works for homes of any value, from modest starter homes to high‑end properties.
- **Flexibility** – Can be combined with other budgeting strategies, such as separate buckets for planned vs. unexpected expenses.
