# HOA Onboarding Process

**What it is** – The set of actions a new homeowner takes to become an official member of their homeowners association (HOA) after closing on a property. It includes updating contact information, receiving payment schedules, gaining access to community resources, and understanding rules and responsibilities.

## Why it matters
- Prevents late fees or penalties for missed HOA dues.
- Gives access to gated entry codes, amenity reservations, and community portals.
- Ensures compliance with association rules, reducing the risk of violations.

## Core Steps (based on [[summaries/session-38_2025-05-17]])
1. **Contact the HOA** – Reach out within the first week after closing to introduce yourself and provide:
   - Legal name and mailing address (as on the recorded deed).
   - Proof of homeowner’s insurance (often required for HOA records).
2. **Obtain payment schedule** – Get the exact due dates, acceptable payment methods, and any initial fees (e.g., transfer fee, reserve fund contribution).
3. **Set up community portal access** – The HOA will typically create an account for you on their online system. This portal provides:
   - Bill statements and payment history.
   - Community announcements and document libraries.
   - Ability to submit maintenance requests.
4. **Collect access credentials** – Gate codes, parking permits, amenity key cards, and any physical entry cards needed for the property’s common areas.
5. **Review governing documents** – Receive the CC&Rs, bylaws, and any recent board minutes. Understanding these helps you avoid accidental violations.
6. **Attend orientation (if offered)** – Some associations hold a brief meeting for new owners to walk through the community’s rules, waste‑recycling schedules, and upcoming events.

## Time Sensitivity
- **First week:** Contact the HOA, provide insurance proof, and obtain portal login. Delaying these steps can result in missed payment notices or lack of access to essential services (e.g., trash pickup schedules managed by the HOA).
- **First month:** Review governing documents and set up automatic payments to avoid late fees.
