# Summary of session‑38 2025‑05‑17

**Main focus:** Practical, time‑sensitive actions for new homeowners during the first week after closing, covering utilities, escrow/property‑tax handling, HOA setup, essential documents, and verifying recorded deed status.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/post-closing-checklist]]** – A 30‑day run‑down of tasks; this summary extracts the most urgent items for the first week.  
- **utility transfer** – Immediate registration of electric, water, gas, trash, and internet in the buyer’s name; service lapses are the most time‑sensitive risk.  
- **[[concepts/property-tax-escrow]]** – Determining whether taxes are paid through escrow or billed directly; verify via mortgage portal, closing disclosure, or lender contact.  
- homeowner’s insurance verification – Confirm policy start date and coverage match the loan requirements.  
- **[[concepts/hoa-onboarding]]** – Updating contact info, obtaining payment schedule, gate codes, and portal access; important but not as urgent as utilities.  
- recorded deed verification – Expected recording timeline (2‑4 weeks) and fastest methods to confirm (county recorder online search or phone inquiry).  
- closing‑disclosure terminology – Look for “Escrow Account,” “Estimated Taxes, Insurance & Assessments,” and “In Escrow” to confirm tax handling.  
- essential post‑closing documents – Copies of closing disclosure, settlement statement, note & deed of trust, homeowner’s insurance declarations, recorded deed, HOA packets, inspection report, and warranty registrations.

## Time‑Sensitive Tasks (First Week)
1. **Utilities** – Call or log into each provider’s portal to confirm account transfer and start date; ensure no back‑dating gaps.  
2. **Escrow/Tax Verification**  
   - Log into the **[[entities/mortgage-servicer-portal]]**; look for an “Escrow” section under the monthly payment breakdown.  
   - Review the **[[entities/closing-disclosure]]** (page 1 “Projected Payments” and page 4 “Escrow Account”) for language like *In Escrow*.  
   - Call the lender if the portal is not yet active.  
3. **Homeowner’s Insurance** – Verify the policy effective date matches the closing date and coverage meets replacement‑cost requirements.  
4. **HOA Setup** – Provide ownership details to the **[[entities/homeowners-association]]**, obtain the payment schedule, access codes, and portal login; schedule any required orientation.  
5. **Address Change / Mail Forwarding** – Set up USPS forwarding within 7 days; update bank, employer, DMV, and credit‑card addresses.  
6. **Document Collection** – Assemble and safely store:  
   - **[[entities/closing-disclosure]]**  
   - Final Settlement Statement (HUD‑1)  
   - Mortgage Note & Deed of Trust  
   - **[[entities/recorded-deed]]** (once received)  
   - Homeowner’s Insurance Declarations Page  
   - Inspection Report & Repair Agreements  
   - HOA governing documents & payment schedule  
   - Warranty registrations  

## How to Verify Escrow vs. Direct Tax Billing
- **Mortgage‑servicer portal** – Presence of an “Escrow” line item for property taxes and insurance.  
- **Closing disclosure** – Look for “Estimated Taxes, Insurance & Assessments” and a dedicated “Escrow Account” section.  
- **Loan documents** – Section titled “Escrow Items” or “Funds for Escrow Items” indicating tax and insurance obligations.  
- **Lender call** – Quick confirmation using the customer‑service number on the closing packet.

## Recorded Deed Timeline & Fastest Check
- Typical recording: **2‑4 weeks** after closing; busy counties may approach 6 weeks.  
- **Fastest verification:**  
  1. Access the **[[entities/county-recorder-office]]** online search portal (search by property address or owner name).  
  2. If no online system, call the recorder’s office and request status while on the phone.  
  3. If still pending after 4 weeks, contact the title or escrow company to confirm submission.

## Takeaway
By prioritizing utility transfers, confirming tax escrow status, securing insurance coverage, and promptly handling HOA onboarding, new homeowners can avoid service interruptions and missed payments. Keeping organized copies of all closing‑related documents and verifying the recorded deed within the first month ensures a smooth transition into ownership.
