## County Recorder Office

The **County Recorder Office** (also known as the county clerk’s office) is a local government organization responsible for recording and maintaining public documents related to real property, such as deeds, mortgages, and liens.  It provides the official, searchable record that proves ownership and encumbrances on a parcel of land.

### Role in the Home‑Buying Process
- After closing, the buyer (or their title/escrow company) submits the deed to the County Recorder Office for filing.
- The office assigns a recording date and returns a **recorded deed** copy, which confirms the transfer of title.
- The office’s online search portal can be used to verify whether a deed has been recorded, a step highlighted in [[summaries/session-38_2025-05-17]].

### Interaction Highlighted in Session 38
In the conversation dated 2025‑05‑17, the new homeowner was advised to:
1. Expect the recorded deed to appear within **2‑4 weeks** after closing.
2. Check the recording status via the **County Recorder Office’s online portal** (search by address or owner name) or by calling the office directly.
3. Follow up with the title or escrow company if the deed is not visible after four weeks.

### Practical Tips
- **Online Search:** Most counties provide a searchable database; have the property address and the buyer’s name ready.
- **Phone Inquiry:** If no online system exists, call the office and provide the closing date and parcel number.
- **Keep Copies:** Retain the recorded deed copy for personal records and future transactions.
