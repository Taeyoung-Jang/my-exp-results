# Homeowners Association (HOA)

A Homeowners Association (HOA) is a **community organization** that establishes and enforces covenants, conditions, and restrictions (CC&Rs) for a residential development, collects dues, and provides shared services such as common‑area maintenance, amenities, and security.

## Key Facts from Session‑38 (2025‑05‑17)
- **HOA onboarding** is part of the first‑week post‑closing tasks. New owners should contact the HOA to update ownership information, obtain the payment schedule, and receive access details like gate codes, parking rules, and portal logins. This step is noted as important but not as time‑critical as utility transfers. [[concepts/hoa-onboarding]]
- The HOA may provide a **community portal** where owners can view dues, submit maintenance requests, and access governing documents.
- While the HOA setup is “not urgent” compared to utilities, completing it early helps avoid late fees and ensures access to community amenities.

## Typical Responsibilities
1. **Collecting regular dues** to fund maintenance of common areas, landscaping, and shared facilities.
2. **Enforcing community rules** (architectural guidelines, pet policies, noise restrictions).
3. **Managing amenities** such as swimming pools, clubhouses, gyms, and security systems.
4. **Maintaining financial records** and providing annual budgets to homeowners.
5. **Coordinating with vendors** for services like trash collection, landscaping, and repairs.

## Practical Tips for New Homeowners
- Request a copy of the **HOA governing documents** (CC&Rs, bylaws, rules & regulations) during onboarding.
- Set up **automatic payments** for HOA dues to avoid missed deadlines.
- Keep a record of **HOA correspondence** and receipts for any special assessments.
- Attend the first HOA meeting or orientation to understand upcoming projects and voting procedures.
