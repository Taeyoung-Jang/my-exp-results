## Color Palette for Cohesive Wardrobes

A well‑chosen color palette ties together the pieces of a **capsule wardrobe**, ensuring that every item can be mixed and matched without looking forced. The goal is to achieve visual harmony while avoiding reliance on monotone black or gray.

### Core Neutral Family
- **Navy** – deep but versatile; pairs with both warm and cool tones.
- **Olive** – earthy, adds subtle depth and works well as a base for layering.
- **Sand / Cream** – light, brightens outfits and provides contrast against darker hues.

These three colors form the backbone of the palette. When combined, any garment in the wardrobe can serve as a top, bottom, or outer layer without clashing.

### Accent Options
- **Muted burgundy or rust** – provides a pop of color while maintaining a muted, sophisticated feel.
- **Soft washed navy** (as a linen shirt) – an extension of the navy base that adds texture.

### Skin‑Tone Guidance
- **Warm undertones**: prioritize olive and sand/cream; they complement the natural warmth of the skin.
- **Cool undertones**: lean toward navy and slate (if used) for a balanced look.

### Integration with Capsule Wardrobe
The palette directly supports the [[concepts/capsule_wardrobe]] approach by limiting the number of distinct colors, which reduces the total number of items needed while maximizing outfit possibilities. For example, a navy linen shirt can be paired with cream chinos, olive trousers, or a burgundy accessory, all without breaking cohesion.

### Seasonal Flexibility
- **Summer**: focus on lighter fabrics (linen, cotton) in the cream and olive shades for maximum breathability.
- **Transitional (spring/fall)**: introduce the navy pieces as the base layer, adding the olive or burgundy as accent underlayers.
- **Winter**: layer navy and olive items under unstructured jackets or light coats, using the cream as a contrasting highlight.
