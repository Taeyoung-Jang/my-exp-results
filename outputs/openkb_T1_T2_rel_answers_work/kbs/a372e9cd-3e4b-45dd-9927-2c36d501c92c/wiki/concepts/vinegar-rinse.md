# Vinegar Rinse for Odor Removal

**Definition**: A post‑wash treatment that adds a small amount of white vinegar to a cold rinse cycle to neutralize lingering industrial detergent scents without leaving a noticeable vinegar smell.

---

## Why It Works
- **Acidic neutralization**: The mild acidity of white vinegar (acetic acid) breaks down fragrance compounds left by strong detergents, rendering them odorless.
- **No residue**: When diluted in a cold rinse, the vinegar evaporates completely during drying, so no vinegar odor remains on the fabric.
- **Fabric safe**: Suitable for most textiles, including cotton, synthetics, and blends; it does not weaken fibers when used in the recommended amounts.

---

## Recommended Usage (from [[summaries/session-39_2025-07-15]])
1. **Amount**: Add **1–2 tablespoons** of white vinegar per load.
2. **Cycle**: Use a **cold rinse** after the primary wash.
3. **Application**: Pour the vinegar into the dispenser for the rinse compartment or directly into the drum before the rinse begins.
4. **Drying**: Air‑dry or use low‑heat drying; the vinegar scent dissipates during drying.

---

## Benefits
- **Odor removal**: Effectively eliminates the lingering scent of industrial detergents used by uniform services.
- **Fabric care**: Keeps fibers fresh without the harshness of additional detergents.
- **Cost‑effective**: White vinegar is inexpensive and readily available.

---

## Practical Tips
- **Do not over‑dilute**: More than 2 Tbsp per load offers no extra benefit and may leave a faint scent.
- **Avoid fabric softeners** in the same rinse; they can counteract the odor‑neutralizing effect.
- **Store vinegar** in a cool, dark place to maintain potency.
