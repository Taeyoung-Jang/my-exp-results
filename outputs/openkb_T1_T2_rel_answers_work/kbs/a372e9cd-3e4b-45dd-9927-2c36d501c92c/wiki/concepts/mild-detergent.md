# Mild Detergent

**Definition**: A mild detergent is a low‑impact cleaning agent formulated with gentle surfactants and minimal fragrances, designed to clean fabrics without degrading fibers or leaving strong residues.

## Why Use Mild Detergent?
- **Preserves Fiber Integrity**: Reduces abrasion and chemical stress on delicate fibers such as wool, cashmere, and synthetic blends.
- **Minimizes Odor Build‑up**: Less fragrance means fewer lingering scents, especially important when dealing with industrial detergent smells from uniform services.
- **Compatible with Cold‑Water Washing**: Works efficiently at lower temperatures, supporting [[concepts/cold-water-washing]] and energy‑saving laundry habits.

## Practical Guidance from Session 39 (2025‑07‑15)
- **Amount**: Use a small quantity (e.g., a teaspoon for a standard load) to avoid excess suds and residue.
- **Application**: Ideal for the "at‑home delicates routine" – combine with cold water, gentle cycles, and inside‑out washing.
- **Post‑Service Care**: After retrieving uniforms washed with industrial detergent, a quick rinse with white vinegar (see [[concepts/vinegar-rinse]]) and mild detergent can neutralize lingering scents.

## Selecting a Mild Detergent
1. **Unscented or “sensitive‑skin” label** – ensures low fragrance content.
2. **Formulated for wool, silk, or delicate fabrics** – often contains enzymes that are gentle on fibers.
3. **Low‑pH formulation** – helps maintain fabric color and prevents fiber weakening.

## Integration with Laundry Workflow
- **Mesh Laundry Bag**: Pair mild detergent with a mesh bag for knits and delicate items to further protect against agitation.
- **Spot‑Cleaning**: For minor stains, dilute a small amount of mild detergent in water and apply locally, reducing the need for full‑load washes.
- **Air‑Drying**: After washing with mild detergent, air‑dry to avoid heat‑related shrinkage.
