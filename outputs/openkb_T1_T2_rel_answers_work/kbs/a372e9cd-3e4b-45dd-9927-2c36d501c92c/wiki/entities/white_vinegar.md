# White Vinegar

White vinegar is a clear, acidic liquid made from fermenting distilled grain alcohol. In the context of **[[summaries/session-39_2025-07-15]]**, it is recommended as a simple, low‑cost solution for removing the lingering industrial detergent scent from work uniforms.

## Key Facts from the Session
- **Purpose:** Acts as an odor‑neutralizer when added to a cold rinse cycle after the service wash.  
- **Amount:** One to two tablespoons per load are sufficient to neutralize fragrance compounds without leaving a vinegar smell.  
- **Application:** Add the measured vinegar to the machine’s rinse compartment during a cold‑water rinse, which aligns with the **[[concepts/cold-water-washing]]** best practice for preserving fabric integrity.  
- **Benefits:**  
  - Breaks down lingering detergent fragrance compounds.  
  - Does not damage fibers of cotton, synthetics, or delicate fabrics when used in the recommended amount.  
  - Complements the use of **[[concepts/mild-detergent]]** for a gentle overall laundry routine.
- **Related Concept:** The practice of using white vinegar falls under the broader **[[concepts/vinegar-rinse]]** technique, a recommended step for refreshing garments after a commercial wash.

## Practical Tips
1. After receiving uniforms from the service, place them in the washing machine.
2. Select a cold‑water rinse cycle (or add a cold rinse to the end of a regular wash).
3. Add **1–2 tbsp of white vinegar** to the rinse compartment.
4. Complete the rinse, then **air‑dry** the garments to finish the odor‑removal process.

White vinegar is a versatile laundry aid that, when used correctly, helps maintain the freshness of work uniforms without compromising fabric quality.
