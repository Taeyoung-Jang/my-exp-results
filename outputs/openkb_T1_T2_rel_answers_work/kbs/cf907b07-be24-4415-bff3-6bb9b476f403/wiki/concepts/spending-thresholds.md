# Spending Thresholds

**Definition**: Spending thresholds are income‑based percentage bands that determine when a purchase should be subject to a waiting period before finalizing the buy.

## Core Idea
The concept proposes three tiers—small, medium, and big—each linked to a specific percentage of a person's monthly take‑home income. When a purchase amount falls into a given tier, a corresponding wait period (none, 48 hours, or one week) is applied. This creates a systematic pause that helps curb impulse buying and aligns spending with budgetary constraints.

## Tier Structure (example: $3,500 monthly take‑home)
| Tier | % of Income | Dollar Range | Wait Period |
|------|-------------|--------------|-------------|
| Small | ~1 % | up to $35 | No wait |
| Medium | ~3 % | up to $105 | 48 hours |
| Big | 7‑10 % | $245‑$350 | 1 week |

The thresholds are flexible; users can adjust the percentages to suit their financial situation or preference for clutter avoidance. The lower the chosen percentages, the more frequently the rule triggers, reinforcing disciplined spending.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of the overall [[concepts/purchase-decision-checklist]] framework, providing the quantitative trigger for when the checklist should be applied with a waiting period.
- Implements the [[concepts/wait-period-rule]] by defining the monetary limits that activate each wait‑time tier.
