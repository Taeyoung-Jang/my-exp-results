# Wait‑Period Rule

**Definition**: A decision‑support rule that requires a pause before completing a purchase, with the length of the pause scaled to the purchase amount as a percentage of one’s monthly take‑home income.

## Core Idea
The rule creates a temporal buffer so that emotional impulses (e.g., stress, boredom, FOMO) have time to subside, allowing the rational assessment from the [[concepts/purchase-decision-checklist]] to dominate the decision.

## Tiered Structure
| Tier | Rough % of Monthly Take‑Home Income | Dollar Range (example $3,500) | Wait Time |
|------|------------------------------------|------------------------------|-----------|
| **Small** | ~1 % | ≤ $35 | No wait (if checklist passes) |
| **Medium** | ~3 % | ≤ $105 | 48 hours |
| **Big** | 7‑10 % | $245‑$350+ | 1 week |

These percentages are a concrete instance of the broader [[concepts/spending-thresholds]] idea: set thresholds that meaningfully affect the monthly budget without causing strain.

## Example Application
For a person with a **monthly take‑home income** of $3,500 (see [[entities/monthly-take-home-income]]), the rule would work as follows:
- A $20 gadget is a *small* purchase → no mandatory wait.
- A $90 online course falls into the *medium* tier → wait 48 hours before buying.
- A $300 ergonomic chair is *big* → wait a full week before committing.

## How It Interacts with the Checklist
When a purchase reaches the *medium* or *big* tier, the buyer should revisit the [[concepts/purchase-decision-checklist]] after the wait period. The pause often clarifies answers to items such as:
- “Would I still want it a week from now?”
- “Does it solve a real problem I have?”
- “Will it add clutter or maintenance to my life?”

## Benefits
- Reduces impulse spending driven by temporary emotions.
- Aligns purchases with long‑term budgeting goals.
- Encourages mindful consumption, supporting a low‑clutter lifestyle.
