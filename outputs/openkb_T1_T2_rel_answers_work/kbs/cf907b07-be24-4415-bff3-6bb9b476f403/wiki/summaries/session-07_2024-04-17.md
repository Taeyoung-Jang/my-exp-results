## Overview
The conversation outlines a practical framework for deciding whether a purchase is worth it, blending a concise checklist with a tiered “wait‑period” rule tied to monthly take‑home income.

## Purchase Decision Checklist
1. Will I realistically use this often?  
2. Would I still want it a week from now?  
3. Am I motivated by stress, boredom, or FOMO?  
4. Does it solve a real problem?  
5. Do I already own something that does the job?  
6. If I had the cash now, would I still trade it for this?  
7. Can I explain the purchase comfortably to a trusted person?  
8. Is “limited‑time” pressure influencing me?  
9. Will it add clutter or maintenance?  
10. Is the cost justified by expected value or joy?  

These items target common impulse buying triggers and align with clutter avoidance goals.

## Wait‑Period Rule
A two‑step rule based on the purchase amount:

- **Small purchases** (≈ 1 % of monthly take‑home) – no wait needed if checklist passes.  
- **Medium purchases** (≈ 3 % of monthly take‑home) – apply a 48‑hour wait.  
- **Big purchases** (≈ 7‑10 % of monthly take‑home) – apply a 1‑week wait.  

The rule creates a pause for the emotional “want” to subside and lets the practical need surface.

## 3‑Tier Threshold Example (Take‑home $3,500/month)
| Tier | Percentage of Income | Dollar Range | Wait Time |
|------|----------------------|--------------|-----------|
| Small | ~1 % | up to **$35** | No wait |
| Medium | ~3 % | up to **$105** | 48 hours |
| Big | 7‑10 % | **$245‑$350** | 1 week |

Choosing the lower end of each range increases the frequency of the wait rule, supporting a minimalist, low‑clutter lifestyle.
