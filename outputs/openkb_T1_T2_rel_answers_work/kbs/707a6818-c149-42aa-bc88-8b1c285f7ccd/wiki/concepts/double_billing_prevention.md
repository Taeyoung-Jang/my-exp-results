## Double‑Billing Prevention

**Definition**: Techniques and best practices that ensure a toll tag is not charged multiple times for the same journey or for vehicles that are only temporarily associated with the account.

### Why Double Billing Happens
- A toll tag linked to more than one vehicle can be read on the road for each vehicle, causing the system to log separate trips.
- Rental or borrowed cars may already have their own toll agreements; adding your tag without proper date control can lead to both the rental company and your personal account charging you.

### Core Practices (from [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]])
1. **Single Primary Vehicle** – Keep the tag primarily attached to one vehicle you drive regularly (e.g., your 2024 Subaru Outback). This limits the number of trips the system attributes to the tag.
2. **Temporary Plate Entries for Rentals**
   - Add the rental’s license‑plate to your toll account **only for the exact rental period**.
   - Remove the plate entry as soon as the rental ends.
   - This avoids overlap with the rental company's own toll tag and prevents duplicate charges.
3. **Avoid Frequent Tag Swaps** – Constantly moving a physical tag between cars increases the risk of mismatched data and billing errors.
4. **Enable Auto‑Reload & Notifications** – While not directly preventing double billing, keeping the account funded and monitoring activity helps you spot unexpected charges quickly.

### Practical Checklist
- [ ] Tag assigned to primary vehicle only.
- [ ] Rental vehicle plate added with start/end dates.
- [ ] Remove temporary plate entry after rental.
- [ ] Review monthly statements for duplicate entries.
- [ ] Use notifications to catch unexpected tolls.

By consistently applying these steps, you can minimize the chance of being double‑billed for tolls across multiple vehicles or rental periods.
