# Summary of session‑09 (2022‑06‑15)

This conversation provides a practical framework for deciding whether to **keep** or **trade** a vehicle, illustrated with a 3‑year analysis for a 2019 Honda CR‑V.

## Core Decision Framework
- Evaluate **maintenance costs** and **reliability trends** as primary financial drivers.
- Factor in **safety/technology upgrades**, **resale value**, and **personal value** considerations.
- Use a **three‑year total cost of ownership (TCO)** view, converting both recurring and one‑time expenses into a single aggregate.

## Checklist / Simple Calculator
1. **Identify line items** for each scenario (keep vs trade).
2. **Quantify** each item over the chosen horizon (e.g., 3 years).
3. **Sum** the totals and compare.

### Typical Line Items
- **Fuel** (annual mileage × fuel price / MPG)
- **Insurance** (monthly premium × 12)
- **Routine maintenance** (oil changes, filters, fluids)
- **Wear items** (tires, brakes)
- **Repair risk allowance** – probability‑weighted estimate for major fixes
- **Depreciation / trade‑in value**
- **Financing costs** (loan payment + interest)
- **Safety/tech benefit value** (subjective dollar value)

## Example Template for a 2019 Honda CR‑V
| Category | Keep (3‑yr total) | Trade (3‑yr total) |
|----------|------------------|--------------------|
| Fuel | `fuel_cost_per_year × 3` | `fuel_cost_new × 3` |
| Insurance | `ins_current × 12 × 3` | `ins_new × 12 × 3` |
| Routine maintenance | `maint_current × 3` | `maint_new × 3` |
| Wear items (tires/brakes) | `wear_current` (one‑time) | `wear_new` (one‑time) |
| Repairs (risk cushion) | `prob_major × estimate` | `0` |
| Depreciation / trade‑in | — | `(price_new – trade_in_value)` |
| Financing | — | `loan_payment × 36 + interest` |
| Avoided repairs | — | `‑ (repairs_keep_estimate)` |

### Key Assumptions for the CR‑V
- **Depreciation:** Expect a further **10–15 %** loss over the next three years; trade‑in value remains relatively strong.
- **Maintenance:** Honda CR‑Vs are inexpensive to maintain; budget a few hundred dollars per year for routine service plus scheduled wear‑item replacements.
- **Repair risk:** Low probability of major failure; allocate a modest allowance (e.g., $300 / yr) unless specific issues are known.
- **Newer model costs:** Lower maintenance and near‑zero repairs in the first three years; fuel economy may be slightly better.

## Decision Guidance
- If `keep_total < trade_total` **and** reliability/safety is acceptable, keep the car.
- If the gap is small or repair risk causes stress, the trade‑off may justify purchasing a newer vehicle.
- Adjust the **subjective safety/tech benefit** value to reflect personal priorities.
