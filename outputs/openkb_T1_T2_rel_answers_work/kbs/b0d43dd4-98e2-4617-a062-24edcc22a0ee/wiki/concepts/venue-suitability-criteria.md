# Venue Suitability Criteria

**Definition**: A set of factors to consider when choosing a public location for a first‑time, low‑pressure meetup.

## Core Criteria (from [[summaries/session-14_2023-05-15]])

- **Noise level** – The space should have enough background chatter to feel public but not so loud that conversation is strained.
- **Seat availability** – Easy to find a seat, preferably with a quieter corner, especially during peak evening hours.
- **Public safety & visibility** – A well‑lit, busy area that offers a sense of security without being intrusive.
- **Accessibility & proximity** – Central location with a short walking distance from common transit routes and nearby backup options.
- **Ambient activity** – An inherent activity (e.g., coffee service, books, library seating) that gives natural conversation starters and reduces pressure.
- **Backup options** – Nearby venues that can serve as fall‑backs if the primary spot is crowded.

## How the criteria were applied in the source document

The assistant evaluated three primary Seattle venues:

- [[entities/elliott-bay-book-company]] – steady flow, quieter corners, and a bookstore setting that supplies ambient activity.
- [[entities/victrola]] – lively but may become buzzy later in the evening.
- [[entities/mr-west]] – comfortable but can fill with laptop‑workers during peak times.

Backup locations mentioned were [[entities/porchlight-coffee]] and [[entities/capitol-hill-library]], both within a short walk and offering a calmer atmosphere.

## Practical checklist

1. Verify the venue’s typical evening noise level.
2. Check seat turnover and likelihood of finding a quiet corner.
3. Confirm the area is well‑lit and has visible foot traffic.
4. Identify at least one nearby backup spot.
5. Align the venue choice with the intended meetup duration (60–90 minutes).
