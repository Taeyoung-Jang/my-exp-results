# Hybrid Work Negotiation

**Definition**: A structured approach to requesting schedule adjustments or additional work‑from‑home (WFH) days by presenting objective data on commute stress, travel time, and productivity.

## Core Elements

1. **Data‑Driven Rationale**
   - Collect quantitative and qualitative metrics during a controlled [[concepts/commute-experiment-design]] (e.g., door‑to‑door minutes, crowding, stress score 1‑5).
   - Use a simple scoring template (see the experiment) to generate a clear picture of which commute patterns minimize stress.

2. **Decision Threshold**
   - Identify a routine that consistently scores **≤3** on the stress scale for at least three of five days in the final test week.
   - If no routine meets this threshold, the data signals a need for a schedule tweak.

3. **Framing the Request**
   - Position the findings as evidence of proactive productivity management rather than a convenience request.
   - Highlight how the proposed change (e.g., a shifted start time or one extra WFH day) directly improves on‑time arrival and focus, supporting team outcomes.

4. **Communication Strategy**
   - Summarize the experiment results in a concise bullet list.
   - Reference specific stress scores and travel‑time improvements.
   - Align the ask with broader team goals (e.g., maintaining consistent output, reducing burnout).

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- The negotiation process relies on the metrics defined in [[concepts/stress-scoring]].
- It is a downstream application of the [[concepts/commute-experiment-design]] framework, turning personal commute data into a business case.

## Example from Source

In [[summaries/session-34_2024-05-15]], the user is advised to:
- Run a 2–3 week commute experiment.
- Track stress and travel time.
- Use the resulting data to pitch a small shift in core hours or an additional WFH day, emphasizing productivity benefits.

## Practical Tips

- Keep the pitch brief: a one‑page summary with key numbers.
- Offer a trial period for the new arrangement, mirroring the experimental mindset.
- Be prepared to discuss alternative routes or timing adjustments as part of the negotiation.
