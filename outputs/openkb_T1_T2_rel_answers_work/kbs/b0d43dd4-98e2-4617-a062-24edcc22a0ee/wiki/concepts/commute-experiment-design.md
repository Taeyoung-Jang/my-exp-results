# Commute Experiment Design

**Definition** – A repeatable, 2–3‑week protocol for measuring how different departure times, routes, and modal swaps affect travel time, crowding, and personal stress, with the goal of informing schedule or remote‑work negotiations.

## Core Structure (derived from [[summaries/session-34_2024-05-15]])

| Week | Focus | Example actions |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Departure‑time windows** – test earlier, normal, later departures. | Monday‑Tuesday 10 min earlier, Wed‑Thu normal, Fri 10‑15 min later. |
| 2 | **Alternative segment** – keep best timing, swap one leg to a different line, bus, or walk to another station (e.g., [[entities/london-overground]] or a nearby stop). | Walk to a secondary station, use a bus, or switch to Overground. |
| 3 | **Combined best‑of‑both** – run the top‑scoring timing + route as a “pseudo‑permanent” commute. | Same timing & route all five weekdays. |

## Variables to Test
- **Departure time** (micro‑windows around peak)
- **Modal choice** (tube, Overground, bus, walking)
- **Station choice** (primary vs nearby alternative)
- **Hybrid schedule** – data can support a shift in core hours or an extra WFH day (see [[concepts/hybrid-work-negotiation]]).

## Tracking Template
Copy the following into a daily note:

```
Time:
Route:
Door‑to‑door minutes:
Crowding (low/med/high):
Stress on arrival (1–5):
Would I repeat this? (yes/no)
```

Stress scores feed into [[concepts/stress-scoring]] analysis.

## Decision Rule
1. Compute average stress and travel time for each variation.  
2. Accept a variation if **stress ≤ 3** and travel time is reasonable on **≥ 3 of 5 days** during Week 3.  
3. If no variation meets the rule, use the collected data to request a modest start‑time shift or an additional work‑from‑home day.

## Handling Irregular Mornings
- Tag each commute as **early‑start** or **normal‑start**.  
- Ensure every variation is tested at least twice per tag.  
- A total of **6–10 scored commutes** provides sufficient evidence.

## Practical Use
The design is low‑cost (no extra spending on housing) and yields quantitative evidence that can be presented to managers when negotiating flexible work arrangements.
