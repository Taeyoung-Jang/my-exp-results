# Setting Expectations via Text

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Techniques for writing short, clear messages that let both parties know the meetup’s duration, tone, and flexibility, reducing pressure and ambiguity.

---

## Why it matters
A well‑crafted pre‑meetup text sets the stage for a comfortable interaction. It signals:
- The **time window** (e.g., 60–90 minutes).
- A **low‑pressure vibe** that keeps the encounter relaxed.
- An **easy out** (“I have something after”) so neither person feels trapped.

## Core elements
1. **Explicit duration** – mention an approximate length rather than an open‑ended invitation.
2. **Casual tone** – use friendly, informal language to avoid sounding like a formal appointment.
3. **Optional exit** – add a brief note about a later commitment to give a natural end point.
4. **Backup hint** – optionally suggest a fallback location in case the first spot is crowded, keeping the plan flexible without over‑planning.

## Examples (from [[summaries/session-14_2023-05-15]])
- **Neutral / Friendly:**
  > "Want to meet at Elliott Bay around 6:30? We can chat for about an hour before I head to something afterward. Totally chill either way."
- **Casual / Flirty:**
  > "How about Elliott Bay at 6:30? We can hang out for a bit and see where the conversation goes. I’ve got something after, but I’d love to steal a little time with you."
- **Including Easy‑out:**
  > "Want to meet at Elliott Bay around 6:30 and hang out for about an hour? I’ve got something after, but it’d be great to catch up a bit and keep it low‑key."
- **Backup plan mention:**
  > "Let’s try Elliott Bay, and if it’s crowded we can just wander to something nearby—Porchlight or even the library works. We’ll play it by ear."
