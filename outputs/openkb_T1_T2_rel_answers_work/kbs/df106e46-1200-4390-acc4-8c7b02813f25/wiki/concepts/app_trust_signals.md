# App Trust Signals

**Definition**: Observable cues that signal an app’s reliability, safety, and legitimacy, allowing users to make rapid risk assessments before downloading.

---

## Core Signals (from [[summaries/session-39_2025-02-15]])

1. **Developer reputation** – Recognizable or reputable organization or developer name. A known developer reduces perceived risk.
2. **Recent update date** – Apps updated within the past few months are more likely to be maintained and free of known bugs.
3. **Authentic reviews** – Human‑written reviews with balanced sentiment, rather than generic or overly‑positive spam.
4. **Realistic screenshots** – Screenshots that accurately reflect the UI and functionality, not generic templates or stock images.
5. **Permission relevance** – Requested permissions that align with the app’s stated purpose (e.g., a maps app asking for location but a notes app requesting location is a red flag).

These signals together form a quick mental _trust checklist_ that can be evaluated in under a minute.

## Practical Usage

When encountering a new app, run through the following short script (derived from the session‑39 conversation):

1. Identify the **developer/organization** (entity).  
2. Check the **last update** timestamp.  
3. Scan a handful of **reviews** for authenticity.  
4. Look at **screenshots** for realistic UI.  
5. Verify that **permissions** requested make sense for the app’s core function.

If any of these signals are absent or questionable, treat the app as high‑risk and consider skipping the install.

---

## Benefits

- **Speed** – Enables rapid, sub‑minute decision making.
- **Privacy protection** – Reduces exposure to unnecessary permissions.
- **Quality control** – Helps avoid low‑quality or malicious apps that could waste storage or degrade device performance.
