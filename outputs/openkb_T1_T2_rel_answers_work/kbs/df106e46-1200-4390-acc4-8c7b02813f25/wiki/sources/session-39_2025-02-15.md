# Conversation session 39

Date: 2025-02-15 14:30

**User:** Have you noticed how easy it is to fall into the “download first, think later” habit with apps these days? I’m curious—how do you decide whether a new app is actually worth trying, and what are your red flags that make you skip it?

**Assistant:** I try to slow the impulse by asking myself a couple of quick, practical questions. If an app clearly solves a real problem I have right now, or offers something I don’t already get from apps I use, it’s usually worth a look. If it feels like pure novelty or I can’t explain why I’d use it a week later, I skip it.

My red flags are pretty simple: apps that demand lots of permissions up front without a clear reason, anything that looks rushed or spammy, or apps that lean heavily on hype but don’t show what they actually do. If I have to work too hard to figure out the value, that’s usually a sign it’s not there.

**User:** When you say “lots of permissions,” which ones are the biggest dealbreakers for you? Like, do you ever allow stuff like contacts/location/photos, or do you avoid those unless it’s absolutely necessary? Also, do you check reviews/screenshots first or just install and see?

**Assistant:** For me it mostly comes down to whether the permission makes sense for what the app is supposed to do. Location, contacts, and photos aren’t automatic dealbreakers, but I only allow them if the app genuinely needs them. A notes app wanting my location is a no. A maps or delivery app asking for it is fine. Same idea with photos or contacts: if it’s clearly tied to a feature I want to use, I’m okay with it; if not, I’m out.

And yes, I usually peek at reviews and screenshots first. They give you a quick feel for whether the app is polished or sketchy, and that’s often enough to decide before installing anything.

**User:** That makes sense. I’ve definitely seen random apps ask for location or contacts and it feels off.

Do you have a quick checklist you run through before installing—like “permissions, last update date, developer name,” etc.? And if you do install, do you tend to grant permissions right away or deny everything first and only turn them on if something breaks?

**Assistant:** I do a quick mental sweep, but it’s more of a vibe check than a formal checklist. I look at who made the app, when it was last updated, whether the reviews sound real, and whether the screenshots look like an actual product rather than a template. If any of that feels off, I usually bail.

When I do install something, I almost never grant permissions up front. I deny everything first and wait to see if the app actually asks for something in context. If it genuinely needs a permission for a feature I’m trying to use, I’ll allow it. If it keeps pushing for access without a clear reason, that’s when I delete it.

**User:** Ever since I upgraded my phone, I’ve been grabbing new apps the moment I hear about them and trying them out the same day. Given that, can you help me turn your “deny everything first” approach into a super quick checklist I can follow in under a minute before I install anything (and another one right after I open it the first time)?

**Assistant:** Sure. Since you want something fast, here’s a version that reads like a quick mental script rather than a formal list.

Before installing, I glance at who made the app, check that it’s been updated recently, look at a couple of reviews to make sure they sound human, and scan the screenshots to see if it looks like a real, working app. If anything feels off, I skip it.

Right after opening it for the first time, I ignore every permission request and see what the app actually tries to do. If it asks for something that clearly matches the feature I’m using, I allow it. If it pushes for access without context, I close it and probably uninstall.

The whole thing takes under a minute once you get used to it.
