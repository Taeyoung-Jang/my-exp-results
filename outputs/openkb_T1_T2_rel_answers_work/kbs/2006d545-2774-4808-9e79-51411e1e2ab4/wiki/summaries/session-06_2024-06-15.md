# Summary: Privacy‑Focused Account Sign‑Ups and SMS 2FA Management

**Key topics**
- [[concepts/online_privacy]] and minimal data disclosure during account creation
- Strategies for separating digital identities using privacy‑focused email providers
- Comparison of 2FA methods: authenticator apps, hardware keys, and SMS
- Trade‑offs between convenience and privacy
- Long‑term management of a dedicated secondary SIM for SMS‑only 2FA
- Risks and mitigations: number recycling, SIM‑swap attacks, carrier policies

## 1. Minimal‑Info Account Sign‑Ups
- Use pseudonymous email addresses not tied to your real name.
- Reserve a small set of “clean” emails for important services.
- Avoid giving a phone number unless absolutely required.

## 2. Preferred 2FA Options
- **Authenticator apps** – no personal data exposed, resistant to common attacks.
- **Hardware security keys** – strongest protection, but less convenient.
- **SMS 2FA** – least private; recommended only when the service refuses other methods.

## 3. Handling SMS‑Only 2FA
- Obtain a **dedicated secondary SIM/line** rather than a VoIP or disposable number. See [[concepts/secondary_sim_management]] for best practices.
- Keep the line on a low‑cost, auto‑renewing plan to prevent inactivity.
- Periodically power the device to confirm the line remains active.

## 4. Mitigating Long‑Term Risks
- **Number recycling**: prevent by maintaining continuous billing and occasional use.
- **SIM‑swap attacks**: secure the carrier account with a strong PIN/password and avoid public exposure of the number.
- Store recovery codes in a password manager; add a secondary [[concepts/two_factor_authentication]] method (app or hardware key) when possible.

## 5. Practical Checklist for a Secure Alternate Number
1. Choose a reputable carrier and a cheap, always‑on plan.
2. Set a strong carrier‑account PIN.
3. Keep the SIM powered on at least once a month.
4. Document which services use the number.
5. Enable additional 2FA methods on those services as a fallback.

**Outcome:** Following these steps lets you maintain privacy‑preserving online identities while still satisfying services that demand SMS verification, with a manageable risk profile.
