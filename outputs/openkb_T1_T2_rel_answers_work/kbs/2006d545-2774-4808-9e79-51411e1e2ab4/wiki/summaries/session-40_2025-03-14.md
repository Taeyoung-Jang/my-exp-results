# Summary of session-40_2025-03-14

**Main contribution:** A concise guide to making natural first impressions at networking events, including ready‑to‑use introduction scripts, graceful exit lines, post‑event follow‑up templates, and advice on handing out business cards without feeling salesy.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/networking_etiquette]]** – Treat networking like a casual social gathering; keep introductions brief and human.
- **Introduction scripts** – Ready‑to‑use lines for three scenarios: approaching a group, one‑on‑one in a line/bar, and answering “What do you do?”.
- **Conversation flow** – Use small, open‑ended questions, react to the other person’s statements, and avoid steering the talk back to your agenda.
- **Exit strategies** – Warm, low‑pressure closing lines that signal you’ll mingle or would like to stay in touch.
- **[[concepts/follow_up_communication]]** – Three email/text templates: (1) enthusiastic follow‑up after a great chat, (2) respectful outreach to senior contacts, (3) gentle reminder if no reply after a week.
- **[[concepts/business_card_etiquette]]** – Offer cards as a convenience at the end of a meaningful conversation or when a concrete next step is mentioned; avoid early or unsolicited hand‑outs.
- **Active listening** – Emphasize reacting to what the other person says to appear engaged and genuine.

## Practical Takeaways
1. **Intro formula:** Name + plain‑English role + a relatable detail (e.g., “I’m Alex, I work in product design—focused on accessibility”).
2. **Scripts:**
   - *Group approach:* “Hey, mind if I join you? I’m Alex.”
   - *One‑on‑one line:* “Lines are good for something, I guess. I’m Alex.”
   - *When asked “What do you do?”:* “I work in product design—mostly on accessibility right now.”
3. **Exit lines:**
   - “I’ve really enjoyed talking—I'm going to wander a bit.”
   - “I don’t want to monopolize you; can I get your info to continue later?”
4. **Follow‑up templates:** Tailored tone for peers, senior figures, and non‑responders, all keeping the message brief and low‑pressure.
5. **Card hand‑out cue:** Offer the card right as the conversation wraps or when a concrete follow‑up is implied (e.g., “Send me that article”).

These guidelines aim to help anyone feel confident and authentic while networking, reducing the urge to “pitch” and fostering genuine connections.
