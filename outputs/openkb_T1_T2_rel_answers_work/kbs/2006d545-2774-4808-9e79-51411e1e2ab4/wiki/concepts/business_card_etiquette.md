# Business Card Etiquette

**Definition:** The practice of presenting your business card in a way that feels like a helpful convenience rather than a sales pitch.

**Context:** Derived from [[summaries/session-40_2025-03-14]], this concept addresses how to hand out cards during networking events.

## Core Principles
- **Timing:** Offer the card at the natural end of a conversation or when a concrete follow‑up is implied (e.g., “Send me that article”). Avoid giving cards too early or to strangers you haven’t engaged with.
- **Tone:** Present the card casually, similar to offering a pen: “I’ll give you my card—it has my direct number.”
- **Relevance:** Use the card as a tool to facilitate a promised next step, not as a generic giveaway.
- **Consistency:** Keep cards up‑to‑date with preferred contact methods (e.g., main phone line) to avoid confusion.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Aligns with [[concepts/networking_etiquette]]: Treat networking as a friendly, low‑pressure interaction.
- Complements [[concepts/follow_up_communication]]: The card serves as a tangible reference for later follow‑up messages.

## Practical Tips
1. **Identify the cue:** When the other person mentions a topic you’ll follow up on, say “Sure, here’s my card; it’ll make it easier.”
2. **Keep it brief:** Hand the card with a short sentence, no additional sales language.
3. **Maintain posture:** Offer the card with a relaxed gesture, maintaining eye contact.
4. **Follow‑up:** Reference the card in your post‑event email (“Thanks for taking my card; as promised …”).

These practices help you hand out business cards naturally, enhancing credibility and connection without feeling salesy.
