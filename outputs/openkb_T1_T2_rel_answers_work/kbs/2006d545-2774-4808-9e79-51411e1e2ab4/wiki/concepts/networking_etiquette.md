# Networking Etiquette

**Definition**: A set of practices that help you make genuine first impressions, keep conversations flowing, and exit gracefully without feeling like you’re delivering a sales pitch.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-40_2025-03-14]])
1. **Treat networking like a casual social gathering** – approach people as you would at a friend’s party, focusing on connection rather than transaction.
2. **Keep introductions concise** – state your name, a plain‑English description of what you do, and a single relatable detail.
3. **Ask open‑ended, low‑stakes questions** – e.g., “What brought you to this event?” or “What are you currently working on?”
4. **Practice active listening** – react to the other person’s statements instead of steering the talk back to your agenda.
5. **Use natural exit lines** – signal you’ll mingle or would like to stay in touch with warm, brief phrases.

## Practical Scripts
- **Approaching a group**: “Hey, mind if I join you? I’m Alex.” Follow with a quick mention of your role if asked.
- **One‑on‑one in a line or bar**: “Lines are good for something, I guess. I’m Alex.” Then ask a light question about their attendance.
- **When asked “So what do you do?”**: “I work in product design—mostly focused on accessibility right now.”
- **Graceful exits**: 
  - “I’ve really enjoyed talking—I’m going to wander a bit.”
  - “I don’t want to monopolize you; can I get your info to continue later?”

## Follow‑Up Communication
Effective post‑event outreach aligns with the etiquette principles: keep messages brief, reference a specific conversation point, and give the recipient an easy out. See [[concepts/follow_up_communication]] for template examples.

## Business Card Hand‑Out
Treat the card as a convenience tool, offering it **at the end of a meaningful exchange** or when a concrete next step is mentioned (e.g., “Send me that article”). Avoid early or unsolicited hand‑outs to prevent a salesy vibe. For detailed guidance, see [[concepts/business_card_etiquette]].
