# Online Privacy

Online privacy refers to the strategies and practices that individuals use to minimize the amount of personal information they disclose while interacting with digital services. It emphasizes **data minimization**, **identity separation**, and **control over personal identifiers**.

## Core Practices
- **Pseudonymous email addresses**: Use email accounts that are not linked to your real name for sign‑ups and communications.
- **Dedicated "clean" identities**: Reserve a small set of email addresses and, when needed, alternative phone numbers solely for important accounts.
- **Minimal data disclosure**: Provide only the required fields during account creation, refusing optional personal details.

## Interaction with Two‑Factor Authentication
Effective online privacy often intersects with [[concepts/two_factor_authentication]]. While strong 2FA improves security, some methods (e.g., SMS) can expose personal phone numbers. Privacy‑focused users therefore:
- Prefer **authenticator apps** or **hardware security keys** that do not reveal phone numbers.
- Resort to SMS only when necessary, using a separate, non‑personal number.

## Role of Secondary SIM Management
When SMS‑only 2FA is unavoidable, the practice ties closely to [[concepts/secondary_sim_management]]. A dedicated secondary SIM provides a carrier‑validated phone number without linking it to the user’s primary identity, balancing accessibility and privacy.

## Benefits
- Reduces tracking and profiling by services.
- Lowers exposure to phishing or SIM‑swap attacks tied to a primary number.
- Enhances control over personal data lifecycle.

## Trade‑offs
- Increased management overhead (multiple emails, SIMs, recovery codes).
- Potential inconvenience when services mandate phone verification.
