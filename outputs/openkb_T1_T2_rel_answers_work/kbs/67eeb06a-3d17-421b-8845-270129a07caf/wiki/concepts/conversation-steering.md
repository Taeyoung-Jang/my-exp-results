# Conversation Steering

**Definition**: Conversation steering refers to intentional tactics used during networking or community events to move dialogue beyond superficial small talk and toward meaningful, purpose‑aligned exchange.

## Core Principles
1. **Set a personal goal** – decide beforehand to meet one interesting person or have a genuine conversation rather than "working the room".
2. **Ask deeper questions** – replace the generic "What do you do?" with prompts such as:
   - "What problem are you currently trying to solve?"
   - "What inspired you to attend this event?"
   - "What outcome are you hoping for from this gathering?"
3. **Listen and reflect** – echo back what the other person shares to show interest and encourage elaboration.
4. **Align with the event’s purpose** – reference any stated agenda or mission to keep the discussion relevant.

## Benefits
- Creates a **more authentic connection** and increases the chance of a lasting professional relationship.
- Helps you **meet your personal goal** quickly, making the event feel productive.
- Reduces awkwardness by giving both parties a clear, substantive focus.

## Practical Tips from [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-12]]
- **Purpose‑driven ask**: "What are you working on now?" or "What care about most in your work?"
- **Avoid agenda‑free chatter**: If the invite lacks a clear purpose, steer the conversation toward any **green flag** topics you can identify.
- **Graceful exit**: After a meaningful exchange, you can say, "I’m going to slip out, but it was really nice talking with you," then follow up with a brief note.
