# Polite Decline

**Definition**: A set of courteous language patterns and strategies for turning down professional or community event invitations while preserving relationships.

## Core Principles
1. **Clarity** – State that you cannot attend without over‑explaining.
2. **Appreciation** – Thank the inviter for thinking of you.
3. **Brevity** – Keep the message short and to the point.
4. **Future Openness** – Optionally invite follow‑up on more aligned opportunities.

## Template Examples (from [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-12]])
| Situation | Template |
|-----------|----------|
| Generic corporate mixer | "Thanks for thinking of me. I’m focusing my time elsewhere right now, so I won’t be able to attend, but I appreciate the invite."
| Vague community event | "I’m trying to be intentional about the events I commit to, and I’m not able to make this one. Please keep me posted on future projects or gatherings with a clearer mission."
| Mission‑driven but misaligned | "I’m glad you reached out. The work you’re doing matters, but I’m concentrating my volunteer time on a specific set of issues right now, so I won’t be able to join. Wishing you a great event."

## When to Use
- When the invitation lacks clear purpose or alignment with your values (see [[concepts/event-screening]] for screening criteria).
- When you need to protect limited time without burning bridges.
- When you want to maintain a positive rapport for future, more relevant collaborations.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/event-screening]]** provides the signals that help decide whether a decline is appropriate.
- **[[concepts/conversation-steering]]** offers techniques for deeper dialogue if you choose to attend instead of decline.

## Practical Tips
- Send the decline promptly after receiving the invite.
- Use a professional tone matching the original invitation.
- If you anticipate future interest, add a line like “I’d love to hear about any upcoming events that align with X.”
