# Physical Cues

**Definition**: Tangible, environmental signals that help the brain distinguish between "work mode" and "life mode," supporting clearer boundaries and smoother transitions.

## Core Idea
Physical cues are concrete, repeatable actions or objects that mark the start or end of a work session. When consistently used, they create a mental association that cues the brain to switch contexts.

## Examples from [[summaries/session-34_2025-06-09]]
- **Designated work spot**: Even in a shared apartment, using a specific desk or nook only for work triggers a work mindset each time you sit there.
- **Locking the office door and turning off lights**: Acts as a clear signal that the workday is over.
- **Closing all browser tabs and storing the laptop**: A visual cue that the workspace is no longer active.
- **Transition habits**: Washing a mug, stretching, or changing clothes immediately after work to reinforce the shift.

## Why It Matters
Physical cues reduce cognitive load by externalizing the decision to start or stop work. They help prevent the "always‑on" feeling that often results from blended home‑office spaces.

## Practical Tips
1. **Choose a unique spot** for work that you avoid during leisure time.
2. **Create a shutdown sequence** that includes at least one physical cue (e.g., lock door, put laptop away).
3. **Make the cue simple** so it can be performed daily without friction.
4. **Pair with a psychological commute** for added reinforcement when the physical distance between work and home is minimal.
