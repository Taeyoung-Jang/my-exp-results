# Travel Option Comparison

**Definition**

Travel Option Comparison is a lightweight framework for evaluating alternative ways to travel (e.g., regional shuttle vs. personal driving) by focusing on a small set of consistent criteria and using a familiar option as a reference point.

**Key Elements**

1. **Core dimensions** – Cost, travel time, reliability, and comfort are the baseline factors.  These are measured qualitatively (e.g., cheaper, about the same, more comfortable) rather than with detailed numeric scores.
2. **Reference option** – Choose the mode you use most often (often the shuttle) and compare all other options to it, labeling each as *better*, *worse*, or *about the same* on each dimension.
3. **Simplicity** – The approach avoids complex scoring tables; instead, it relies on quick mental snapshots after a few trial trips.

**Process**

1. Identify the primary travel need (origin, destination, time of day).
2. Record the perceived cost, duration, reliability, and comfort for the reference mode.
3. For each alternative mode, note whether it is:
   - **Cheaper or more expensive**
   - **Faster or slower**
   - **More reliable or less reliable**
   - **More comfortable or less comfortable**
4. Summarize the comparison in a short phrase (e.g., “cheapest but slow,” “fast and comfy but pricey”).
5. If the baseline dimensions are roughly equal, move to **[[concepts/tie_breaker_factors]]** to decide.
