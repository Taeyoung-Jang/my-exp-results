# Psychological Commute

**Definition**: A brief, intentional micro‑ritual that replicates the feeling of leaving a workplace, creating a mental boundary between work mode and personal mode when the physical separation is minimal.

## Why it matters
When the office is located within the same apartment (e.g., an office directly below a living space), the brain receives few environmental cues that the workday has ended. A *psychological commute* supplies those cues, helping to:
- Reduce the tendency to drift back into work after shutdown.
- Reinforce the end‑of‑day routine and improve work‑life balance.
- Provide a clear, repeatable signal that the day has transitioned.

## Core elements (from [[summaries/session-34_2025-06-09]])
1. **Physical cue** – Lock the office door, turn off the lights, and verbally announce the end of the day. This acts like exiting a building.
2. **Transition habit** – Perform a quick activity that is not work‑related, such as:
   - A short walk around the block (real or imagined).
   - Washing your face or changing into casual clothing.
   - Playing a designated “after‑work” playlist.
3. **Time constraint** – The routine should take less than five minutes so it is easy to adopt consistently.
4. **Exception handling** – If an urgent, time‑sensitive matter arises, treat it as an exception, handle the task, then repeat a condensed version of the psychological commute before resuming personal activities.

## Relationship to other concepts
- It complements [[concepts/workday-rituals]] by providing the **closing** component of a daily ritual set.
- It works alongside [[concepts/physical-cues]] (e.g., dedicated work spot, laptop storage) to reinforce the mental switch.
- It can be incorporated into a broader [[concepts/decision_checklist]] for ending the workday, ensuring that the user evaluates whether an exception truly warrants breaking the rule.

## Practical examples
- **Lock & announce**: Close the office door, turn off the lamp, and say aloud, “Workday over, I’m home now.”
- **Quick walk**: Step outside for a two‑minute stroll around the building, mimicking a commute.
- **Change & cue**: Swap work‑day shoes for slippers and put on a favorite relaxing playlist.

## When to use
Any situation where the workspace is geographically adjacent to the living area—especially apartments with a home office on a lower floor, studio setups, or co‑working spaces within the residence.
