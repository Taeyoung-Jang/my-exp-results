## Summary of Session 5 (2024‑06‑09)

This document offers a lightweight method for comparing travel options—specifically a regional shuttle versus personal driving—and presents a concise decision checklist.

### Core Ideas
- **Travel option comparison** – Focus on a few repeatable dimensions (cost, time, reliability, comfort) and use a single reference option for quick “better / worse / same” judgments. [[concepts/travel_option_comparison]]
- **Tie‑breaker factors** – When cost and time are similar, consider mental effort, schedule flexibility, seat/space availability, and endpoint hassle (parking, drop‑off distance). [[concepts/tie_breaker_factors]]
- **Decision checklist** – Six yes/no questions that can be answered in seconds, with the rule “if ≥ 3 answers are yes, drive.” [[concepts/decision_checklist]]
    1. Am I carrying anything bulky or heavy?  
    2. Am I traveling very early or very late?  
    3. Is the shuttle running infrequently right now?  
    4. Will parking be easy at both ends?  
    5. Is traffic likely to be light?  
    6. Do I need schedule flexibility today?  
    7. Will the shuttle drop‑off point be inconvenient for this trip?
- **Underlying themes** – Reducing mental load, maximizing flexibility, and evaluating endpoint convenience. (mental effort, flexibility, parking convenience)

### Practical Takeaway
Apply the checklist before each trip; a majority of “yes” answers reliably indicate that driving will provide a smoother, more convenient experience despite similar baseline costs and travel times.
