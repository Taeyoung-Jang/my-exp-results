# Summary of session-34_2025-06-09

**Main contribution**: practical strategies to create physical and psychological boundaries between work and personal life when home and office share the same space.

## Key concepts
- **[[concepts/physical-cues]]** – designating a specific spot or using objects (locking door, turning off lights) that signal “work mode” vs “life mode”.
- **Start ritual** – brief opening habits (coffee, laptop at set time, short walk) that cue the brain to begin work.
- **Shutdown ritual** – simple end‑of‑day steps such as closing tabs, noting tomorrow’s top task, storing the laptop, and a transition habit (wash mug, stretch, change clothes).
- Handling urgent tasks – treat interruptions as exceptions, perform a shortened shutdown after the urgent item, and avoid cascading extra work.
- **[[concepts/psychological-commute]]** – micro‑rituals that mimic a real commute (lock door, say out loud, quick walk, change clothes, playlist) to mark the shift from work to home.
- **Boundary rules** – “no‑going‑back‑downstairs” guidelines with clear exceptions (time‑sensitive emergencies, forgotten essential items) and a note‑capture system for non‑urgent thoughts.
- **Quick commute ideas (<5 min)** – short walk around the block, washing face, changing attire, or playing a designated after‑work playlist.

## Practical takeaways
1. **Create a dedicated work spot** even in a shared apartment; consistent use trains the brain.
2. **Adopt a start and shutdown ritual** that are simple enough to perform daily.
3. **Lock the office door and turn off lights** as a physical cue when the day ends.
4. **Define explicit no‑return rules** with specific, limited exceptions.
5. **Use a brief psychological commute** to reinforce the mental boundary.
6. **Document urgent interruptions** and repeat the shutdown routine afterwards.

These strategies help maintain a sustainable work‑life balance when the physical separation between home and office is minimal.
