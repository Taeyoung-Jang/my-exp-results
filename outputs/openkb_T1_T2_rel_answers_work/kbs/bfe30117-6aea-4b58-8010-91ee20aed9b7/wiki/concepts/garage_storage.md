# Garage Storage

Garage storage refers to the set of best‑practice methods for keeping emergency supplies—water, food, lighting, medication, and first‑aid items—organized, protected, and ready for use when a garage is the primary storage location. The concept is especially relevant for low‑stress preparedness plans that rely on a readily accessible space without requiring a dedicated pantry.

## Key Practices
- **Location and Positioning**: Store items off the floor on sturdy shelving or pallets to avoid moisture from the concrete and to keep supplies away from direct sunlight. This helps mitigate temperature spikes that can shorten shelf life.
- **Sealed Containers**: Use airtight plastic bins or metal shelving units to protect food from critters, dust, and humidity. For water, keep bottles upright in sturdy containers and avoid filling them to the brim to allow for expansion in cold weather.
- **Temperature Management**: Prolonged temperatures above the mid‑80s °F (≈30 °C) accelerate flavor loss in water and cause oil‑rich foods to go rancid. While brief spikes are tolerable, aim to store the most temperature‑sensitive items (e.g., nut butters, boxed meals) indoors if the garage regularly exceeds this range.
- **Rotation Routine**: Implement a simple rotation schedule—pull 1‑2 items into regular meals each month and replace them on the next grocery run. Rotate bottled water every 6–12 months and food every 6–12 months to keep supplies fresh.
- **Safety Checks**: Periodically inspect seals on cans, ensure batteries in flashlights are fresh, and verify that power banks remain charged.
