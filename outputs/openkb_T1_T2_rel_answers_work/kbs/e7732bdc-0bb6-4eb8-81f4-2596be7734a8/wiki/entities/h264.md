# H.264 (AVC)

H.264, also known as AVC (Advanced Video Coding), is a video compression **product** that balances high visual quality with relatively modest file sizes. It is the default choice for many online platforms, including [[entities/youtube]], and is supported by a range of encoding tools such as [[entities/adobe-media-encoder]] and [[entities/handbrake]].

## Key Facts from Session 07
- **Profile & Level**: High profile, Level 5.2, suitable for 4K UHD (3840×2160) at 23.976 fps.
- **Bit depth**: 8‑bit (standard for H.264).
- **Chroma Subsampling**: 4:2:0.
- **Bitrate Settings**: Recommended VBR target 50–55 Mbps with a max of 75–90 Mbps for high‑quality YouTube uploads.
- **Encoding Options**: Two‑pass encoding is advised for more predictable bitrate control; CABAC entropy coding should be enabled.
- **Keyframe Interval**: Approximately 2 seconds to aid streaming stability.
- **Audio**: Typically paired with AAC 48 kHz, 320 kbps.

These settings were outlined in the [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-15]] as part of a **two‑step workflow** for exporting 4K content:
1. Export a high‑quality mezzanine master (using [[entities/prores]] or [[entities/dnxhr]]).
2. Encode the final delivery with H.264 (or H.265) according to the parameters above.
