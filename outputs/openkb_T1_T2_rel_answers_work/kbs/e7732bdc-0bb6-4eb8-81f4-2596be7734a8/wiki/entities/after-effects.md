# Adobe After Effects

**Type:** product

Adobe After Effects is a professional motion‑graphics, compositing, and visual‑effects software used widely in video post‑production.

## Role in session‑31_2025‑03‑14
In the discussion, After Effects is mentioned as one of the key creative applications alongside DaVinci Resolve and Blender. The advice focuses on how hardware choices affect its performance:
- **CPU:** After Effects benefits more from higher single‑core clock speeds than from a very high core count, so a fast 8‑12‑core CPU is recommended.
- **RAM:** 64 GB is suggested as a minimum for comfortable 8K workflows, with 128 GB eliminating most paging concerns when using After Effects.
- **GPU & VRAM:** While the GPU is the primary priority for Resolve and Blender, After Effects also gains from a strong GPU, though it leans more on the CPU.
- **Cache & storage:** The workflow advice recommends a dedicated fast NVMe drive for the After Effects disk cache, linking to the broader [[concepts/cache-optimization]] and [[concepts/nvme-storage]] concepts.
- **Workstation extras:** Features such as ECC RAM, Thunderbolt, and 10 GbE networking are discussed as optional for creative setups; these can benefit After Effects users who handle large media assets.
