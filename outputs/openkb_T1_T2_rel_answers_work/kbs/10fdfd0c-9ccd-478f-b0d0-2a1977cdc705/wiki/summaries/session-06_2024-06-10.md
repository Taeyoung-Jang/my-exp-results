## Packing Checklist (Pet‑Specific)

- Regular food & measured portions  
- Water bottles & collapsible bowls  
- Leash/harness + seat‑belt harness or crate  
- Familiar bed or thick foam pad  
- Towels for paws, waste bags  
- Meds, arthritis supplements, calming chews or vest  
- Vet records/vaccination proof  

## Arthritis & Comfort Tips

- Use a thick foam pad or supportive dog bed in the back seat.  
- Short pre‑drive walk to warm joints.  
- Schedule main outings mid‑morning after warming up.  
- Keep the car cool, offer water every hour‑two.  
- Watch for limping, excessive panting, reluctance to weight‑bear – cut the outing.

## Quick Rest‑Stop Vetting (under 1 min)

1. Open satellite view; locate large dark‑green patches adjacent to parking.  
2. Search keywords: “state rest area”, “wayside park”, “roadside park”.  
3. Avoid: “travel plaza”, “truck stop”, barren lots.  
4. Look for trees, picnic shelters, or playgrounds as shade cues.  

## Dog Rules & Back‑up Options

- **[[entities/madison-wisconsin]]** – UW Arboretum & Picnic Point: on‑leash year‑round, no seasonal closures. Backup: Vilas Park loop or residential streets near Lake Wingra.  
- **Baraboo/Dells** – Mirror Lake State Park: on‑leash, dogs prohibited only from main swim beach. Backup: Ochsner Park (Baraboo) or quiet neighborhood strolls.  
- **[[entities/lake-geneva-wisconsin]]** – Big Foot Beach State Park: on‑leash, dogs off the designated swim beach. Backup: Maple Park residential loop or Seminary Park, both with good tree cover and gentle footing.  

## Takeaways

- A loop with 2‑night stays keeps driving time low and offers recovery days for a senior Lab.  
- Prioritizing shaded, green rest areas and flat, water‑adjacent trails reduces joint strain and overheating.  
- Simple visual vetting and keyword searches let you find safe stops on the fly.  
- Leashed, calm, familiar environments—combined with non‑prescription calming aids—provide the most comfortable travel experience for older dogs.
