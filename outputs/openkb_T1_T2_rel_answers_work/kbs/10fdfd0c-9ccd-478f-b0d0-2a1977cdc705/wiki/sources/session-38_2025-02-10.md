**User:** I’m moving into a new apartment soon and I’m trying to think through how to make the transition smooth—what are the most important lease clauses or building policies I should double-check before move-in, and what questions should I ask the landlord so there aren’t any surprises later?

**Assistant:** Before you move in, it helps to go back through the lease with an eye for anything that affects your day‑to‑day living or your wallet. Make sure you understand exactly how rent is calculated, when it’s due, how late fees work, and what utilities you’re responsible for. Pay attention to rules around maintenance and repairs, especially who handles what and how quickly the landlord is expected to respond. Check policies on guests, noise, pets, subletting, parking, and storage, since those often catch people off guard. It’s also worth confirming the move‑in/move‑out condition requirements and how the security deposit is handled.

When you talk to the landlord, ask how to submit maintenance requests, whether there are any planned building repairs or renovations coming up, and what the actual move‑in logistics look like—things like elevator reservations, loading zones, and any fees. It’s also good to clarify how package delivery works, what happens if something breaks outside normal hours, and whether there are any building quirks they recommend you know about before settling in.

Keeping the conversation practical and specific usually prevents surprises later.

**User:** One wrinkle for me is pets: the lease on my new place is strict—before they handed me the keys, I had to sign a notarized addendum agreeing the apartment would remain animal-free the entire term, with surprise inspections if anyone complained. Given that, what should I ask the landlord (or get in writing) about how those inspections work and what counts as a violation, so I don’t accidentally trip the policy?

**Assistant:** It helps to pin down the practical mechanics of the policy so you’re not guessing later. Ask the landlord to explain exactly how an inspection is triggered, how much notice you’ll get, and who will enter your unit. It’s reasonable to get in writing that inspections happen only after proper notice and at reasonable times, even if they’re “surprise” in the sense that you don’t control the date.

You can also ask them to spell out what they consider evidence of a violation. For example, whether things like a visiting friend’s pet, stray pet hair on clothing, or noise from another unit could be mistaken as yours. Clarifying what standard they use before declaring a breach can save headaches.

It’s also fair to confirm how they document an inspection, what happens if there’s a complaint that turns out to be unfounded, and whether you’ll have a chance to respond before any penalties are applied. Getting those pieces in writing gives you a clear, predictable process and reduces the risk of misunderstandings.

**User:** Can you give me a concrete checklist of questions to email the landlord about the inspection process—like notice timing, who can enter, how they document “evidence,” what happens after an unfounded complaint, and whether temporary situations (pet-sitting, a guest bringing an animal inside briefly, service/assistance animals, etc.) are treated differently? Also, is it reasonable to ask for their policy in writing even if the addendum is already signed?

**Assistant:** Here’s a simple checklist you can send, phrased as individual questions rather than anything too formatted:

What is the exact process that triggers an inspection after a complaint, and how much notice will you give me before entering the unit?  
Who is allowed to enter during an inspection, and will I be notified of their names or roles beforehand?  
How do you document an inspection, and what counts as sufficient evidence of a violation?  
How do you handle situations where a complaint turns out to be mistaken or unfounded, and will I have an opportunity to respond before any action is taken?  
How do you treat temporary or incidental situations, such as a guest briefly bringing an animal inside, pet‑sitting off‑site that results in pet items in my home, or noise that could be coming from another unit?  
How are legitimate service or assistance animals handled under your policy, and what documentation would you need if that ever became relevant?

And yes, it’s completely reasonable to ask for the full inspection policy in writing, even if you’ve already signed the addendum. You’re not renegotiating the rule—you’re just asking for clarity on how it’s enforced, which is in everyone’s interest.
