# Inspection Process and Notice Requirements

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A systematic approach landlords use to inspect rental units, including how inspections are triggered, notice periods, who may enter, evidence documentation, and tenant rights, especially under strict no‑pets addendums.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Trigger Mechanisms**
   - Inspections are typically initiated after a formal complaint (e.g., alleged pet violation) or as part of routine enforcement of lease clauses.  The tenant should receive a clear explanation of what constitutes a trigger.

2. **Notice Requirements**
   - Tenants must be given reasonable advance notice before entry.  The exact timing can vary by jurisdiction, but most states require at least **24‑48 hours** notice for non‑emergency inspections.  The notice should specify:
     - Date and approximate time window
     - Reason for entry
     - Names or roles of the individuals who will enter the unit.
   - For "surprise" inspections tied to a no‑pets addendum, the landlord should still provide written notice that respects local legal minimums.

3. **Authorized Entrants**
   - Only individuals directly involved in the inspection (e.g., property manager, maintenance staff, or a third‑party inspector) should be allowed entry.  Tenants can request identification and the purpose of each entrant.

4. **Documentation & Evidence**
   - Landlords should document the inspection with a written report, photos, or video, noting:
     - Observed conditions
     - Specific evidence of a violation (e.g., pet hair, equipment damage)
   - The tenant should receive a copy of this documentation within a reasonable timeframe.

5. **Handling Unfounded Complaints**
   - If an inspection concludes that the complaint was mistaken, the tenant should have the opportunity to respond before any penalty is applied.  The landlord must retain the complaint records and provide a written explanation of the outcome.

6. **Temporary or Incidental Situations**
   - The policy should distinguish between permanent violations and temporary incidents such as:
     - A guest briefly bringing an animal into the unit
     - Pet‑sitting items left on the premises
     - Noise that may actually originate from a neighboring unit.
   - Clear guidelines help avoid penalizing tenants for isolated, non‑intentional events.

7. **Service & Assistance Animals**
   - Legally protected service or assistance animals are typically exempt from no‑pet rules.  The landlord may request documentation verifying the animal’s status, but cannot treat it as a violation.

## Practical Checklist (derived from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-10]])
- Ask how an inspection is triggered and the required notice period.
- Confirm who may enter the unit and how they will be identified.
- Request the format used to document inspections and evidence.
- Clarify the process for responding to unfounded complaints.
- Understand how temporary or incidental animal presence is treated.
- Inquire about the handling of service/assistance animals and any required paperwork.
- Request a written copy of the full inspection policy, even if the addendum is already signed.
