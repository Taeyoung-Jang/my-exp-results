# Kimchi (product)

Kimchi is a traditional Korean **fermented cabbage** dish, seasoned with chili pepper, garlic, ginger, and often fish sauce. It is celebrated for its **tangy, spicy, and slightly sour** profile, making it a staple in Korean cuisine and a popular entry point to fermented foods.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-39_2025-11-14]]
- Mentioned as a **beginner‑friendly fermented flavor** alongside miso and mild kimchi varieties.  
- Recommended in the **[[concepts/cuisine-exploration-strategy]]** as a safe way to experience funk without overwhelming intensity.  
- Suggested as a **gentler cousin** alternative when a menu item features stronger fermented ingredients like [[entities/stinky-tofu]] or [[entities/shrimp-paste]].
- Included in the **[[concepts/questioning-script-restaurant]]** advice: ask servers if a dish is “lightly fermented” or “mildly tangy” to gauge if it aligns with the desired level of funk.

## Practical tips
1. **Ask the server**: "Is this kimchi mild or bold?" to determine if it fits a beginner’s palate.
2. **Look for menu cues**: items labeled as “house kimchi” or “mild kimchi” often indicate a less intense version.
3. **Pairing**: combine kimchi with familiar dishes (e.g., grilled salmon) to balance the flavor while still exploring fermentation.
