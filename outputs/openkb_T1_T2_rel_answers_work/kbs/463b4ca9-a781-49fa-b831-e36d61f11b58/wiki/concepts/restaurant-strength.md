# Restaurant Strength

**Definition**: Restaurant Strength refers to the distinctive culinary focus or signature dishes that a restaurant consistently excels at, serving as a reliable indicator of what to order for a satisfying experience.

## Insights from [[summaries/session-11_2025-08-14]]
- The assistant advises looking for dishes that the kitchen prepares frequently or that are highlighted as the restaurant’s specialty. These are considered the safest bets because they showcase the establishment’s core competency.
- Observing a busy, confident kitchen often signals that the restaurant is operating at its peak, reinforcing the reliability of its signature offerings.
- When a special aligns with the restaurant’s known strengths—such as using seasonal ingredients in a familiar style—it becomes a lower‑risk choice.

## New insights from [[summaries/session-39_2025-11-14]]
- **Questioning script**: Using the concise script from [[concepts/questioning-script-restaurant]] (e.g., “Is the flavor mild or bold?”, “How spicy is it?”, “What’s the texture?”, “Anything that surprises first‑timers?”) helps quickly gauge whether a dish aligns with the restaurant’s strength or pushes beyond it.
- **Menu cues**: Look for house specials, signature items, or dishes that consistently turn over. When translations are missing, staff reactions to pointed menu items and visual cues (photos, icons) can indicate whether a dish fits the restaurant’s core strengths.
- **Portion strategy**: Small‑plate venues, tasting menus, and lunch specials let you test a restaurant’s strength without committing to a full, potentially risky portion.
- **Flavor framing**: Understanding descriptors like “bold” (strong but approachable) versus “funky” (fermented, earthy) lets you select items that stay within the restaurant’s strength while still offering a touch of adventure. This ties into [[concepts/beginner-friendly-fermented-flavors]] for low‑intensity fermented options.

## Connection to other concepts
- Relates to [[concepts/default-dish]]: Your default order should often be a dish that reflects the restaurant’s strength.
- Supports [[concepts/cuisine-exploration-strategy]]: Leveraging restaurant strength is a core tactic for safely expanding culinary horizons.

## Practical application
1. **Identify the core**: Spot dishes the kitchen prepares most often, those highlighted as specials, or items with clear visual cues on the menu.
2. **Use the script**: Ask quick, friendly questions (from [[concepts/questioning-script-restaurant]]) to confirm that a dish’s flavor, spice, and texture fit the restaurant’s known strengths.
3. **Leverage portion sizes**: Opt for small plates, tasting portions, or lunch specials to sample the strength without overspending.
4. **Prioritize familiar descriptors**: Favor dishes described as “mild,” “balanced,” or “lightly fermented” when you want a gentle introduction to new flavors.
5. **Align specials with strengths**: When a daily special builds on the restaurant’s signature ingredients or techniques, treat it as a low‑risk opportunity to explore within the established strength.
