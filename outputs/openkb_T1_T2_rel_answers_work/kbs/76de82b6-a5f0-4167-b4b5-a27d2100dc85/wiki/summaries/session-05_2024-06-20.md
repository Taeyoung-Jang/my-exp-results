## Summary

The assistant outlines practical habits and conversational tools that help couples maintain a solid, healthy partnership over time. Central recommendations focus on low‑pressure communication and a recurring [[concepts/relationship-check-in]]. The guidance includes a set of 5‑7 weekly prompt questions, strategies for introducing difficult topics without turning them into debates, and adjustments for relationships that have lasted several years.

### Key Concepts
- [[concepts/low-pressure-communication]]: casual, non‑judgmental dialogue about feelings and needs.  
- [[concepts/relationship-check-in]]: regular, structured conversation to align expectations.  
- [[concepts/feelings-and-needs]]: expressing personal experience rather than assigning blame.  
- curiosity in relationships: staying interested in each other's growth.  
- gratitude: acknowledging positive actions regularly.  
- intimacy: checking in on emotional/physical closeness.

### Recommended weekly check‑in prompts
1. What moment this week made you feel especially close?  
2. Did anything leave you feeling off or disconnected?  
3. Is there a need you’ve been having that I might not have noticed?  
4. What did I do that you appreciated more than I realized?  
5. How do you feel about the balance between time together and time apart?  
6. Is there any stress you’d like me to know about?  
7. What small change could we each try next week to make things smoother?

### Tips for handling tougher topics
- Frame statements from your own experience (e.g., “I’ve been feeling…”) rather than as accusations.  
- Choose a calm moment and ask permission to discuss.  
- Emphasize understanding and teamwork over winning an argument.  
- Keep the tone gentle and avoid turning the check‑in into a performance review.

### Adapting for long‑term partnerships
- Add prompts about personal growth, life rhythms, and future plans.  
- Include a gentle check on intimacy and evolving expectations.  
- Focus on mutual support for ongoing stressors and upcoming changes.  
- Maintain curiosity and gratitude to prevent stagnation.
