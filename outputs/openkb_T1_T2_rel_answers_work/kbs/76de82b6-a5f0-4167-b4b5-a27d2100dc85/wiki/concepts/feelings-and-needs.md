# Feelings and Needs

**Definition**: A communication approach that focuses on sharing one’s own emotional state and personal requirements, using “I‑statements” rather than blame, to promote understanding and connection in relationships.

## Core Elements
- **Self‑focused language** – Frame statements around your own experience (e.g., “I feel…”, “I need…”).  
- **Clarity and specificity** – Clearly name the feeling and the concrete need behind it.  
- **Non‑judgmental tone** – Avoid labeling the partner’s actions as good or bad; keep the focus on personal impact.
- **Reciprocal listening** – Invite the partner to reflect back what they heard, ensuring both sides feel heard.

## Why It Matters
In the conversation from [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-20]], the assistant recommends using feelings‑and‑needs language when bringing up tougher topics during a relationship [[concepts/relationship-check-in]].  
This method helps shift discussions from blame to collaboration, reducing the risk of arguments turning into debates.

## Practical Application in Weekly Check‑Ins
The assistant suggests a set of prompts that embed feelings‑and‑needs principles, such as:
- “What felt good this week?” (invites positive feelings)
- “Did anything leave you feeling off or disconnected?” (elicits negative feelings)
- “Is there something you’ve been needing that I might not have noticed?” (explicit need‑statement)

Using these prompts encourages partners to surface both pleasant and challenging emotions in a safe, low‑pressure environment.

## Benefits
1. **Enhanced empathy** – Partners hear directly what the other experiences.  
2. **De‑escalation** – Framing concerns as personal experience avoids triggering defensiveness.  
3. **Alignment** – Regularly expressing needs helps synchronize expectations over time.
