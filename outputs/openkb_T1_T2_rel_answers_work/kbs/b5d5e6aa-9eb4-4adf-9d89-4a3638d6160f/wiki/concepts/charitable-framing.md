# Charitable Framing

**Definition**: Charitable framing is the skill of restating another's argument as the most reasonable and coherent version, without implying agreement.

## Relation to Steelmanning
Charitable framing underpins [[concepts/steelmanning]]; it is the mental step that transforms a neutral restatement into a robust “steelman.”

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-35_2025-08-15]])
- **Ritual pause**: Before responding, pause to identify the speaker’s goal and underlying fear.
- **Goal‑first, fear‑second structure**: "You aim to X and worry that Y."
- **Neutral language**: Avoid sarcasm or dismissive phrasing.
- **Self‑validation**: Check that you genuinely feel a reasonable person could hold the view.

## Practical Techniques
1. **30‑second spoken script** – A short template that names the other’s goal and concern, then asks for confirmation.
2. **Silent version** – Internally state: “Their aim is … Their worry is …”.
3. **Mental‑pause ritual** – The brief interruption aligns with [[concepts/mental-pause-ritual]].

## Benefits
- Reduces escalation in heated discussions.
- Improves empathy and active listening.
- Strengthens argumentative clarity by forcing you to understand the opponent before critique.
