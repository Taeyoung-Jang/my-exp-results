# Blender Evaluation Criteria

This concept synthesizes the primary factors to consider when comparing blenders, as outlined in [[summaries/session-11_2025-06-15]]. It groups technical specs, design elements, and user‑experience aspects that together determine real‑world performance versus marketing hype.

## Core Criteria

| Criterion | Why it matters | Practical assessment |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Motor & Power Interaction** | Wattage alone is a weak proxy; the synergy between motor, blade design, and jar shape creates effective torque and vortex. | Observe whether ingredients are pulled down without constant stirring; test with ice, nuts, and fibrous greens.
| **Blade Design** | Blade geometry influences how quickly foods are shredded and how evenly they circulate. | Look for reviews that show blades cutting through tough items in a few seconds.
| **Jar Shape & Geometry** | Narrow or well‑channeled jars generate a stronger vortex, improving consistency and reducing wall‑riding. | Examine videos for smooth ingredient flow; note any “ingredients riding up the sides” complaints.
| **Noise Level** | High‑power blenders are inevitably loud; treat as a preference unless noise is extreme or impacts usage. | Compare decibel ratings or user reports; weigh against performance needs.
| **Build Quality & Warranty** | Longer warranties often reflect confidence in durability and parts longevity. | Check brand warranty length and coverage; note repeated failure reports despite warranty.
| **Real‑World Performance vs. Marketing** | Marketing terms like “pro blades” or “cyclonic action” can be vague; rely on hands‑on or video tests that demonstrate actual blending outcomes. | Prioritize independent video reviews that perform nut butter, crushed ice, and green‑smoothie tests.

## How to Use the Criteria
1. **Identify the most relevant tasks** (e.g., smoothies, nut butter, soups). 
2. **Match each task to the criteria** – a model that handles ice well must have strong motor‑blade‑jar synergy.
3. **Cross‑reference reviews** for each criterion, looking for consistent patterns rather than isolated anecdotes.
4. **Balance subjective factors** (noise, size) against objective performance indicators.
