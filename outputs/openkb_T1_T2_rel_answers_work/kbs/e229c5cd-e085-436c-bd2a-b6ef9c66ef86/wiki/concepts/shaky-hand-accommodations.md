# Shaky‑Hand Accommodations

**Definition**: Strategies, tools, and workflow adjustments that enable individuals with shaky or tremorous hands to engage in creative activities while minimizing the need for fine, precise movements.

## Core Principles
- **Broad, palm‑based motions**: Favor actions that use the whole hand or arm rather than finger dexterity.
- **Ergonomic, chunky‑handle tools**: Tools with large, rounded grips reduce the need for a steady grip.
- **Low‑mess environments**: Contain materials to limit cleanup, which can be challenging for those with tremor.
- **Texture as a feature**: Embrace natural wobble or texture created by hand tremor as an aesthetic element.

## Practical Applications (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-03-11]])
1. **Air‑dry clay hand‑building**
   - Use **[[entities/das-clay]]** or **[[entities/crayola-air-dry-clay]]**, which stay soft and are easy to shape.
   - Work on a **[[entities/silicone-baking-mat]]** to keep the surface clean.
   - Shape with palms; a **damp kitchen sponge** smooths surfaces without precise finger work.
2. **Tool selection**
   - Choose **[[entities/rubber-clay-tool]]** or wooden tools with chunky handles for pressing and flattening.
   - Avoid fine‑pointed tools that require steady fingers.
3. **Finishing**
   - Apply **[[entities/acrylic-paint]]** directly; no sanding needed.
   - Seal with **[[entities/clear-matte-varnish]]** for durability.

## Tips for Success
- Keep a **small bowl of water** and a rag nearby for quick wiping.
- Work in short sessions; fatigue can increase tremor.
- Embrace irregularities; uneven rims or textures can become intentional design features.
- Use a **silicone mat** to contain any clay crumbs and prevent dust.
