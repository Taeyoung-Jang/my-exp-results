# Make‑Ahead Cooking

Make‑Ahead Cooking is the practice of preparing food components—or entire dishes—well before the day they will be served, so that final assembly or reheating on the event day is quick, low‑stress, and ensures consistent quality.

## Why it matters for small events
For a gathering of 25‑30 guests, time and kitchen space are limited. By cooking items ahead you can:
- Reduce the amount of active cooking on the day of the event.
- Keep hot foods warm without drying out (e.g., braised chicken thighs).
- Allow flavors to develop overnight (stock, braising liquid, dressings).
- Minimize last‑minute complications, letting the host enjoy the party.

## Core steps (as applied in [[summaries/session-08_2024-06-12]])
| Phase | Tasks | Day‑Before tag |
|-------|-------|----------------|
| **Protein** | Fully braise chicken thighs, then cool in the braising liquid. | ✅ |
| **Vegetarian main** | Bake or par‑bake mushroom‑leek galette; cool and store. | ✅ |
| **Grains & veg** | Cook farro, roast vegetables, and store separately; prepare olive‑oil‑lemon dressing. | ✅ (partial) |
| **Salads** | Wash and dry greens; slice cucumbers and tomatoes; keep vinaigrette separate. | ❌ (final assembly day‑of) |
| **Appetizers** | Prepare olives & nuts; bake warm bite pieces (tart squares or stuffed mushrooms). | ✅ |
| **Beverages** | Mix mocktail base (citrus juice, ginger syrup, cranberry juice). Add sparkling water only when serving. | ✅ |

## Benefits highlighted in the source
- **Flavor development**: Braising liquid and mocktail base improve after an overnight rest.
- **Convenience**: Most items can be refrigerated and simply reheated, freeing up the host for guest interaction.
- **Consistency**: Portion sizes and ingredient quantities are locked in early, reducing waste.
