# Portion Control

## Overview
Portion control refers to managing the amount of food consumed in a snack or meal so that it provides enough energy to sustain performance without diminishing appetite for subsequent meals. In the context of daily meal patterns, appropriate portion sizes help maintain [[concepts/energy-stability]] while supporting a flexible routine as discussed in [[concepts/meal-pattern-flexibility]].

## Key Guidance from Session 08 (2024‑07‑15)
The conversation outlines practical, work‑friendly snack portions that are small enough to avoid feeling like a second lunch yet sufficient to smooth out mid‑afternoon energy dips.

- **Nuts**: about **¼ cup** (≈ 1 oz), a small handful.
- **Crackers + cheese**: **3–4 whole‑grain crackers** with a **single thin slice** of cheese (≈ 1 oz total).
- **Yogurt**: one **single‑serve cup**, typically **5–6 oz**.
- **Fruit**: one **small apple**, a **clementine**, or **½ banana**.

These quantities are described as “the size of your palm” or “two‑three bites,” providing a balance of quick and sustained carbohydrate and protein sources.

## Why It Matters
- **Energy steadiness**: Controlled portions give a modest calorie boost that can prevent the afternoon slump without causing a post‑snack crash.
- **Appetite preservation**: Keeping snacks modest protects hunger cues for dinner, supporting overall dietary balance.
- **Ease of implementation**: Simple, portion‑based guidelines are easy to remember and apply at work or on the go.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- Tying snack size to personal hunger signals aligns with [[concepts/meal-pattern-flexibility]].
- Proper portion sizing contributes to consistent energy levels, a core component of [[concepts/energy-stability]].
