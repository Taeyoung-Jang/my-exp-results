# Mini Fuel‑Up

**Definition**: A *mini fuel‑up* is a brief, protein‑ or fat‑rich snack consumed between main meals to maintain [[concepts/energy-stability]] without requiring a dedicated break.

---

## Core Characteristics
- **Portability**: Easy to eat at a desk or on‑the‑go (e.g., yogurt cup, protein shake, trail mix).
- **Low preparation**: Requires little to no prep time; often ready‑to‑eat from the pantry or fridge.
- **Nutrient density**: Focuses on protein, healthy fats, or a balanced mix of carbs and fiber to blunt blood‑sugar spikes.
- **Small portion**: Typically 100‑200 kcal, enough to provide a quick energy boost without feeling like a full meal.

---

## Recommended Mini Fuel‑Up Options (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-03-16]])
| Food | Why it works |
|------|--------------|
| Small yogurt (Greek or regular) | High protein, probiotic gut benefits |
| Banana with a spoonful of peanut butter | Quick carbs + sustained fat/protein |
| Handful of nuts or trail mix | Healthy fats and protein, low mess |
| Cheese stick + whole‑grain crackers | Balanced protein/fiber combo |
| Hummus with pre‑cut veggies | Plant protein + fiber, easy to dip |
| Protein shake or drinkable yogurt | Sippable, completely hands‑free |
| Small smoothie (protein powder + fruit) | Hydrating, nutrient‑dense, portable |

---

## Typical Scheduling Patterns
- **Mid‑morning** (around 10 am): One mini fuel‑up to bridge breakfast and lunch.
- **Mid‑afternoon** (around 2 pm): A second mini fuel‑up to prevent the post‑lunch slump.
- Adjust timing based on personal appetite; even a single snack between 10 am‑2 pm can be sufficient for low‑appetite individuals.

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader **[[concepts/meal-pattern-flexibility]]** approach, allowing people to keep a large evening dinner while still supporting daytime energy.
- Supports **[[concepts/energy-stability]]** by providing steady nutrient input.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with **[[concepts/desk-friendly-snacks]]**, which are the practical food forms that enable mini fuel‑ups.
- Complements **[[concepts/one-big-meal-routine]]**, offering a strategy for those who prefer one main meal.

---

## Implementation Tips
1. **Stock a grab‑and‑go pantry**: keep single‑serve yogurts, nuts, protein bars, and pre‑cut veggies within arm’s reach.
2. **Pre‑portion**: Use small containers or snack bags to avoid over‑eating.
3. **Pair protein with a bit of fruit or veg**: stabilizes blood sugar while keeping the snack satisfying.
4. **Stay hydrated**: Often thirst is mistaken for low energy; a water bottle nearby helps.

---

*Mini fuel‑ups* provide a flexible, low‑effort way to keep energy levels steady throughout a busy day, especially for those following a **[[concepts/one-big-meal-routine]]**.
