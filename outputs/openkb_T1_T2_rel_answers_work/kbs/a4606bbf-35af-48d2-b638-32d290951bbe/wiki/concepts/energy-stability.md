# Energy Stability

Energy stability refers to the ability to maintain steady physical and mental performance throughout the day without sudden drops in focus, mood, or fatigue. In the context of daily nutrition, it is largely influenced by **when** meals are consumed, **what** is eaten, and the **size** of any supplemental snacks.

## Key Drivers

- **Meal‑pattern flexibility** – Aligning meals with natural hunger cues while keeping at least one predictable anchor (e.g., a work break) helps avoid long periods without fuel, supporting a smoother energy curve. See [[concepts/meal-pattern-flexibility]].
- **Portion control** – Small, balanced snacks (e.g., a quarter‑cup of nuts, a single‑serve yogurt, or a few crackers with cheese) provide a quick energy boost without suppressing appetite for the next main meal. See [[concepts/portion-control]].
- **Timing of snacks** – Consuming a snack 30–60 minutes before a typical mid‑afternoon slump (around 3–4 pm) can pre‑empt the dip, keeping energy levels more even.

## Practical Indicators

- **Stable focus**: Ability to concentrate without frequent lapses.
- **Even mood**: Minimal irritability or light‑headedness between meals.
- **Controlled hunger**: No strong cravings or pre‑meal rumination.

When these signs waver—e.g., afternoon fatigue, irritability, or strong hunger before the next meal—it suggests the current pattern may be compromising energy stability.

## Example from Session 08

The guidance in [[summaries/session-08_2024-07-15]] illustrates a simple self‑experiment: compare an earlier lunch versus adding a modest afternoon snack. The option that yields clearer focus and fewer energy dips is taken as the more effective strategy for maintaining energy stability.
