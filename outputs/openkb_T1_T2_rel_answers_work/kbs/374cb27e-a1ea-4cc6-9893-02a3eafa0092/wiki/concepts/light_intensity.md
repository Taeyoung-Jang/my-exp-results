# Light Intensity

**Definition**: Light intensity is the measure of how bright a light source appears, typically expressed in lumens or lux, and it directly influences human alertness, mood, and the regulation of the [[concepts/circadian_rhythm]].

## Relevance to Daily Rhythm
- High intensity, especially cool‑toned light, in the morning and early day promotes wakefulness and cognitive performance.
- Reducing intensity in the late afternoon and evening helps signal the body to transition toward sleep.

## Practical Guidance from [[summaries/session-03_2024-07-15]]
- **Morning/Daytime**: Aim for bright, cool light (e.g., natural sunlight or high‑lumens indoor lighting) to boost alertness.
- **Late Afternoon**: Soften exposure by using darker‑tinted [[entities/sunglasses]] and a [[entities/brimmed_hat]] when outdoors, lowering the overall intensity reaching the eyes.
- **Evening/Night**: Switch to warm‑white lighting in the 2200–2700 K range, delivering 400–800 lumens per lamp. Use lower‑placement fixtures such as [[entities/floor_lamp]] or [[entities/table_lamp]] to create a gentle pool of light rather than bright overhead illumination.

## Interaction with [[concepts/color_temperature]]
- Intensity and color temperature work together: bright, cool light (higher intensity, higher temperature) signals daytime, while dimmer, warm light (lower intensity, lower temperature) cues evening.

## Implementation Tips
1. **Outdoor Tapering**: Wear [[entities/sunglasses]] with stronger tint and a [[entities/brimmed_hat]] about three hours before bedtime to reduce intensity without compromising safety.
2. **Indoor Lighting**: Choose warm‑white LED bulbs ([[entities/led]]) such as [[entities/warm_white_led_bulb]] and place them at eye‑level. Dim them gradually as bedtime approaches.
3. **Lumens Guidance**: For a small apartment, 400–800 lumens per lamp provides sufficient visibility while remaining non‑stimulating.

## Summary
Managing light intensity—through timing, source selection, and placement—supports optimal alertness during the day and smooth transition to sleep at night, aligning with the body's natural circadian rhythm.
