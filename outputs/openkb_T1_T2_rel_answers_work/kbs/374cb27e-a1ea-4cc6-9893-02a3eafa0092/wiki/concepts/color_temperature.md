# Color Temperature

**Definition**: Color temperature describes the hue of a light source, expressed in kelvins (K). Lower values (≈2200–3000 K) appear warm (more red/yellow), while higher values (≈5000–6500 K) appear cool (more blue‑white).

## Role in Human Physiology
- **Circadian rhythm**: Cool, high‑kelvin light in the morning stimulates the body’s internal clock, promoting alertness. Warm, low‑kelvin light in the evening signals the brain to wind down, aiding sleep onset.
- **Blue‑light component**: Higher color temperatures contain more blue wavelengths, which can suppress melatonin production; lower temperatures reduce this effect.

## Practical Guidance
### From [[summaries/session-03_2024-07-15]]
- **Morning/Daytime**: Use bright, cool‑toned lighting (5000 K or higher) or natural sunlight to boost focus.
- **Late Afternoon & Evening**: Shift to warm‑white lighting in the 2200–3000 K range.
  - Recommended fixtures: [[entities/warm_white_led_bulb]] LEDs providing 400–800 lumens per lamp.
  - Placement: lower‑level sources such as [[entities/floor_lamp]]s or [[entities/table_lamp]]s to avoid overhead daylight cues.
- **Outdoor Tapering**: When outdoors later in the day, use sunglasses with darker tint and a [[entities/brimmed_hat]] to reduce perceived intensity without eliminating safety.

### Additional tips from [[summaries/session-35_2025-01-12]]
- **Reading & study spaces**: Choose a small, warm desk lamp (e.g., a [[entities/desk_lamp]]) that emits light in the 3000–4000 K range. This provides enough ambient illumination to keep the screen from standing out as a bright spot while remaining comfortable for prolonged reading.
- **Screen‑ambient matching**: Adjust laptop or monitor brightness to roughly match the surrounding light level, a practice described in [[concepts/screen_brightness_matching]]. When the ambient light is steady and low (such as in a library), keeping the screen brightness close to that level is sufficient; a dedicated reading lamp is only needed when you switch to paper.
- **Avoiding harsh contrast**: Position the lamp to the side of the workspace rather than directly in front of the screen. This reduces glare and helps maintain a gentle contrast between the display and the surrounding area.
- **Visual breaks**: Incorporate regular short breaks to look at distant objects, supporting eye comfort alongside appropriate color temperature and lighting choices. See [[concepts/visual_breaks]].

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/light_intensity]]**: Color temperature works together with intensity; bright cool light is most stimulating, while dim warm light is most relaxing.
- **[[concepts/circadian_rhythm]]**: Aligning color temperature with the time of day helps synchronize the circadian system.
- **[[concepts/ambient_lighting]]**: Consistent, side‑placed ambient lighting complements the chosen color temperature, reducing eye strain during extended tasks.

## Implementation Tips
1. **Select appropriate bulbs**: Choose LED bulbs labeled "warm white" (~2700 K) for evening spaces and "neutral white" (~3500 K) for reading areas.
2. **Use dimmers**: Gradually reduce brightness as bedtime approaches to reinforce the low‑temperature signal.
3. **Screen settings**: Enable warm‑color modes on devices to mimic lower color temperatures after sunset, and match screen brightness to ambient light levels.
4. **Lamp choice**: A small warm [[entities/desk_lamp]] or a low‑intensity [[entities/table_lamp]] works well for study zones; avoid overly bright overhead fixtures.
5. **Maintain visual breaks**: Follow the 20‑20‑20 rule (every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds) to complement lighting strategies.
