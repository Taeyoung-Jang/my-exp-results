## Circadian Rhythm

**Definition**: The circadian rhythm is an innate ~24‑hour cycle that synchronizes physiological processes—such as sleep‑wake timing, hormone release, body temperature, and alertness—with environmental cues, primarily light.

### How Light Influences the Rhythm
- **Light intensity** and **color temperature** are the strongest external signals (zeitgebers) that reset the internal clock.
- Bright, cool‑toned light in the morning signals day and promotes alertness, while warmer, dimmer light in the evening cues the body to prepare for sleep.
- **Blue light** from screens can mimic daylight, delaying melatonin production if exposure occurs late.

### Practical Guidance from [[summaries/session-03_2024-07-15]]
- **Morning/Daytime**: Seek bright, cool light (natural sunlight or cool‑tone LEDs) to reinforce the wake‑up phase.
- **Late Afternoon/Evening**: Gradually reduce light intensity and shift to warmer tones (≈2200‑3000 K) to ease the transition toward the sleep phase.
- **Night**: Use warm‑white lighting (e.g., [[entities/warm_white_led_bulb]]) placed at waist or eye level (e.g., [[entities/floor_lamp]], [[entities/table_lamp]]) and dim it over the final hour before bed.
- **Screen Use**: Lower brightness and enable warm‑color modes to limit blue‑light impact.

### Tapering Schedule (Illustrative)
1. **~3 h before bedtime** – Outdoors: wear darker tinted [[entities/sunglasses]] and a [[entities/brimmed_hat]] to soften sunlight.
2. **~2 h before bedtime** – Stay in shade, keep sunglasses on.
3. **~1 h before bedtime** – Move indoors; switch to warm, low‑intensity lighting.

### Why It Matters
Aligning light exposure with the circadian rhythm supports:
- Improved sleep quality and faster onset.
- Enhanced daytime focus and mood.
- Reduced risk of circadian‑related health issues (e.g., metabolic disruption, mood disorders).
