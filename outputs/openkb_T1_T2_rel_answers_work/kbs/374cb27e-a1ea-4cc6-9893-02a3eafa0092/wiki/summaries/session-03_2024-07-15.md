# Summary of session-03_2024-07-15

**Main focus**: Practical strategies to manage light exposure—timing, intensity, and color temperature—to align with the body’s [[concepts/circadian_rhythm]] while staying safe and functional.

## Key points
- **Morning and daytime**: Bright, cool‑toned light (natural sunlight or indoor equivalents) promotes alertness.
- **Late afternoon/evening**: Gradually reduce [[concepts/light_intensity]] and shift to warmer color temperatures (≈2200–3000 K) to signal the brain that day is ending.
- **Outdoor taper schedule**:
  * **~3 h before bedtime** – wear darker‑tinted [[entities/sunglasses]] and a [[entities/brimmed_hat]] to soften incoming sunlight.
  * **~2 h before bedtime** – seek shade, keep sunglasses on, let the sun appear lower.
  * **~1 h before bedtime** – transition indoors.
- **Indoor night lighting**:
  * Warm‑white bulbs (2200–2700 K) delivered by [[entities/warm_white_led_bulb]] LEDs, providing 400–800 lumens per lamp.
  * Use 2–3 lamps placed at waist‑ or eye‑level (e.g., [[entities/floor_lamp]]s with fabric shades, [[entities/table_lamp]]s with diffusers) instead of overhead fixtures.
  * Dim lamps gradually as sleep approaches; keep a single focused warm desk lamp if work is needed.
- **Screen use**: Lower brightness, enable warm color mode to reduce blue‑light impact.
- **Safety**: Maintain sufficient visibility for tasks; the tapering methods avoid “cave” darkness while limiting stimulatory cues.

## Practical recommendations
1. **Sunglasses & hat** – simple tools to cut light intensity outdoors without sacrificing visibility.
2. **Bulb selection** – warm‑white (2200‑3000 K) LEDs; avoid cool‑white daylight bulbs after sunset.
3. **Lamp placement** – lower‑level lighting creates a “pool” effect, reducing the daylight‑like overhead cue.
4. **Gradual dimming** – either via dimmer switches or swapping to lower‑lumens lamps as bedtime nears.
