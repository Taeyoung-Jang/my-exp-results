## Library

A **library** is a place dedicated to the collection, organization, and access of information resources, typically featuring quiet, low‑intensity indoor lighting that supports focused reading and study.

### Role in [[summaries/session-35_2025-01-12]]
The session discusses how the steady, low [[concepts/ambient_lighting]] of a library influences optimal screen and lighting setups:
- **Screen brightness** should be matched to the ambient level, following the principle of [[concepts/screen_brightness_matching]].
- **Visual breaks** are recommended to alleviate eye strain, especially during long study periods in the library environment ([[concepts/visual_breaks]]).
- Adjusting **color temperature** toward a warmer tone can further reduce fatigue, aligning with the library’s typical lighting conditions ([[concepts/color_temperature]]).
- The low [[concepts/light_intensity]] of library interiors makes a small, warm [[entities/reading_lamp]] or [[entities/desk_lamp]] useful only when transitioning to paper materials; otherwise, the laptop screen should remain the primary light source.

### Practical Recommendations Derived from the Document
1. **Match screen brightness to the library’s ambient light** to avoid a stark contrast between screen and surroundings.
2. **Use a small reading lamp** only when reading printed documents; keep it off while working on the laptop.
3. **Maintain regular visual breaks** by looking at distant objects to relax eye muscles.
4. **Prefer warmer lighting** (around 3000‑4000 K) to complement the library’s low‑light setting, reducing blue‑light exposure.

### Related Entities

- [[entities/desk_lamp]] – can supplement ambient light when needed.
