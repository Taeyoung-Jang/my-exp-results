# [[entities/floor_lamp]]

A **floor lamp** is a standing light fixture that typically uses a tall pole and one or more bulbs to illuminate a space from floor level. In the context of *session-03_2024-07-15*, the floor lamp is recommended as part of an evening lighting setup to support a healthy [[concepts/circadian_rhythm]].

## Key facts from the document
- **Purpose**: Used to create a gentle "pool" of light at waist or eye level, reducing the daylight‑like cue from overhead fixtures.
- **Bulb type**: Paired with **warm‑white LED** bulbs (≈2200–2700 K) – see [[entities/warm_white_led_bulb]].
- **Brightness**: Suggested output of **400–800 lumens** per lamp, providing enough visibility for a small apartment without being overly stimulating.
- **Placement**: Positioned on the floor, often with a fabric shade, to diffuse light and keep the illumination warm and soft, aligning with the recommended shift in [[concepts/color_temperature]] for the evening.
- **Interaction with other items**: Works alongside a [[entities/table_lamp]] for task lighting and can be combined with accessories like [[entities/brimmed_hat]] (outdoor sun‑shielding) and [[entities/sunglasses]] (daytime light management) to form a comprehensive light‑exposure strategy.

## Practical recommendations
1. **Choose warm‑white LED bulbs** (see [[entities/led]]) within the 2200–2700 K range to avoid stimulating the brain’s alertness pathways.
2. **Use 2‑3 floor lamps** in a small living area to distribute soft light evenly, avoiding bright overhead lights that mimic daylight.
3. **Dim gradually** as bedtime approaches, either via a dimmer switch or by swapping to a lower‑lumens bulb, reinforcing the natural tapering schedule described in the session.
4. **Combine with a table lamp** for focused tasks if needed, keeping the desk lamp’s light warm and moderate in brightness.
