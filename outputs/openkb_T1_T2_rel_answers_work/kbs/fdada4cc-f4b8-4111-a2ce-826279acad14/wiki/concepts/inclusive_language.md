## Inclusive Language

**Definition**: Inclusive language refers to word choices and phrasing that respect diverse identities and beliefs, avoiding assumptions, stereotypes, or overly specific personal details that could alienate or marginalize readers.

### Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-06_2022-06-15]])
- **Focus on values, not doctrine** – Highlight universal qualities such as mindfulness, curiosity, respect, and balance rather than describing specific rituals or theological points.
- **Avoid prescriptive or comparative statements** – Do not suggest that one belief system is superior or imply that others should adopt the same practices.
- **Keep descriptions concise and workplace‑relevant** – Use brief, professional language that connects personal values to work style and team dynamics.
- **Offer optional, non‑religious alternatives** – Provide versions of introductions that convey the same ethos without naming a faith, ensuring broader accessibility.

### Practical Tips
1. **Use neutral value terms** (e.g., *mindfulness, collaboration, growth*) that resonate across cultures.
2. **Steer clear of ritual specifics** such as prayer times, dietary rules, or festival details.
3. **Frame personal identity as a contributor to professional behavior** rather than a credential.
4. **Test phrasing with diverse colleagues** to catch unintended exclusivity.

### Application Example
> "I’m a practicing Hindu, and the values I pull from that—mindfulness, curiosity, and respect—shape how I like to work and collaborate."
> 
> This sentence follows inclusive language guidelines by emphasizing values, staying concise, and avoiding detailed religious exposition.
