# Daily Prayer Routine

The **Daily Prayer Routine** concept captures practical methods for fitting the five obligatory Islamic prayers (Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha) into a standard 9 am–5 pm work schedule. It draws on the advice presented in [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]] and connects to related workplace‑faith concepts.

## Core Schedule
| Prayer | Typical Timing in a Workday | Practical Tip |
|--------|----------------------------|--------------|
| [[entities/fajr]] | 20‑30 minutes before waking for work | Perform wudu, pray at home before leaving. |
| [[entities/dhuhr]] | During lunch break | Use a quiet corner, meeting room, or car; keep it brief. |
| [[entities/asr]] | Late afternoon, before leaving or upon arrival home | Step out of the office or pray immediately after getting home. |
| Maghrib | Soon after sunset, usually right after arriving home | Pray within minutes of entering the house or in the car if needed. |
| Isha | After dinner, before bedtime | Treat as a wind‑down ritual; can be done in a private room at home. |

## Managing Space Constraints
- Small meeting rooms, unused offices, storage areas, or a parked car can serve as temporary prayer spaces.
- A compact travel mat enables quick set‑up and removal, keeping the practice discreet.
- If no dedicated space is available, a clean corner of a desk can be used, provided it does not disrupt work.

## Consistency Strategies
- Anchor each prayer to existing daily activities (wake‑up, lunch, commute, evening routine).
- Keep wudu refreshed throughout the day to reduce friction.
- If a prayer is missed, perform it as soon as possible without self‑criticism.
- View prayers as short, intentional pauses rather than interruptions.

## Benefits
Implementing a structured daily prayer routine helps new Muslims maintain religious obligations while preserving work productivity, reduces anxiety about missing prayers, and fosters a sense of spiritual continuity throughout the day.
