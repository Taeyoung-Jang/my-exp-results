**User:** Do you have a favorite system for keeping a computer “tidy” over time—like naming conventions, folder structures, or tags—and how do you decide what goes where without it turning into chaos?

**Assistant:** I don’t have a personal “favorite,” but a simple approach works well for most people and avoids chaos:

Use a small number of high‑level folders based on what something *is* (like “Work,” “Personal,” “Media”). Inside each, name files by what they contain and, if useful, the date—something like “ProjectProposal_2026‑03” rather than “final_v3_reallyfinal.”

Tags are good when something belongs in more than one place. I think of folders as the “home” for a file and tags as extra cross‑links.

The trick to keeping it tidy is deciding the purpose of each folder ahead of time and sticking to that rule. If I can’t immediately tell where a file should go, that’s a sign the system has too many categories and needs simplifying.

The best system is the one that’s easy enough for you to use even when you’re tired or in a hurry.

**User:** I'm deep in the middle of reorganizing my digital file structure, so that's pretty much where all my focus is right now. Given that, would you recommend I start by defining a few top-level folders first and then sort everything into them, or is it better to tackle it by project/category and let the top-level structure emerge as I go?

**Assistant:** If you’re already knee‑deep in it, it’s usually smoother to sketch a few broad top‑level folders first. They don’t have to be perfect—just enough to give you a backbone so you’re not sorting into a vacuum.

Once those are in place, go through your files and let the finer structure take shape as you notice patterns. If a top‑level folder starts feeling too vague or overloaded, you can split or rename it as you go.

Starting with a light framework keeps you from reinventing the structure every time you pick up a new batch of files, but it still leaves room for the system to evolve naturally.

**User:** Can you suggest a concrete set of 5–8 top-level folders that works for most people, and a quick rule-of-thumb for what *doesn’t* belong in each? Also, how would you handle “inbox”/downloads so it doesn’t become a dumping ground again?

**Assistant:** You can cover most needs with a small set of broad buckets. Something like “Work,” “Personal,” “Finances,” “Media,” “Reference,” “Archive,” and “Apps/Utilities” usually works for most people. Each one has a simple boundary: Work holds anything tied to your job or active professional projects and nothing purely personal; Personal is for life‑admin, hobbies, and anything not connected to work; Finances is only money‑related documents, not general paperwork; Media is just photos, videos, music, and creative assets, not documents; Reference is stable information you keep for later, not active projects; Archive is where you move completed or inactive things, not anything you still touch; Apps/Utilities is only installers, scripts, and technical odds and ends, not actual work product.

For the inbox or Downloads problem, the easiest method is to treat it like an actual inbox: it stays empty by default. Set a small rule, like clearing it once a day or every few days, and move everything to its proper home immediately when you touch it. If something needs deciding, park it in a single “To Sort” folder so the chaos doesn’t spread, and process that folder on a schedule. The key is keeping only temporary, just‑arrived files there and never letting them “live” in Downloads.
