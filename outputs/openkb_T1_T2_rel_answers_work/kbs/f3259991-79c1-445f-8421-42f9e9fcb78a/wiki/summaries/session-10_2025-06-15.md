# Summary of session-10_2025-06-15

## Core ideas
- **High‑level folder set**: [[entities/work]], [[entities/personal]], [[entities/finances]], [[entities/media]], [[entities/reference]], [[entities/archive]], [[entities/apps_utilities]] (7‑8 buckets) [[concepts/folder_hierarchy]].
- **Boundary rules** for each bucket (what does *not* belong) to prevent overlap.
- **Inbox/Downloads management**: Keep empty, process daily, use a single “To Sort” folder for undecided items [[concepts/inbox_management]].
- **Naming convention**: `ProjectName_YYYY‑MM` for folders; `Category_Description_YYYY‑MM‑DD` for files, enabling quick chronological sorting [[concepts/naming_conventions]].
- **Active vs. completed work**: Keep active projects under `Work/Projects`; when finished, move the whole project folder to `Work/Archive` (no duplication) – a plain‑text approach.
- **Tags as cross‑links**: Use tags when a file naturally belongs to multiple top‑level buckets, while folders act as the primary “home.”

## Recommended top‑level folders (5‑8)
1. **Work** – job‑related files; excludes personal items.  
2. **Personal** – life admin, hobbies; excludes work‑related files.  
3. **Finances** – money‑related docs; excludes generic paperwork.  
4. **Media** – photos, video, audio; excludes text documents.  
5. **Reference** – stable info you keep for later; excludes active projects.  
6. **Archive** – completed or inactive items; excludes anything you still use.  
7. **Apps/Utilities** – installers, scripts, technical odds‑and‑ends; excludes work output.

## Practical workflow
- **Sketch top‑level folders first** to give a backbone.
- **Iteratively refine** subfolders as patterns emerge.
- **Process the inbox daily**: move each item immediately to its proper home; undecided items go to a `To Sort` folder for scheduled review.
- **When a project finishes**, move its folder to `Work/Archive` (or `Archive/OldProjects`) to keep the active area clean.

## Takeaway
A lightweight, pre‑defined folder scaffold combined with clear naming and disciplined inbox processing yields a maintainable, chaos‑free digital workspace.
