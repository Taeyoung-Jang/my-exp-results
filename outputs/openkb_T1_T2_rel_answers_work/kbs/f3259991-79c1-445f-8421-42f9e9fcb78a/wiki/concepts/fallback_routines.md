# Fallback Routines

**Definition**: A fallback routine is a minimal, time‑boxed version of a regular habit that can be executed when time, energy, or environment are limited. It preserves the cue‑response loop of the full routine while drastically reducing execution time.

## Why Fallback Routines Matter
- Maintain *habit continuity* even on chaotic days.
- Prevent the erosion of streaks that can occur when a full routine feels unattainable.
- Provide a psychologically reassuring “plan B” that lowers resistance to starting.

## Structure in Session 35 (2025‑07‑06)
In [[summaries/session-35_2025-07-06]], three core anchors (sleep wind‑down, minimal workout, daily planning) were each paired with a **2‑minute fallback**:

| Anchor | Full Routine (≈10 min) | Fallback (≈2 min) |
|--------|------------------------|-------------------|
| **Sleep wind‑down** | Device silence → dim lights → wash face → brush teeth → change into sleep clothes → calming media → breathing | Device silence → dim lights → wash face → lie down and breathe |
| **Minimal workout** | Overhead stretch → squats → wall push‑ups → hip hinge → mobility rolls | Overhead stretch → shoulder rolls → ten deep breaths |
| **Daily planning** | Open notes → write “one must‑do” → list meetings/travel → choose personal action → reality check | Open notes → write a single sentence of the most important task |

These fallbacks keep the essential cue (e.g., **first pause of the day**) and a brief action that signals the brain the habit has been performed.

## Design Principles (linked to [[concepts/routine_anchoring]])
1. **Cue‑first**: Anchor the fallback to the same invariant cue used for the full routine (e.g., first sip of coffee, arrival in a room).
2. **Time limit**: Keep the duration under two minutes to ensure feasibility.
3. **Essential action**: Preserve at least one core movement or mental step that represents the habit’s purpose.
4. **Scalable**: The fallback can be expanded back to the full routine when circumstances improve.

## Interaction with Time‑Zone Changes
When crossing multiple time zones, fallback routines help bridge the adjustment period. By executing the same cue‑based fallback regardless of local clock, the body receives consistent signals, supporting smoother [[concepts/time_zone_adaptation]].

## Creating Your Own Fallbacks
1. Identify the **key outcome** of the habit (e.g., signal to brain for sleep, raise heart rate, clarify priorities).
2. Choose the **simplest action** that achieves that outcome.
3. Bind it to an **always‑present cue**.
4. Practice until the 2‑minute version feels automatic.
