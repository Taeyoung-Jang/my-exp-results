## Key Concepts
- **Friendship longevity** – depends on consistency, ease, and mutual effort [[concepts/friendship-longevity]].
- **Effort balance** – healthy friendships feel mutual; effort should not feel one‑sided [[concepts/effort-balance]].
- **Communication patterns** – distinguish between “busy” (warm but infrequent) and “drifting” (flat tone, no initiative) [[concepts/communication-patterns]].
- Low‑pressure check‑in – simple, open‑ended messages that invite honesty without pressure.
- Matching effort – after repeated vague replies, align your outreach with the other person’s level.
- Concrete invitation – specific date/time proposals clarify interest and reduce ambiguity.

## Signals of Interest vs. Disinterest
1. **Busy but engaged**: slower replies, warm tone, occasional offers to meet later.  
2. **Drifting out**: flat tone, no follow‑up, avoidance of concrete plans.

## Practical Strategies
- **Pattern observation**: Look for consistent signs over multiple interactions before concluding disinterest.  
- **Check‑in phrasing**: “Hey, I miss talking with you. I know life gets hectic, but I wanted to see how you’re doing and if you’re up for catching up sometime.”  
- **Follow‑up attempt**: If the first check‑in gets a vague reply, send one more genuine attempt after a short pause.  
- **Decision point**: After two vague responses, shift to matching effort rather than continuing to initiate.  
- **Specific invite example**: “Hey, I’d love to catch up – are you free for coffee next Tuesday or Wednesday?”

## Managing Effort
- **Initial stage**: Offer low‑pressure check‑ins.  
- **After vague replies**: Give one more attempt, then align your outreach with the other’s level.  
- **If they respond concretely**: Re‑engage and maintain mutual effort.  
- **If they remain vague**: Allow the friendship to settle naturally without over‑investing.

## Takeaway
Maintain adult friendships by monitoring communication patterns, using low‑pressure yet clear check‑ins, and adjusting your effort to match the other person’s engagement level.
