# Communication Patterns

**Communication patterns** describe the observable ways friends interact over time, allowing us to infer whether a relationship is merely *busy* or gradually *drifting* apart. The concept was articulated in the discussion of adult friendships in [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-12]].

## Core Dimensions
- **Frequency vs. Warmth** – A busy friend may reply more slowly but still uses a warm tone, offers alternative times, and shows genuine interest when they do engage.
- **Tone & Initiative** – Drifting signals include flat or perfunctory replies, a lack of follow‑up, and avoidance of concrete plans.
- **Consistency** – Patterns become meaningful when observed across multiple interactions rather than isolated incidents.

## Signal Categories (from the source)
| Category | Typical Behaviors | Interpretation |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Busy‑but‑Engaged** | Slow replies, warm language, occasional “let’s reschedule” offers | Still values the connection; life constraints are the barrier. |
| **Drifting Out** | Flat tone, no initiative, vague or non‑committal responses to invites | Decreasing interest; the friendship may be naturally fading. |

## Practical Application
1. **Observe patterns** – Note tone, response time, and initiative over several weeks.
2. **Differentiate** – Consistent warmth despite delay suggests busyness; repeated vagueness signals drifting.
3. **Adjust effort** – Align your outreach with the other person’s level of engagement, a principle that ties into [[concepts/effort-balance]].
4. **Use low‑pressure check‑ins** – Simple, open‑ended messages help confirm interest without pressure.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- [[concepts/effort-balance]]: Communication patterns inform how much effort is mutual and when to recalibrate.
- [[concepts/friendship-longevity]]: Recognizing healthy patterns supports long‑lasting friendships.

## Example from Session 7
> *“Someone being busy still shows little signs of wanting to stay connected—maybe slower replies, but warm ones; maybe fewer hangouts, but they offer another time.”* – illustrates the **busy‑but‑engaged** pattern.

> *“When someone’s drifting out, the tone goes flat, the conversation feels like you’re dragging it forward, and there’s no real effort to meet you halfway.”* – captures the **drifting** pattern.

By systematically tracking these communication cues, individuals can make more informed decisions about when to invest further, when to match effort, or when to let a friendship evolve naturally.
