# Summary of Session 9 (2024‑06‑16)

**Core idea:** Treat friendship maintenance as a series of small, easy touchpoints rather than a big, scheduled task. This keeps connections alive without adding pressure. [[concepts/low-pressure-touchpoints]]

## Everyday low‑effort cues
- Send a quick voice note while walking.  
- Share a meme or a photo that reminds you of a friend.  
- Drop a simple “thinking of you” message.

## Flexible routines
- Set up optional, low‑commitment rituals such as an occasional monthly call, an ongoing text thread, or a brief weekly check‑in that can shrink to a 15‑minute call when schedules are tight. [[concepts/flexible-routines]]

## Keeping weekly meet‑ups low‑pressure
- Frame the gathering as *optional*: “If it works this week, great; if not, no stress.”  
- Vary the format: full sit‑down, quick drink, short walk, or a brief call while cooking.  
- Respond warmly when someone can’t attend, reinforcing that friendship isn’t tied to perfect attendance.

## Backup communication options (8‑10 ideas)
1. Quick voice note at the end of the day.  
2. Share a photo or short video from your day.  
3. Send a “weekly update” text.  
4. Ten‑minute call while one person is cooking or commuting.  
5. Short “walk‑and‑talk” while one person is out.  
6. Light‑hearted group chat check‑in.  
7. Drop a funny meme or inside joke for the group.  
8. Run a “question of the day” thread that anyone can answer.  
9. Brief group video hello.  
10. Share an updated collaborative playlist.

## Default low‑pressure script for cancelled plans
> “Looks like this week’s not going to line up, but no worries at all. Let’s just do a quick check‑in instead—I’ll send something later, and we can regroup next week if it works.”

## Underlying principle
Friendships thrive on *consistency, not intensity*; small, genuine touches over time are more sustainable than occasional big events. [[concepts/consistency-vs-intensity]]
