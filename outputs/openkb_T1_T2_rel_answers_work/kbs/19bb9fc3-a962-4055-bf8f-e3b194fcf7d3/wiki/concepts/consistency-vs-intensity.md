# Consistency vs. Intensity in Friendships

**Definition**: The idea that friendships are healthier and more sustainable when they rely on frequent, low‑effort touchpoints (consistency) rather than sporadic, high‑commitment events (intensity).

## Core Insight
The conversation in [[summaries/session-09_2024-06-16]] highlighted that friends tend to stay connected when they prioritize *consistent* small gestures—like quick voice notes, memes, or brief check‑ins—over trying to schedule large, intensive gatherings that can feel like obligations.

## Why Consistency Works
- **Low pressure**: Small actions require little planning, reducing the risk of feeling forced.
- **Presence over perfection**: Regular brief interactions keep people present in each other's lives without demanding perfect attendance.
- **Adaptability**: Consistent touchpoints can easily shift format (text, call, meme) to match current energy levels.

## Risks of Over‑emphasizing Intensity
- **Scheduling stress**: Large, infrequent meet‑ups often clash with busy adult schedules.
- **Perceived obligation**: When friendships are defined by big events, missing one can feel like a breach of commitment.
- **Burnout**: The effort needed for intensive gatherings can lead to fatigue, making future meetings less likely.

## Practical Applications
- **Adopt low‑effort routines** such as daily or weekly micro‑interactions (see [[concepts/low-pressure-touchpoints]]). 
- **Create flexible structures** that allow friends to choose the level of engagement each time (see [[concepts/flexible-routines]]).
- **Use backup options** (voice notes, shared photos, quick calls) to maintain continuity when a planned meetup falls through.
