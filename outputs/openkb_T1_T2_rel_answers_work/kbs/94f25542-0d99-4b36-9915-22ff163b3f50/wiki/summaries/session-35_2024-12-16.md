# Summary of session-35_2024-12-16

**Topic:** Practical workflow for changing a personal name and ensuring all relevant records are updated.

## Key Points
- A legal name change triggers an **[[concepts/administrative-ripple-effect]]**: every system that stores the old name must be updated.  
- The most annoying part is the sheer number of hidden places, not the court filing itself.  
- The assistant outlines a prioritized update order that moves from the most official identifiers outward:  
  1. Court order → certified copies  
  2. **[[entities/social-security-administration]]**  
  3. State ID / driver’s license (DMV)  
  4. Banks, credit‑cards, employer HR, insurance, passport  
  5. Utilities, medical portals, schools, retirement accounts, travel programs, digital accounts.  
- **[[concepts/common-forgotten-accounts]]** that often cause trouble include retirement accounts, obscure subscriptions, airline frequent‑flyer programs, and email usernames.  
- A checklist is provided, broken into *Must do first*, *Next*, and *Later* sections, with guidance on proof types and tracking.  
- The user requests a tracker in table form, adding columns for priority, method, dates, and status. The template pre‑populates the most common entries (SSA, DMV, passport, two banks, employer HR, health & auto insurance, utilities, primary care, and the three credit bureaus).  
- Certified copies: most people order 3‑5 certified copies to handle original‑document requests while keeping spares.  
- Tips for institutions demanding the original court order: bring originals, ask if they can photocopy and return, use tracked mail, and include prepaid return envelopes.
