# Equifax

**Type:** organization

Equifax is one of the three primary credit reporting agencies in the United States. It collects and maintains consumer credit information used by lenders, landlords, and other entities to assess creditworthiness.

## Relevance to the name‑change process

In the guidance provided in [[summaries/session-35_2024-12-16]], Equifax is listed among the credit bureaus that often need to be updated after a legal name change. Updating Equifax (as well as Experian and TransUnion) is important because credit reports are frequently checked for identity verification, and an outdated name can cause delays or denials.

## Typical proof requirements

When updating your name with Equifax, a **certified copy of the court order** (or a scanned copy if accepted) is usually required, alongside a government‑issued ID. This aligns with the broader [[concepts/name-change-process]] and the [[concepts/administrative-ripple-effect]] of ensuring all official records reflect the new name.

## Commonly forgotten aspect

Credit bureaus are a classic example of the [[concepts/common-forgotten-accounts]] that people overlook during a name change, leading to potential issues with credit checks later on.
