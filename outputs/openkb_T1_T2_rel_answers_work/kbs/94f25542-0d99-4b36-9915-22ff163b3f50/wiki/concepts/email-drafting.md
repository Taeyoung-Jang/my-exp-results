## Email Drafting

**Definition**: Email drafting is the process of creating clear, concise, and appropriately toned email messages for specific communication goals.

### Core Elements
- **Subject line** – Sets expectations and encourages the recipient to open the message. Example subject lines from the source: "Interest in Upcoming Opportunities" and "Checking In About Possible Work".
- **Opening greeting** – Establishes a polite tone (e.g., "Hi [Name],").
- **Purpose statement** – Quickly states why you are writing, often expressing interest in work or a request.
- **Relevant credentials** – Briefly mention qualifications or status, such as union membership, in a natural way.
- **Call to action** – Invites the recipient to respond or schedule a conversation.
- **Closing** – Leaves a courteous sign‑off.

### Tone Variations
The source document illustrates three common tones:
- **Standard** – Balanced, professional yet relaxed.
- **Formal** – More polished and structured.
- **Casual** – Friendly and informal.

### Union Membership Mention
A key detail in the example drafts is how to reference union membership without sounding formal. Suggested phrasing includes:
- "feel free to confirm my union membership—I’ve been in good standing with the electrical workers' union since last year."
- Alternate one‑liners provide flexible options for contractors.
