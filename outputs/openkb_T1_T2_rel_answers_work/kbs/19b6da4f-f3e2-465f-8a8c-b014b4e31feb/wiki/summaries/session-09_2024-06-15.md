# Summary of session-09_2024-06-15

## Decision‑making: eating out vs. cooking at home
- **Pros of eating out**: convenience, variety, novelty, minimal prep.  
- **Cons of eating out**: higher cost, less control over ingredients, often higher salt/fat.  
- **Pros of cooking at home**: ingredient control, cost savings, grounding activity, healthfulness.  
- **Cons of cooking at home**: time and mental energy required.  
- **Key decision factors**: available time, mental bandwidth, budget, health goals.  
- Central idea: people choose based on the *easy button* vs. *saving money/health* trade‑off. [[concepts/eating_out_vs_home_cooking]]

## Low‑effort home‑meal philosophy
- Emphasize **simple, minimally processed ingredients** while staying fast.  
- Keep a core **pantry & freezer stock** that can be mixed with a small fresh add‑on.  
- Use **rotation planning** to consume fresh produce early and rely on frozen/shelf‑stable items later.  
- Goal: reduce food waste while maintaining satisfying meals. [[concepts/low_effort_meals]] [[concepts/pantry_management]]

## Five “tired‑night” meal templates (original & minimal‑processing tweak)

1. **Garlic‑olive‑oil pasta**  
   - Staples: [[entities/dry_pasta]], [[entities/olive_oil]], garlic, red‑pepper flakes, frozen spinach.  
   - Fresh add‑ons: cherry tomatoes or basil.

2. **Fried‑rice bowl**  
   - Staples: rice, [[entities/soy_sauce]], sesame oil, frozen peas/mixed veggies, eggs.  
   - Fresh add‑ons: green onions or spinach.

3. **Quick ramen / broth‑and‑noodle bowl**  
   - Staples: [[entities/instant_ramen]] or plain noodles, [[entities/miso_paste]] or stock cubes, frozen broccoli/corn, eggs.  
   - Fresh add‑ons: mushrooms.

4. **Sheet‑pan chicken (or tofu) & veg**  
   - Staples: [[entities/chicken_thighs]] or frozen [[entities/tofu]], [[entities/olive_oil]], basic seasoning, carrots/potatoes.  
   - Fresh add‑on: zucchini or bell pepper.

5. **Bean‑and‑greens skillet**  
   - Staples: canned beans, [[entities/olive_oil]], garlic, spices, frozen kale/greens.  
   - Fresh add‑on: lemon or fresh greens.

All templates stress ingredient control, quick prep (<20 min), and reliance on long‑lasting staples.

## Simple rotation plan
- **Early week**: use fresh tomatoes, basil, mushrooms, green onions, zucchini, fresh greens.  
- **Mid‑late week**: shift to frozen spinach, peas, broccoli, carrots, potatoes.  
- Cook extra rice or potatoes once per week to have a base for multiple meals.  
- Keep a running “fresh‑produce‑first” checklist to avoid spoilage. *(food‑waste prevention concept expressed in plain text)*

## Emergency backup options (no‑cook)
1. **Assembly‑only plate**: whole‑grain bread, quality cheese, fruit.  
2. **5‑minute salad**: pre‑washed greens, canned chickpeas, olive oil & vinegar.  
3. **Microwavable homemade soup**: frozen soup portion or broth cube with toast.  
- These keep ingredient control without active cooking. *(emergency meal options concept expressed in plain text)*

## Overall takeaways
- A **core stock** of dry, canned, and frozen items plus a small rotating fresh list enables fast, healthy meals on low‑energy days.  
- Prioritizing **minimally processed ingredients** preserves nutritional quality while staying convenient.  
- A structured **rotation and planning routine** minimizes waste and keeps the pantry tidy.
