# Communication Contexts

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Situational factors—such as stakes, power dynamics, familiarity, and emotional intensity—that influence which language (Portuguese or English) and what level of [[concepts/formality-level]] to employ.

## Core Elements
1. **Stake Level**
   - High‑stakes interactions (e.g., customer support, workplace disputes, negotiations) demand clearer wording and a more formal tone to prevent misunderstandings.
2. **Power / Service Dynamics**
   - When one party holds a service or authority role (e.g., a support agent), a respectful, slightly formal style signals professionalism.
3. **Familiarity**
   - Greater familiarity allows for a more casual tone; lower familiarity calls for formality.
4. **Emotional Tension**
   - Anticipated or actual tension raises the need for precise, calm language.
5. **Accuracy Needs**
   - Topics involving money, deadlines, responsibilities, or technical details require higher precision and thus a more formal register.

## Application in Real‑World Scenarios (from [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]])
- **Customer Support** – Use a slightly formal, clear tone; offer language switch options while keeping the conversation respectful.
- **Workplace Chat** – Mirror the interlocutor’s language but stay one notch more formal; suggest written follow‑up for complex points.
- **Disagreements / Sensitive Talks** – Prioritize clarity, propose English if the other switches, but also suggest moving to writing or involving a bilingual colleague to avoid nuanced errors.
