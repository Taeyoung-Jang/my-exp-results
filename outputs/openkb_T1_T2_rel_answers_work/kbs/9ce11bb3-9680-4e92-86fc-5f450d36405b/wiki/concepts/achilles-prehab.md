# Achilles Pre‑hab

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A structured set of eccentric calf exercises and mobility work designed to strengthen the Achilles tendon, improve ankle flexibility, and reduce injury risk during high‑volume endurance activities such as long‑distance hiking or running.

---

## Core Elements (as defined in [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-13]])

1. **Eccentric Calf Protocol**  
   - **Straight‑knee drop:** 3 sets of 12 reps per leg, 3‑second lowering phase.  
   - **Bent‑knee drop:** 3 sets of 12 reps per leg, 3‑second lowering phase.  
   - Performed on every strength day; an optional light set can be added on a mobility day if comfort allows.
   - If irritation occurs, reduce to 2 sets for the next session.

2. **Mobility & Stretching**  
   - Post‑eccentric calf stretch (standing or wall stretch) for 30 s per leg.  
   - Ankle dorsiflexion drills (e.g., band‑assisted pull‑ups) for 2 × 15 reps.

3. **Safety/Adjustment Rules**  
   - **Morning tightness:** Cut that day’s workload by half.  
   - **Sharp pain during a session:** Stop immediately; replace the next two days with easy walking.  
   - **Soreness > 48 h:** Reduce weekly volume by ~25 % and freeze pack‑weight progression.  
   - **Uphill/downhill aggravation:** Shorten the session, avoid steep grades until the tendon feels normal.

---

## How It Fits Into a Training Plan
- Integrated into the **strength days** of a progressive overload schedule (see [[concepts/progressive-overload]]).  
- Complements pack‑weight progression for backpacking preparation, ensuring the tendon can handle increased load without over‑stress.
- Serves as a preventative layer before entering higher‑impact phases such as long runs or heavy‑pack hikes.

---

## Benefits
- Increases tendon stiffness and load tolerance.  
- Improves ankle range of motion, supporting better gait mechanics.  
- Reduces incidence of Achilles tendonitis and related setbacks.
