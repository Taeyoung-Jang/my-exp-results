# Real Break Definition

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A purposeful, uninterrupted pause that lets the body and mind reset before resuming tasks.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Complete cessation of activity** – No multitasking, no standing, and no “on‑the‑go” actions. The break must be a true stop, not a quick shuffle to the next chore.
2. **Physical rest** – Sitting or lying down, drinking water, or a brief, gentle stretch that does not raise the heart rate significantly.
3. **Mental reset** – Engaging in a low‑effort, satisfying activity (e.g., listening to one song, sipping tea, looking out a window) that gives the mind a brief, pleasant distraction.
4. **Duration** – Typically 5‑15 minutes, long enough for breathing to normalize and for a sense of lightness to return.

## Why Real Breaks Matter

In the **session‑38_2025-03‑12** guidance, real breaks are highlighted as essential for people experiencing breathlessness or low energy. They prevent tasks from piling up and help avoid the “push‑through” habit that can worsen fatigue. By inserting real breaks, the body can:

- Stabilize breathing and heart rate
- Reduce perceived exertion for the next activity
- Offer a psychological cue that rest is part of the plan, not a failure

## Practical Examples (from the source)

- **Sit with a drink** – Place a cup of water or tea on a table and simply sip, without checking a phone.
- **One‑song pause** – Play a favorite song, close your eyes, and let the music finish.
- **Window gaze** – Look outside for a few minutes, noticing colors or movement, without mental tasks.
- **Gentle stretch** – Perform a few slow, easy stretches that feel restorative rather than strenuous.

These activities are intentionally low‑effort but give a sense of ritual, making the break feel rewarding instead of wasted time.
