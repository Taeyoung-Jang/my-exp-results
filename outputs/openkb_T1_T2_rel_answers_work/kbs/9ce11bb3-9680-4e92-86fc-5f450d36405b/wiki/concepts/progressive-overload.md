# Progressive Overload

**Definition**

Progressive overload is the systematic, incremental increase of training variables—such as volume, intensity, frequency, or load—to stimulate physiological adaptations while keeping injury risk low. The core idea is that the body only adapts when it is challenged beyond its current capacity, so the stress must be raised in a controlled manner.

---

## Application in the 16‑Week Backpacking Plan

The schedule described in [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-13]] uses progressive overload in four main ways:

1. **Duration/Volume** – Week‑by‑week targets for run/hike time and weekend mileage increase steadily (e.g., from 45 min runs in week 1 to 75 min by week 7, and from 6 mi weekend hikes to 16 mi by week 14).
2. **Pack Weight** – Load is added gradually, starting with no pack, then 5 lb, and eventually reaching 15‑20 lb, following the guidance in [[concepts/pack-weight-guidelines]].
3. **Intensity Elements** – Hills and longer continuous efforts are introduced after the initial easy weeks, providing a modest intensity boost without excessive strain.
4. **Recovery (Down‑Weeks)** – Every fourth week the total load is reduced (shorter hikes, lighter pack) to allow the body to consolidate gains and lower injury risk. This built‑in deload is a key safety component of progressive overload.

---

## Key Components

| Variable | How it progresses in the plan |
|----------|-------------------------------|
| **Time/Distance** | Incremental weekly targets, with occasional step‑backs (down‑weeks). |
| **Load (Pack Weight)** | Starts at 0 lb, adds 5 lb increments, peaks at the expected trip weight (≈15‑20 % of body weight). |
| **Intensity** | Introduces hills and longer continuous sessions after the first month. |
| **Frequency** | Consistent five‑day training week, maintaining regular stimulus. |

---

## Interaction with Injury Prevention

The plan couples progressive overload with a focused Achilles tendon pre‑hab routine (see [[concepts/achilles-prehab]]). By increasing load slowly and monitoring tendon response, the risk of overuse injuries is reduced. Safety rules—such as cutting volume in half if morning tightness occurs—ensure that overload remains within a safe envelope.

---

## Summary

Progressive overload is the backbone of the 16‑week training strategy for the multi‑day backpacking trip. By systematically raising duration, distance, pack weight, and intensity while incorporating regular down‑weeks and targeted pre‑hab, the plan aims to improve endurance and strength without exceeding the body's capacity to adapt.
