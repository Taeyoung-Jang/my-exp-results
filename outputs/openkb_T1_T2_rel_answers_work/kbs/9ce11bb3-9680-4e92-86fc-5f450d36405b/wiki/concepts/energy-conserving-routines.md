# Energy-Conserving Routines

**Definition**: A systematic way of planning daily activities, chores, and errands that deliberately limits continuous exertion, builds in frequent, purposeful rest periods, and separates tasks into *main* and *supporting* components to protect energy levels, especially for individuals experiencing mild breathlessness.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Time‑blocking for health** – Allocate fixed blocks for activities (e.g., 8‑10 am, 10‑11 am break) rather than a free‑flow schedule. This creates predictable pacing and reduces the risk of over‑exertion. [[concepts/time-blocking-for-health]]
2. **Real‑break definition** – Breaks are *inactive* periods where no chores or multitasking occur; they involve quiet, low‑effort activities that allow breathing and heart rate to settle. [[concepts/real-break-definition]]
3. **Main vs. supporting tasks** – A *main task* requires sustained effort (e.g., grocery shopping, laundry). A *supporting task* is a light follow‑up (e.g., folding a few items, wiping a counter) that can be paused easily.
4. **Energy‑conserving routines** – Structure a week so each day has a primary focus, a secondary optional task, and ample open space for recovery.
5. **Plan B pacing** – When unexpected fatigue occurs, prioritize perishables, stop further unpacking, and extend rest. This failsafe prevents escalation of breathlessness.

---

## Practical Example from Session 38 (2025‑03‑12)

The source document presents a concrete “Grocery Day” plan that embodies the concept:

- **Normal day** – Drive → shop (≈40 min) → immediate perishables unload → *real break* (20 min) → pantry unload → optional extra round.
- **Tired‑day variation** – Later start, shorter shop, longer rest after perishables, one short unpacking round.
- **Plan B** – If winded, only cold items go directly to fridge/freezer; other items stay in bags for several hours; stop if breathing does not settle.

These steps illustrate how to apply **time‑blocking**, **real breaks**, and **main/supporting task** separation to a single routine.

---

## Benefits

- Reduces cumulative fatigue by preventing long, uninterrupted exertion.
- Provides clear signals for when to pause (breathing not normal after a few minutes of rest).
- Improves adherence to self‑care because the schedule feels achievable and flexible.
- Offers a safety net (Plan B) that minimizes risk of over‑exertion.
