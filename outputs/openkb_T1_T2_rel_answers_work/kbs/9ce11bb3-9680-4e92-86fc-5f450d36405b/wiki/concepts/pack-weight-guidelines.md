# Pack Weight Guidelines

**Definition**: A set of recommendations for determining the appropriate load to carry in a backpack during multi‑day endurance training and trips, expressed as a proportion of the hiker’s body weight, and a progressive schedule for increasing that load safely.

## Core Principles
- **Body‑weight percentage**: Typical backpacking loads range from **15 % to 25 % of body weight**. For users prone to tendon issues (e.g., Achilles tightness), aim toward the lower end (~15 %). For stronger, leaner hikers, 20 %–25 % feels natural.
- **Progressive overload**: Load is increased gradually alongside hike duration, mirroring the **[[concepts/progressive-overload]]** principle used in the weekly training plan.
- **Safety integration**: Load increases are coordinated with **[[concepts/achilles-prehab]]** work; if Achilles symptoms appear, weight progression is paused.

## Application in the 16‑Week Plan (session‑12)
| Week | Typical Pack Weight | Notes |
|------|--------------------|-------|
| 1‑2  | None               | Focus on base conditioning.
| 3‑4  | 5 lb (~5 % BW)     | Introduced after easy mileage.
| 5‑7  | 8‑12 lb (~10‑15 % BW) | Incremental adds every 1‑2 weeks.
| 8‑10 | 12‑14 lb (~15‑18 % BW) | Aligns with longer weekend hikes.
| 11‑13| 15‑18 lb (~18‑20 % BW) | Approaches expected trip load.
| 14‑15| 18‑20 lb (~20‑22 % BW) | Back‑to‑back simulation, near‑peak weight.
| 16   | 10‑12 lb (taper)   | Light load to keep legs fresh.

**Key take‑aways**:
- Load is never added on a *down‑week*; the week of reduced mileage also holds the previous weight.
- The maximum training load caps at the expected trip weight (≈15‑20 % BW). No training session exceeds that to avoid over‑stress.
- Adjustments: If Achilles discomfort arises, **hold** the weight, reduce mileage, or drop to the previous week’s load.

## Practical Steps for New Hikers
1. **Calculate target weight**: Multiply body weight (lb) by 0.15‑0.20. Example: 150 lb × 0.18 = 27 lb.
2. **Start empty** for the first 2 weeks to build base endurance.
3. **Add weight gradually**: Use a small load (5 lb) in week 3, then increase 2‑3 lb every 1‑2 weeks, matching the schedule above.
4. **Use a comfortable pack**: Ensure torso length and hip belt fit before adding weight.
5. **Test load on easy hikes** before committing to longer, heavier outings.

## Interaction with Training Concepts
- The weight progression follows the same **gradual increase** logic as the weekly mileage and intensity, embodying [[concepts/progressive-overload]].
- Maintaining **[[concepts/achilles-prehab]]** exercises mitigates the added stress on the Achilles tendon from uphill walking with a load.
