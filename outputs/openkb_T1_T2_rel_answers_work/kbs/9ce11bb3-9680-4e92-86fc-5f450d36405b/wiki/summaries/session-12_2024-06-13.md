# Summary of session-12_2024-06-13

**Purpose**: Provide the user with a concrete, low‑injury‑risk training solution for a 3–4‑day backpacking trip, including a detailed 16‑week schedule, Achilles‑tendon pre‑hab, pack‑weight progression, and basic gear & nutrition recommendations.

## Key Decision
- Recommended the multi‑day backpacking trip as the most rewarding option with the lowest risk compared to a marathon or single‑day long hike.

## 16‑Week Progressive Schedule
- **Structure**: Mon‑Fri training sessions (run/hike, strength, mobility) plus **weekend long hikes** (Sat) and rest (Sun).  
- **Progression**: Gradual increase in duration, mileage, and **pack weight** (none → 5 lb → up to 20 lb), with a down‑week every 4th week.  
- **Final weeks** focus on back‑to‑back hike simulation and a taper week.  
- The schedule follows the principles of [[concepts/progressive-overload]].

## Achilles Pre‑hab
- Eccentric calf protocol performed on strength days:  
  - Straight‑knee: 3 × 12 reps per leg  
  - Bent‑knee: 3 × 12 reps per leg  
  - 3‑second lowering, optional reduction to 2 sets if irritation.  
- Clear safety rules for tightness, sharp pain, lingering soreness, and grade‑related aggravation.  
- Detailed guidance is captured under [[concepts/achilles-prehab]].

## Pack Weight Guidelines
- Target **15–20 % of body weight** for the actual trip; training peaks at the expected weight.  
- Progressive load introduced alongside hike duration, described in [[concepts/pack-weight-guidelines]].

## Gear & Nutrition Checklist (high‑level)
- Trail shoes/boots, well‑fitting backpack, moisture‑managing layers, headlamp, sleep system, optional trekking poles.  
- Balanced meals, pre‑hike carbs, easy‑digest carbs during hikes, regular hydration and electrolytes.
