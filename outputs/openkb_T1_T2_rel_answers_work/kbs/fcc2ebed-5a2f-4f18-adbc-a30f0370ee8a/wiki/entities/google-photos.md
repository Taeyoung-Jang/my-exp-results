# Google Photos (product)

Google Photos is a cloud‑based photo backup and synchronization service provided by [[entities/google]]. It allows users to automatically upload photos and videos from their devices, store them securely in the cloud, and access them across platforms such as Android and iOS.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-32_2025-09-12]]
- **Automatic backup**: When enabled, the app continuously syncs new media, making recovery easy after device loss or replacement.
- **Privacy considerations**: The session highlights the need to review encryption settings, two‑factor authentication, and which data categories are being uploaded. While Google Photos offers strong security, end‑to‑end encryption is not always enabled by default, so users should verify [[concepts/end-to-end-encryption]] options where available.
- **Data minimization**: To keep a minimal cloud footprint, the checklist suggests disabling shared‑album syncing and limiting additional metadata uploads, aligning with the [[concepts/data-minimization]] principle.
- **Risk assessment**: Potential risks include account compromise, provider breaches, and unintended use of uploaded media for personalization. Users are advised to perform a [[concepts/risk-assessment]] by reviewing account access, device list, and app permissions.

## Recommended security checklist (derived from the session)
1. **Enable encryption** – Check the app’s security settings for any available end‑to‑end encryption options.
2. **Activate two‑factor authentication** on the Google account linked to Google Photos.
3. **Audit connected devices** – Remove any unfamiliar devices from the account.
4. **Review app permissions** – Revoke third‑party access that isn’t needed.
5. **Limit data types** – Turn off optional features such as automatic sharing, facial recognition, or location tagging if privacy is a priority.
