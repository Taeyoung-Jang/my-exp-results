# Google Contacts

Google Contacts is a cloud‑based contact management service that stores individual address‑book entries and syncs them across devices linked to a Google account. It is part of the broader Google ecosystem and integrates with Android, Gmail, and other Google services.

## Key facts from [[summaries/session-32_2025-09-12]]
- **Backup role:** In the session discussion, Google Contacts is mentioned as a primary way to keep contacts safely backed up while minimizing a larger cloud footprint. Keeping this sync enabled ensures contacts can be restored easily after a device loss or reset.
- **Privacy & security considerations:** The advice highlights reviewing account‑level security (e.g., two‑factor authentication) and confirming which data types are synced. Google Contacts benefits from the overall security measures of the Google account, but users should still enable [[concepts/end-to-end-encryption]] where available and be aware of [[concepts/risk-assessment]] regarding account compromise.
- **Data‑minimization:** The checklist suggests limiting synced data to essential items. For contacts, this means keeping the service on while disabling unnecessary app‑specific backups, aligning with [[concepts/data-minimization]].

## Recommended settings (per session‑32 guidance)
1. Ensure two‑factor authentication is enabled for your Google account.
2. Verify that only the necessary devices are signed in to your Google account.
3. Keep Google Contacts syncing turned on for reliable contact restoration.
4. Review the list of apps that have access to your contacts and revoke any that are unnecessary.
5. Combine contacts sync with other backup practices (e.g., device backups) to maintain a balanced, low‑footprint cloud strategy.
