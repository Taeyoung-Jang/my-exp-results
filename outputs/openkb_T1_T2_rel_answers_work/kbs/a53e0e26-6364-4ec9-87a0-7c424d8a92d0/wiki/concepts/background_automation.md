# Background Automation

**Definition**: Background automation refers to technology features that operate silently after an initial setup, handling tasks without demanding ongoing user interaction.

## Core Characteristics
- **Invisible operation** – the system works without popping up notifications, prompts, or requiring frequent adjustments.
- **Context‑aware triggers** – actions are initiated based on reliable cues such as time of day, location, or presence rather than manual commands.
- **Low cognitive load** – users are freed from decision‑making and monitoring, supporting the broader goal of [[concepts/mental_load_reduction]].
- **Stable defaults** – sensible, pre‑configured settings that rarely need fine‑tuning, aligning with [[concepts/low_maintenance_design]].

## Examples from Session 4 (2024‑06‑15)
- **Smartphone** automatic brightness and focus modes that adapt to time or location, then stay silent.
- **Smartwatch** passive health tracking (continuous heart‑rate, sleep monitoring) that records data without prompting the wearer.
- **Smart home** routines such as lights turning on at sunset or a thermostat switching to eco mode when the house is empty; these rely on time‑ or presence‑based triggers and need no further user input.

## Benefits
- Reduces mental effort and clutter, contributing to a feeling that the technology is a helpful assistant rather than a new task.
- Increases reliability; predictable, deterministic triggers (time or presence) tend to mis‑fire less than complex AI‑driven guesses.
