# Intentional Morning Routine

An **Intentional Morning Routine** is a deliberately designed set of minimal, analog habits that shift the start of the day from autopilot to a hands‑on, focused state. It emphasizes simple physical cues and brief written prompts that can be completed in under two minutes, reducing mental load while fostering a sense of direction.

## Core Elements
- **Tactile Rituals** – Physical objects (e.g., a stone, pen, or coin) placed at key locations act as touchpoints that trigger a moment of reflection. [[concepts/tactile_ritual]]
- **Morning Touchpoint** – A quick, repeatable cue linked to an existing habit (e.g., coffee brewing) that prompts a brief action such as straightening a desk or noting a priority. [[concepts/morning_touchpoint]]
- **Paper Prompt Card** – A three‑line card or notebook template with concise prompts that guide focus, priority, and a reminder. [[concepts/paper_prompt_card]]
- **Morning Settings Template** – A variant of the prompt card formatted for notebook use, allowing daily “settings” like mode, primary focus, and note to self. [[concepts/morning_settings_template]]
- **Quick Review Method** – A lightweight way to scan the last few days’ settings for patterns without extensive journaling. [[concepts/quick_review_method]]

## Design Principles
- **Low‑Maintenance Design** – The routine is built to require less than two minutes, avoiding the creation of a new task list. [[concepts/low_maintenance_design]]
- **Mental Load Reduction** – By limiting prompts to three lines and using physical cues, the routine keeps cognitive overhead minimal. [[concepts/mental_load_reduction]]
- **Background Automation Awareness** – While the routine uses simple manual actions, it acknowledges the broader context of habit automation and seeks to interrupt unwanted autopilot. [[concepts/background_automation]]

## Benefits
1. **Increased Presence** – Physical cues ground the mind in the present moment.
2. **Clear Daily Direction** – A single priority line focuses effort.
3. **Flexibility** – Multiple prompt variations (practical, reflective, calming, momentum‑focused) let users choose a vibe that fits their mood.
4. **Ease of Review** – The quick review method turns past entries into a visual dashboard, supporting subtle adjustments.

## Example Prompt Sets
- **Practical**: "Today’s anchor task…", "Must‑remember detail…", "One small win to aim for…"
- **Reflective**: "How I want to show up…", "What I’ll give my best energy to…", "Something I want to notice today…"
- **Calming**: "What I’m carrying this morning…", "What I can set down…", "Where I want my attention to rest…"
- **Momentum**: "One step forward…", "What supports that step…", "End‑of‑day feeling I’m aiming for…"

## Implementation Steps
1. Choose a physical **touchpoint** (stone, coin, etc.) and place it by the door or bedside.
2. Create a stack of **paper prompt cards** or reserve the top of a notebook for the **morning settings template**.
3. Each morning, use the chosen prompt set to fill in three lines (~30‑45 seconds).
4. Store the completed card in a simple stack or binder clip; periodically move older cards to an envelope.
5. Weekly, perform a **quick review** of the last 3‑4 entries to spot repeated themes and decide on one tiny adjustment.
