# Zoom Rental Decision

**Concept definition** – A decision framework that helps cinematographers determine whether renting a zoom lens (e.g., a 24‑70 mm or 70‑200 mm) will improve efficiency, image quality, or workflow for a particular shoot.

## Core criteria

1. **Shoot tempo and flexibility**
   - Fast‑paced, run‑and‑gun situations where lens swaps would break rhythm favor zoomes. This is common in [[concepts/b-roll-workflow]] such as event coverage or multi‑location days.
2. **Physical constraints**
   - Tight spaces or limited ability to reposition the camera make a variable‑focal lens advantageous.
3. **Stabilization needs**
   - When in‑body stabilization (IBIS) is weak, a lens with optical IS (e.g., [[entities/lens-is]]) can provide smoother handheld shots. See [[concepts/stabilization-choice]] for a deeper dive.
4. **Gimbal usage**
   - A gimbal reduces but does not eliminate the benefit of a zoom; the zoom still speeds up off‑gimbal handheld work. Related entity: [[entities/gimbal]].
5. **Aperture considerations**
   - For controlled lighting (e.g., office interviews), an f/4 zoom (such as [[entities/24-70mm-f4]]) usually suffices. The heavier f/2.8 version is only justified for low‑light pockets.
6. **Lens focal range relevance**
   - A 24‑70 mm covers the most useful range for typical corporate B‑roll, while a 70‑200 mm ([[entities/70-200mm]]) is better suited to events or distant compression shots.

## Decision flow (derived from [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]])

- If the day is **solo, controlled, and you have a full set of fast primes**, you can often skip the zoom.
- If you need **quick shifts between wide and tight framing** without rebalance time, rent the 24‑70 mm f/4.
- Opt for a **70‑200 mm** only when you anticipate long‑lens shots (e.g., distant interview subjects or event coverage).
- Consider the **strength of your IBIS** (entity [[entities/ibis]]): strong IBIS reduces the need for lens‑based stabilization.

## Summary
The Zoom Rental Decision concept ties together shoot speed, stabilization strategy, gimbal use, and aperture weight to guide whether a zoom lens rental will meaningfully improve a production.
