# Proof of Ownership

**Proof of ownership** refers to any verifiable evidence that demonstrates a claimant’s right to an item being reported in an insurance loss.  It is a critical component of the [[concepts/insurance-claim-documentation]] process because adjusters need to confirm that the damaged or missing property actually belongs to the policyholder.

## Typical Evidence Types
- **Photographs** – Images of the item in its original location (e.g., “photo from prior location”) or of the item as it appears in the storage unit after damage.
- **Receipts / Purchase Records** – Printed or digital receipts, bank‑statement purchase records, or order confirmations that show the date, vendor, and price.
- **Inventory Lists** – The storage unit’s own inventory sheet or a personal inventory document that lists the item.
- **Serial Numbers / Model Information** – When available, serial numbers or model identifiers (e.g., [[entities/samsung-tv]], [[entities/bookshelf-speakers]]) can be recorded, though the concept page notes that lack of a serial number can be mitigated by a detailed description and photo.

## Recording Proof in a Spreadsheet
The session‑39 guidance recommends a dedicated **Proof Available** column in the loss‑tracking spreadsheet.  Example entries include:
- `photo from prior location`
- `receipt uploaded`
- `bank‑statement purchase record`
- `listed on unit inventory`

Consistent labeling helps the adjuster quickly locate supporting documentation.

## Estimating Value vs. Documenting Proof
While the **Estimated Value** column should reflect the *replacement cost* (see [[concepts/replacement-cost-estimation]]), the proof column focuses solely on ownership evidence, not on valuation.

## Practical Checklist (derived from [[summaries/session-39_2025-02-13]])
1. Photograph each item from multiple angles before and after damage.
2. Capture close‑ups of damage and the surrounding storage area.
3. Gather any receipts, bank statements, or order confirmations.
4. If no receipt exists, note the source of proof (e.g., prior photo, inventory list).
5. Record the proof type in the spreadsheet’s **Proof Available** field.

By systematically collecting and documenting these items, claimants can streamline the adjuster’s review and improve the likelihood of a fair settlement.
