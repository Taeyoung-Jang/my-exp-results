# Replacement Cost Estimation

Replacement Cost Estimation is the process of determining the amount it would cost today to purchase a comparable item in similar condition, rather than the amount originally paid. This figure is what insurers typically require when assessing loss values.

## Why It Matters
- Aligns with adjuster terminology ("replacement cost" vs. original purchase price).
- Provides a fair, market‑based basis for claim payouts.
- Helps avoid under‑ or over‑valuing items, reducing disputes.

## Core Steps (as described in [[summaries/session-39_2025-02-13]])
1. **Identify the Item** – Record the category, description, and any known details (e.g., [[entities/samsung-tv]], [[entities/bookshelf-speakers]], [[entities/power-drill]].)
2. **Find a Comparable Model** – Search a major retailer or price‑comparison site for a mid‑range equivalent. Choose a model that mirrors the quality and features of the lost item.
3. **Use Current Market Price** – Record the listed price of that comparable model as the *Replacement Cost*.
4. **Document the Source** – Note where the price was obtained (online listing, store catalog, etc.) and include a screenshot or link if possible.
5. **Optional: Record Original Cost** – If you remember the exact amount paid, capture it in a separate column ("Original Cost") for reference.

## Practical Tips
- **Avoid Premium Versions** – Do not select the highest‑priced model unless you can prove you owned one; stick to a reasonable mid‑range option.
- **Use Consistent Categories** – Group items (electronics, clothing, tools, etc.) to streamline searches for comparable prices.
- **Document Evidence** – Pair the replacement cost with proof of ownership such as photos, receipts, or inventory lists (see [[concepts/proof-of-ownership]]).
- **Spreadsheet Integration** – Include both **Original Cost** and **Replacement Cost** columns in your loss inventory sheet; this prevents confusion and satisfies adjuster requests.
