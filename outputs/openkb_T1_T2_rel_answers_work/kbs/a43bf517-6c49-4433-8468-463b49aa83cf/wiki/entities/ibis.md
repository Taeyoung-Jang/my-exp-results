# In‑Body Image Stabilization (IBIS)

**Type:** product

In‑Body Image Stabilization (IBIS) refers to a camera’s internal mechanism that shifts the sensor to counteract camera shake, providing stabilization for any attached lens.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]]
- The document discusses IBIS when weighing the benefit of a zoom lens versus primes for a solo corporate documentary shoot.
- **Stabilization impact:** When the camera’s IBIS is strong, the advantage of a lens with optical stabilization (e.g., [[entities/lens-is]]) diminishes, though a zoom often still feels smoother due to its own IS system.
- **Workflow consideration:** A robust IBIS reduces the urgency to rent a zoom solely for stabilization, shifting the decision more toward speed and focal‑range convenience as outlined in [[concepts/zoom-rental-decision]].
- **Combination with lens IS:** The assistant notes that using both IBIS and lens‑based IS together yields the cleanest handheld results for slow push‑ins, especially in [[concepts/b-roll-workflow]] scenarios.
