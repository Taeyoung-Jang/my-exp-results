# Camera Gimbal

A camera gimbal is a motorized stabilization system that keeps a camera level and smooth during handheld movement. In the context of the *session‑05_2024‑06‑15* discussion, it is considered as a tool that can reduce—but not eliminate—the need for a zoom lens on solo corporate documentary shoots.

## Key points from the session
- The gimbal helps smooth handheld b‑roll, especially when paired with primes, but it does not fully replace the workflow speed advantage of a [[concepts/zoom-rental-decision]] such as renting a [[entities/24-70mm-f4]].
- When used, the gimbal lessens the benefit of lens‑based stabilization (e.g., [[entities/lens-is]]) because the device itself provides steady motion.
- Weight and balance are affected by the choice of lens; an f/4 zoom is lighter and thus easier to balance on the gimbal compared to heavier f/2.8 options.
- The decision to rent a zoom alongside a gimbal depends on the shoot’s pace and the need for quick framing changes, tying into the broader [[concepts/b-roll-workflow]].
