## Lens Optical Image Stabilization (IS)

Lens Optical Image Stabilization (IS) is a technology built into a camera lens that compensates for handheld camera shake, allowing for smoother handheld footage and sharper images at slower shutter speeds.

### Role in session‑05_2024‑06‑15
- The assistant recommends using lens IS in combination with in‑body stabilization (IBIS) for the cleanest handheld look, especially for slow push‑ins or subtle movements. [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]]
- Lens IS is highlighted as a factor when choosing between a 24‑70 mm f/4 zoom and using fast primes; its presence can make a zoom lens feel smoother compared to non‑stabilized primes.

### Related concepts and entities
- **Stabilization choice**: The decision to rely on lens IS versus IBIS is part of the overall [[concepts/stabilization-choice]].
- **Zoom rental decision**: Lens IS can influence whether a zoom rental is justified, as discussed in [[concepts/zoom-rental-decision]].
- **Associated hardware**:
  - [[entities/ibis]] – in‑body image stabilization that works alongside lens IS.
  - [[entities/lens-is]] – the same entity, provided for direct linking.

### Practical considerations
- Lens IS adds weight to the lens, affecting gimbal balance; therefore, when using a gimbal, the lighter f/4 version of a zoom may be preferred.
- In contexts with strong IBIS, the advantage of lens IS diminishes, but it still often provides smoother handheld footage.
