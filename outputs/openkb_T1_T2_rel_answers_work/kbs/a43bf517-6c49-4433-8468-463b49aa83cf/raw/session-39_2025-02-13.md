**Assistant:** You can keep the spreadsheet simple but complete. A good structure is to have one column for the item name, one for a short description, another for the estimated value, one for when and where you bought it, one for the condition before the water damage, one for the type of damage or note that it’s missing, and one for whatever proof you have—such as photos, receipts, or serial numbers.

For the proof column, it’s usually enough to note something like “receipt uploaded,” “photo attached,” or “bank statement shows purchase,” so you don’t have to hunt through folders later.

As for what to gather, think in terms of documenting the item, the condition before the incident, and the damage itself. Photograph the item from a couple of angles, the damage close‑up, and the surrounding area of the storage unit so the context is clear. Collect any receipts, emails, order confirmations, or warranty cards that show you owned the item. If something is missing entirely, photograph the empty spot or container where it should have been. Finally, keep a quick note of dates and who you spoke with, since adjusters often ask for that later.
