# Summary of Session 33 (2024‑12‑15)

This document captures a conversation about **food allergies**, **food intolerances**, and practical strategies for safely re‑introducing foods after completing **allergy immunotherapy**.

## Key Points
- **Allergy vs. Intolerance**: An allergy involves an immune response that can cause hives, swelling, breathing difficulty, or anaphylaxis, whereas an intolerance leads mainly to digestive discomfort without systemic danger. food allergy food intolerance
- **Post‑immunotherapy Re‑introduction**: After the final maintenance visit, patients are advised to start with very small portions of previously avoided foods, ideally when well‑rested, and to monitor for reactions.
- **Observation & Symptom Thresholds**: Early signs include mouth itching, mild hives, or throat tightness. Serious signs (requiring epinephrine) are swelling, breathing trouble, dizziness, or rapid symptom progression. symptom monitoring
- **Emergency Preparedness**: Even without prior anaphylaxis, keep an **epinephrine auto‑injector** nearby and have another adult present during challenges. epinephrine auto injector
- **Medications to Consider**: Antihistamines may mask early symptoms and are often advised to be avoided before a challenge; asthma control should be optimal, but inhalers are not typically withheld unless directed. medication masking
- **Patient‑Provider Communication**: A concise checklist of questions for the allergist helps obtain a personalized written protocol covering dosing, timing, symptom thresholds, medication guidance, emergency steps, and which foods need in‑office challenges. patient‑provider communication

## Checklist of Questions for the Allergist (ready to paste into a portal message)
1. **Dosing / Portion Schedule**
   - Starting portion size and incremental increases?
   - Required interval between tastes?
   - Total observation period after each dose?
2. **Observation & Symptom Thresholds**
   - Symptoms that mandate immediate stop?
   - Mild symptoms acceptable to monitor?
   - Warning signs indicating escalation?
3. **Medications**
   - Should antihistamines be avoided beforehand?
   - Any asthma or other meds to hold or take?
   - Meds to have on hand during the attempt?
4. **Epinephrine & Emergency Steps**
   - Symptoms that trigger epinephrine use?
   - When to call 911 even after epinephrine?
5. **In‑Office Challenges**
   - Which foods require clinic‑based testing?
   - How to schedule those challenges?

These items provide a framework for safe **food challenge protocol** at home and ensure clear guidance from the clinician. food challenge protocol
