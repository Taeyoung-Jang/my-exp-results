# Email Drafting

**Email Drafting** is the process of creating written messages that clearly convey intent, maintain a professional tone, and facilitate the desired response. It involves structuring the email with a purposeful subject line, a courteous greeting, concise body content, and a clear call to action.

## Core Elements

1. **Subject Line** – Summarizes the email purpose in a few words. Example from [[summaries/session-13_2025-06-15]]: *"Scheduling a Call to Review Estimate"*.
2. **Greeting** – Sets a friendly yet professional tone.
3. **Purpose Statement** – Quickly states why the email is being sent (e.g., to schedule a call, review an estimate).
4. **Specific Details** – Provides concrete information such as time slots, time zones, and any required pre‑meeting materials.
5. **Request for Action** – Asks the recipient to confirm a time, send an updated estimate, or indicate expected call length.
6. **Closing** – Ends with a polite sign‑off that reinforces willingness to collaborate.

## Applying Email Drafting in Practice

In the example from [[summaries/session-13_2025-06-15]], the email demonstrates several best‑practice techniques:

- **Clear Subject** that indicates the email’s intent.
- **Time Zone Specification** ("after 3 PM Eastern") to avoid confusion.
- **Exact Time Options** ("Wednesday at 3:30 PM ET" or "Thursday at 4 PM ET") — a recommendation drawn from [[concepts/scheduling_calls]].
- **Request for Updated Estimate** to ensure the conversation is based on the latest information.
- **Inquiry About Call Duration** to help the sender plan their schedule.
- **Professional yet Friendly Tone**, aligning with [[concepts/professional_communication]].
