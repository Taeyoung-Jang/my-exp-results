# Summary of Session 5 (2024‑06‑15)

**Topic:** Practical approach to optimizing a regular long‑distance commute.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/commute-optimization]]** – focusing on low‑effort levers (timing, route, mode) before major changes.  
- **[[concepts/timing-experiment]]** – a controlled mini‑experiment using two departure times within a 15‑20 min window.  
- **[[concepts/commute-metrics]]** – three core metrics: total travel time, variability (consistency), and post‑arrival stress (0‑5 scale).  
- **[[concepts/commute-logging-template]]** – a simple daily table for capturing data.  
- **[[concepts/stress-management]]** – adding enjoyable elements (music, podcasts, coffee stop) after basic friction points are smoothed.

## Findings & Recommendations
1. **Start with Timing** – Shifting departure by even 10‑15 min often yields the biggest perceived improvement.  
2. **Route Tweaks as Second Step** – Compare the usual path with one or two alternatives at the same adjusted times; small detours can reduce bottlenecks.  
3. **Mode Changes Later** – Consider park‑and‑ride or transit only after timing and route have been tested, as they require more planning.  
4. **Experiment Design** – Alternate two departure times (usual vs. earlier/later) at least twice each within a week to control for day‑to‑day noise.  
5. **Metrics to Track**
   - *Total minutes door‑to‑door* – raw efficiency.  
   - *Variability* – consistency across runs.  
   - *Stress on arrival* – subjective quality‑of‑life indicator (0 = none, 5 = extreme).  
   - Fuel cost is a secondary note; it rarely changes with small timing shifts.  
6. **Data Sufficiency** – Two‑to‑three runs per departure time (≈ 1 week of alternating trials) is usually enough to identify the smoother slot.  
7. **Enjoyment Add‑Ons** – Once friction is reduced, introduce pleasant activities (music, podcasts, a short walk, coffee) to transform the commute into a neutral or positive experience.
