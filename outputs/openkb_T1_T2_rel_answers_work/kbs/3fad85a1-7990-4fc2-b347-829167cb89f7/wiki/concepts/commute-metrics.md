# Commute Metrics

**Definition**: The primary quantitative and qualitative indicators used to assess how well a daily commute performs across time, reliability, and well‑being.

---

## Core Metrics (as outlined in [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]])

1. **Total travel time** – Measured in minutes from door‑to‑door.  This captures the raw efficiency of the commute and is the easiest figure to record.
2. **Variability (consistency)** – The day‑to‑day spread of travel time.  Low variability means the commute is predictable, which is often more valuable than a marginally faster but erratic trip.
3. **Stress level** – A subjective rating (0–5) taken immediately upon arrival.  It reflects the quality‑of‑life impact and is the most direct proxy for commuter well‑being.
4. **Fuel cost (optional)** – Although not a primary focus for short timing tweaks, noting fuel consumption can be useful when evaluating larger changes such as mode swaps.

---

## Why These Metrics Matter
- **Efficiency vs. Predictability**: A commute that saves a few minutes but fluctuates wildly can increase anxiety.  Balancing total time with low variability yields a smoother experience.
- **Well‑being Focus**: Stress level captures the human side of commuting, aligning with the broader goal of making travel feel "easy" rather than exhausting.
- **Decision‑Making**: By tracking these three core metrics during a timing experiment (see [[concepts/timing-experiment]]), commuters can objectively decide which departure slot or route offers the best overall experience.

---

## Practical Application
The document proposes a simple daily logging template (see [[concepts/commute-logging-template]]) that records each metric:
```
Date:
Departure time:
Arrival time:
Total minutes door‑to‑door:
Traffic impression (light / normal / heavy):
Stress on arrival (0–5):
Anything unusual today:
``` 
Collecting two‑to‑three entries per departure time provides enough data to compare average travel time, variability, and stress, allowing a quick determination of the optimal schedule.
