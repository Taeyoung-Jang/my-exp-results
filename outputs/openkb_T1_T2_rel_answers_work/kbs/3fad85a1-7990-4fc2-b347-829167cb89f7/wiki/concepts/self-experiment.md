# Self-Experiment

**Definition**
A *self‑experiment* is a brief, low‑stakes personal trial where you deliberately change a habit or schedule—such as starting work earlier—and observe the effects on key performance indicators. It is designed to gather objective data without large commitments, allowing you to decide whether a change should become permanent.

**Core Steps**
1. **Set a clear variable** – e.g., shift the start time of your workday by 30–60 minutes.
2. **Define metrics** – use a concise set of [[concepts/productivity-metrics]] such as sleep quality, morning focus, meeting effectiveness, stress level, and energy dips.
3. **Log daily** – record data each day using a quick template (e.g., a one‑line rating system) to keep the process lightweight.
4. **Run for a fixed period** – typically two weeks, enough to capture patterns but short enough to avoid fatigue.
5. **Analyze trends** – compare pre‑ and post‑experiment values, looking for upward movement in positive signals and stability in stress or dips.

**Relation to Timing Experiments**
A self‑experiment is a specific form of a broader [[concepts/timing-experiment]], focusing on the timing of daily activities. While timing experiments can involve multiple variables, the self‑experiment isolates a single change (start time) for clearer attribution.

**Key Benefits**
- Minimal time investment for data collection.
- Provides objective, quantifiable evidence rather than gut feeling.
- Flexible: can be applied to sleep, commute, meeting schedules, or other routines.
