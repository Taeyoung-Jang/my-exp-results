# Timing Experiment

**Definition**: A timing experiment is a short, controlled study where a commuter—or any routine‑based individual—tests two (or more) departure or start times within a limited window (e.g., 15–20 minutes) to determine which results in lower travel time, less variability, reduced stress, and higher overall productivity.

## Core Elements
- **Controlled Alternation**: Alternate the chosen times every other day (or in a balanced sequence) to reduce the impact of random factors like accidents, weather, or fluctuating energy levels.
- **Minimum Trials**: Conduct at least two runs for each time slot, typically over a one‑week period, to gather enough data for a reliable comparison.
- **Metrics Tracked**:
  - Total door‑to‑door minutes
  - Variability (consistency across runs)
  - Post‑arrival stress (0‑5 rating)
  - Optional notes on traffic conditions or unusual events
  - **Productivity signals** (when applied to work start times): sleep quality, wake‑up feeling, early‑day focus, midday energy dip, afternoon stamina, meeting effectiveness, overall stress, and perception of the routine as natural or forced. These align with the broader [[concepts/productivity-metrics]] framework.

These metrics align with the broader [[concepts/commute-metrics]] framework and can be complemented by personal productivity metrics when the experiment targets work‑day timing.

## Procedure (Based on [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]] and [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-13]])
1. **Select Times**: Choose your usual departure/start time and one alternative that is 15–20 minutes earlier or later.
2. **Schedule Runs**: Alternate the two times across the week (e.g., Monday‑usual, Tuesday‑alternative, Wednesday‑usual, etc.).
3. **Log Data**:
   - For commute‑focused runs, use the simple daily log template from [[concepts/commute-logging-template]].
   - For work‑day timing runs, adopt the one‑line daily log template suggested in session‑39: 
     `SleepQ _ /5 | Wake _ /5 | AM Focus _ /5 | Midday Dip Y/N | PM Stamina _ /5 | Early Mtgs _ /5 | Stress _ /5 | Natural/Forced: _`
4. **Analyze**:
   - Compute average travel time (or productivity ratings) for each slot.
   - Assess variability (standard deviation or range).
   - Compare average stress scores and, for work‑day tests, look for upward trends in focus, meeting effectiveness, and “natural vs forced” ratings while ensuring stress and energy dips do not rise.
   - Follow the simple trend‑interpretation rule from the self‑experiment guidance: upward or stable positive indicators suggest keeping the earlier start; declining focus, more dips, higher stress, or frequent “forced” entries advise reverting.
5. **Decision**: Choose the departure/start time that consistently offers the lowest combined stress and variability (and improves productivity metrics when relevant), even if it isn’t the absolute fastest.
