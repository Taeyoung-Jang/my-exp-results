# Productivity Metrics

**Definition**: A collection of concise, numeric indicators used to evaluate how changes to daily scheduling—such as an earlier work start time—affect an individual’s productivity, energy, and stress levels.

## Core Indicators (from [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-13]])
| Metric | Description | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep quality | Perceived restfulness of the previous night | 1‑5 |
| Wake‑up feeling | How refreshed you feel upon rising | 1‑5 |
| AM Focus | Ability to concentrate during the first two work hours | 1‑5 |
| Midday energy dip | Presence of a noticeable slump (yes/no, optional note) | Y/N |
| PM stamina | Sustained energy and focus in the afternoon | 1‑5 |
| Early‑meeting effectiveness | How productive early meetings feel | 1‑5 |
| Overall stress | General stress level for the day | 1‑5 |
| Natural/forced perception | Whether the new routine feels natural or forced (text) | – |

These metrics are purpose‑built for a short‑term **self‑experiment** to test a timing shift, aligning with the [[concepts/self-experiment]] approach.

## How to Use
1. **Daily logging** – Fill the one‑line template each day (see the source summary).  
2. **Rating consistency** – Use the 1‑5 Likert scale to keep data comparable across days.  
3. **Trend analysis** – After a two‑week period, look for upward trends in AM Focus, Early‑meeting effectiveness, and the Natural/forced perception while monitoring that Stress and Midday dip frequencies do not increase.

## Interpretation Rule
- **Keep earlier start**: Majority of positive metrics (AM Focus, Early‑meeting effectiveness, Natural) show stable or improving scores, and Stress/energy dips stay flat or decline.
- **Revert**: Noticeable drops in focus, rising stress scores, more frequent midday dips, or a predominance of “forced” entries.
