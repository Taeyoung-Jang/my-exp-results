# Recovery Adjustments

**Definition**: Practical strategies for altering workout intensity, volume, or frequency in response to signs of fatigue or overreaching, ensuring long‑term progress and injury prevention.

## Core Principles

1. **Monitor Early Indicators**
   - Decreased performance on lifts or swim sets
   - Elevated perceived exertion (RPE) despite unchanged load
   - Mood, motivation, or sleep disturbances
2. **Adjust Load Before Stagnation**
   - Reduce weight by one increment (e.g., 5 %)
   - Drop a primary lift from the session
   - Convert a strength day to mobility‑only work
3. **Modulate Frequency**
   - Swap a heavy strength day for a lighter technique session
   - Insert an extra rest or active‑recovery day (e.g., gentle walk)
4. **Tailor Cardio Intensity**
   - Lower swim effort from moderate to easy on the fatigued day
   - Reschedule optional easy cardio to a non‑training day
5. **Progressive Re‑introduction**
   - After a recovery week, gradually restore original load and volume over 1–2 weeks.

## Application in Session 10 (2025‑06‑16)

The weekly template in [[summaries/session-10_2025-06-16]] includes explicit **adjustment cues**:
- If feeling worn down, *dial back* Tuesday and Saturday lifts (lighter loads or fewer sets).
- Turn Thursday’s lighter strength day into pure mobility if needed.
- Reduce swim intensity on Wednesday or Friday when swim‑related fatigue appears.
- Optional easy cardio can be moved from Thursday to Sunday to create more recovery space.

These steps embody the principles above, offering a clear, actionable path for maintaining a balanced training load while preventing burnout.
