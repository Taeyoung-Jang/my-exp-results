# Effort Target (RPE / RIR)

**What it is**
- **RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)**: a subjective scale (typically 1‑10) that rates how hard a set feels.
- **RIR (Reps‑In‑Reserve)**: the number of repetitions you could still complete with good form before failure.

Both metrics provide a way to quantify effort without relying on exact percentages of a one‑rep max, making them adaptable to daily fluctuations in strength, fatigue, or swim performance.

**How it is applied in the weekly template**
- The strength sessions in [[summaries/session-10_2025-06-16]] prescribe loads using RIR ranges (e.g., *1‑2 RIR* for heavy compound lifts, *2‑3 RIR* for accessories, *3‑4 RIR* for light technique work).
- This guides the lifter to select a weight that feels challenging yet leaves a few reps “in the tank,” supporting sustainable progression.

**Typical RIR targets**
| Training Day | Main Lift RIR | Accessory / Light Lift RIR |
|--------------|---------------|----------------------------|
| Tuesday (heavy) | 1‑2 RIR (5‑8 reps) | 2‑3 RIR (8‑12 reps) |
| Thursday (light) | 3‑4 RIR (8‑12 reps) | 3‑4 RIR (10‑15 reps) |
| Saturday (moderate) | 1‑2 RIR (4‑6 reps) | 2‑3 RIR (6‑10 reps) |

**Why use RPE/RIR**
- **Flexibility**: Adjusts for day‑to‑day variations in energy, sleep, or swim fatigue.
- **Recovery‑friendly**: By leaving reps in reserve, the athlete reduces cumulative fatigue, which aligns with the overall [[concepts/balanced_training_schedule]] and supports the [[concepts/recovery_adjustments]] strategy outlined in the plan.
- **Progress tracking**: Over weeks, a consistent RIR can be lowered (e.g., moving from 2‑3 RIR to 1‑2 RIR) to indicate strength gains without needing formal testing.

**Practical tips**
1. After each set, ask yourself how many more quality reps you could have performed.
2. Use a simple notebook or app to note the RIR chosen and any deviations (e.g., “2 RIR – felt easy”).
3. When feeling unusually fatigued (e.g., after a hard swim), increase RIR by 1–2 to protect recovery.

**Linkage to other concepts**
- The effort targeting system is a core component of a [[concepts/balanced_training_schedule]].
- It provides a concrete lever for implementing [[concepts/recovery_adjustments]] when signs of overreaching appear.
