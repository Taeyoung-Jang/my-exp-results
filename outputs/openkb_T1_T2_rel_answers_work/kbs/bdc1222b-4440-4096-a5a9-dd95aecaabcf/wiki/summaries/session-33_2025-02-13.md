# Summary of session-33_2025-02-13

**Topic:** Practical privacy and security hardening for an Apple ecosystem after consolidating data to iCloud.

---

## Overview
The user has moved photos, notes, and passwords to their Apple ID and now wants a systematic review of settings to avoid lock‑outs and exposure. The assistant provides a concise checklist, explains the trade‑offs between a **recovery contact** and a **recovery key**, and details how the contact can view their role and how to safely change trusted phone numbers.

---

## Key Concepts
- **Strong, unique password**  
- **Two‑Factor Authentication** (2FA)  
- **Trusted phone numbers**  
- **Account recovery options** – recovery contact vs. recovery key [[concepts/account-recovery-options]]  
- **iCloud sync & encryption** [[concepts/icloud-sync-encryption]]  
- **Device list management**

## Step‑by‑step Checklist
### macOS (System Settings)
1. **Password & 2FA** – Settings ► *your name* ► **Sign‑In & Security** → verify password strength, ensure 2FA is on.  
2. **Trusted numbers** – Same section ► **Two‑Factor Authentication** ► **Trusted Phone Numbers** → add/remove.  
3. **Signed‑in devices** – Settings ► *your name* ► scroll to device list → remove unknown devices.  
4. **Recovery contact** – Settings ► *your name* ► **Sign‑In & Security** ► **Account Recovery** → add contact [[entities/recovery-contact]].  
5. **Recovery key** – Same area ► toggle **Recovery Key** (optional) – store safely [[entities/recovery-key]].  
6. **iCloud sync** – Settings ► *your name* ► **iCloud** → toggle categories (Photos, Keychain, Notes, etc.).  
7. **iCloud backup (Mac)** – Not a full device backup; ensure key categories are enabled.

### iPadOS (Settings app)
1. **Password & 2FA** – Settings ► *your name* ► **Sign‑In & Security** → verify.  
2. **Trusted numbers** – Same path ► **Two‑Factor Authentication** ► edit numbers.  
3. **Signed‑in devices** – Settings ► *your name* ► device list → remove.  
4. **Recovery contact** – Settings ► *your name* ► **Sign‑In & Security** ► **Account Recovery** → add contact [[entities/recovery-contact]].  
5. **Recovery key** – Same area ► enable if desired [[entities/recovery-key]].  
6. **iCloud sync** – Settings ► *your name* ► **iCloud** → toggle services.  
7. **iCloud Backup** – Settings ► *your name* ► **iCloud** ► **iCloud Backup** → enable and confirm recent backup.

---

## Recovery Contact vs. Recovery Key
- **Recovery contact** – Low‑risk, easy to change, provides a verification code during lock‑out flow. Recommended for most users.  
- **Recovery key** – Powerful but unforgiving; loss = permanent lock‑out. Use only if you can store it securely (e.g., printed, encrypted vault).  
- **Suggested setup:** Keep trusted phone number current, add a single trusted recovery contact [[entities/recovery-contact]], and **skip** the recovery key unless you have a reliable storage method.

---

## How the Recovery Contact Sees the Request
1. On their iPhone/iPad: Settings ► *their name* ► **Sign‑In & Security** ► **Account Recovery** – your name appears under “People you’re helping.”  
2. On their Mac: System Settings ► *their name* ► **Sign‑In & Security** ► **Account Recovery** – same view.  
3. When you need help, they tap your name, receive a short verification code from Apple, and read it to you. You then enter that code during the recovery process.

---

## Updating Trusted Phone Numbers Safely
1. **Add** the new number first while the old one still works.  
2. Verify the new number via the code sent by Apple.  
3. **Remove** the old number.  

This ordering ensures you never have zero trusted numbers, avoiding lock‑out loops.

---

## Takeaways
- Perform the checklist on each device regularly.  
- Prefer a recovery contact over a recovery key for typical users.  
- Verify the contact’s visibility and test the flow by confirming the contact’s screen.  
- Update trusted numbers by adding then removing to keep account access uninterrupted.
