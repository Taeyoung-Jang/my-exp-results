## Summary of session-05_2025-01-23  

The conversation offers practical recommendations for reducing the stark silence of a small home office while keeping distractions minimal.

### Key suggestions
- **Background sound**: Use low‑volume white‑ or brown‑noise apps (e.g., [[entities/dark_noise]], [[entities/noisli]], [[entities/endel]]) that allow EQ fine‑tuning and avoid noticeable loops. Brown noise is noted for better masking of sharp sounds like a ticking clock. See also [[concepts/white_noise_vs_brown_noise]] and [[concepts/background_sound_masking]].
- **Desk fan**: A small fan adds a steady hum, improves airflow, and provides subtle acoustic masking without visual clutter.
- **Soft furnishings**: Prioritize a **low‑pile rug** as the most impactful first step; it absorbs echo while allowing casters to roll. Preferred materials include flat‑weave wool or dense synthetic fibers. This contributes to [[concepts/acoustic_dampening]]. Curtains are secondary and only effective with sizable windows; acoustic panels are more noticeable and best if fabric‑wrapped.
- **Chair mat**: Avoid hard plastic mats on a low‑pile rug, as they reflect sound and negate the rug’s dampening effect.

### Practical details
- **Rug size**: Cover the front half of the desk and chair area to maximize sound absorption.
- **App sound mixes**: Choose steady broadband sounds such as soft fan, light rain, or brown noise (in [[entities/dark_noise]]) and gentle wind or subdued café murmur (in [[entities/noisli]]) at low volume.
- **EQ control**: Adjust tone layers to keep the sound “invisible” over long periods.
