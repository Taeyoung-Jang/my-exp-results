# Summary of Session 33 (2025‑04‑24)

**Main focus:** Practical advice for a first‑time visitor to a bustling street‑food market, covering how to prioritize atmosphere, pick stalls, and order efficiently in noisy settings.

## Key Points

1. **Start with atmosphere** – Take a slow lap around the market’s perimeter to get a feel for the vibe before committing to any stall. This helps you spot attractive stalls later. [[concepts/atmosphere_immersion]]
2. **“One slow lap” strategy** – Walk the outer edge first to avoid tight crowds, pause, and note recurring aromas. [[concepts/market_navigation]]
3. **Choosing the first stall** – Trust the scent that catches your attention twice; follow the “double‑smell” cue. [[concepts/scent_guided_selection]]
4. **Green flags for stall quality**
   - A steady, relaxed line of customers.
   - Vendor moves calmly and confidently.
   - Fresh‑looking ingredients, no puddles of sauce. [[concepts/green_flags_stall_quality]]
5. **Red flags to avoid**
   - Messy, sticky prep areas.
   - Food sitting under heat lamps for too long.
   - Confused or dissatisfied customers. [[concepts/red_flags_stall_quality]]
6. **Tie‑breaker when stalls look equal** – Favor the stall where locals repeatedly order the same dish; a small, clear menu or cooking‑to‑order are secondary cues. [[concepts/tie_breaker_local_preference]]
7. **Ordering quickly in a loud market** – Mimic the rhythm of the person ahead, point to the desired item, hold up the number with fingers, and have payment ready. A smile and nod smooth the interaction. [[concepts/noise_resilient_ordering]]
