# Scent‑Guided Selection

**Definition**: A decision‑making heuristic for navigating crowded street‑food markets that relies on noticing a scent twice—first when it catches your attention, then again from a different angle—to identify a stall worth trying.

## How It Works
1. **Initial lap** – While performing a slow, outer‑edge lap (see [[concepts/market_navigation]]), stay alert to any aromas that stand out.
2. **Double‑smell cue** – If you encounter the same smell, pause, continue walking, and notice if the scent reappears from another direction. This repetition signals a potentially high‑quality offering.
3. **Prioritization** – Use the scent cue to select the first stall, before evaluating other visual green or red flags.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Complements [[concepts/atmosphere_immersion]] by encouraging you to soak in the market’s vibe before making a choice.
- Works alongside visual **green flags** (steady line, confident vendor, fresh ingredients) and **red flags** (messy prep area, stale food) as described in [[concepts/green_flags_stall_quality]] and [[concepts/red_flags_stall_quality]].
- Serves as a tie‑breaker when stalls appear equal, similar to the logic in [[concepts/tie_breaker_local_preference]].
- Helps reduce decision overload, which can be mitigated further with [[concepts/noise_resilient_ordering]] techniques in noisy environments.
