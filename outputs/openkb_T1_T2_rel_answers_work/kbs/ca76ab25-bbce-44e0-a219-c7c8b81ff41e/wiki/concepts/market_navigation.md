## Market Navigation

**Definition**: Market Navigation refers to the set of strategies and heuristics used to move through bustling street‑food markets while minimizing overwhelm, locating quality stalls, and preserving the enjoyment of the atmosphere.

### Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-04-24]])

1. **Outer‑edge lap** – Begin by walking the perimeter of the market. This keeps you out of the densest crowds, lets you map stall locations, and provides a calm entry point.
2. **Atmosphere immersion** – Before committing to any food, spend a few minutes soaking up the sights, sounds, and smells to establish a mental map of the space. See also [[concepts/atmosphere_immersion]].
3. **Scent‑guided selection** – Pay attention to aromas that repeat as you circulate. A scent that catches you twice signals a promising stall. Related concept: [[concepts/scent_guided_selection]].
4. **Visual green flags** – Look for a steady, relaxed line, confident vendor movements, and fresh ingredients. These visual cues help decide which stalls to prioritize. See [[concepts/green_flags_stall_quality]].
5. **Visual red flags** – Avoid stalls with sticky, chaotic prep areas, food sitting under heat lamps, or confused customers. See [[concepts/red_flags_stall_quality]].
6. **Tie‑breaker criteria** – When multiple stalls appear equal, favor the one where locals repeatedly order the same dish. This leverages community preference. See [[concepts/tie_breaker_local_preference]].
7. **Noise‑resilient ordering** – In loud environments, copy the rhythm of the person ahead, point to the desired item, hold up the quantity with fingers, and have payment ready. This reduces friction and keeps the line moving. See [[concepts/noise_resilient_ordering]].

### Practical Walk‑through
1. **Enter** the market, locate the outer boundary, and start a slow lap.
2. **Observe** crowd density, stall layouts, and recurring scents.
3. **Mark** stalls with appealing aromas; if a smell repeats, note it for a later revisit.
4. **Assess** each promising stall using green/red flag criteria.
5. **If** two stalls pass the visual test, apply the tie‑breaker based on local patronage.
6. **Approach** the chosen stall using the noise‑resilient ordering steps.
