## Red Flags for Stall Quality

**Definition**: Observable signs that a street‑food stall is likely to offer sub‑par or unsafe food, prompting you to skip it.

### Key Red Flags (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-04-24]])
1. **Messy, sticky, or neglected prep area** – Surfaces that look dirty rather than simply busy suggest poor hygiene.
2. **Food sitting under heat lamps for too long** – Stagnant, overly warm dishes may be past their optimal freshness.
3. **Confused or dissatisfied customers** – Patrons who leave looking unhappy or who seem unsure about what they received signal a problem with the stall’s service or product.

### How These Flags Interact with Other Concepts
- Contrast these red flags with the **[[concepts/green_flags_stall_quality]]** to make quick quality decisions.
- Use the **[[concepts/market_navigation]]** strategy to scan stalls efficiently and spot red flags early.
- While prioritizing **[[concepts/atmosphere_immersion]]**, keep an eye out for these visual warnings before committing.
- The presence of red flags can outweigh **[[concepts/scent_guided_selection]]** cues; a great smell does not compensate for obvious hygiene issues.
- When deciding between similar stalls, consider **[[concepts/tie_breaker_local_preference]]** only after confirming no red flags are present.
- In noisy environments, the **[[concepts/noise_resilient_ordering]]** approach helps you act quickly once a red flag is identified.
