# White Noise vs Brown Noise

White noise and brown noise are two common types of broadband sound used for [[concepts/background_sound_masking]]. Both aim to reduce the perception of distracting, sharp noises (like a ticking clock) by introducing a continuous auditory backdrop, but they differ in spectral characteristics and perceived comfort.

## Spectral Characteristics
- **White noise** contains equal energy across all audible frequencies, resulting in a high‑frequency “hissy” quality.
- **Brown noise** (also called red noise) emphasizes lower frequencies, producing a deeper, smoother sound that lacks the high‑frequency hiss of white noise.

## Masking Effectiveness
- The deeper spectrum of brown noise tends to **mask sharp, high‑frequency sounds** more effectively, making it preferable when the goal is to hide ticking clocks or similar clicks.
- White noise can still mask a broad range of sounds but may feel more intrusive over long periods because of its bright, high‑frequency content.

## User Preference
- Preference is largely personal. Some find brown noise more soothing for extended listening, while others may prefer the neutrality of white noise.
- In practice, listeners often choose the version that feels least noticeable during the tasks they are performing.

## Practical Application in Home Office Settings
- In the conversation captured in [[summaries/session-05_2025-01-23]], brown noise was recommended as the better choice for masking the ticking clock in a quiet home office.
- Both noise types can be generated with fine‑tuned EQ controls in apps such as [[entities/dark_noise]], [[entities/noisli]], and [[entities/endel]], allowing users to adjust the balance to their comfort level.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- Using either noise type contributes to overall [[concepts/acoustic_dampening]] by filling the acoustic space and reducing perceived echo.
- Selecting the appropriate noise profile is part of a broader strategy for creating comfortable background sound environments.
