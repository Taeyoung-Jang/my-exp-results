# Tower of London

The Tower of London is a historic castle on the north bank of the River Thames in [[entities/london]]. It serves as a museum, historic site, and former royal palace, attracting millions of visitors each year.

## Relevance to session‑08_2023-01-15
In the planning conversation for a day‑out that requires frequent sit‑breaks, the Tower of London was offered as the optional "must‑see" stop after visiting the [[entities/british-museum]] and [[entities/borough-market]]. The assistant highlighted two main queue‑related pain points:

1. **Security check** before entering the grounds.
2. **Main entry queue** for ticket scanning.

To minimise standing time, the advice was to:
- Pre‑book a **timed‑entry ticket** (reduces queue length).
- Inform staff of the need for seated waiting; they can direct visitors to a bench near the entrance.
- Use the **accessibility‑queue‑management** strategies described in [[concepts/accessibility-queue-management]].

Inside the Tower, seating is limited, so the recommendation is to plan a short circuit of the exhibition areas and then rest on the benches along the river‑side outer wall.

## Practical tips from the itinerary
- **Transport:** Take the Jubilee line from [[entities/london-bridge-station]] to [[entities/tower-of-london]] (station name: Tower Hill).
- **Timing:** Arrive around 14:00–14:30 after lunch at Borough Market; allocate roughly one hour on site, with a brief sit‑break on the river‑side bench.
- **Queue mitigation:** Use timed tickets and request seated waiting; staff are accustomed to providing alternative waiting areas for visitors with mobility limitations.
