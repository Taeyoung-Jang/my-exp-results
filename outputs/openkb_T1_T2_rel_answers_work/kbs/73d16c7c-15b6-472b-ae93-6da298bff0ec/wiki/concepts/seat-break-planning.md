# Seat‑Break Planning

Seat‑Break Planning is the practice of deliberately inserting short, scheduled rest periods—typically every 45–60 minutes—into a day‑out itinerary to prevent fatigue and accommodate mobility needs.  The approach combines three core elements:

1. **Timing** – Divide activities into blocks of roughly an hour, then schedule a 5–10 minute sit‑break before fatigue sets in.  This rhythm keeps energy levels steady and reduces the need for long recovery pauses later.
2. **Venue selection** – Choose stops that offer reliable seating such as museum cafés, atriums, market food‑halls, or river‑bank benches.  When planning a route, map out the nearest rest point for each activity block so you never travel more than a few minutes away from a place to sit.
3. **Preparation** – Pack lightweight comfort items (comfortable shoes, layered clothing, a small water bottle) and optional aids like a portable seat cushion.  See [[concepts/comfort-packing]] for packing details.

### How it was applied in the source document
In the London example from [[summaries/session-08_2023-01-15]], Seat‑Break Planning shaped a concrete schedule:
- **British Museum** – first sit‑break at the Great Court Café (10:45), second at the Great Court benches (11:30).
- **Borough Market** – sit‑break in the Borough Market Kitchen indoor seating (12:30) and a second pause at a nearby coffee shop (13:30).
- **South Bank** – rests at the Tate Modern foyer (14:15), Royal Festival Hall (15:10), and a riverside café (16:00).
- **Tower of London** – uses timed tickets to minimise queue standing and asks staff for a bench‑side waiting area, a tactic that aligns with [[concepts/accessibility-queue-management]].

### Benefits
- **Reduced physical strain** – regular pauses keep joints (e.g., arthritis) from being over‑used.
- **Improved enjoyment** – shorter, more frequent rests make museums, markets, and sightseeing feel less like a marathon.
- **Flexibility** – the plan can be adjusted on‑the‑fly; if a venue is unexpectedly crowded, you can shift the next sit‑break to the nearest café or bench.
