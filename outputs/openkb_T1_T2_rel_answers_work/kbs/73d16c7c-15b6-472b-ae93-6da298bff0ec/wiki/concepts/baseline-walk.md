# Baseline Walk

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** A low‑effort daily walking habit that serves as an easy anchor for consistency.

## Overview
The *baseline walk* is a short, minimally demanding walking session—typically 10–15 minutes—that acts as a reliable, low‑pressure anchor in a walking habit. Its primary purpose is to make it virtually impossible to miss a day, thereby reinforcing habit identity and streak continuity.

## Key Elements
- **Ease of execution** – The baseline should feel so simple that skipping feels unreasonable.
- **Consistency over volume** – Prioritizes showing up every day rather than meeting strict distance or time targets.
- **Flexibility** – On low‑energy or sore days, the baseline can be further reduced (e.g., a 2–3 minute “recovery walk”).
- **Bonus walks** – Longer walks are treated as optional, enjoyable extensions rather than obligations.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Supports [[concepts/habit-formation]] by providing a concrete, repeatable action that builds routine.
- Connects with [[concepts/recovery-walk]] as a scaled‑down version for days when energy is low.

## Practical Guidance
1. **Set a minimal time** – Aim for roughly ten minutes of easy walking.
2. **Choose a pleasant route** – Pick a park, tree‑lined street, or familiar path to make the walk enjoyable.
3. **Anchor to an existing cue** – Link the walk to an existing daily activity (e.g., after morning coffee).
4. **Treat longer walks as bonuses** – When motivation is high, extend the walk naturally.
5. **Adapt on tough days** – Reduce to a short stroll or a few minutes of walking around the block to keep the habit alive.
