# Accessibility & Queue Management

**Definition**: Strategies and resources that help visitors who cannot stand for long periods navigate entry lines and waiting areas with minimal discomfort.

---

## Core Principles
1. **Advance Research** – Before visiting, check the venue’s website for sections titled *Accessibility*, *Visitor Information*, or *Mobility*. Look for keywords such as “timed entry”, “priority entry”, “alternative queuing”, “seating in waiting area”, or “limited‑mobility access”.
2. **Timed‑Entry Tickets** – Purchasing a timed‑entry ticket is often the most effective way to skip or reduce the main queue. It assigns a specific arrival window, allowing you to wait elsewhere or arrive just before your slot.
3. **Communicate Needs On‑Site** – When you arrive, politely inform staff of your limitation (e.g., “I have difficulty standing for long periods”). Staff at most UK attractions can direct you to a bench‑side waiting area, provide a seated waiting pass, or let you step out of line and return when called.
4. **Identify Rest Points** – Map out nearby cafés, lobbies, or public benches where you can sit while waiting. In the London itinerary, these include the Great Court café at the [[entities/british-museum]], the indoor seating at the Borough Market Kitchen, and benches along the River Thames near the [[entities/tower-of-london]] and [[entities/south-bank]].
5. **Leverage Accessibility Passes** – Some venues offer specific passes for visitors with mobility challenges, granting early entry or dedicated seating in queues.

---

## Application to the Sample Itinerary (see [[summaries/session-08_2023-01-15]])
- **British Museum** – Use the Great Court café or scattered benches for the first sit‑break. The museum’s website lists a *Mobility* page that details timed‑entry slots and seating options.
- **Borough Market** – The market’s website notes that the adjacent food hall provides indoor seating; staff can suggest nearby cafés if the main area is crowded.
- **Tower of London** – The biggest queue risk points are the security check and ticket scanning. By pre‑booking a timed ticket and requesting a seated waiting area from staff, you can avoid long standing periods.
- **South Bank Walk** – The promenade naturally offers frequent benches; however, checking the Transport for London (TfL) site for accessibility notes on station lifts (e.g., at [[entities/london-bridge-station]]) ensures smooth transfers.
