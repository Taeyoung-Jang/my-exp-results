# Recovery Walk

**Definition**: A *recovery walk* is a brief, gentle stroll—often just 2–3 minutes—taken on days when you feel sore, fatigued, or otherwise low on energy. Its purpose is to maintain the rhythm of a walking habit without adding physical strain.

## Core Elements
- **Length**: Significantly shorter than the regular [[concepts/baseline-walk]] (e.g., 2–3 minutes or a quick lap around the block).
- **Intensity**: Low‑pace, relaxed, and focused on movement rather than exertion.
- **Purpose**: Acts as a *recovery version* of the habit, ensuring consistency and preserving the identity of "someone who walks every day."

## How It Relates to Habit Formation
- Supports [[concepts/habit-formation]] by providing a low‑pressure anchor when the usual baseline is too demanding.
- Helps avoid missing days, which can weaken habit streaks and motivation.
- Encourages a flexible mindset: the habit adapts to body signals rather than forcing a rigid routine.

## Practical Tips (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-01-15]])
1. **Scale Down**: On a sore day, replace the standard 10‑15 minute baseline with a 2‑3 minute gentle walk.
2. **Keep It Simple**: A short stroll around the block, a few laps in a nearby park, or even stepping outside for a quick breath are sufficient.
3. **Mindset Shift**: Treat the recovery walk as a *mini‑habit*—a quick, intentional pause that still counts toward your daily walking streak.
4. **Gradual Return**: After a recovery walk, you can naturally extend the duration if you feel better, without feeling forced.

## Benefits
- Maintains **consistency** without overtaxing the body.
- Reduces the risk of **habit abandonment** caused by occasional low‑energy periods.
- Provides a mental cue that the walking habit is adaptable and forgiving.

## When to Use a Recovery Walk
- After intense physical activity that leaves you sore.
- On days with high mental or physical fatigue.
- When injury or illness limits your ability to engage in longer walks, but mobility is still possible.
