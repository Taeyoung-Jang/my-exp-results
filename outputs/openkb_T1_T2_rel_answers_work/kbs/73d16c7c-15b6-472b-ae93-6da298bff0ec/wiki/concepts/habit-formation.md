# Habit Formation

**Definition**: The process of forming walking habits using cues, consistency, and low‑pressure baselines.

## Core Elements

1. **Cue Integration** – Pair walks with enjoyable activities (e.g., podcasts, music) so the walk becomes a treat rather than a chore.
2. **Consistency Over Rigid Targets** – Emphasize a daily routine (“walk every day”) instead of strict distance or time goals; flexibility helps the habit endure.
3. **Low‑Pressure Baseline** – Establish a minimal, easy-to‑meet walk (e.g., 10‑15 minutes). This baseline acts as a *anchor* that is hard to miss, reinforcing the identity of “someone who walks daily.” [[concepts/baseline-walk]]
4. **Recovery Adaptation** – On sore or low‑energy days, shrink the baseline to a very short “recovery walk” (2‑3 minutes or a brief lap) to keep the rhythm without strain. [[concepts/recovery-walk]]
5. **Identity Framing** – View the habit as part of personal identity; labeling oneself as a daily walker boosts motivation and reduces resistance.

## Practical Tips from Session 37

- **Attach Enjoyable Media**: Listen to a favorite podcast or playlist while walking.
- **Vary Routes**: Choose pleasant settings like parks or tree‑lined streets to keep walks fresh.
- **Anchor to Existing Routines**: Walk right after morning coffee or during a natural break.
- **Treat Longer Walks as Bonuses**: Once the baseline is reliable, let extended walks happen naturally rather than forcing them.
- **Use Low‑Pressure Anchors**: Ensure the baseline is so easy it feels impossible to skip.
