# End‑of‑Day Scan

**Definition**: A short (~1 minute) nightly routine where you quickly review the day's pocket‑notebook page, move time‑sensitive items to a calendar, and mark items that need further attention.

## Core Steps
1. **Flip to the day's page** – Open the notebook to the most recent entry.
2. **Identify actionable items** – Look for appointments, deadlines, or follow‑up tasks.
3. **Transfer to calendar** – Enter any time‑bound information into a digital or paper calendar.
4. **Mark for later** – Use the edge‑marking technique (tiny dot or slash on the page edge) to flag items you want to revisit.
5. **Close the notebook** – Finish the scan and set the notebook aside.

## Why It Works
- **Low friction**: Takes about a minute, fitting easily into evening routines.
- **Externalizes memory**: Moves critical details out of the brain and onto reliable external systems (calendar, edge marks).
- **Prevents backlog**: Regularly clears the notebook of items that could otherwise pile up and be forgotten.

## Practical Tips from Session 32 (2025‑02‑15)
- Pair the scan with **edge‑marking** while you write; the mark becomes the visual cue during the scan, eliminating extra steps.
- Keep the notebook **chronological** and avoid complex indexing; the scan compensates for lack of structure.
- For users with memory challenges (e.g., post‑concussion), the combined habit of marking and scanning strengthens both capture and retrieval.
