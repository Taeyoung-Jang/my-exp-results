# Universal Red Flags

**Definition**: A set of acute symptoms that supersede routine self‑care guidance and require immediate medical attention, typically via [[entities/urgent-care]] or the [[entities/emergency-room]].

## Core Red‑Flag Symptoms (as presented in [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]])
- Sudden severe pain
- Trouble breathing or shortness of breath
- Chest pain
- Confusion, altered mental status, or fainting
- Any symptom that rapidly worsens

These signs are considered *non‑negotiable* triggers for seeking urgent care, regardless of the underlying condition (cold/flu, headache, stomach bug, etc.).

## Why They Matter
Red‑flag symptoms often indicate potentially life‑threatening conditions such as:
- Respiratory compromise (e.g., asthma exacerbation, pneumonia)
- Cardiovascular events (e.g., myocardial infarction)
- Neurologic emergencies (e.g., stroke, meningitis)
- Severe infection or sepsis

Prompt evaluation can prevent complications and improve outcomes.

## Interaction with Triage Logic
In the health‑self‑triage framework of [[concepts/triage-categories]], red flags act as an overriding rule:
> If any universal red flag is present, then **skip** the "wait it out" or "call my doctor" branches and proceed directly to urgent care or the emergency department.

This ensures that the decision tree prioritises safety over convenience.

## Relation to Symptom‑Duration Thresholds
While many conditions are managed by monitoring duration (see [[concepts/symptom-duration-thresholds]]), red flags are *time‑independent*: their presence alone mandates escalation, even if the symptom has just begun.

## Practical Application
When using the one‑screen checklist from the session‑14 summary, the very first line reads the universal red flags. Users are instructed to:
1. Scan for any of the listed red‑flag signs.
2. If present, immediately pursue [[entities/urgent-care]] or [[entities/emergency-room]] care.
3. Otherwise, follow the condition‑specific "if‑then" guidance.
