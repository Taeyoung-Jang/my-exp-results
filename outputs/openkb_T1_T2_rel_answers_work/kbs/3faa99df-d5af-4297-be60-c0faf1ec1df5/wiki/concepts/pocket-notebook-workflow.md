# Pocket Notebook Workflow

The **pocket notebook workflow** is a low‑effort system for quickly capturing everyday information in a small, always‑handy notebook. It emphasizes speed, minimal structure, and easy retrieval, making it suitable for people who want organization without a heavy‑weight system.

## Core Principles
- **Chronological writing**: Write entries in the order they occur; avoid sections, indexes, or elaborate headings. This keeps the notebook ready for "open‑write‑close" use.
- **Edge‑marking**: When you jot down something that will need later attention (e.g., an appointment, a name, a location of an item), place a tiny dot or slash on the outer edge of that page. The mark serves as a visual cue for later review without adding extra pages.
- **One‑minute end‑of‑day scan**: Spend about a minute each night flipping through the day’s page, following the edge marks, moving time‑sensitive items to a calendar, and noting anything worth keeping. This habit consolidates captured data and prevents the notebook from becoming a forgotten pile.
- **Back‑cover map**: Reserve the inside of the back cover for a static quick‑reference list (frequent phone numbers, active tasks, or reference notes). It stays in place and does not require ongoing maintenance.
- **Default home for objects**: Pair the notebook habit with a designated spot for keys, wallet, glasses, etc., reducing decision‑fatigue.

## How It Connects to Other Concepts
- This workflow exemplifies [[concepts/low-friction-organization]] by providing a minimal‑maintenance capture system.
- The nightly review is the practical application of [[concepts/end-of-day-scan]] within a paper‑based context.
