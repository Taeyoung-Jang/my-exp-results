# Streptococcal Pharyngitis (Strep Throat)

Streptococcal pharyngitis is a bacterial infection of the throat caused by *Streptococcus pyogenes*. It typically presents with sudden sore throat, fever, swollen lymph nodes, and sometimes white patches on the tonsils.

## Key points from the checklist (session‑14)
- **When to call a doctor**: if sore throat lasts **more than three days**, shows **white patches**, or is accompanied by **fever and swollen glands** – the checklist advises seeking a clinician for possible **strep testing**. This aligns with the [[concepts/symptom-duration-thresholds]] used throughout the guide.
- **When to seek urgent care / ER**: if swallowing becomes difficult, there is drooling, or breathing feels restricted. These are listed under the **urgent care/ER** red‑flag section, referencing the broader [[concepts/universal-red-flags]].
- The sore‑throat section is part of the document’s overall [[concepts/triage-categories]] which group common minor ailments and map them to “wait it out”, “call my doctor”, or “urgent care/ER”.
