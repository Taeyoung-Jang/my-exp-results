## Influenza (Flu)

Influenza, often called the flu, is a contagious respiratory virus that causes fever, cough, sore throat, body aches, and fatigue. It spreads seasonally and can range from mild to severe illness.

### Relevance to the session‑14 checklist
The document *session‑14_2024-06-15* includes a dedicated **Cold / Flu** section that uses Influenza as a reference point for symptom‑duration thresholds and care‑seeking decisions. The checklist provides concrete cut‑offs for fever and symptom duration that help users decide whether to **wait it out**, **call a doctor**, or go to **urgent care/ER**.

### Symptom‑duration thresholds
- Fever **under 102°F** lasting **fewer than three days** → home care.
- Fever **102–103°F** persisting **more than three days** → call your doctor.
- Fever **over 103°F** or fever with breathing difficulty → seek urgent care or the **ER**.
- If symptoms do **not improve by day 7** (e.g., congestion, cough) → call your doctor.
- Sudden onset of fever with aches or a new cough/fever should prompt **COVID/flu testing**.

These thresholds align with the broader [[concepts/symptom-duration-thresholds]] used throughout the checklist.

### When to seek higher‑level care
- Any **universal red flag** such as sudden severe pain, trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, or rapidly worsening symptoms triggers immediate urgent care/ER. See [[concepts/universal-red-flags]].
- Persistent or worsening flu‑like symptoms that meet the doctor‑level thresholds should prompt a call to a primary care provider.
- Severe or uncontrolled fever, especially with respiratory distress, warrants a visit to **urgent‑care** facilities or the **emergency‑room**.
