## Summary of Session 14 (2024‑06‑15)

This document provides a concise, screenshot‑ready health‑self‑care checklist that uses an **if‑then decision framework** to guide when to *wait it out*, *call a doctor*, or seek *urgent care* / *ER* for common minor ailments. It emphasizes **universal red flags** that override all other guidance.

### Key Concepts
- **Universal red flags** – sudden severe pain, breathing difficulty, chest pain, confusion, fainting, or rapidly worsening symptoms trigger urgent care or the [[entities/emergency-room]]. [[concepts/universal-red-flags]]
- **Symptom duration thresholds** – specific cut‑offs (e.g., fever < 102°F < 3 days, vomiting improving within 24‑48 h) determine the appropriate action. [[concepts/symptom-duration-thresholds]]
- **Fever thresholds** – under 102°F (home care), 102‑103°F > 3 days (doctor), > 103°F (urgent care/ER). *(plain text)*
- **Dehydration signs** – dark urine, dizziness, very little output indicate escalation. *(plain text)*
- **Strep testing** – sore throat > 3 days, white patches, fever, swollen glands prompt doctor contact for possible [[entities/streptococcal-pharyngitis]]. *(plain text)*
- **COVID/flu testing** – new sudden fever/aches or cough/fever combo suggests testing for [[entities/covid-19]] or [[entities/influenza]]. *(plain text)*
- **Triage categories** – the checklist groups conditions by topic (cold/flu, sore throat, headache, stomach bug, back pain/sciatica, cuts, burns) and maps each to the three action levels. [[concepts/triage-categories]]

### Structure of the Checklist
1. **Universal red flags** – top‑level warning.
2. **Cold / Flu** – guidance on congestion, cough, fever, and when to consider COVID/flu testing.
3. **Sore throat** – criteria for home care vs. strep evaluation vs. urgent care.
4. **Headache** – usual pattern vs. persistent or alarming features.
5. **Stomach bug** – vomiting/diarrhea duration, dehydration, and blood presence.
6. **Back pain / Sciatica** – improvement timeline, functional impact, and red‑flag neurological signs.
7. **Cuts** – bleeding control, edge integrity, infection risk.
8. **Burns** – size, depth, blister size, and location considerations.

Each line follows a tight “**if X then Y**” format, making it easy to scan on a phone screen. The document also includes brief notes on when to consider COVID/flu testing and when specific symptoms (e.g., white patches on throat) warrant strep testing.

Overall, the checklist serves as a practical self‑triage tool that balances symptom monitoring with clear escalation thresholds.
