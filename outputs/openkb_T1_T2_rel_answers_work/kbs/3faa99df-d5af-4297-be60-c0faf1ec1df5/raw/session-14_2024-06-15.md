**Assistant:** Here’s a simple, conversational way to think about it without turning it into a rigid list. It’s general guidance only, not a diagnosis, but it can help you decide when to rest versus when to get help.

For things like colds, a couple of days of congestion, sore throat, mild cough, or feeling run‑down is usually something you can ride out with rest, fluids, and over‑the‑counter comfort measures. If a “cold” starts improving by day three to five, that’s the usual pattern. The moments to worry are when you develop trouble breathing, chest pain, a high fever that won’t come down, or symptoms that aren’t gradually trending better by about a week.

For headaches, it’s generally fine to wait it out if it behaves like your typical headaches, responds to hydration or a pain reliever, and fades within a few hours. You should get help sooner if a headache is suddenly the worst you’ve ever felt, comes with confusion, fainting, vision changes, weakness, trouble speaking, or neck stiffness, or if it lasts more than a couple of days without easing up.

For stomach bugs, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea for a day or two—especially if you can keep small sips of fluid down—can usually be managed at home. The red flags are signs of dehydration like dizziness, very little urine, or feeling faint, as well as vomiting that won’t stop, blood in vomit or stool, severe belly pain, or symptoms lasting beyond two to three days without improvement.

For minor muscle strains, soreness that comes on after activity and gradually improves over a few days is usually fine to treat with rest and gentle movement. You should check in with a clinician if there’s sharp pain that doesn’t let you bear weight, numbness or tingling, swelling that gets worse instead of better, or pain that’s still significant after about a week of taking it easy.

If something ever feels dramatically out of proportion, hits suddenly and severely, or comes with chest pain, difficulty breathing, confusion, or fainting, that’s the “don’t wait” category. Otherwise, gradual improvement is the key sign you’re in safe territory, and lack of improvement after several days is a reasonable time to get a professional opinion.
