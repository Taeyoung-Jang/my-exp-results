# Record Retention

**Overview**
Record retention is the practice of defining how long various types of documents should be kept before they can be safely discarded. Proper retention protects legal rights, ensures tax compliance, and reduces clutter.

## Core Retention Timeline
| Category | Typical Retention Period |
|----------|--------------------------|
| **Identity & Vital Records** (birth certificates, passports, SSN cards, marriage/divorce papers) | Forever |
| **Property & Long‑Term Agreements** (titles, deeds, major loans) | Forever |
| **Tax Returns & Major Financial Records** | 7 years |
| **Routine Financial Statements & Bills** | 1 year (or until needed for tax filing) |
| **Any Personal Information Not Needed for Legal/Tax Purposes** | Immediate shredding |

These periods are derived from the guidance in [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-16]] and reflect common legal and tax standards.

## How Retention Relates to Organization
A simple folder structure aligns with the retention schedule:
- **Identity & Vital Records** – stored forever, both paper (fire‑proof safe) and digital (encrypted cloud folder).
- **Property & Long‑Term Agreements** – stored forever.
- **Taxes – [Year]** – one folder per tax year, kept for 7 years.
- **Active Policies & Contracts** – kept until the agreement expires or is superseded.
- **Current Year Financial** – kept for 1 year, then shredded or archived.

This mirrors the recommendations in [[concepts/document-organization]], ensuring that the physical and digital filing systems support the retention policy.

## Security Considerations
When retaining sensitive documents for long periods, apply [[concepts/security-best-practices]]:
- Use a fire‑proof lockbox or safe for paper originals.
- Encrypt digital scans and store them in a reputable cloud service.
- Limit local unencrypted copies and use a password manager for access credentials.

## Practical Tips
1. **Label folders clearly** with the retention category (e.g., “Forever – Identity”, “Taxes – 2022”).
2. **Schedule periodic reviews** (e.g., semi‑annual) to move items that have reached the end of their retention window to the shred bin.
3. **Document the process** in a short SOP so you can onboard others or revisit the system later.

By adhering to a defined retention timeline, you minimize the risk of losing important legal proof while keeping storage manageable.
