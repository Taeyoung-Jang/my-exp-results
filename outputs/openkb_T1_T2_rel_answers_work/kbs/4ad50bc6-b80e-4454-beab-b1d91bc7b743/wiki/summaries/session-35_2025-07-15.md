# Summary of Session 35 (2025‑07‑15)

**Topic:** How to systematically document a residential water‑damage incident for an insurance claim and ensure proper handling of removed contents.

---

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/inventory-documentation]]** – Detailed, photo‑supported list of every item packed out, including condition notes.  
- **[[concepts/chain-of-custody]]** – Log tracking who handled items, when, and where they were stored, to prevent loss or disputes.  
- **Storage fees** – Understanding policy coverage for storage costs and who pays them.  
- **[[concepts/pack-out-process]]** – Vendor procedures for labeling, boxing, and moving contents.  
- **[[concepts/red-flags-contents-handling]]** – Warning signs such as vague inventories, missing photos, bulk line items, or undocumented disposals.  
- **Insurance claim organization** – Centralized digital folder, receipts, communications, and timelines for a clean claim package.

---

## Documentation Checklist
1. **Photos & Videos** – Capture source of leak, affected rooms, and each damaged item.  
2. **Chronological Notes** – Dates, actions taken (e.g., shut‑off water), and progression of damage.  
3. **Receipts & Estimates** – All costs, from drying equipment to temporary lodging.  
4. **Communications Log** – Emails, claim numbers, and phone call summaries with the adjuster.  
5. **Digital Folder** – Store all above items in one location for easy sharing.

---

## Concrete Questions for Adjuster/Contractor
### Pack‑out & Inventory
- “Can you provide a full inventory of every item packed out, with photos and condition descriptions?”  
- “How are boxes labeled to match the inventory list?”  
- “Who maintains the inventory – your team, the pack‑out company, or the [[entities/adjuster]]?”

### Chain of Custody
- “Do you have a chain‑of‑custody log showing removal, handling, and storage dates? May I receive a copy?”  
- “If items are moved again, will the log be updated and shared with me?”

### Storage & Fees
- “What is the expected storage duration for my items?”  
- “Which part of my policy covers storage fees, and are they billed directly to the insurer?”  
- “Will I be notified before any additional storage charges are incurred?”

---

## Documents to Request
- Complete inventory list with photos.  
- Labeling/box list that corresponds to the inventory.  
- Chain‑of‑custody record.  
- Pack‑out and storage invoices submitted to insurance.  
- Scope of work from the [[entities/contractor]] or [[entities/pack-out-company]].

---

## Red Flags to Watch
- Vague inventory entries (e.g., “kitchen items”).  
- Missing or unlinked photos.  
- Generic box labels without item references.  
- Unexplained disposals or “non‑salvageable” designations without visual proof.  
- Delayed provision of inventory or logs.

---

## Sample Written Request to Adjuster
```
Subject: Request for Detailed Documentation and Confirmation of Procedures

Dear [Adjuster Name],

To ensure a clear and efficient claim process, I request the following:
1. A detailed, photo‑supported inventory for every item removed.
2. Copies of the labeling system and chain‑of‑custody records.
3. Confirmation that no items will be declared non‑salvageable or disposed of without my written consent.
4. Advance notice before any boxes are opened or contents are valued, so I may be present.

Thank you for your cooperation.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
```

---

## Takeaway
By treating the claim like a structured story—what happened, what was damaged, and the associated costs—and by demanding transparent, documented processes, you protect yourself from missing items, denied expenses, and unnecessary delays.
