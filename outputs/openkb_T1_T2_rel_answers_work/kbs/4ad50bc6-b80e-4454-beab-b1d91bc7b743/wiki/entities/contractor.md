# General Contractor

**Type:** Organization

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-35_2025-07-15]]

## Overview
The General Contractor is the party responsible for overseeing the repair work and the removal (pack‑out) of damaged contents after a water‑damage incident. In the context of the water‑damage claim discussed in session‑35, the contractor is coordinating the removal of kitchen cabinets and hall‑closet items, arranging their storage, and interfacing with the insurance adjuster.

## Key Responsibilities (as highlighted in the session)
- **Create a detailed inventory** of every item removed, including photos and condition notes. This aligns with the [[concepts/inventory-documentation]] best practice.
- **Implement a labeling system** that matches each box to the inventory list, supporting the [[concepts/pack-out-process]].
- **Maintain a chain‑of‑custody log** that records who handled the items, when they were moved, and where they are stored, satisfying the [[concepts/chain-of-custody]] requirement.
- **Communicate storage details** such as expected duration, fees, and which part of the policy covers those costs.
- **Provide copies of all documentation** (inventory, labeling, chain‑of‑custody, invoices) to the homeowner and the [[entities/adjuster]].
- **Allow homeowner presence** when boxes are opened or contents are valued, to prevent disputes.

## Interaction with Other Parties
- Works directly with the **insurance adjuster** ([[entities/adjuster]]) to ensure the claim is supported by proper documentation.
- May coordinate with a **pack‑out company** ([[entities/pack-out-company]]) if a specialized vendor is used for the removal and storage of contents.
- Aligns with the broader role of a **contractor** ([[entities/contractor]]) in managing repair scopes and timelines.

## Red‑Flag Areas to Monitor
Homeowners should watch for the following warning signs in the contractor’s handling of contents, as outlined in [[concepts/red-flags-contents-handling]]:
- Vague inventory entries (e.g., “kitchen items” without specifics).
- Missing or unlinked photos for packed items.
- Generic box labels that cannot be matched to the inventory.
- Unexplained disposals or “non‑salvageable” designations without homeowner consent.

## Summary
The General Contractor plays a central role in documenting and managing the physical aspects of a water‑damage claim. Proper adherence to inventory documentation, chain‑of‑custody, and transparent communication helps ensure a smooth claim process and protects the homeowner from denied expenses or missing items.
