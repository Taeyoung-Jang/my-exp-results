# Budgeting for Emergency Kits

Creating a functional 72‑hour emergency kit does not have to be expensive, but thoughtful budgeting ensures essential items are covered without overspending. The recommendations below synthesize the cost estimates from [[summaries/session-02_2025-05-15]] and outline how to allocate funds across the most critical categories.

## Cost Tiers
| Tier | Typical Spend | What It Covers |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| **Basic** | $50 – $100 | Inexpensive LED flashlights, a small power bank, a basic first‑aid kit, a simple hand‑crank or battery‑powered radio, and essential hygiene supplies. |
| **Enhanced** | $150 – $300 | Higher‑lumens flashlights, larger capacity power banks, a comprehensive first‑aid kit with premium items, a rugged radio, and upgraded hygiene and comfort items (e.g., premium soaps, toilet‑safe bags). |

## Budget Allocation Guidance
1. **Lighting (15‑20 %)** – Prioritize reliable LED flashlights/headlamps and fresh spare batteries. The cheapest options still meet safety needs, while higher‑end models add durability and longer runtimes.
2. **Power (15‑20 %)** – A medium‑size power bank (≈10 000 mAh) per person provides critical phone charging capability. Larger capacity banks increase cost but reduce the need for multiple units.
3. **Medical & First‑Aid (20‑25 %)** – A basic kit is inexpensive, yet an enhanced kit adds higher‑quality bandages, antiseptics, and more extensive OTC medications, increasing the budget.
4. **Information (5‑10 %)** – A modest battery‑powered radio is affordable; a hand‑crank model raises the price but offers reliability without needing batteries.
5. **Hygiene & Comfort (10‑15 %)** – Include wipes, soap, trash bags, and optional toilet‑safe bags. Premium brands increase cost modestly.
6. **Pet Supplies (5‑10 %)** – Set aside funds for a few days of pet food, medications, and waste bags. This category is essential for households with animals.

## Balancing Cost and Preparedness
- **Start with the Basics:** Secure water, food, and lighting first. These items form the core of [[concepts/emergency-preparedness]] and have the highest priority.
- **Iterative Upgrades:** Once the basic kit is complete, allocate remaining funds to improve durability, capacity, and comfort.
- **Leverage Rotation:** Frequent [[concepts/rotation-schedule]] checks (e.g., annually for batteries, semi‑annually for food) extend the life of purchased items, effectively stretching the budget.

## Practical Tips
- **Buy in Bulk:** Purchasing flashlights or batteries in multi‑packs reduces per‑unit cost.
- **Use Discounts:** Look for sales on first‑aid kits and power banks, especially during off‑season months.
- **DIY Options:** Simple items like a manual can opener can be sourced cheaply from hardware stores.
- **Track Expenses:** Maintaining a small spreadsheet helps you see where you’re spending and where you can trim costs for future upgrades.

By following these budgeting principles, households can assemble a reliable emergency kit that fits their financial situation while covering all essential survival needs.
