# Rotation Schedule for Emergency Supplies

**Related concepts:** [[concepts/emergency-preparedness]], [[concepts/budgeting]]

**Source:** [[summaries/session-02_2025-05-15]]

---

## Overview
A rotation schedule defines how often each component of a 72‑hour emergency kit should be inspected, refreshed, or replaced. Regular rotation ensures that supplies remain usable, safe, and effective when a disaster strikes.

## Recommended Intervals
| Supply Category | Check Frequency | Replacement Trigger |
|----------------|----------------|---------------------|
| Shelf‑stable food (canned, dry, bars) | Twice a year | After 6–12 months of storage or if packaging is damaged |
| Batteries (AA/AAA, etc.) | Annually | When voltage drops or after a year of storage |
| Prescription medications | With each refill | Keep a 3‑day emergency stash that matches the current prescription |
| Pet food (dry/kibble) | Every 2–3 months | When a new bag is opened or after a few months of storage |
| First‑aid kit consumables (bandages, antiseptic) | Annually | When any item is used or expires |
| Flashlights/headlamps & spare batteries | Annually (test functionality) | Replace dead or weak batteries; replace lights if they dim |
| Power banks | Annually (charge test) | Replace if capacity drops below 80% of rated value |
| Radio (battery‑powered/hand‑crank) | Annually (test) | Replace batteries or faulty units |

## Implementation Tips
1. **Create a calendar reminder** – Mark the month for each category (e.g., food in March/September, batteries in June).
2. **Label expiration dates** – Use a marker or label maker on each item with the next inspection date.
3. **Batch rotate** – When you replace a set of food cans, move the older ones to the front of your pantry for regular use.
4. **Document changes** – Keep a simple log (paper or digital) noting the date of each rotation.
5. **Budget planning** – Allocate a small yearly budget for replacements; see [[concepts/budgeting]] for cost‑management strategies.

## Why It Matters
A well‑maintained rotation schedule reduces the risk of discovering expired food, dead batteries, or missing medication during an emergency. It also spreads replacement costs over time, making the overall emergency‑preparedness effort more affordable and reliable.
