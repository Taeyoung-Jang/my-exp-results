# Chain of Custody

**Definition**: A chain‑of‑custody is a structured, time‑stamped record that tracks the movement, handling, and location of personal property from the moment it is removed from a damaged residence until it is returned or disposed of. The log provides proof that items have not been lost, altered, or improperly accessed, which is critical for insurance claims.

---

## Why It Matters in a Water‑Damage Claim

In the *session‑35_2025‑07‑15* conversation, the assistant emphasizes that a clear chain‑of‑custody protects the policyholder from disputes over missing or damaged contents. Without this documentation, insurers may deny reimbursement for items that cannot be verified.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Timestamped Entries** – Record the exact date and time each item or box is:
   - Pulled from the home
   - Handed to a pack‑out crew or contractor
   - Loaded onto a transport vehicle
   - Received at a storage facility
   - Re‑delivered for restoration or return
2. **Responsible Parties** – Identify who performed each action (e.g., [[entities/pack-out-company]], [[entities/contractor]], or the insurance [[entities/adjuster]]). 
3. **Item Identification** – Link each log entry to a specific inventory record and photo (see [[concepts/inventory-documentation]]). 
4. **Location Tracking** – Note the storage unit address and any subsequent moves.
5. **Signature or Electronic Acknowledgment** – Obtain a signature or digital confirmation from each handler.

---

## How to Obtain and Verify the Log

- **Ask Directly**: "Do you have a chain‑of‑custody log showing when items were removed, who handled them, and when they arrived at storage? Can I get a copy?"
- **Request Updates**: If items are moved again, request that the log be updated and shared.
- **Cross‑Check with Inventory**: Ensure each log entry corresponds to an entry in the [[concepts/inventory-documentation]] list and that photos match the described condition.

---

## Red Flags

- No chain‑of‑custody log provided or only a vague summary.
- Handlers are unnamed or listed only as "crew".
- Missing timestamps or inconsistent dates.
- No copies of the log are offered, or the vendor says they will "send it later" without a deadline.

These warnings align with the broader set of [[concepts/red-flags-contents-handling]] identified for pack‑out and storage vendors.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/inventory-documentation]]** – The chain‑of‑custody builds on a detailed inventory; both must reference the same item IDs and photos.
- **[[concepts/pack-out-process]]** – Proper labeling and tracking during pack‑out create the foundation for a reliable custody chain.
- **[[concepts/red-flags-contents-handling]]** – Missing or incomplete custody records are a primary red flag.

## Practical Summary

For a water‑damage claim, insist on a complete, signed chain‑of‑custody log and keep a copy in your claim folder (see the checklist in [[summaries/session-35_2025-07-15]]). This documentation safeguards your belongings, supports reimbursement, and streamlines communication with the adjuster and any contractors involved.
