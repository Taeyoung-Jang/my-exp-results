**User:** I’m trying to get better at scheduling across different time zones—especially when people are spread across the U.S. How do you usually recommend coordinating meeting times so nobody shows up at the wrong hour?

**Assistant:** The easiest way is to anchor everything to a single reference time and stick with it. Most people use Eastern Time since a lot of companies default to it, but any zone works as long as everyone agrees on it.

When you send out a meeting time, always include the time zone explicitly and avoid things like “noon my time.” Tools help a lot too—calendar invites automatically shift the time to each person’s local zone, so sending an actual invite rather than just a message removes a lot of risk. If you’re coordinating by chat, dropping in the converted times for each region can prevent confusion, especially around daylight‑saving changes.

In short, pick one reference zone, label it clearly, and let calendars handle the rest.

**User:** Could you give me a quick cheat sheet for the common U.S. time zones (PT/MT/CT/ET), like how many hours apart they are, and any gotchas around daylight saving time?

**Assistant:** Sure. In the U.S., the four main continental time zones line up in a simple one‑hour step moving east. Pacific is the earliest, then Mountain one hour later, Central another hour later, and Eastern one more after that. So Eastern is three hours ahead of Pacific, two ahead of Mountain, and one ahead of Central.

They all observe daylight saving time at the same moment in the spring and fall, which keeps their relative spacing consistent. The only real gotcha is that a few places don’t follow DST at all, like most of Arizona and Hawaii, so Mountain Time can briefly line up differently if someone is in one of those regions.

**User:** Those examples help—could you also show what happens around the DST transition? Like, if we scheduled something for 10:00 AM ET on the Monday right after the spring-forward change, would the PT/MT/CT conversions still be the same?

**Assistant:** Yes, the conversions stay the same. The continental U.S. time zones all “spring forward” at the same moment, so the one‑hour spacing between ET, CT, MT, and PT doesn’t change. That means a 10:00 a.m. ET meeting on the Monday right after the switch is still 9:00 a.m. CT, 8:00 a.m. MT, and 7:00 a.m. PT.

The only wrinkle is for places that don’t observe DST, like most of Arizona and all of Hawaii. In those spots the offset shifts relative to the other zones, but the main four U.S. time zones stay aligned with each other.
