# Daylight Saving Time

## Definition
Daylight Saving Time (DST) is the seasonal adjustment of clocks that moves time forward by one hour in the spring and back by one hour in the fall.

## Application in the United States
All four continental U.S. time zones—[[entities/eastern-time]], [[entities/central-time]], [[entities/mountain-time]], and [[entities/pacific-time]]—observe DST and change at the same moment. Because they shift simultaneously, the relative offsets between these zones remain constant throughout the year.

## Effect on Time‑Zone Offsets
The consistent shift means that the [[concepts/time_zone_offset]] relationships (e.g., ET is three hours ahead of PT) do not change during DST. This stability simplifies scheduling across zones when a common [[concepts/reference_time_zone]] such as Eastern Time is used as the anchor.

## Exceptions
Two major U.S. regions do **not** follow DST:
- Most of [[entities/arizona]] (except the Navajo Nation) stays on Mountain Standard Time year‑round.
- All of [[entities/hawaii]] remains on Hawaii‑Aleutian Standard Time year‑round.
In these areas the offset relative to the four main zones shifts when DST is in effect elsewhere.
