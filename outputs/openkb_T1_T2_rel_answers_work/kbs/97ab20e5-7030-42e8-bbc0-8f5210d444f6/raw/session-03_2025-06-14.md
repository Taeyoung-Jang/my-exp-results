**User:** I’m trying to get better at planning and executing marketing campaigns without everything turning into a last-minute scramble. If you were setting up a simple, repeatable workflow for campaign launches (from brief to launch to post-mortem), what would your checklist and timeline look like?

**Assistant:** You can keep this really simple by thinking in three phases—before, during, after—and giving each phase a clear owner and deadline.

In the planning phase, start with a brief that defines the goal, the audience, the core message, the offer, the channels, the budget, and how you’ll measure success. Once that’s aligned, lock the timeline, assign responsibilities, and draft all creative and copy. While that’s happening, set up tracking, build the landing pages or assets, and get any approvals you’ll need. This phase usually works best if it starts about four to six weeks before launch, depending on how complex your campaign is.

In the production and launch phase, finalize creative, build everything in-platform, QA every link and tracking setup, and run a small internal review to check message consistency and brand accuracy. Aim to have everything finished a few days before go‑live so the actual launch day is just a matter of pushing things live and verifying that data is flowing correctly.

In the post‑launch and wrap‑up phase, monitor early performance daily, make quick optimizations, and then, after the campaign ends or stabilizes, pull results against your original goals. Hold a short post‑mortem within a week: what worked, what didn’t, what to repeat, and what to avoid next time. Document it in one place so future campaigns get easier instead of starting from scratch.

That structure alone—clear brief, early asset development, pre‑launch QA, and a quick post‑mortem—usually eliminates most of the last‑minute chaos.

**User:** Could you turn that into a concrete week-by-week timeline (say for a 4-week and a 6-week runway) with a checklist by phase, and include who typically owns each step (PMM, demand gen, creative, web/dev, analytics)? Also, what are the top 5 “don’t skip” QA checks right before launch?

**Assistant:** Here’s a simple way to frame both timelines without using formal bullet points, while still keeping everything clear and scannable.

For a four‑week runway, week one is owned mostly by product marketing: they finalize the brief, align on goals and audience, confirm the offer, define channels, sync with demand gen, and loop in creative, web, and analytics so everyone knows what’s coming. Week two shifts to creative, demand gen, and web/dev: creative drafts concepts and copy, demand gen builds the channel plan and targeting, and web/dev starts any landing pages or tracking setups. Analytics confirms success metrics and begins configuring reporting. Week three is where everything gets built: creative produces final assets, demand gen sets up campaigns in-platform, web/dev finishes landing pages and tagging, and analytics validates tracking. Week four is reserved for QA, approvals, launch-day prep, and then the actual go‑live with analytics closely watching early data.

For a six‑week runway, the flow is the same but slower and more controlled. Week one is brief creation by product marketing. Week two is alignment across demand gen, creative, web/dev, and analytics. Week three is early creative and copy development while web/dev gets a jump on any heavier builds. Week four is production of all final assets and full channel setup. Week five is dedicated QA, approvals, and soft tests. Week six is launch, monitoring, and early optimization.

As a phase checklist, the planning phase belongs mainly to product marketing for the brief, to demand gen for channel strategy, to creative for initial concepts, and to web/dev and analytics for technical scoping. The build phase belongs to creative for all asset production, to demand gen for campaign setup, to web/dev for landing pages and tracking, and to analytics for measurement configuration. The launch and post‑mortem phase belongs to demand gen for execution, to analytics for data validation and reporting, and to product marketing for the final readout and lessons learned.

Right before launch, the five QA checks you never want to skip are verifying every link and UTM, confirming conversion tracking fires correctly, making sure creative versions match what was approved, checking that audience targeting and budgets are correct in every platform, and loading the landing page on both desktop and mobile to confirm speed and functionality.

**User:** One more constraint: I’m going to be heads-down for the next couple of weeks since I have four campaign launches due back-to-back before the month ends. Given that, can you suggest the minimum “must-do” version of your 4-week workflow that I can realistically follow, plus what I should delegate vs. personally review?

**Assistant:** In your situation, think of a compressed version of the four‑week workflow that keeps only the steps that truly prevent mistakes. Everything else gets delegated.

The minimum version starts with you owning a very tight brief for each campaign. Keep it to goals, audience, offer, channels, and success metric. Once that’s written, immediately hand it to demand gen, creative, web/dev, and analytics so they can start without waiting for you.

Next, delegate all asset creation, all in‑platform builds, and all tracking setup. Your only involvement during this middle stretch should be approving the core message and the top creative concept. If those two things are right, most downstream work stays aligned even if you’re not checking every draft.

As launch week approaches, you personally review only three things: the final landing page, one representative ad per channel, and the tracking validation summary from analytics. Everything else—budgets, targeting, variations, version control—should be owned by demand gen and checked by them.

Right before launch, the only QA items you should personally verify are that links work, tracking fires, and the landing page loads properly on mobile. Delegate all platform‑level QA to demand gen and all creative accuracy checks to the creative lead.

This keeps you focused on the brief, the message, the final experience, and the data integrity, while the teams handle the production and mechanical steps that don’t need your direct attention.

**User:** Can you give me a one-page “launch packet” template I can reuse for each of these campaigns (brief + key dates + owners + links to assets + launch-day checklist), and a quick Slack/Email message I can send to the team to kick each one off?

**Assistant:** Here’s a simple one‑page packet you can copy for every campaign. It keeps everything in one place without being heavy or formal.

Launch Packet Template:

Campaign name goes at the top, followed by a short paragraph capturing the brief: the goal, the audience, the core message or offer, the channels you’re using, and the primary success metric. Right under that, add a short timeline section with four dates: brief finalized, assets due, build/QA window, and launch date. If there’s a post‑launch readout date, include that too.

Next, add an owners section written as a quick set of role assignments in sentence form. For example: product marketing owns the brief and message, demand gen owns channel setup and launch, creative owns asset production, web/dev owns landing pages and tracking implementation, and analytics owns measurement and reporting.

Below that, add a short paragraph linking out to working files. Include the link to the brief, the creative folder, the landing page or preview environment, the tracking plan or tag sheet, and the campaign build sheet if you use one.

Close with a launch‑day checklist written as a simple flow. Start with confirmation that links and UTMs are working, tracking tests are passing, and the landing page loads cleanly on desktop and mobile. Follow with a note that ads or emails are approved, audiences and budgets are confirmed by demand gen, and analytics has verified data is flowing. End with who is pressing “go live” and at what time.

Kickoff Message (Slack or Email):

Hi team — sharing the launch packet for our upcoming campaign: [link]. The brief is final, timelines are locked, and roles are noted inside. Creative can begin production immediately, web/dev can start on the page and tagging, and demand gen can start building channels. Analytics, please set up tracking based on the success metric listed. Let’s aim to have everything ready for QA by the date shown. Ping me if anything looks unclear. Thanks all — excited to get this one out the door.
