# Boundary Setting

**Boundary Setting** is the practice of explicitly communicating when you will be unavailable and establishing clear rules for others to follow during that period. It is a core component of preparing for an uninterrupted break, ensuring that work does not seep back in and that you can truly disconnect.

## Key Elements
- **Announce exact dates**: Inform your team, manager, and key partners of the start and end dates of your offline period.
- **Designate a backup contact**: Assign a specific person who will handle urgent matters and make decisions in your absence.
- **Define what counts as urgent**: Set criteria (e.g., blocks another team, impacts a deadline, requires same‑day action) so colleagues know when to bypass the boundary.
- **Create friction**: Remove or sign out of work apps, turn off notifications, and delete shortcuts to make accidental check‑ins harder. See [[concepts/notification_friction]] for more on this technique.
- **Communicate via out‑of‑office**: Use firm auto‑reply messages that state you will not be checking messages and direct all urgent issues to the backup contact. Refer to [[concepts/out_of_office]] for template guidance.
- **Empower the backup**: Explicitly grant permission for the backup person to make decisions without waiting for you.

## Process Overview (from [[summaries/session-35_2025-06-21]])
1. **Friday afternoon** – Send the announcement, share the backup contact, and hand off active tasks.
2. **Set OOO messages** – Deploy email and Slack auto‑replies that reinforce the boundary.
3. **Create friction** – Sign out of email/Slack, remove work apps from devices, and shut down the laptop.
4. **Weekend logistics** – Confirm keys, pet care, and personal errands to avoid mid‑vacation interruptions.
