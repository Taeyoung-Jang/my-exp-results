# Summary of Session 01 (2024‑06‑15)

**Key Context**  
- User evaluates transportation choices: personal car, public transit, bike, car‑share, ride‑hailing.  
- Provides detailed weekly mileage, parking, insurance, fuel, and maintenance numbers.  
- Wants a concrete weekly plan and a break‑even analysis for dropping the $180/month work parking pass in favor of $18/day.

**Main Findings**  
- Fixed costs (insurance, parking, maintenance) dominate the budget; reducing miles only modestly lowers total monthly spend.  
- Switching 40 % of trips to transit/bike cuts fuel from ~​$66 / mo to ~$39 / mo, yielding a total ~​$540 / mo (car + $105 transit pass). See [[concepts/cost-comparison]].  
- Using transit/bike for most weekday trips and keeping the car only for occasional needs reduces fuel to $15‑$25 / mo, still around $515‑$530 / mo due to unchanged fixed costs. This illustrates a [[concepts/hybrid-commuting]] approach.  
- Selling the car would eliminate fixed costs, leaving only the $105 transit pass plus occasional car‑share/ride‑hailing, resulting in the largest savings.

**Hybrid Week Planning**  
1. **Commute (7 mi each way, 5 days/week)** – Prefer bike or transit; only drive on 1‑2 days when conditions make it clearly better.  
2. **Weekday errands** – Use bike or transit; reserve car for large grocery runs, late‑evening plans, or poor weather.  
3. **Weekend trips** – Keep the car for longer trips; otherwise consider car‑share if no car is needed.

**Parking Cost Break‑Even**  
- Monthly pass: $180.  
- Daily rate: $18 → break‑even at 10 work‑day drives per month. See [[concepts/break-even-analysis]].  
- Expected driving: 4‑8 days/month → daily parking cost $72‑$144, **cheaper than the pass**.  
- Recommendation: drop the monthly pass, pay daily when the car is used.

**Implications**  
- Financial savings are modest unless the car is sold; biggest impact comes from eliminating fixed costs.  
- Non‑financial gains (less stress, lower wear, predictable commute) are significant in the hybrid approach.
