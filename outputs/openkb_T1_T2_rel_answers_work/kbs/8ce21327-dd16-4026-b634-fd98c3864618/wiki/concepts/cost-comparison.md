# Cost Comparison

**Scope** – This concept captures the quantitative comparison of transportation expenses presented in [[summaries/session-01_2024-06-15]]. It focuses on the relative impact of fixed versus variable costs when mixing a personal car with public transit, biking, car‑share, and ride‑hailing.

## Core Findings from the Session

1. **Fixed costs dominate** – Insurance ($140 / mo), work parking ($180 / mo), and annual maintenance/registration (~$75 / mo) remain unchanged regardless of mileage. These fixed expenses account for the bulk of the monthly budget.
2. **Fuel is the main variable cost** – At 28 mpg and $4.20 / gal, fuel drops from about $66 / mo (full 110 mi / wk) to $15‑$25 / mo when most weekday trips shift to transit or bike.
3. **Scenario breakdown**
   - **40 % shift to transit/bike** – Fuel ≈ $39 / mo; total ≈ $540 / mo (car + $105 transit pass).
   - **Hybrid weekday (transit/bike for most trips, car only 1‑2 days)** – Fuel ≈ $15‑$25 / mo; total ≈ $515‑$530 / mo.
   - **Sell the car** – Eliminates insurance, parking, maintenance, and fuel; cost ≈ $105 / mo (transit pass) plus occasional car‑share/ride‑hailing, yielding the greatest savings.
4. **Break‑even parking analysis** – Monthly parking ($180) vs. daily rate ($18). The break‑even point is 10 work‑day drives per month. Driving only 4‑8 days saves $36‑$108 per month by paying daily.

## Practical Implications
- **Financial** – Meaningful savings only arise by eliminating fixed costs (selling the car) or by reducing parking days below the break‑even threshold.
- **Non‑financial** – Reduced driving stress, lower vehicle wear, and more predictable commuting when using transit or bike.
