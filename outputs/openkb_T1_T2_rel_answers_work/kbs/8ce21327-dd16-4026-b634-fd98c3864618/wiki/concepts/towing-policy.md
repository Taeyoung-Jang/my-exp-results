# Towing Policy

A **towing policy** outlines the circumstances under which a vehicle may be towed, the process for issuing warnings, the authority that can order a tow, and the parties responsible for executing the tow. In the context of residential communities, understanding this policy helps residents avoid unexpected fines and vehicle removal.

## Key Elements (from [[summaries/session-31_2024-12-13]])

- **Warning Procedure**: Residents want to know whether a warning is issued before a tow is executed. The policy should specify the number of warnings, the method of notification (e.g., signage, written notice), and the time allowed to correct the violation.
- **Authorization**: Clarify who has the authority to order a tow—typically the [[entities/hoa]] board, the [[entities/property-manager]], or a designated security team.
- **Tow Company**: Identify the contracted [[entities/tow-company]] and provide contact information or a policy sheet. This ensures transparency and allows residents to verify the legitimacy of the tow.
- **Documentation**: Residents should request written documentation of the towing policy, including any forms or addenda that detail the process. This can be part of the broader parking addendum.
- **Appeal Process**: Outline steps for contesting a tow, such as contacting the HOA or property manager, providing evidence, and any deadlines for filing an appeal.

## Related Concepts

- [[concepts/parking-permits]] – Determines which vehicles are authorized to park and thus subject to the towing policy.

## Practical Tips for Residents

1. **Ask for Written Policy**: When communicating with the [[entities/property-manager]] or [[entities/hoa]], request the towing policy in writing, along with the tow company’s details.
2. **Understand Warning Signals**: Confirm whether visual warnings (e.g., signs) or written notices are used before a tow.
3. **Know the Appeal Procedure**: Keep a record of who to contact and the timeline for disputes.
4. **Keep Documentation Handy**: Store the parking addendum, map of assigned spaces, and towing policy in an easily accessible location.

By clarifying these aspects, residents can avoid inadvertent violations and ensure they have recourse if a tow occurs.
