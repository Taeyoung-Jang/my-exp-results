## Summary of Session 10 (2022‑09‑18)

**Main focus:** How a city’s overall vibe (or [[concepts/city-rhythm]]) is shaped, how long it takes to adapt (see [[concepts/adaptation-period]]), and practical habit‑based strategies for synchronising with either a faster or slower urban tempo.

### Factors shaping a city’s vibe
- History, density, climate, and dominant resident demographics.  
- Built environment and design of public spaces.  
- Typical work‑hour patterns and leisure orientation.

### Typical adaptation timeline
- Initial sense of rhythm: **a few weeks**.  
- Full behavioural adjustment: **3–6 months**, when routines feel natural (as described in [[concepts/adaptation-period]]).

### 30‑day “pace reset” plans
#### Faster city
- **Week 1 – Observation:** Leave home slightly earlier, match walking speed, reply to messages within a few hours.  
- **Week 2 – Tightening:** Set fixed errand times, accept spontaneous plans, impose a structured workday.  
- **Week 3 – Decisiveness:** Make quick choices on food, transit, scheduling; respond to emails/texts the same day.  
- **Week 4 – Comfort:** Maintain the pace without forcing it; settle into a reliable commute and short‑notice social handling.

#### Slower city
- **Week 1 – Downshift:** Allow extra travel time, let errands take their natural length, defer message replies to later in the day.  
- **Week 2 – Buffering:** Build time buffers, schedule social time earlier and less densely, end work without lingering urgency.  
- **Week 3 – Cadence:** Walk at a relaxed pace, cluster errands, permit longer gaps between digital responses.  
- **Week 4 – Automation:** Commutes become unhurried, social plans are scheduled well in advance, communication remains responsive but not immediate.

### Three keystone habits for any city
1. **Morning structure** – Define start‑time and initial urgency. *Fast city*: firmer, earlier start; *Slow city*: extra breathing room, later start.  
2. **Social availability** – How open you are to plans. *Fast city*: stay open to last‑minute invites; *Slow city*: plan ahead, leave downtime.  
3. **Micro‑decision handling** – Speed of everyday choices. *Fast city*: choose quickly to avoid mental drag; *Slow city*: deliberately pause to allow a relaxed rhythm.

These habits serve as pivots; adjusting them reshapes the overall pace and accelerates adaptation.
