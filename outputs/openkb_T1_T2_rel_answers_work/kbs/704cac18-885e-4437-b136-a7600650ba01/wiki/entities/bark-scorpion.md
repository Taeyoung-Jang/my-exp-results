# Bark Scorpion

**Type:** other

The bark scorpion (*Centruroides* spp.) is a small, highly venomous scorpion native to arid regions of the southwestern United States and northern Mexico. They are known for:

- **Indoor intrusion:** Driven indoors by extreme heat, they seek cool, dark hiding spots such as under shoes, in closets, or beneath stored boxes.
- **Habitat preferences:** They hide in crevices, under debris, and near moisture sources where prey insects congregate.
- **Risk to humans and pets:** Their sting can cause intense pain and, in sensitive individuals, systemic symptoms.

## Relevance to Scorpion‑Proofing
The advice in [[summaries/session-36_2024-09-18]] focuses on preventing bark scorpions from entering living spaces and safely monitoring their presence. Key tactics include:

- **Sealing entry points** (gaps around pipes, baseboards, doors) – part of the broader [[concepts/scorpion-proofing]] strategy.
- **Reducing indoor insects** to limit the scorpion’s food source.
- **Using sticky traps** placed in travel corridors; the traps are effective because bark scorpions readily walk over unscented glue surfaces.
- **Kid‑ and pet‑safe monitoring stations**: Enclosed glue‑board housings (see [[entities/catchmaster]], [[entities/protecta]]) and DIY designs using [[entities/pvc-pipe]] or simple cardboard boxes.
- **Safe glue removal** for pets, following the [[concepts/pet-safe-glue-removal]] guidelines.

## Practical Tips from the Session
- **Typical trap locations:** baseboard near bedroom doors, behind nightstands, under sinks, behind couches, and near garage door edges.
- **Trap design:** Use standard [[entities/glue-board]]s placed in recessed, enclosed stations (DIY [[concepts/diy-trap-design]]) to keep paws and fingers away.
- **Maintenance:** Check traps weekly and replace them when dusty or after about a month.
