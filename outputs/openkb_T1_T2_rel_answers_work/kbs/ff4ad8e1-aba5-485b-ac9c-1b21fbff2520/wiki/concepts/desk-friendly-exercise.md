# Desk‑Friendly Exercise

**Definition**: Desk‑friendly exercise refers to movement routines that can be performed while seated or standing at a workstation, requiring little space and low intensity, making them suitable for busy workdays.

## Core Characteristics
- **Space‑efficient**: No need for dedicated floor area; movements are done in the chair or immediate standing space.
- **Low‑impact**: Emphasizes joint‑friendly actions that avoid excessive strain on the knees and hips.
- **Low RPE**: Targets an effort level around RPE 3 (easy, no sweat), aligning with the principle of [[concepts/low-rpe-activity]].
- **Playful**: Incorporates game‑like or rhythmic elements to keep the activity enjoyable, tying into [[concepts/playful-micro-movement]].
- **Scalable**: Can be shortened or made even gentler if the user starts to feel warm or sweaty, following a simple **deload** rule.

## Typical Desk‑Friendly Moves (from [[summaries/session-31_2025-04-12]])
| Category | Standing Version | Desk‑Friendly Version |
|----------|------------------|-----------------------|
| **Mobility** | Hip circles, ankle rolls, torso twists | Seated hip circles, seated ankle rolls, rib‑cage twists |
| **Balance** | Single‑leg stands, calf raises | Seated leg lifts, seated calf raises |
| **Posture** | Shoulder‑blade squeezes, chest opener, neck rolls | Same actions performed while seated |
| **Leg Activation** | Half‑range squats, split‑stance dips | Chair‑hover pulses, seated leg swings |
| **Coordination** | Lateral steps, shadow shuffles | Seated weight shifts, tiny “defensive slides” |

Each movement is designed to be completed in 3–8 minutes, with a **30‑second emergency version** that can be done any time: 15 seconds of joint mobility followed by 15 seconds of balance.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/knee-friendly-exercises]]**: Desk‑friendly routines avoid deep knee bends and high impact, making them inherently knee‑friendly.
- **[[concepts/progression-and-deload]]**: Users can progress by adding slight range or repetitions, and deload by reducing motion if they feel warm.

## Practical Tips
1. **Pair with existing cues** – Attach the routine to moments like after sending an email or while waiting for coffee.
2. **Keep it playful** – Use music or a quick mental “game” (e.g., count how many times you can touch a desk edge) to sustain motivation.
3. **Monitor intensity** – If you start to sweat, immediately shorten the range of motion and slow the tempo.

Desk‑friendly exercise provides a minimalist, enjoyable way to maintain daily movement, supporting overall health without disrupting work flow.
