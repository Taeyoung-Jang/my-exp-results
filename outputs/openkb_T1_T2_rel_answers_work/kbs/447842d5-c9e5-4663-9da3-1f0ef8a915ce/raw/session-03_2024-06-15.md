**User:** I’m trying to get better at planning meals and grocery shopping around specific dietary needs—can you help me set up a simple system for choosing recipes and checking ingredient labels so I don’t miss anything?

**Assistant:** Sure. You can keep this very simple by using the same small routine every time you pick a recipe or shop.

Start by choosing just a few “safe” recipes you already know fit your dietary needs. When you want to try a new one, skim the ingredient list slowly and look for your usual red‑flag items. If anything looks unfamiliar, do a quick search before adding it to your plan. Saving a screenshot of your restricted ingredients on your phone makes that step faster.

For grocery trips, make one running shopping list and mark any item that requires a label check with a little symbol or color. When you’re in the store, pause long enough to read the label for those marked items only. Focus on the allergen or dietary section first, then double‑check the ingredients from top to bottom.

Over time you’ll get used to the pattern, and the whole thing becomes automatic: choose recipe, scan for red flags, add items to your list, check only the marked labels at the store.

**User:** You know I have to avoid all soy and gluten, so I need to make sure those filters are applied permanently. Can you adjust the system you suggested so every recipe check and grocery label scan automatically flags anything with soy or gluten (including common hidden sources)?

**Assistant:** Absolutely. You can bake your soy‑and‑gluten filters right into the routine so you never have to remember them manually.

When you look at any recipe, treat “soy” and “gluten” as automatic search terms. Read the ingredient list once just to spot the obvious items, then do a second, quicker pass where you look only for the usual sneaky ones—things like soy sauce, miso, tamari, malt, brewer’s yeast, and anything wheat‑based. If something has a vague name like “natural flavors,” check it before approving the recipe.

For grocery shopping, use the same idea. Anything you add to your list that isn’t already a safe brand gets the “check label” mark. In the store, jump straight to the allergen line first, then scan for those common hidden forms of soy and gluten. If the label is unclear, treat it as a no until you can verify it.

Once these checks become a fixed part of the steps—recipe scan, add to list, label check—you won’t need to remember separate rules. The soy‑and‑gluten screen will just happen automatically every time.

If you haven’t verified it before, default to no and look for an alternative.
