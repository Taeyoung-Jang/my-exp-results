# Summary of session-03_2024-06-15

**Date:** 2024‑06‑15  

The conversation develops a practical system for people who must avoid soy and gluten. It outlines a repeatable routine for recipe selection and grocery shopping, then delivers a concise copy‑paste checklist split into two sections – **Scan for soy** and **Scan for gluten** – followed by a short **When in doubt** decision rule.

## Core workflow
1. **Choose a safe recipe** or quickly scan a new recipe for red‑flag ingredients.  
2. **Add items to a running shopping list** and mark any that need label verification.  
3. **In‑store label check** focuses on the allergen line and the flagged terms.  
4. The soy‑and‑gluten filters become automatic, eliminating the need to remember separate rules.  

This workflow corresponds to [[concepts/recipe-screening-workflow]].

## Soy red‑flag terms
- soy, soybeans, soy protein, soy protein isolate, soy flour, soy oil (unless highly refined), soy lecithin (if reactive), edamame, tofu, tempeh, miso, tamari, soy sauce, shoyu, textured vegetable protein, hydrolyzed vegetable protein, vegetable broth, vegetable protein, vegetable gum, seasonings, natural flavors.  
- **Assumption:** if the source isn’t specified, treat as containing soy.

## Gluten red‑flag terms
- wheat, barley, rye, malt, malt extract, malt vinegar, brewer’s yeast, wheat starch (unless gluten‑free), hydrolyzed wheat protein, modified food starch, thickeners, flavorings, seasonings, natural flavors.  
- Oats are safe only when labeled gluten‑free.  
- **Assumption:** unspecified sources are treated as containing gluten.

## Decision rule – “When in doubt”
- If a label states **“may contain”** or **“processed in a facility with soy/gluten,”** consider the product unsafe unless the brand has been previously verified as safe for the user’s sensitivity level.  

This rule aligns with [[concepts/when-in-doubt-rule]] and the label‑checking step ties to [[concepts/allergen-label-checking]].
