# Allergen Label Checking

**Definition**: A disciplined process for reviewing food ingredient lists and allergen statements on product packaging to identify and avoid specific allergens such as soy and gluten.

## Core Steps (derived from [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]])
1. **Identify flagged ingredients**: When scanning a product, first locate the dedicated allergen line (if present). Then search the full ingredient list for known red‑flag terms (e.g., soy, wheat, malt, hydrolyzed vegetable protein, natural flavors, etc.).
2. **Assume presence on uncertainty**: If a term appears without a clear source (e.g., “natural flavors” or “seasonings”), treat it as containing the allergen.
3. **Apply the "when‑in‑doubt" rule**: If the label includes statements like “may contain soy” or “processed in a facility with gluten,” consider the item unsafe unless you have previously verified the brand as safe.  See [[concepts/when-in-doubt-rule]] for the decision logic.
4. **Mark items for verification**: In a shopping list, tag any product that requires a label check with a symbol or color, so you pause to perform the steps above while in the store.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The checking routine is part of a broader **recipe screening workflow** that first filters recipes for allergen content before purchasing ingredients.  See [[concepts/recipe-screening-workflow]].
- It operationalizes the **when‑in‑doubt rule**, turning a high‑level safety principle into concrete label‑reading actions.  See [[concepts/when-in-doubt-rule]].

## Practical Tips
- Keep a quick‑reference checklist of soy and gluten red‑flag terms (as provided in the source document) handy on your phone.
- Use consistent symbols (e.g., ⚠️) on your shopping list to remind you to perform label checking.
- If a product’s allergen statement is ambiguous, default to exclusion and seek an alternative.
