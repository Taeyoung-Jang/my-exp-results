# Recipe Screening Workflow

The **Recipe Screening Workflow** is a structured routine for people who need to avoid soy and gluten. It was introduced in [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]] and provides a simple, repeatable set of steps that turn ingredient checking into an automatic habit.

## Core Steps
1. **Select a baseline recipe** – start with a few trusted recipes that are already known to be safe.
2. **Initial scan** – read the ingredient list quickly to spot obvious soy or gluten items.
3. **Targeted red‑flag scan** – perform a second, focused pass looking specifically for the soy and gluten red‑flag terms (e.g., soy sauce, malt, hydrolyzed wheat protein, natural flavors, etc.).
4. **Add to shopping list** – place all needed items on a running list, marking any that have not been verified as safe.
5. **In‑store label check** – when shopping, pause on the marked items, read the allergen line first, then verify the presence of any red‑flag ingredients.
6. **Decision rule** – apply the [[concepts/when-in-doubt-rule]]: if a label says “may contain” or indicates processing in a facility with soy or gluten, treat the product as unsafe unless you have a prior trusted verification.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The workflow relies on **[[concepts/allergen-label-checking]]** to efficiently verify product labels during the shopping phase.
- It incorporates the **[[concepts/when-in-doubt-rule]]** as the final safety decision point.

## Benefits
- **Consistency** – the same routine is used for every recipe and shopping trip, reducing the chance of missed allergens.
- **Efficiency** – by limiting detailed checks to flagged items, the process saves time while maintaining safety.
- **Automation** – the soy and gluten filters become mental defaults, making the workflow feel automatic over time.
