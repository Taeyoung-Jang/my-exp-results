# When-in-Doubt Rule

**Definition**: A quick decision guideline that classifies any food product with ambiguous allergen information as unsafe, unless the brand has been previously verified as safe for the user’s sensitivity.

## Core Principle
- If a label includes phrases such as **“may contain”** or **“processed in a facility with soy or gluten,”** the item should be avoided.
- When an ingredient is listed without a clear source (e.g., “natural flavors,” “seasonings”), assume it may contain the allergen.
- The rule only makes an exception when the consumer has already confirmed that a specific brand reliably meets their dietary restrictions.

## Application in Workflow
The rule is integrated into the broader **[[concepts/recipe-screening-workflow]]** and **[[concepts/allergen-label-checking]]** processes described in the source document. After scanning a recipe or product label for known red‑flag terms, the *When‑in‑Doubt Rule* provides the final safety check before adding the item to a shopping list or meal plan.
