# Summary of Session 34 (2024‑07‑21)

**Main focus:** Practical advice for establishing comforting morning routines after moving, with a special emphasis on a grounding “first‑thing‑you‑see” corner for small apartments in hot, dry climates.

## Key Ideas
- **Morning anchoring rituals** – simple sensory habits (favorite drink by a window, short neighborhood walk) help map a new environment and foster ownership. ([[concepts/morning_rituals]])
- **“First‑thing‑you‑see” corner** – a small, uncluttered surface that you notice immediately on waking; it should contain 2‑3 elements that are calming, personal, and low‑maintenance. ([[concepts/first_thing_you_see_corner]])
- **Desert‑inspired corner recipes**
  1. **Ceramic bowl setup:** sand‑colored bowl on a nightstand, a tiny [[entities/aloe_vera]] in a terra‑cotta pot, plus a smooth stone or desert glass.
  2. **Narrow wall shelf:** miniature barrel cactus or pet‑safe succulent, paired with a small landscape print.
  3. **Windowsill arrangement:** squat planter with haworthia (plain text), next to a low wooden block holding a favorite photo.
- **Pet‑safe succulents** – haworthia, [[entities/gasteria]], and [[entities/sempervivum]] are non‑toxic, tolerate bright indirect light, and survive occasional missed waterings. ([[concepts/pet_safe_succulents]])
- **Plants to avoid** – jade plant, snake plant (plain text), and [[entities/euphorbia]] (toxic or irritating to pets).
- **Light‑intensity cue** – washed‑out coloration or reddish/orangey leaf edges signal excess light before scorching occurs; adjust placement accordingly. ([[concepts/light_intensity_cues]])
- **Maintenance tips** – rotate pots weekly, provide shade from harsh afternoon sun, allow soil to dry between waterings.
