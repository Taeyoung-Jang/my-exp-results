## Pet‑Safe Succulents

Pet‑safe succulents are plant species that pose little to no toxicity risk to common household pets (cats, dogs) while also tolerating the bright, dry conditions often found in small apartments in desert‑like climates. They are ideal for creating a grounding "first‑thing‑you‑see" corner as described in [[concepts/first_thing_you_see_corner]] and complement low‑maintenance [[concepts/morning_rituals]].

### Key Criteria
1. **Non‑toxic to pets** – The plant should not contain poisonous compounds that could harm cats or dogs if ingested.
2. **Low water demand** – Ability to survive occasional missed watering, making them suitable for busy routines.
3. **Tolerance of bright, indirect light** – Can thrive near a sunny window without scorching, especially when monitored using [[concepts/light_intensity_cues]].
4. **Compact growth** – Fits comfortably on narrow shelves, nightstands, or windowsills in small spaces.

### Recommended Pet‑Safe Succulents
- **[[entities/gasteria]]** – Small, rosette‑shaped leaves, safe for pets, prefers bright indirect light.
- **[[entities/hawthornia]]** – Often marketed as "Haworthia," non‑toxic, tolerates dry indoor air, and holds color well.
- **[[entities/sempervivum]]** – Also known as "hen and chicks," pet‑safe, forms tight rosettes, and endures neglect.
- **[[entities/aloe_vera]]** – Though mildly soothing to skin, it is generally considered safe for cats and dogs in small amounts and thrives in warm, sunny spots.

### Succulents to Avoid (Toxic or Irritating)
- **[[entities/jade_plant]]** – Contains compounds toxic to pets.
- **[[entities/euphorbia]]** – Sap is irritating and can cause severe reactions.
- Snake plant (Sansevieria) – Not listed in the whitelist; mentioned here for completeness but should be referred to by name only.

### Practical Tips for Use
- **Placement**: Position the plant on a narrow wall shelf near the door or on a windowsill, pairing it with a personal object (photo, stone) to create a calming focal point.
- **Light Management**: Watch for washed‑out foliage or reddish leaf tips as early signs of excessive light; adjust distance from the window accordingly, following the guidance in [[concepts/light_intensity_cues]].
- **Watering**: Allow the soil to dry completely between waterings; a week or more is typical in hot, dry climates.
- **Rotation**: Turn the pot weekly to ensure even growth and prevent one side from becoming sun‑bleached.
