## Cold Weather Safety Thresholds

**Definition**: A set of practical wind‑chill bands that inform how long you can stay outdoors and what clothing layers you should use to avoid discomfort and frost‑bite.

**Key Thresholds** (derived from [[summaries/session-12_2023-01-18]]):

- **> 20 °F** – Generally safe with standard winter gear. Minimal extra precautions needed.
- **10 °F – 0 °F** – Increase insulation: heavier coat, wind‑blocking outer layer, full coverage of ears, hands, and neck. Limit exposure to 30–60 minutes.
- **–10 °F – –20 °F** – Require a true winter shell or parka, insulated mittens, and full‑face protection. Plan for the lower end of the 30‑minute window; pause indoors if numbness appears.
- **≤ –20 °F** – High‑risk zone. Wear comprehensive wind‑proof outer shell, thick insulation, mittens, face mask/balaclava, and warm boots. Keep time outside brief and seek shelter at the first sign of skin stinging or numbness.

These thresholds are not medical limits but serve as *rules of thumb* to gauge comfort and risk.

### Interaction with Moisture
When conditions are damp—wet snow, high humidity, or personal sweating—the perceived cold intensifies. As noted in [[concepts/moisture_cold_amplification]], you should shift each wind‑chill band down one step (e.g., a damp 10 °F feels like 0 °F) and dress accordingly.

### Relationship to Wind‑Chill Perception
Understanding how wind removes heat is essential. See [[concepts/wind_chill_perception]] for the physiological basis that underlies these safety thresholds.

### Practical Use
- **Before heading out**: Check the forecasted wind chill.
- **Select the appropriate band** and follow the recommended clothing checklist.
- **Adjust for moisture** by moving to the next colder band.
- **Monitor** for early signs of numbness; retreat to warmth if observed.

These guidelines help you make informed decisions about outdoor activities in cold, windy environments.
