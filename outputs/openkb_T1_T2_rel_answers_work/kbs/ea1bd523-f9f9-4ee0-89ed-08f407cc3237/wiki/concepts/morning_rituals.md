# Morning Rituals

**Definition**: A set of simple, repeatable actions performed at the start of the day that create a sense of continuity, ownership, and calm in a new environment.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-34_2024-07-21]])
- **Sensory anchor** – e.g., preparing a favorite drink and enjoying it by a window you haven’t claimed yet.
- **Brief outdoor walk** – a short, early‑day stroll that maps the neighborhood and reduces the feeling of foreignness.
- **Micro‑setup** – arranging a "first‑thing‑you‑see" corner with a few intentional items (plant, personal object, stone) to signal ownership the moment you wake.

## Why They Work
- **Routine continuity** provides a psychological bridge between the old and new living spaces.
- **Physical movement** (the walk) stimulates spatial awareness and releases endorphins.
- **Visual focus** on a small, curated corner reduces overwhelm and creates a calming focal point.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/first_thing_you_see_corner]] – the specific micro‑setup that serves as a visual anchor.
- [[concepts/pet_safe_succulents]] – low‑maintenance plants often used in the corner for a natural touch.
- [[concepts/light_intensity_cues]] – how to gauge window light to keep the corner plants healthy.

## Practical Tips
1. **Choose a consistent drink** (tea, coffee, citrus water) and pair it with a view.
2. **Schedule a 5‑10 minute walk** each morning, preferably before the heat peaks.
3. **Design the corner** using a small surface, a pet‑safe succulent (e.g., hawthornia, gasteria, sempervivum), and a personal object like a photo or smooth stone.
4. **Monitor light**; if leaves turn washed‑out or reddish, adjust distance from the window (see [[concepts/light_intensity_cues]]).
