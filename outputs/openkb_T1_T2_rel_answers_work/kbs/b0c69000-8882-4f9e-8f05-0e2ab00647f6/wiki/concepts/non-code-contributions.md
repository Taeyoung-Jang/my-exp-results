# Non‑Code Contributions

**Definition**: Activities that impact a project or team without producing directly observable code artifacts, such as architecture decisions, documentation, mentoring, incident response, research, and other forms of knowledge sharing.

## Why they matter

In the context of personal activity tracking, non‑code contributions fill the gaps left by automatic contribution graphs like those of [[entities/github]] or [[entities/gitlab]]. While those platforms capture commits, pull requests, and releases, they miss the invisible work that often drives project success. Highlighting non‑code contributions ensures a more honest and holistic record of professional impact.

## Key elements from the source

- **Planning & Architecture**: Leading design discussions, documenting trade‑offs, and making strategic decisions.
- **Mentoring & Onboarding**: Guiding new hires through deployments, code reviews, and best‑practice sharing.
- **Documentation & Decision Logs**: Creating or updating internal docs, writing decision records, and summarising experiments.
- **Incident & Debugging Work**: Participating in on‑call rotations, troubleshooting production issues, and post‑mortem analyses.
- **Research & Experiments**: Prototyping tools or proofs‑of‑concept that may not be shipped publicly.

These items should be recorded in a lightweight “footnote” to the GitHub graph, as described in [[concepts/footnotes-to-graph]], to avoid duplicating visible activity.

## How to capture them

Use a simple template (see [[concepts/template-design]]) that includes a **Non‑Code Contributions** section. Keep entries brief—focus on outcomes and impact rather than detailed processes. Example entries from [[summaries/session-10_2024-07-15]] illustrate this approach:

- "Led two architecture discussions for an upcoming migration and documented the trade‑offs for the team."
- "Mentored a new hire through their first deployment."

## Relation to other concepts

- Part of a broader **activity‑tracking** strategy that treats work history as a narrative [[concepts/activity-tracking]].
- Serves as the content layer for the **footnotes‑to‑graph** approach, adding context where the contribution graph is silent.
- Implemented using the shared **template‑design** to keep records consistent and easy to share.
