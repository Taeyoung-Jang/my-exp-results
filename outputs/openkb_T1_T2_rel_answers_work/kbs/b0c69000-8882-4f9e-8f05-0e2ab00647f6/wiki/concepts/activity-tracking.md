# Activity Tracking as Narrative

**Definition**: Activity tracking as narrative is the practice of recording professional work as a chronological story, focusing on outcomes, decisions, and context rather than raw metrics or exhaustive commit lists.

## Core Idea
- Instead of a "brag sheet" that lists every commit, hour, or pull request, developers write brief, high‑level summaries that convey *what* was achieved and *why* it mattered.
- This narrative acts as a complement to automatic contribution graphs provided by platforms like **GitHub** and **GitLab**, filling in the invisible work that those graphs cannot show.

## Why Use a Narrative Approach?
- **Clarity** – Readers get a quick sense of impact without sifting through technical details.
- **Trustworthiness** – By separating automatic logs from human‑written context, the record remains honest and easy to verify.
- **Scalability** – A few sentences per period (weekly, monthly, or quarterly) keep the effort lightweight.

## Practical Guidelines (from [[summaries/session-10_2024-07-15]])
1. **Separate automatic logs** – Let the GitHub contribution graph stand on its own; use the narrative as a footnote layer.
2. **Focus on outcomes** – Highlight reliability improvements, time saved, or strategic decisions rather than raw code changes.
3. **Avoid duplication** – Do not repeat commits, pull‑request titles, timestamps, or metrics already visible on the graph.
4. **Include non‑code work** – Architecture discussions, mentoring, incident handling, and documentation belong in the story.
5. **Keep it brief** – A few bullet points or short paragraphs per period are sufficient.

- **Personal Journaling** – Helps developers reflect on growth and prepares material for performance reviews.
- **Team Visibility** – Enables managers to see the full scope of effort without parsing code history.
- **Portfolio Presentation** – Provides a concise, impact‑focused story for external audiences.
