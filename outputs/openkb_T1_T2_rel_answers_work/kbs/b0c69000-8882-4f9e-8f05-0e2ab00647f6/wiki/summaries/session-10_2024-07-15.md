# Summary of session-10_2024-07-15

**Main contribution:** Provides practical advice and ready‑to‑use Markdown templates for developers to record work that isn’t visible on public contribution graphs, while keeping the record concise and non‑duplicative.

## Key concepts
- **Activity tracking as narrative** – Treat work history like a running story rather than a scoreboard. [[concepts/activity-tracking]]
- **Separation of automatic logs and human context** – Use [[entities/github]]/[[entities/gitlab]] contribution graphs for public code and add a lightweight “footnote” layer for private or non‑code effort. [[concepts/footnotes-to-graph]]
- **Scope of what to include** – Capture private repo work, architecture decisions, mentoring, experiments, docs, incident response, etc., but avoid repeating visible commits, PRs, timestamps, or metrics. [[concepts/non-code-contributions]]
- **Template design** – A simple quarterly/monthly Markdown structure with sections: Private Work Highlights, Non‑Code Contributions, Notes for Future Me, Context Not Reflected on GitHub. Also a condensed one‑page version for performance reviews. [[concepts/template-design]]

## Practical outputs
1. **Full Markdown template** for personal logs (headings, bullet points).
2. **One‑page version** suitable for performance reviews, with redacted details but clear impact statements.
3. Example entries illustrating private work (internal service reliability, proof‑of‑concept tool) and non‑code contributions (architecture discussions, mentoring).

## Guidance on what to omit
- Any activity already visible on the public contribution graph (commits, PRs, public repos).
- Detailed timestamps or raw metrics; focus on outcomes, decisions, and impact.

## Benefits
- Keeps a trustworthy, readable record without becoming a “brag sheet.”
- Enables easy sharing of invisible work for reviews or team updates.
- Minimal maintenance effort; can be updated monthly or quarterly.
