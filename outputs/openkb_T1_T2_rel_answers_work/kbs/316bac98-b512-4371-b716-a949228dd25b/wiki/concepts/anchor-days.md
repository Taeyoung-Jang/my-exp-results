# Anchor Days

**Definition**

Anchor Days are the fixed running sessions in a weekly schedule that act as reference points for a beginner endurance program. They give a predictable rhythm—typically an easy run, a longer run, and an optional paced effort—while keeping the overall plan loose enough to adapt to how the athlete feels.

**Why they matter**

- **Consistency** – Having set days creates a habit loop, making it easier to run regularly without the plan feeling overwhelming.
- **Recovery balance** – Because the intensity of anchor days is deliberately low (except the occasional gentle push), the body can recover naturally between sessions.
- **Flexibility** – The surrounding, non‑anchor time can be used for rest, cross‑training, or light activity, preventing burnout.
- **Life integration** – Anchor days are placed on days that naturally fit the runner’s schedule (e.g., Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, Sunday), so training does not dominate personal time.

**Typical structure (as described in [[summaries/session-38_2025-03-11]])**

| Day | Role | Approx. duration / mileage |
|-----|------|----------------------------|
| Tuesday | Easy run (anchor) | 45‑60 min (~5 mi) |
| Thursday | Easy run with optional quality work (anchor) | 45‑60 min (~5 mi) |
| Saturday | Long run (anchor) | 1/3 of weekly mileage, gradually increasing |
| Sunday | Short shake‑out (anchor) | 2‑3 mi, very easy |

**Integration with other concepts**

- During a **[[concepts/cutback-week]]**, the same anchor days are kept but the mileage and duration are reduced, preserving habit while giving the body a break.
- **[[concepts/quality-sessions]]** such as strides or a short tempo can be tacked onto an anchor day (e.g., strides after Tuesday’s easy run) without compromising recovery.
- For athletes who combine running with other sports, anchor days can be aligned with a **[[concepts/weekly-multi-sport-rhythm]]**, ensuring that non‑running activities complement rather than clash with the core runs.

**Practical tip**

If you ever feel overwhelmed, protect the anchor days first—especially the long run—by shortening or swapping the shorter easy runs. This keeps the overall training rhythm intact while allowing flexibility for life’s demands.
