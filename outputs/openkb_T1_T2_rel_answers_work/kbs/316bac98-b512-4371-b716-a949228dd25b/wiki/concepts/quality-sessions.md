# Quality Sessions

**Definition**: Small, targeted workouts—such as strides, short tempo runs, or light pick‑ups—designed to improve running economy and form while keeping overall training stress low.

## Why they matter
- Provide a **stimulus for adaptation** beyond easy mileage without triggering the fatigue of full‑blown speedwork.
- Fit neatly within a **flexible marathon plan** that prioritises consistency and recovery.
- Help maintain **motivation** by adding variety to the routine.

## Typical components (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-03-11]])
| Component | Description | Typical placement |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Strides** | 6‑8 repetitions of ~20 seconds at fast, relaxed pace, with a walk or easy jog back to start. | End of a Tuesday run, every other week. |
| **Short tempo** | 10 minutes at a comfortably hard steady pace, sandwiched between a 10‑15 minute warm‑up and easy cool‑down. | Inserted into a Thursday run, keeping total run time 45‑60 minutes. |

## Guidelines for integration
1. **Frequency** – 1‑2 quality sessions per week are enough for beginner marathoners.
2. **Placement** – Align them with your **anchor‑days** ([[concepts/anchor-days]]): usually after an easy weekday run so that the main long run stays fully recovered.
3. **Intensity** – Keep effort moderate; the aim is to feel “sharp” but not exhausted.
4. **Recovery** – Follow the session with easy jogging and respect the next **cutback‑week** ([[concepts/cutback-week]]) where volume drops, allowing the body to fully adapt.

## Relationship to other concepts
- **Anchor‑days** provide the structural backbone (easy, medium, long) while quality sessions add a focused stimulus on top of those days.
- During a **cutback‑week**, quality sessions can be reduced or omitted to prioritize recovery.
- In a broader **weekly‑multi‑sport‑rhythm** ([[concepts/weekly-multi-sport-rhythm]]), quality sessions can complement cross‑training days without overloading the athlete.

## Practical example (4‑week cycle)
- **Week 1‑3**: Tuesday – 5 mi easy + 6‑8 strides; Thursday – 5 mi easy with 10‑minute tempo.
- **Week 4 (cutback)**: Reduce or skip the tempo, keep strides optional.

By keeping quality sessions brief, purposeful, and well‑timed, you gain speed and efficiency gains while preserving the consistency and low‑risk recovery strategy essential for successful marathon training.
