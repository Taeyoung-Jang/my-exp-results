# Stand‑up Paddleboarding (SUP)

Stand‑up paddleboarding, commonly abbreviated as **SUP**, is a water sport where the participant stands on a board and propels themselves using a paddle. It is highlighted in [[summaries/session-03_2024-07-11]] as an ideal **[[concepts/complementary-outdoor-activity]]** to complement a regular cycling routine because it shifts effort to the core and upper body, minimizing leg strain.

## Key Facts from the Session
- **Low‑maintenance gear**: The core kit consists of an **[[entities/inflatable-board]]**, a **[[entities/paddle]]**, a **[[entities/leash]]**, and a **[[entities/pfd]]**. Optional cold‑water gear includes a light wetsuit.
- **Ease of integration**: SUP fits neatly into a **[[concepts/weekly-multi-sport-rhythm]]**, suggested as a short 30‑40 minute “refresh day” amid a 50‑mile‑per‑week cycling schedule.
- **Adventure mindset**: Even on calm lakes or sheltered bays, paddling can provide an **[[concepts/adventure-mindset]]** by exploring scenic shorelines, bends, or small islands.
- **Beginner‑friendly locations**: Recommended spots are flat‑water environments such as lakes, slow inlets, or sheltered bays that offer scenic interest without demanding advanced skills.

## Benefits
- **Low impact**: Minimal stress on joints and legs, making it a gentle cross‑training option.
- **Portability**: Inflatable boards are easy to transport and store, reducing logistical overhead.
- **Motivation**: Pairing the activity with a favorite podcast or playlist enhances enjoyment and helps maintain consistency.
