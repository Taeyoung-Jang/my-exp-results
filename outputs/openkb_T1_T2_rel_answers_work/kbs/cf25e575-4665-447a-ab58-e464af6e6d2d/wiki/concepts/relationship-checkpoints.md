# Relationship Checkpoints

**Definition**: A structured list of 5‑9 probing questions that couples can discuss to gauge whether their partnership is moving into a serious, long‑term stage and to identify areas of alignment or potential red‑flags.

---

## Overview
Relationship checkpoints are practical conversation prompts designed to surface core values, expectations, and habits that matter most when a partnership deepens. They were articulated in [[summaries/session-06_2024-07-15]] as a way to move beyond superficial milestones (e.g., moving in) toward deeper relational health.

---

## Core Checkpoints (Early‑Stage Alignment)
1. **Future Vision (5‑year outlook)** – Where do you see yourself living, what lifestyle rhythm, and how does that align?
2. **Money Mindset** – How do you view spending, saving, risk, and financial responsibilities?
3. **Conflict Style** – How do you handle serious disagreements?
4. **Family Definition** – What does “family” mean now and in the future (kids, extended family, chosen family)?
5. **Independence Needs** – How much alone time and personal space do you require?
6. **Support Expectations** – What support looks like during stress or personal growth?

Each question is paired with follow‑up prompts to dig deeper and with indicators of **good alignment** versus **red flags**. Successful answers typically show shared values, openness, and the willingness to negotiate, while red‑flags reveal secrecy, incompatibility, or unilateral patterns.

---

## Later‑Stage Checkpoints (Beyond the Early Phase)
7. **Joint Decision‑Making** – How do we make big decisions together? What is the process when we disagree?
8. **Everyday Commitment** – What does commitment look like in day‑to‑day moments (tiredness, stress, routine)?
9. **Navigating Major Life Changes** – How will we handle career shifts, health issues, moves, or family crises?

These later checkpoints focus on collaborative processes and resilience when life becomes unpredictable.

---

## How to Use the Checkpoints
- **Schedule a dedicated conversation** and work through each question together, using the listed follow‑ups.
- **Listen for alignment cues** (shared values, mutual respect) and note any red‑flag patterns.
- **Revisit periodically**, as priorities evolve with life stages.

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts
Relationship checkpoints are a concrete tool for achieving [[concepts/relationship-alignment]] and for recognizing the shift described in [[concepts/relationship-seriousness]]. By systematically exploring these questions, couples can move from casual compatibility to a deeper, more intentional partnership.
