# Handoff Routine

A **handoff routine** is a repeatable, low‑drama procedure that parents use when transferring a child between households. It focuses on predictability, emotional safety, and clear communication, helping the child feel secure during transitions.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-04-13]])
- **Fixed Timing** – Arrive exactly at the agreed time (e.g., 5:00 p.m. or school dismissal) and keep the exchange brief.
- **Neutral or Consistent Location** – Front door, driveway, school pickup area, or a designated public spot.
- **Simple Script** – A short, warm greeting such as “Hi sweetheart, I’m here. Ready to go?”
- **Emotional Acknowledgment** – If the child is upset, say something like “I know it’s hard to switch homes. We’ll get through this together,” then continue calmly.
- **Avoid Pitfalls** – No probing questions, guilt‑laden comments, or parental arguments in view of the child.
- **Fallback Planning** – Include a late‑arrival notice clause (e.g., 15‑minute notice) and a child‑focused meltdown clause that stresses calm continuation.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Supports [[concepts/low_drama_transitions]] by minimizing tension during handoffs.
- Relies on [[concepts/predictable_routines]] to keep bedtime, homework, and screen rules consistent across homes.

## Practical Implementation
1. **Agree on a Standard Location & Time** – Document the default exchange point and exact time in the co‑parenting agreement.
2. **Create a Brief Greeting Cue** – Both parents use the same phrasing to signal the transition.
3. **Prepare the Child in Advance** – Pack bags the night before and rehearse the handoff cue.
4. **Maintain Calm Posture** – Parents stay neutral, avoid discussing grievances, and keep the goodbye short.
5. **Document Emotional Check‑ins** – After the exchange, parents can text a concise, factual note about the child’s mood if needed.

## Sample Clause (see [[summaries/session-33_2025-04-13]])
> "Both parents will support smooth transitions by staying calm, keeping goodbyes brief, and acknowledging the child’s feelings. If the child becomes upset, the exchange proceeds without prolonged discussion, and any relevant information is shared afterward in a brief factual message."
