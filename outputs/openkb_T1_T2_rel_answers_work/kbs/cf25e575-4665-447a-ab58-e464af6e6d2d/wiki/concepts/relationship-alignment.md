# Relationship Alignment

**Relationship Alignment** refers to the extent to which partners share core values, expectations, and behavioral patterns that support a sustainable, long‑term partnership. It is evident when both people can navigate major life domains—future vision, finances, conflict, family, independence, support, decision‑making, daily commitment, and major life changes—without persistent friction.

## Core Elements
- **Shared Vision**: Compatibility in how each partner envisions the next five years (living situation, lifestyle, personal goals).
- **Financial Harmony**: Similar attitudes toward spending, saving, risk, and financial responsibility.
- **Conflict Style**: A mutual approach that prioritises repair over victory, avoiding stonewalling or blame.
- **Family Outlook**: Aligned views on children, extended family involvement, and the broader definition of family.
- **Independence Balance**: Respect for each other's need for personal space while maintaining intimacy.
- **Support Expectations**: Agreements on how partners provide emotional and practical support during stress or growth.
- **Joint Decision‑Making**: Collaborative processes for big choices, ensuring both voices are heard.
- **Everyday Commitment**: Consistent emotional labor and care in routine moments, not just during smooth periods.
- **Adaptability to Change**: Joint strategies for handling career shifts, health issues, relocations, or family crises.

## Alignment vs. Red Flags
- **Good Alignment**: Open dialogue, willingness to compromise, mutual respect, and consistent effort to meet each other's needs.
- **Potential Red Flags**: Secrecy about finances, one‑sided decision power, chronic stonewalling, fundamentally opposite family desires, or imbalance in emotional labor.
