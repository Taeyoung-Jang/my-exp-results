# Relationship Seriousness

**Definition**: Relationship seriousness describes the transition from a comfortable, enjoyable partnership to one where partners actively build a shared future, make joint decisions, and integrate each other's lives.

## Core Indicators
- **Joint Decision‑Making**: Partners consider each other's perspectives in everyday choices and larger life moves.
- **Future Planning**: Conversations about five‑year visions, family structures, and lifestyle preferences become natural.
- **Emotional Safety**: Discussing fears, boundaries, and less‑pleasant topics feels safe rather than threatening.
- **Consistent Support**: Showing up for each other during stress, not just during fun moments.
- **Mutual Investment**: Time, energy, and resources are willingly allocated toward the relationship’s growth.

## Relationship Checkpoints
The concept is operationalized through a set of **relationship checkpoints** that reveal seriousness. These include questions on future vision, money mindset, conflict style, family expectations, independence, support, joint decision‑making, everyday commitment, and handling major life changes. Good alignment on these checkpoints signals seriousness, while red‑flag patterns suggest a less serious stage.
