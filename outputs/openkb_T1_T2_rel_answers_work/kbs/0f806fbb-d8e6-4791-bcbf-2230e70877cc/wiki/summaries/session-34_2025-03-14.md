# Summary of session‑34_2025‑03‑14

**Main focus** – A conversational primer for newcomers to trading, contrasting buying stocks with using derivatives and outlining safe, low‑risk ways to experiment with options.

---

## Key Concepts
- **Stocks vs. derivatives** – Stocks represent ownership; derivatives (e.g., options) are bets on price movement without owning the underlying.
- **Leverage** – One option contract controls 100 shares, turning a small premium into exposure to a much larger notional position [[concepts/options-leverage]].
- **Time decay (theta)** – Options lose value as expiration approaches, even if the underlying price stays flat [[concepts/options-time-decay]].
- **Greeks** – Delta (price sensitivity), Theta (time decay), Vega (volatility sensitivity) are the primary indicators for daily option behavior.
- **Defined‑risk spreads** – Debit spreads (long‑call spread) and credit spreads (short‑call/put spread) cap maximum loss while offering limited upside [[concepts/defined-risk-spreads]].

---

## Practical Advice for Small Accounts
1. **Start with simulation or tiny positions** – Treat the trade as a learning fee, not a money‑making venture.
2. **Single long call/put** – Worst‑case loss equals the premium paid; simplest way to see leverage and decay.
3. **Debit spreads** – Lower cost than a naked call, defined loss, but also capped profit; good for beginners seeking structure.
4. **Credit spreads** – Benefit from time decay; risk is limited to the width of the spread minus the credit received.
5. **Position sizing** – Keep exposure well below account size; avoid large leverage that could “blow up” the account.

---

## Choosing Expiration & Strike Spacing
- **Expiration** – Longer expirations reduce theta pressure; very short expirations accelerate decay.
- **Strike spacing** – Wider spacing costs more but allows more upside; tighter spacing is cheaper but limits profit earlier.
- Aim for an expiration that gives enough time to observe price moves without excessive decay.

---

## Daily Checklist for a Call Debit Spread
- **Stock direction** – Is the underlying moving toward the long strike?
- **Theta impact** – Is the spread losing value faster than expected when the stock is flat?
- **Vega change** – Has implied volatility dropped (IV crush) causing unexpected loss?
- **Time to expiration** – Are we close enough that moves feel “sharp” and less predictable?

### Credit‑Spread Checklist (mirror image)
- Verify the underlying stays away from the short strike.
- Monitor theta benefiting the position.
- Watch for volatility spikes that can widen the spread.
- Adjust expectations as expiration approaches.

---

## Common Pitfalls
- Taking on more leverage than understood.
- Ignoring time decay and letting options “melt.”
- Overlooking volatility shifts (vega) that can erode value.
- Using spreads without grasping the interaction of two legs.
- Letting emotions drive trade size or timing.

---

## Take‑away
For beginners, start with tiny, simulated or real trades, focus on a single long option or a modest defined‑risk spread, track delta/theta/vega, and treat every trade as a data point rather than a profit pursuit. This disciplined, low‑risk approach builds the intuition needed for more advanced strategies.
