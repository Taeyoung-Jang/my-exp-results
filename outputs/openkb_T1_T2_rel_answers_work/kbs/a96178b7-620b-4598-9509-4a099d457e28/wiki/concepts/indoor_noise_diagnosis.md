# Indoor Noise Diagnosis Procedure

**Overview**

The indoor noise diagnosis procedure is a practical, low‑tech workflow for identifying the primary pathways through which external or internal sounds enter a room. It is designed for homeowners or office occupants who want to reduce distracting hums—such as traffic noise—without investing in professional acoustic treatment.

**Key Steps**

1. **Baseline Listening**
   - Choose a time when the unwanted noise (e.g., highway hum) is clearly audible.
   - Record a short impression of the sound level and character (low‑mid rumble, steady hiss, etc.).

2. **Blanket‑over‑Window Test**
   - Hang a heavy blanket or comforter so it fully covers the window frame, overlapping the edges.
   - Listen again from the same spot. If the hum drops noticeably, the window is a major transmission path.
   - *Related concept*: [[concepts/window_acoustic_mitigation]]

3. **Ear‑Sweep Around the Window Frame**
   - With the blanket removed, move your ear slowly along the perimeter of the window—trim, sill, and corners.
   - Mark any spot where the sound becomes louder or sharper (use painter’s tape).
   - These hotspots often indicate air gaps that can be sealed with weatherstripping or caulk.
   - *Related concept*: [[concepts/indoor_noise_diagnosis]]

4. **Vent and Duct Check**
   - Place your ear near HVAC vent grilles and listen for the same hum.
   - Briefly block the vent with a towel (do not obstruct airflow for long). If the noise diminishes, the ductwork is transmitting the sound.

5. **Door Gap Test**
   - Roll a towel or a small piece of fabric against the bottom of the door threshold.
   - Listen for any reduction in the hum. A noticeable change points to gaps around the door that let in ambient noise.

6. **Interpretation Matrix**
   | Test Result | Likely Source | Recommended Fix |
   |-------------|---------------|-----------------|
   | Blanket reduces noise | Window glazing/poor curtains | Install heavy curtains with blackout lining, seal frame gaps |
   | Ear‑sweep hotspot | Air leaks around frame | Apply weatherstripping or caulk |
   | Vent block reduces noise | Duct transmission | Add duct lining or seal vent openings |
   | Door towel reduces noise | Door gap | Install door sweep or seal gaps |
   | No change in any test | Structural transmission | Use [[concepts/sound_masking_strategies]] such as pink/brown noise or a quiet fan |

**Practical Tips**
- No special equipment is needed beyond a blanket, painter’s tape, and a phone for note‑taking.
- Perform the steps in the order listed to avoid redundant effort.
- Document each observation in a simple table to track which fixes have the greatest impact.
