# Sound Masking Strategies

**Definition**: Sound masking involves adding a deliberately chosen background sound to make an unwanted noise less noticeable or intrusive.

## Why It Works
The human brain focuses on quiet or constant sounds. By introducing a steady, pleasant noise, the brain’s attention shifts away from the intrusive hum, reducing the perceived annoyance.

## Types of Masking Noise
- **Pink noise** – balanced across octaves, feels smoother and blends well with low‑frequency traffic hum.
- **Brown noise** – even deeper, emphasizing the lowest frequencies, useful for very rumbling sounds.
- **White noise** – contains equal energy across frequencies; can feel harsh compared to pink/brown.
- **Mechanical sounds** – a small desk fan or air purifier provides natural‑sounding, low‑level airflow noise.

## Placement Guidelines
- Position the source **between the window and the listening position**, typically in a corner near the window, so the mask fills the space before the hum reaches you.
- Keep the source **away from microphones** used in calls; angle it toward a wall to diffuse the sound.
- Adjust volume so the masking sound is audible but not dominant; it should sit just below the level of the traffic hum.

## Practical Steps from Session 08 (2024‑07‑14)
1. Choose pink or brown noise (or a quiet fan) as the masking source.
2. Place the device in a corner close to the window, but not directly on the desk.
3. Test the effect during a typical traffic‑hum period; lower the volume until the hum feels softened rather than replaced.
4. If calls are affected, move the device a few feet away or point it toward a wall.
