# Low‑Maintenance Plant Selection

**Definition**: A design approach that prioritises species with natural tidy habits, low water and pruning requirements, non‑aggressive root systems, and reliable performance in the target climate.

## Core Principles
1. **Compact Growth Habit** – Select cultivars that stay within a predictable mature size, reducing the need for regular trimming (e.g., [[entities/dwarf_inkberry_holly_strongbox]], [[entities/compact_abelia_kaleidoscope]]).
2. **Non‑Invasive Roots** – Choose plants whose roots do not encroach on structures or neighboring beds, preventing fence damage and future relocation work.
3. **Climate Compatibility** – Match plant hardiness to the local USDA zone (zone 7 in the reference design) to avoid winter loss and extra winter care.
4. **Water Efficiency** – Opt for drought‑tolerant species when possible, limiting irrigation demands.
5. **Seasonal Interest with Minimal Upkeep** – Favor plants that provide year‑round texture or repeat bloom without the need for dead‑heading or heavy fertilisation.

## Application in [[summaries/session-34_2025-03-15]]
The session‑34 garden plan illustrates these principles:
- **Evergreen anchor**: [[entities/dwarf_inkberry_holly_strongbox]] – a dwarf holly that remains rounded and requires little pruning.
- **Flowering shrub**: [[entities/compact_abelia_kaleidoscope]] – offers long‑lasting blooms and stays within a 2‑3 ft envelope.
- **Ornamental grass**: [[entities/feather_reed_grass_avalanche]] – provides movement and height, planted in tight clumps (≈18 in spacing) for low maintenance.
- **Climbers**: [[entities/evergreen_clematis_armandii]] (evergreen) and [[entities/compact_viticella_clematis]] (flowering) are chosen for their controlled growth on modern panels, avoiding the rampant behavior of species like wisteria or running bamboo.

## Practical Checklist
- Verify USDA hardiness zone.
- Review mature size and growth habit.
- Confirm root behavior (non‑spreading, non‑suckering).
- Plan planting distances (≈1 ft from fence, ~3 ft between shrubs, ~18 in between grasses).
- Choose climbers that cling to simple supports rather than sprawling.

By adhering to these guidelines, designers can achieve attractive, functional landscapes that stay beautiful with minimal ongoing effort.
