# Window Acoustic Mitigation

## Definition
Window acoustic mitigation refers to practical, low‑cost measures that lessen the intrusion of external sounds—especially low‑frequency traffic hum—through residential windows.

## Core Strategies (from [[summaries/session-08_2024-07-14]])
1. **Heavy, dense window coverings**
   - Use curtains or drapes with substantial weight and a thick blackout lining.
   - Ensure the fabric extends several inches beyond the frame on all sides.
   - Hang the curtains slightly away from the glass to create a thin air gap, which adds extra attenuation.
2. **Blanket‑over‑the‑window test**
   - Temporarily cover the window with a heavy blanket or comforter.
   - If the hum drops noticeably, the window is a primary transmission path, confirming the need for better curtains or sealing.
3. **Sealing gaps**
   - Identify any cracks around the window frame with an ear sweep (see [[concepts/indoor_noise_diagnosis]]).
   - Apply weatherstripping, foam tape, or a bead of caulk to close these leaks.

## Diagnostic Context
The mitigation steps are part of a broader **indoor noise diagnosis** workflow. After confirming the window’s contribution via the blanket test, the next phase involves checking for smaller air leaks around the sash, as recommended in [[concepts/indoor_noise_diagnosis]].

## Complementary Approaches
When structural transmission limits how much sound can be blocked, **sound masking** becomes useful. Choosing pink or brown noise, or a quiet fan, can blend with the traffic hum, making it less distracting. See [[concepts/sound_masking_strategies]] for masking recommendations.

## Practical Implementation Checklist
- [ ] Hang a heavy blanket over the window for a quick test.
- [ ] Measure curtain weight and ensure a blackout liner.
- [ ] Install curtains with >2‑inch overlap on each side and a 1‑2 inch gap from the glass.
- [ ] Perform an ear sweep around the frame; mark any leak spots.
- [ ] Apply weatherstripping or caulk to sealed areas.
- [ ] If masking is needed, place a pink/brown noise source near the window but away from the microphone.
