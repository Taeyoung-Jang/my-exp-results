# Privacy Landscaping

**Definition**: A design approach that creates visual screening in outdoor spaces without making the area feel enclosed, using a balance of structural elements and selective planting.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Rhythmic Structure** – Use repeating anchors (e.g., modern panels or trellises) spaced to provide privacy but leave visual gaps.  
2. **Height Management** – Choose a panel or plant height that blocks sightlines (typically 6‑8 ft) while maintaining openness.  
3. **Strategic Planting Offsets** – Position plants about 1 ft from fences and maintain ~3 ft between shrubs (or 18 in between grasses) to avoid crowding.  
4. **Low‑Maintenance Selections** – Prioritize species with compact habits, non‑aggressive roots, and minimal pruning needs.  
5. **Invasive Species Awareness** – Exclude plants known to spread aggressively (e.g., running bamboo, wisteria, honeysuckle).

---

## Application in Session‑34 (2025‑03‑15)

The design outlined for a 40‑ft backyard fence in USDA zone 7 demonstrates privacy landscaping:

- **Three modern panels** (≈4 ft wide, ~7 ft tall) provide vertical screening while keeping the yard light.  
- **Plant buffer zones** between panels use a single evergreen shrub, a deciduous flowering shrub, and optional grass clumps to fill gaps without forming a dense hedge.  
- **Plant choices** include [[entities/dwarf_inkberry_holly_strongbox]] (evergreen shrub), [[entities/compact_abelia_kaleidoscope]] (deciduous shrub), and [[entities/feather_reed_grass_avalanche]] (ornamental grass).  
- **Climbers** such as [[entities/evergreen_clematis_armandii]] and [[entities/compact_viticella_clematis]] grow on the panels, adding greenery without bulk.
- **Spacing guidelines** follow a 1‑ft offset from the fence and 3‑ft shrub spacing, aligning with [[concepts/spacing_guidelines]].
- The plan highlights **[[concepts/low_maintenance_plant_selection]]** and references **[[concepts/invasive_species_awareness]]** by explicitly avoiding running bamboo, wisteria, and honeysuckle.
