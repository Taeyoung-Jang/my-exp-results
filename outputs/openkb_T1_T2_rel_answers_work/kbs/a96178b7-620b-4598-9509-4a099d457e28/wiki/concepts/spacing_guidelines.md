# Spacing Guidelines for Plantings

**Purpose**: Provide clear, actionable spacing rules that create a private yet airy yard, minimise maintenance, and integrate well with modern trellis structures.

## Key Measurements from [[summaries/session-34_2025-03-15]]
- **Fence offset**: Plant all items about **1 ft** away from the fence. This allows airflow, easy access for pruning, and prevents roots from pressing against the wood.
- **Panel dimensions**: Use **≈4 ft‑wide** modern panels (cedar or slim metal). Space panels **6–7 ft** apart to keep a rhythmic visual flow.
- **Panel height**: Target **~7 ft** for effective sight‑line blocking without a wall‑like feel.
- **Shrub spacing**: Position each shrub **~3 ft** from its neighbours (and from the panel edges). This gives each plant room to develop its natural shape without crowding.
- **Grass clump spacing**: For a trio of ornamental grasses, plant the stems **≈18 in (1.5 ft)** apart, forming a soft mound that fills gaps without turning into a dense hedge.

## Practical Layout Example
1. Install three 4‑ft panels along the 40‑ft fence, each separated by 6‑7 ft of open space.
2. In each open interval, plant either a single shrub (e.g., [[entities/dwarf_inkberry_holly_strongbox]]) **or** a small cluster of grasses (e.g., [[entities/feather_reed_grass_avalanche]]) using the spacing rules above.
3. Attach climbers such as [[entities/evergreen_clematis_armandii]] or [[entities/compact_viticella_clematis]] to the panels with simple eye‑let hooks; the defined panel width keeps vines upright and tidy.

## Why These Distances Matter
- **Airiness**: The 1‑ft offset and 6‑7‑ft panel gaps maintain visual openness while still providing privacy.
- **Low‑maintenance**: Adequate spacing reduces competition for water and nutrients, minimising the need for frequent trimming.
- **Root health**: Keeping plants a foot from the fence limits root encroachment that can damage wooden structures.
- **Aesthetic rhythm**: Repeating a pattern of panel‑shrub‑panel‑grass creates a structured yet natural look, tying into [[concepts/privacy_landscaping]] and [[concepts/modern_trellis_design]].
