# Transition Routines Between Prayer and Work

**Definition**: A set of brief, intentional actions that frame the end of a prayer session and the start of work activities, helping maintain focus, reduce mental friction, and preserve the calm cultivated during prayer.

---

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-31_2025-05-15]])

1. **Settle Phase (≈1 min)** – Before beginning the prayer, take a slow breath, unload any immediate screen distractions, and lay out the prayer rug deliberately. This primes the mind for a shift out of work mode.
2. **Prayer Execution** – Perform wudu if needed and pray at a natural, unhurried pace.
3. **Post‑prayer Pause (1‑2 min)** – Remain seated briefly after completing the prayer. Use a few quiet breaths or a short supplication to acknowledge the completion of the spiritual act.
4. **Transition Phase (≈1 min)** – Stand slowly, fold the rug with intention, and take one final deliberate breath before returning to the workstation. This marks a clear boundary between prayer and work.

---

## Why It Matters
- **Maintains Continuity**: The routine creates a smooth arc rather than a jarring stop‑start, preserving the mental calm gained during prayer.
- **Reduces Cognitive Load**: By externalising the shift with concrete steps, the mind doesn’t need to juggle the to‑do list while praying.
- **Supports Consistency**: A repeatable sequence reinforces habit formation and makes it easier to schedule regular prayer breaks.

## Practical Tips
- **Physical Cue**: Use a distinct spot or a small object (e.g., a marker or prayer mat) to remind you of the transition sequence.
- **Mental Cue**: Verbally note the next work task on a sticky note or in a planner before you finish the prayer, then return to it after the final breath.
- **Timing**: Keep the entire routine under five minutes to fit within typical calendar blocks (e.g., a fifteen‑minute Dhuhr slot).
