# Cancellation Protocol

**Definition**: A concise, two‑step procedure that ensures last‑minute volunteer absences are covered promptly without overburdening the coordinator.

**Context**: Developed for a Saturday food pantry operated by rotating volunteer teams (see [[concepts/rotating-teams]]) in a faith‑based setting. The protocol is outlined in [[summaries/session-09_2022-05-15]].

**Procedure**
1. **Team‑first alert** – When a volunteer cannot attend, they send a single message in their team’s group chat as early as possible (ideally the night before, no later than two hours before start).
2. **On‑call backup response** – The designated on‑call backup for that team confirms within 15 minutes and steps into the vacant slot.
3. **Escalation** – If no backup replies within 15 minutes, the alert is automatically forwarded to the next week’s team, giving them the opportunity to fill the gap.

**Roles Involved**
- The *volunteer* who cancels (initiates the alert).
- The *on‑call backup* (part of the same team, per [[concepts/volunteer-roles]]).
- The *next‑week team* as a secondary safety net.

**Key Benefits**
- **Reduced coordinator load** – The coordinator does not need to intervene unless both primary and secondary teams are unavailable.
- **Predictability** – Volunteers know exactly whom to contact and the timing expectations.
- **Burnout prevention** – By sharing responsibility, no single person feels constant pressure to cover gaps.

**Implementation Tips**
- Clearly list the on‑call backup for each team in the roster template.
- Set up the group chat with a simple keyword (e.g., “cancel”) that triggers the forward to the next team automatically (many messaging platforms support bots or simple forwarding rules).
- Encourage volunteers to send the cancellation notice at least the night before; if that isn’t possible, enforce the two‑hour cutoff.
