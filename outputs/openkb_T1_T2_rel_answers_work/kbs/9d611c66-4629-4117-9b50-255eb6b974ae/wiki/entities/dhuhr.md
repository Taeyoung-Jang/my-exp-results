## Dhuhr (Midday Prayer)

Dhuhr is the second of the five daily Islamic prayers, offered after the sun passes its zenith and begins its decline. It is performed while standing, bowing, and prostrating, following a set sequence of recitations.

### Key facts from [[summaries/session-31_2025-05-15]]
- The user schedules a **fifteen‑minute block** on their work calendar each afternoon specifically to perform Dhuhr in a calm environment.
- A recommended **structure** for this slot includes:
  1. **Settle (≈1 min):** Step away from the screen, breathe, and lay out the prayer rug deliberately – an instance of [[concepts/grounding-ritual]].
  2. **Perform wudu** if needed, then pray at a natural pace, anchored to the routine of the day – an example of [[concepts/habit-anchor]].
  3. **Post‑prayer pause (1–2 min):** Sit quietly, take a few breaths, and optionally recite a short supplication.
  4. **Transition (≈1 min):** Fold the rug, take a final breath, and smoothly return to work – following a [[concepts/transition-routine]].
- The user is advised to note any pending tasks on a sticky note or mental cue before prayer, freeing mental space for focused worship.

### Related concepts
- **Grounding Ritual:** Simple physical cues (e.g., slow breath, hand on chest) that signal a shift from work mode to prayer.
- **Habit Anchor:** Linking Dhuhr to an existing daily habit (such as the calendar block) to make the practice automatic.
- **Transition Routine:** A brief, intentional sequence that marks the end of the prayer and prepares the mind for returning to work.

These practices support consistent, mindful performance of Dhuhr even within a busy work schedule.
