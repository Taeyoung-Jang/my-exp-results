# Renters Insurance

**Definition**: A renters insurance policy provides coverage for a tenant's personal property, liability for injuries or damage caused to others, and additional living expenses if the rental unit becomes uninhabitable.

## Core Coverage Areas
- **Personal Property**: Replaces or reimburses the value of a renter's belongings (clothing, electronics, furniture, bikes, storage‑unit items, etc.).
- **Liability**: Pays for legal costs and damages if the renter is held responsible for injury to a guest or damage to another person's property.
- **Loss‑of‑Use (Additional Living Expenses)**: Covers temporary housing costs, such as a hotel stay, when the rental unit cannot be lived in due to a covered loss.

## Important Distinctions Highlighted in [[summaries/session-06_2023-07-15]]
- **Building Policy vs. Renters Policy**: The building owner's insurance covers the structure (walls, roof, plumbing) but does **not** cover the tenant's personal items or liability.
- **Actual Cash Value vs. Replacement Cost**: 
  - *Actual Cash Value (ACV)* pays the depreciated value of the lost item.
  - *Replacement Cost* pays the amount needed to buy a new, comparable item. Renters should check the declarations page to know which method applies.
- **Commonly Overlooked Items**: Small, high‑value items such as laptops, jewelry, bike storage, and belongings stored in external units are often neglected when setting limits.

## Policy Management After Moving
- **Address Update**: Notify the insurer of the new unit number and building details; this is covered under [[concepts/policy_update]].
- **Limits and Riders**: Review coverage limits and consider adding scheduled item riders for expensive personal items (e.g., laptop, jewelry, camera gear).
- **Premium Impact**: Changing location can affect the premium due to factors like crime rates, fire response, and building construction.

## Roommate Considerations
- A renter’s policy typically **does not** cover a roommate’s belongings or liability unless the roommate is added as a named insured. See [[concepts/roommate_policy]] for details on adding roommates, shared limits, and claims history implications.

## Action Checklist for Renters
1. Verify whether the policy uses **actual cash value** or **replacement cost**.
2. Update the insurer with the new address and unit information.
3. Review and adjust coverage limits and deductible as needed.
4. Add scheduled riders for high‑value items.
5. Decide if a roommate will get a separate policy or be added to the existing one, and read the fine print on shared coverage.
