# Roommate Insurance Considerations

Understanding how roommates affect renters insurance is essential for proper coverage and risk management. The following points synthesize the guidance from [[summaries/session-06_2023-07-15]].

## Coverage of a Roommate’s Belongings
- **Not automatic:** A standard renters policy protects only the named insured’s personal property. A roommate’s items are excluded unless they are specifically added.
- **Options:**
  1. The roommate obtains an independent renters policy.
  2. The primary policyholder adds the roommate as a *named insured* (see [[concepts/roommate_policy]]).

## Liability Implications
- Liability protection follows the same rule: it covers the named insured’s actions. A roommate’s liability is not covered unless they are listed on the policy.
- Splitting rent does **not** alter liability limits; each occupant should have their own liability coverage if not shared on a single policy.

## Adding a Roommate to One Policy
### Advantages
- Single bill and unified coverage limits simplify administration.
- Shared liability protection for both occupants.
- Both parties can claim against the same pool of personal‑property limits.

### Drawbacks
- **Shared claims history:** Any claim filed becomes part of the joint record, potentially affecting future premiums for both parties.
- **Limit exhaustion:** A large loss for one roommate reduces the remaining limits available for the other.
- **Per‑person caps:** Some insurers impose individual caps within a shared policy (e.g., electronics, jewelry).
- **Payout allocation:** The insurer may dictate how funds are divided among claimants; unclear language can lead to disputes.

### Fine‑Print Items to Review
- Definition of "named insured" and whether both roommates have equal rights to modify the policy.
- Whether the insurer requires separate inventories for each occupant.
- Any per‑person limits on high‑value items.
- Conditions for adding or removing a roommate (e.g., notice periods, underwriting approvals).

## Updating After a Move
- Notify the insurer of the new address and unit details (building type, security features) – see [[concepts/policy_update]].
- Re‑evaluate coverage limits and consider **scheduled item riders** for high‑value personal belongings.
- Be aware that a change of address can affect the premium due to location‑based rating factors.

## Decision Checklist
1. Determine whether the roommate will obtain a separate renters policy or be added to yours.
2. If adding, confirm the insurer’s requirements for named insureds and any per‑person limits.
3. Review the shared limits to ensure they cover the combined value of both occupants’ belongings.
4. Update the policy with the new address and any new security features.
5. Document each roommate’s high‑value items, possibly via separate rider schedules.
