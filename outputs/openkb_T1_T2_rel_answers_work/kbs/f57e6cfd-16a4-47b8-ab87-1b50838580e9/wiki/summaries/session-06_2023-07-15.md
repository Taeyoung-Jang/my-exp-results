# Summary of session-06_2023-07-15

**Main topic:** Practical differences between renters insurance and building coverage, and how to manage a personal belongings policy after moving.

## Key Points

- **Building vs. renters insurance** – A building policy protects the structure only; renters insurance covers personal belongings, liability, and loss‑of‑use (e.g., hotel costs). [[concepts/renters_insurance]]
- **Commonly missed items** – Clothing, electronics, bikes, storage‑unit contents, and the distinction between actual cash value (ACV) and replacement cost. 
- **Checking coverage type** – Look at the declarations page for a line stating “replacement cost” or “actual cash value.” If unclear, review settlement language or contact the insurer.
- **Policy updates after a move** –
  - Notify insurer of the new address (unit number, building type, security features). [[concepts/policy_update]]
  - Review limits, deductible, and consider scheduled item riders for high‑value items (laptop, jewelry, camera gear). 
  - Updating address can affect the premium due to location‑based rating factors (crime, fire response, construction). 
- **Roommate considerations** –
  - A roommate’s belongings are not automatically covered; they need their own policy or must be added as a named insured. [[concepts/roommate_policy]]
  - Splitting rent does not change liability coverage; each person needs liability protection.
  - Adding a roommate to a single policy simplifies billing but shares claims history, limits, and liability. Watch for per‑person limits and how payouts are allocated. 
- **Action checklist** –
  1. Locate the declarations page to confirm ACV vs. replacement cost.
  2. Update address and verify building details with the insurer.
  3. Re‑evaluate coverage limits and deductible for the new unit.
  4. Add scheduled riders for high‑value personal items.
  5. Decide whether the roommate gets a separate policy or is added to yours; read the fine print on shared limits and claims history.
