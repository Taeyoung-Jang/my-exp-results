# Cast‑Iron Skillet (product)

The cast‑iron skillet is a heavy, heat‑conductive cooking vessel prized for its ability to maintain steady temperature, develop fond, and transition from stovetop to oven. In the **session‑09_2024‑06‑12** plan it is the primary tool for the weekly sauté‑focused experiments.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-09_2024-06-12]]
- Used for all **sautéing** sessions in Week 1, providing the high, even heat needed to explore the **heat level** knob.
- Ideal for creating a deep **fond** when searing mushrooms in Week 3, which feeds into the pan‑sauce experiments.
- Its retained heat supports the **sensory‑feedback‑loop** by letting the cook quickly assess browning, texture, and flavor development.
- Works together with the companion [[entities/sheet-pan]] for the roasting sessions in Week 2.

## Relevance to Core Concepts
- Demonstrates **[[concepts/foundation-techniques]]** by being the essential tool for mastering sautéing, roasting, and pan‑sauce creation.
- Serves as a platform for **[[concepts/experiment-driven-learning]]**, allowing the learner to adjust a single variable (e.g., heat, pan crowding) each session.
- Enhances the **[[concepts/sensory-feedback-loop]]** by providing consistent heat that makes visual and tactile cues reliable.

## Practical Tips
1. **Pre‑heat gradually** to avoid warping and to achieve the desired browning without burning.
2. **Avoid overcrowding**; the skillet’s mass retains heat but excess moisture from a crowded pan will steam instead of brown.
3. **Use a small amount of oil or butter** to develop a good sear, then deglaze for pan sauces.
4. **Clean and season** after each use to maintain its non‑stick surface and longevity.
