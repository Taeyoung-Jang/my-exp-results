# Sensory Feedback Loop

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A systematic process where cooks observe visual, tactile, and taste cues after each experimental tweak, using those signals to judge success and decide the next adjustment.

---

## Overview
The sensory feedback loop is a core component of **[[concepts/experiment-driven-learning]]**. It turns every cooking session into a short experiment: a single variable (the “knob”) is changed, the dish is cooked, and the result is evaluated through concrete sensory criteria. The loop then informs whether the change moved the dish toward the desired outcome.

---

## How It Works in Practice
1. **Select a single knob** – e.g., heat level, pan crowding, cut size, oven temperature, deglazing liquid, reduction time, dough hydration, or rest time.
2. **Cook the dish** using the chosen knob setting.
3. **Observe sensory cues**:
   - **Visual** – browning, caramelization, color uniformity.
   - **Tactile** – texture of vegetables, dough elasticity, sauce viscosity.
   - **Taste** – balance of flavor, richness, acidity, or mouthfeel.
4. **Judge the result** against a brief checklist (e.g., “edges browned without mush,” “sauce coats a spoon lightly”).
5. **Record the finding** and decide the next adjustment for the following session.

---

## Examples from [[summaries/session-09_2024-06-12]]
- **Sauté heat level:** Look for color on the edges without burning; successful tweak = crisp edges, firm interior.
- **Roasting oil amount:** Aim for a light gloss that promotes browning; success = glossy, browned vegetables, not soggy.
- **Pan sauce reduction time:** Sauce should cling lightly to a spoon; too thin runs off, too thick becomes syrupy.
- **Dough hydration:** More water yields a softer, extensible dough; overly sticky indicates excess.

These examples illustrate how the loop provides immediate, actionable feedback, reinforcing learning of the **[[concepts/foundation-techniques]]**.
