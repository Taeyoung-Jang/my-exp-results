# Grab Order (Consistent Retrieval)

**Definition**: A fixed, top‑to‑bottom sequence for pulling pre‑prepared components from the fridge so that dinner can be assembled with minimal decision‑making.

## Core Elements
1. **Predictable shelf placement** – grains on the top shelf, roasted vegetables in the middle, proteins on the bottom, sauces in the door.
2. **Standardized container labels** – each container shows the food, prep source, and a “use‑by” date (see [[concepts/meal_prep_labeling]]).
3. **Fixed retrieval pattern** – every night the user grabs items in the same order: grain → vegetables → protein → sauce.

## Why It Works
- **Reduces cognitive load** by turning the nightly routine into a habit rather than a series of choices.
- **Speeds up dinner prep**; with the order set, the only remaining task is heating and plating.
- **Supports batch‑cooking with building blocks** – the same components can be recombined into different cuisines without additional searching ([[concepts/building_blocks]]).

## Example from [[summaries/session-36_2025-02-12]]
During the Sunday prep plan the cook stores:
- [[entities/quinoa]] in a clear container labeled “Quinoa — Sun Prep — Use by Fri” on the top shelf.
- A mixed‑roast of broccoli, peppers, and onions (on a [[entities/sheet-pan]]) labeled “Roast Veg — Sun Prep — Use by Fri” in the middle shelf.
- [[entities/chicken-thighs]] and [[entities/tofu]] containers on the bottom shelf, each with its own “use‑by” date.
- Two sauces – [[entities/cilantro-lime-sauce]] and [[entities/soy-ginger-sauce]] – in the fridge door.

Each evening at 6 PM the user follows the **Grab Order**:
1. Grab the quinoa container.
2. Grab the roasted veg container.
3. Grab the protein specified for that night (chicken or tofu).
4. Grab the matching sauce.

The consistent sequence makes the process almost automatic, turning a potentially chaotic weeknight into a reliable, low‑effort routine.
