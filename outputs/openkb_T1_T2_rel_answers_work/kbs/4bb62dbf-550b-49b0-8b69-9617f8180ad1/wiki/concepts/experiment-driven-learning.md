# Experiment‑Driven Learning

## Definition
Experiment‑Driven Learning is a practice‑oriented approach where each learning session focuses on adjusting a single controllable factor (the “knob”) and evaluating the outcome through direct sensory feedback.  The method builds intuition quickly by isolating cause‑and‑effect relationships.

## Core Principles
- **Single‑Variable Focus** – Only one parameter (e.g., heat level, pan crowding, cut size) is altered per session.
- **Sensory Feedback Loop** – Learners judge success using clear visual or taste cues such as browning, texture, or sauce thickness.  See [[concepts/sensory-feedback-loop]] for the feedback framework.
- **Foundation Techniques** – The approach is applied to core cooking skills (sautéing, roasting, pan sauces, simple dough) that transfer across many dishes.  See [[concepts/foundation-techniques]].

## Application in the Cooking Plan (see [[summaries/session-09_2024-06-12]])
- **Week 1 – Sautéing**: Adjust heat, pan crowding, or cut size; evaluate browning and uniform doneness.
- **Week 2 – Roasting**: Vary oven temperature, oil amount, or vegetable size; look for caramelized edges vs. even cooking.
- **Week 3 – Pan Sauces**: Change fond depth, deglazing liquid, or reduction time; assess sauce richness and coating ability.
- **Week 4 – Simple Dough**: Modify hydration, rest time, or thickness; feel dough extensibility and observe cooking texture.

The plan explicitly uses a **cast‑iron skillet** and a **sheet pan** (see [[entities/cast-iron-skillet]] and [[entities/sheet-pan]]) as the primary tools, keeping the environment consistent while the experimental knobs change.

## Benefits
- Accelerates skill transfer by teaching underlying principles rather than memorizing recipes.
- Encourages curiosity and low‑stakes experimentation, reducing fear of failure.
- Develops a personal sensory vocabulary that can be applied to any cuisine.
