# Foundation Techniques

**Definition**: A set of essential, repeatable cooking skills that form the basis for a wide range of dishes, allowing cooks to experiment and adapt without strict recipes.

## Core Techniques Identified

The session‑09_2024-06-12 document outlines four foundational techniques for a beginner‑to‑intermediate home cook:

1. **Sautéing** – quick, high‑heat cooking in a pan, emphasizing heat control, pan crowding, and cut size.
2. **Roasting** – dry‑heat cooking on a sheet pan, focusing on oven temperature, oil level, and vegetable size.
3. **Pan sauces** – deglazing a hot surface to capture fond, then reducing and finishing the sauce; variables include sear depth, deglazing liquid, and reduction time.
4. **Simple dough work** – making quick pasta, focaccia, or flatbread, with attention to hydration, rest time, and thickness.

These techniques are intentionally chosen for a vegetarian kitchen equipped with a **[[entities/cast-iron-skillet]]** and a **[[entities/sheet-pan]]**. Each is paired with a set of *experiment knobs* (single variables to tweak per session) and clear sensory criteria for judging success, embodying an [[concepts/experiment-driven-learning]] approach.

## Why They Matter
- **Transferability**: Mastery of these methods lets a cook apply the same principles to countless ingredients and recipes.
- **Confidence Building**: Repeating a technique with minor variations reinforces muscle memory and intuition.
- **Low Barrier to Entry**: The techniques require minimal equipment and inexpensive, readily available ingredients.

## Connection to Sensory Feedback
The plan couples each technique with a *sensory feedback loop*—specific visual or taste cues (e.g., edge browning, sauce coating a spoon) that indicate whether the experimental adjustment succeeded. This embodies the [[concepts/sensory-feedback-loop]] principle, helping learners internalize cause‑and‑effect relationships in cooking.

## Practical Implementation
The document provides a 4‑week checklist where each session targets one knob for a given technique, includes quick "riff" ideas for variation, and specifies what to look for after cooking. This structure makes the foundational techniques concrete and actionable.
