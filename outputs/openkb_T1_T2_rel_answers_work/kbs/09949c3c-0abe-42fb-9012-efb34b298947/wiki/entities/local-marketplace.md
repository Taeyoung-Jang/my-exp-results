## Local Marketplace (e.g., Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace)

**Type:** other

**Definition**: A local online marketplace is a web‑based platform where individuals list second‑hand items—such as furniture, appliances, and household goods—for sale or trade within a specific geographic area. Popular examples include Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace.

**Key facts from [[summaries/session-08_2024-06-15]]**:
- The document recommends using a local marketplace to acquire inexpensive used furniture when opting for an unfurnished rental.
- Purchasing through these platforms can keep upfront costs low enough to achieve a favorable [[concepts/housing-break-even-analysis]] for a five‑month stay.
- Items sourced from a local marketplace are often resellable, supporting the [[concepts/used-furniture-market]] strategy of buying cheap and recouping a portion of the expense.
- The ease of finding listings nearby reduces transport costs and aligns with the [[concepts/short-term-lease-economics]] considerations discussed in the conversation.

**Relevance to other entities and concepts**:
- Connected to the broader discussion of [[concepts/rental-tradeoffs]] when deciding between furnished and unfurnished housing.
- Often used by newcomers in a [[entities/city]] to quickly furnish temporary living spaces.

**Typical use cases**:
1. **Short‑term relocation** – acquire essential items (bed frame, sofa, desk) without long‑term commitment.
2. **Cost‑saving** – achieve a net housing cost well below the furnished‑rental premium.
3. **Resale potential** – items bought can be listed back on the same marketplace for a partial refund, aiding the break‑even calculation.
