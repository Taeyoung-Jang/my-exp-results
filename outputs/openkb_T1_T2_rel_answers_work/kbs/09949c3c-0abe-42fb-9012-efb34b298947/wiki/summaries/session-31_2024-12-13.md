## Overview
The document provides a concrete 2‑week onboarding checklist for a new role, broken into daily time‑blocks, a set of copy‑paste manager questions, and examples of safe versus unsafe “small meaningful wins”.

## Key Concepts
- **Onboarding checklist** – a day‑by‑day schedule for the first 10 business days, organized into short focused blocks. [[concepts/onboarding-checklist]]
- **Relationship building** – early meetings with manager, immediate teammates, and cross‑functional partners to understand work rhythms and preferences. [[concepts/relationship-building]]
- **Clarifying expectations** – dedicated manager touch‑points to surface priorities, success metrics, and communication styles.  
- **Observation before action** – time allocated for reviewing docs, shadowing meetings, and mapping information flow.  
- **Small meaningful wins** – low‑risk contributions (e.g., fixing documentation, creating templates, cleaning up permissions). [[concepts/quick-wins]]
- **Unsafe quick wins** – changes to core workflows or tooling without sufficient context.

## Manager Questions (copy‑paste)
```
What are the top one or two priorities you’d like me to focus on in my first month?
How will we know I’m on the right track in the first 60–90 days?
Who should I meet with first, and is there any team history or context I should know before I do?
How do you prefer to communicate day‑to‑day, and what’s the best way to keep you updated?
Is there anything I should avoid jumping into too quickly?
```

## Safe “Small Meaningful Wins”
1. Update or tidy outdated documentation that the team uses regularly.  
2. Create an optional template (e.g., meeting‑notes) to streamline recurring work.  
3. Take ownership of a low‑impact, repetitive task that others find annoying.  
4. Refine a slide deck, hand‑off doc, or onboarding note for clarity.  
5. Audit and fix broken links or stale permission settings.  
6. Set up a lightweight daily/weekly summary channel to improve visibility.

## Unsafe Quick Wins (to avoid early)
- Re‑architecting core workflows or team rituals without understanding their rationale.  
- Proposing or implementing tool changes, permission overhauls, or major restructurings before gaining context.
