## Summary of session-08_2024-06-15

The conversation walks through a practical cost‑benefit comparison between **furnished** and **unfurnished** rentals for a five‑month stay in a new [[entities/city]]. Key points include:

- **Pros/Cons of furnished rentals** – immediate move‑in, no upfront furniture costs, but higher monthly rent and limited control over style/quality. See [[concepts/rental-tradeoffs]].
- **Pros/Cons of unfurnished rentals** – lower rent and ability to customize, but requires an upfront investment in furniture and effort to set up and later resell it. See [[concepts/rental-tradeoffs]].
- For a ~5‑month period, furnished housing often appears cheaper when factoring time and hassle, yet unfurnished can be cheaper if the renter sources **used furniture**, minimizes spend, and expects a reasonable resale value. See [[concepts/used-furniture-market]].
- A rough **break‑even analysis**: a $2,300/month furnished unit vs. a $1,900/month unfurnished unit saves $400/month ($2,000 total). To beat this, total net cost of buying‑and‑reselling used items should stay well below $2,000. See [[concepts/housing-break-even-analysis]].
- Suggested **budget and item list** for the unfurnished option (used purchase price → expected resale):
  - Bed frame & mattress: $300‑$400 → $200‑$250 resale
  - Small sofa or chair: $150‑$250 → $75‑$150 resale
  - Desk/table + chair: $100‑$150 → $50‑$100 resale
  - Basic cookware & dishes: $50‑$100 → $10‑$20 resale (often sourced from [[entities/local-marketplace]])
  - **Net cost estimate**: $400‑$600 after resale, comfortably under the furnished premium.
