# Safe Quick Wins

**Definition**: Small, low‑impact actions a new team member can complete within the first two weeks that demonstrate value, build trust, and avoid disrupting existing processes.

These wins are a core part of an effective [[concepts/onboarding-checklist]] and support [[concepts/relationship-building]] by showing attentiveness to team needs.

---

## Why They Matter
- **Visibility**: The results are easy for the team to see and appreciate.
- **Low risk**: They do not alter core workflows, tools, or decision‑making structures.
- **Credibility**: Delivering a tangible improvement quickly signals reliability and a collaborative mindset.

---

## Typical Safe Quick Wins (from [[summaries/session-31_2024-12-13]])
1. **Update outdated documentation** – Clean up or clarify internal guides that the team frequently references.
2. **Create an optional template** – For meeting notes, status updates, or hand‑off documents, providing a consistent format without mandating a process change.
3. **Own a small recurring task** – Take over a low‑stakes, repetitive activity that others find annoying (e.g., weekly report compilation).
4. **Improve an existing slide deck or hand‑off note** – Enhance clarity, visual consistency, or completeness.
5. **Audit and fix broken links or stale permissions** – Ensure that shared resources are accessible and current.
6. **Set up a lightweight communication check‑in** – A brief daily or weekly summary channel that helps surface information without adding ceremony.

These examples align with the broader idea of [[concepts/quick-wins]]: delivering immediate, observable value.

---

## Guidelines for Choosing Safe Wins
- **Scope**: Keep the effort small (a few hours to a day) and the impact localized.
- **Visibility**: Choose something the team regularly uses so the benefit is obvious.
- **No process overhaul**: Avoid altering established workflows, tools, or team rituals.
- **Seek manager approval**: Confirm the idea with your manager before investing time.

---

## Unsafe Quick Wins (to avoid early on)
- Reworking core workflows or team rituals without understanding their purpose.
- Proposing or implementing new tools, permission structures, or major process changes.

These actions can create friction and undermine the trust you are trying to build.
