## Rental Trade‑offs

**Type:** Concept

### Overview
The rental‑tradeoffs concept captures the key advantages and disadvantages of selecting a *furnished* versus an *unfurnished* apartment, especially for short‑term moves. It draws on the practical example in [[summaries/session-08_2024-06-15]], where a five‑month stay is evaluated.

### Pros of Furnished Rentals
- Immediate move‑in; only a suitcase needed.
- No upfront cost for furniture purchase or transport.
- Less time spent assembling, arranging, or later reselling items.
- Often paired with flexible short‑term lease terms, linking to [[concepts/short-term-lease-economics]].

### Cons of Furnished Rentals
- Higher monthly rent (e.g., $2,300 vs. $1,900 in the example).
- Limited control over style, quality, or condition of the provided furnishings.
- Potentially higher overall cost for a multi‑month stay.

### Pros of Unfurnished Rentals
- Lower base rent, saving $400 per month in the cited scenario.
- Freedom to select furniture that matches personal taste and needs.
- Ability to own furniture and take it with you after the lease, reducing long‑term housing costs.
- Opportunity to source inexpensive items from the *local marketplace* and resell them, connecting to [[concepts/used-furniture-market]].

### Cons of Unfurnished Rentals
- Upfront expenditure on essential items (bed, sofa, table, cookware, etc.).
- Time and effort required to purchase, transport, set up, and later resell furniture.
- Risk of lower resale value, which affects the net cost.

### Decision Factors
1. **Length of Stay** – For stays under ~6 months, the convenience premium of furnished housing often outweighs the rent difference.
2. **Market Conditions** – If the local‑marketplace offers abundant, low‑price used furniture, the net cost of unfurnished can be far lower.
3. **Personal Bandwidth** – Assess willingness to manage buying, moving, and selling items.
4. **Break‑Even Analysis** – Compare total rent premium to the net cost of used‑furniture acquisition and resale; see [[concepts/housing-break-even-analysis]] for a structured approach.
