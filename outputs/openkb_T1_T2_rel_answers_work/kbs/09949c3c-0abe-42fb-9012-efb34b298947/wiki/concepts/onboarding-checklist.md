# Onboarding Checklist

**Definition**: A structured, time‑blocked plan that guides a new team member through relationship building, expectation setting, observation, and early contribution during the first 10 business days.

---

## Core Components

1. **Day‑by‑Day Schedule** – A concrete agenda with 1‑hour to 3‑hour blocks covering:
   - Manager intro and priority clarification
   - Systems setup and workspace onboarding
   - Short informal introductions with immediate teammates and later cross‑functional partners
   - Observation periods (reviewing docs, shadowing meetings, mapping information flow)
   - Identification and execution of a *small meaningful win*.
2. **Manager Question Set** – A ready‑to‑copy list of five questions that surface priorities, success metrics, communication preferences, and early‑stage boundaries.
3. **Quick‑Win Guidance** – Examples of low‑risk contributions (e.g., tidying documentation, creating templates, fixing broken links) and a warning about unsafe actions such as re‑architecting core workflows or proposing tool changes too early.

---

## Relationship Building

The checklist emphasizes early **[[concepts/relationship-building]]** through short, agenda‑light chats that focus on teammates’ current work, frustrations, and collaboration preferences. This builds trust and surfaces hidden team norms before any substantive work begins.

---

## Quick Wins

A dedicated section encourages the new hire to select a **[[concepts/quick-wins]]** activity that:
- Is visible yet low‑impact
- Helps relieve a pain point for the team
- Requires minimal decision‑making authority

Unsafe quick‑wins (e.g., overhauling workflows or tooling) are explicitly flagged to avoid early friction.

---

## Benefits

- **Accelerated trust**: Early personal connections reduce onboarding latency.
- **Clear expectations**: Regular manager touch‑points ensure alignment on priorities and success metrics.
- **Early contribution**: A tangible win signals engagement and adds immediate value.
- **Context awareness**: Observation blocks prevent premature changes to established processes.
