# Daily Routine Structure

A **daily routine structure** is a lightweight, repeatable framework that helps new employees maintain control over a meeting‑heavy schedule while still making progress on high‑impact work.

## Core Elements
1. **Morning Priority & Quick Win**
   - Set a single, clear priority for the day.
   - Complete a small high‑focus task before the first meeting to gain early momentum.
2. **60‑Second Meeting Capture**
   - After each call, spend ~60 seconds noting what happened, what you owe, and what others owe you.
3. **Micro‑Gap Utilization**
   - Treat 5–10 minute gaps as either a **micro‑win** (tiny, self‑contained task) or a **micro‑reset** (stretch, hydrate). See [[concepts/micro-gap-utilization]].
4. **Midday Focus Block**
   - Use the longest lunch‑time gap (20–30 min) for a slightly larger chunk of work, such as an onboarding module.
5. **Late‑Afternoon Low‑Effort Wrap‑Up**
   - Handle easy follow‑ups, organize notes, and prepare tomorrow’s priority.
6. **Action‑Item Tracker**
   - A single daily note with “Things I Owe” and “Waiting on Others” sections to avoid hunting through multiple documents.

## Sample Template
The template introduced in [[summaries/session-32_2025-11-12]] provides headings for each of the elements above and can be copied into any notes app.

## Supporting Practices
- **Agenda Clarification**: Use low‑key phrasing to surface meeting goals without seeming pushy. Refer to [[concepts/agenda-clarification-techniques]] for example prompts.
- **Timeframe Specification**: When requesting agenda details, frame the request in terms of the meeting’s timeframe to keep it concise (see [[concepts/timeframe-specification]]).

## Benefits
- Restores a sense of control despite a wall‑to‑wall calendar.
- Ensures actionable items are captured and easily retrievable.
- Reduces cognitive overload by limiting context switches.
- Builds a habit of daily reflection and planning.
