## Micro‑Gap Utilization

**Definition**: A low‑friction strategy for turning 5–10 minute meeting gaps into either a *micro‑win* (tiny, self‑contained task) or a *micro‑reset* (brief physical or mental break), helping maintain momentum without heavy context‑switching.

### Core Elements
1. **Single‑mode approach** – Choose for the whole day whether the gaps will be used for micro‑wins (e.g., watching a short onboarding video segment, adding a quick note) or micro‑resets (stretch, hydrate, breathe).  
2. **Micro‑win examples** – capturing a 60‑second meeting summary, replying to a short email, reviewing a single slide, or progressing a tiny chunk of an onboarding module.
3. **Micro‑reset examples** – standing up, taking a glass of water, doing a brief eye‑movement exercise, or a minute of mindful breathing.
4. **Alignment with daily rhythm** – integrates with the broader [[concepts/daily-routine-structure]] by filling the “tiny gaps” between back‑to‑back calls without adding new, large tasks.

### Why It Matters
- Prevents the feeling of a "wall‑to‑wall" day where time seems to vanish.
- Keeps cognitive load low; short, self‑contained actions avoid deep context switches.
- Accumulates measurable progress on onboarding modules or action‑item lists over the course of a week.

### Practical Steps (from [[summaries/session-32_2025-11-12]])
1. **Identify the gap** – after a meeting ends, note any remaining minutes before the next call.
2. **Choose the mode** – if you’ve committed to micro‑wins for the day, pick a tiny task; otherwise, do a micro‑reset.
3. **Execute quickly** – limit the activity to the actual gap length (5–10 min) and resist the urge to start a larger project.
4. **Log the outcome** – add a brief line in your daily note under the appropriate section (e.g., “Micro‑win: watched 2‑min onboarding video”).
