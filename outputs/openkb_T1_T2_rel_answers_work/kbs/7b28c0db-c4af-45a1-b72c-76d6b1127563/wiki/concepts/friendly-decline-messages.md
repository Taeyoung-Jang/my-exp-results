# Friendly Decline Messages

**Definition**: A friendly decline message is a brief, appreciative response that turns down an invitation without offending the inviter, often providing a brief reason or a future reconnection hint.

## Core Elements
- **Appreciation** – Start by thanking the inviter.
- **Clear Refusal** – State that you cannot attend, using concise language.
- **Reason (Optional)** – Offer a simple context, such as a personal project, to legitimize the decline.
- **Future Outlook** – Suggest a specific timeframe or a vague future opportunity to keep the relationship warm.

## Variations Highlighted in [[summaries/session-02_2025-09-12]]
1. **Short, low‑detail** – e.g., “Thanks for the invite! I’m busy with a backyard project this month.”
2. **Personal, bummed tone** – e.g., “I’m genuinely bummed to miss it; the renovation is tying me up.”
3. **Group‑chat casual** – informal phrasing that feels conversational rather than an announcement.
4. **Specific timeframe** – mentioning “mid‑next month” or “once the reno’s done” to set expectations.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/communication-tone-variation]] – Adjusting the emotional tone (brief, bummed, warm) of the decline.
- [[concepts/timeframe-specification]] – Including explicit future windows to reconnect.

## Practical Tips
- Keep the message under two sentences for text messages.
- Use simple, relatable language (no formal jargon).
- Match the medium: slightly more complete for email, very concise for group chats.
- Align the tone with your relationship to the invitees; closer friends may merit a warmer phrasing.
