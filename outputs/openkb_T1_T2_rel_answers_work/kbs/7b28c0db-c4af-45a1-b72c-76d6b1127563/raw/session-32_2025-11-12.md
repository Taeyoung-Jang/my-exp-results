**Assistant:** It can feel like the whole day evaporates when you’re bouncing from one meeting to the next, but a few small tweaks can give you back a sense of control.

One trick is to create tiny “anchors” in your day. Before each meeting starts, jot down the one thing you want to walk away with. After it ends, take sixty seconds to note any action items. It keeps your brain from blending everything together and gives you a running record you can actually refer to later.

If your schedule has even the smallest gaps, treat them as intentional reset moments. A quick stretch, a glass of water, or skimming your notes helps break the feeling of an endless Zoom tunnel. And if you have any say in scheduling, try blocking a short “focus window” once a day, even if it’s only twenty minutes. People tend to respect a blocked calendar if it looks official.

Finally, set one clear priority for the day each morning. You don’t need a long to‑do list; just decide on the single thing you want to feel good about finishing. Even on meeting-heavy days, checking off one meaningful task keeps the whole day from feeling lost.
