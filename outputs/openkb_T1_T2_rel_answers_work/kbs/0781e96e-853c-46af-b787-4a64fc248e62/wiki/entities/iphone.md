# iPhone

**Type:** product

The iPhone is Apple’s line of smartphones that forms the core of the iOS ecosystem. It provides tight integration with Apple’s wearables, health platform, and services, making it a pivotal device for users who rely on seamless syncing and convenience.

## Role in Wearable Ecosystems

- Serves as the primary companion for the [[entities/apple-watch]], enabling instant background sync of health data and notifications.
- Supports Apple Pay for contactless payments directly from the watch and phone.
- Interacts with third‑party wearables such as [[entities/garmin]] devices, though integration is less automatic than with Apple’s own products.

## Integration with Apple Health

- Acts as the hub for the [[concepts/apple-health-sync]] workflow; health metrics from Apple Watch flow directly into the Health app without user intervention.
- When paired with Garmin devices, health data (steps, heart rate, sleep) can be forwarded to Apple Health, but the sync is manual and less immediate.

## Interaction with Garmin Devices

- Garmin watches can receive notifications from an iPhone, but users cannot reply directly from the watch.
- Payment functionality via [[entities/garmin-pay]] is limited compared to Apple Pay, which works natively with the iPhone.
- Syncing of Garmin data occurs when the Garmin Connect app is opened or the watch reconnects, reflecting the [[concepts/battery-life-tradeoff]] between longer battery life and automatic sync.

## Battery Considerations

- The iPhone’s battery life is independent of the wearable but influences how often users can charge both devices. Users often charge the Apple Watch nightly due to its ~1‑day battery, whereas Garmin models like [[entities/garmin-venu-3]] or [[entities/garmin-forerunner-265]] can last a week.
