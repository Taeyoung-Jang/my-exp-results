## Sleep & HRV Metrics

**Definition**: Sleep and heart‑rate‑variability (HRV) metrics are quantitative measurements used to evaluate sleep quality, recovery status, and autonomic nervous system balance. They are core signals in modern health‑tracking wearables.

### Why the metrics matter
- **Sleep quality**: Provides insight into sleep stages, duration, disturbances, and overall restorative value. Better sleep tracking helps users understand patterns that affect daytime energy and long‑term health.
- **HRV**: Reflects the variation in time between heartbeats. Higher HRV generally indicates good recovery and lower stress, while decreasing HRV can signal fatigue or over‑training.

### Wearable ecosystem perspective
- **[[entities/garmin]]** devices (especially the [[entities/garmin-venu-3]], [[entities/garmin-forerunner-265]], and [[entities/garmin-vivovactive-5]]) are praised for their **deep sleep analysis** and **HRV/recovery scores**. They capture sleep stages, movement, and HRV continuously, then translate the data into actionable insights like *Body Battery* and *readiness* scores.
- **[[entities/apple-watch]]** also tracks sleep and HRV, but its analytics are less granular than Garmin’s. Apple focuses on sleep duration and basic HRV trends within the Health app.
- **[[entities/fitbit]]** and **[[entities/samsung-galaxy-watch]]** provide basic sleep and HRV data, suitable for casual users but lacking the detailed recovery metrics found in Garmin.

### Integration with health platforms
- Data from Garmin can be exported to **[[concepts/apple-health-sync]]**, where basic metrics (steps, heart rate, sleep) appear in Apple Health. However, the richer HRV‑derived scores (e.g., *Body Battery*) remain inside Garmin Connect.
- The **[[concepts/health-tracking-depth]]** of a device directly influences how useful the sleep/HRV metrics are for long‑term health trends.

### Practical considerations
- **Battery life**: Accurate, continuous HRV and sleep monitoring require a device that can run several days without charging. This aligns with the **[[concepts/battery-life-tradeoff]]** – Garmin’s multi‑day battery supports uninterrupted tracking, whereas the **[[entities/apple-watch]]** typically needs daily charging.
- **iPhone integration**: For iPhone users, the **[[concepts/iphone-integration]]** experience is smoother with Apple Watch, but Garmin still provides reliable sync (though not as seamless).
- **Gotchas**: When using Garmin, users should be aware of **[[concepts/garmin-gotchas]]**, such as limited notification replies and lack of universal payment support.

### Model recommendations for sleep/HRV focus
The **[[concepts/garmin-model-recommendations]]** highlight three Garmin models that balance sleep/HRV depth with decent iPhone notifications and 5‑7 day battery life:
- **[[entities/garmin-venu-3]]** – best overall health suite with AMOLED display.
- **[[entities/garmin-forerunner-265]]** – lighter, sportier, still strong sleep/HRV.
- **[[entities/garmin-vivovactive-5]]** – affordable, core metrics covered.

These models excel at delivering continuous, high‑quality **[[concepts/sleep-hrv-metrics]]** without the need for nightly charging.
