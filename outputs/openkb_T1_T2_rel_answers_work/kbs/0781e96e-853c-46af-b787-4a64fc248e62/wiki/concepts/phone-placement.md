# Phone Placement

**Definition**: Phone placement is the practice of deliberately locating a smartphone in a spot that creates physical friction, making it less convenient to grab the device and thereby helping reduce habitual checking and overall screen time.

---

## Why it matters
In the *session-35_2024-06-15* conversation, the assistant recommends keeping the phone **out of arm’s reach** at night—e.g., on a dresser across the room or in the hallway. This simple change breaks the reflex of reaching for the phone the moment you wake up or lie down, curbing the “doom‑checking” habit that often inflates daily usage.

---

## Core principles
1. **Physical distance** – Place the phone far enough that you have to get out of bed or stand up to retrieve it.
2. **Routine separation** – Pair the placement with a dedicated alarm clock (preferably a [[concepts/dumb-alarm-clock]]) so the phone is not the default wake‑up device.
3. **Consistent location** – Use the same spot each night to build a predictable habit, making the friction reliable.
4. **Contextual cues** – Position the phone where visual cues (like a lamp or a nightstand) reinforce the idea that the phone belongs elsewhere during sleep.

---

## Practical tips from the source
- **Nighttime strategy**: Put the phone on a dresser across the room or in a hallway instead of on the bedside table.
- **Morning buffer**: Before touching the phone, perform a small non‑phone task (e.g., brushing teeth, making coffee) to create a mental break.
- **Complementary devices**: Use a **basic alarm clock** rather than a smart speaker; the clock provides a simple wake‑up signal without the temptation of voice assistants.
