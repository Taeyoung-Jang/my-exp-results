# Distraction Reduction

**Definition**: The practice of using dedicated, single‑purpose gadgets or habits to limit the attention‑draining notifications and temptations of a smartphone.

## Why it matters
In the conversation from [[summaries/session-35_2024-06-15]], the user expressed a desire to reduce phone‑time by adopting tools that do one thing well and avoid the constant pull of apps and alerts. The assistant highlighted that distraction reduction is achieved when a device:
- Performs its core function without extra features (e.g., an analog‑watch that only tells time).
- Requires no frequent charging or software updates, removing the need to check battery status.
- Lacks connectivity that could surface notifications.

## Implementation tips from the source
1. **Physical separation** – Place the phone on a dresser across the room or in a hallway at night. This simple change leverages the concept of [[concepts/phone-placement]] to break the reflex of immediate phone checking.
2. **Use a "dumb" device** – Opt for a basic alarm clock rather than a smart speaker to avoid voice‑assistant interruptions. See [[concepts/dumb-alarm-clock]] for more details.
3. **Create a buffer routine** – Perform a small, non‑phone task (e.g., brush teeth, make coffee) before touching the phone in the morning. This habit adds friction and reinforces the distraction‑reduction goal.
