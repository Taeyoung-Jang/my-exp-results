# Dumb Alarm Clock

A **dumb alarm clock** is a standalone time‑keeping device that provides only the core function of waking you up, without any smart features such as voice assistants, notifications, or internet connectivity. Because it lacks these distractions, it can serve as a powerful tool for reducing reliance on a smartphone, especially during bedtime and morning routines.

## Why use a dumb alarm clock?
- **Distraction reduction** – By removing the phone from the immediate reach at wake‑up and sleep times, it prevents the habitual checking of messages, news, or apps that often happen when a smart device is used as an alarm. [[concepts/distraction-reduction]]
- **Physical separation** – Placing the phone away from the bed (e.g., on a dresser across the room) creates a small friction point that encourages you to get up before accessing the phone. [[concepts/phone-placement]]
- **Simplicity** – The device offers a single, reliable function: ringing at the set time. This simplicity reduces the temptation to engage with additional features such as music streaming or smart home controls.

## Practical setup tips (from [[summaries/session-35_2024-06-15]])
1. **Choose a basic model** – Select a simple, inexpensive [[entities/basic-alarm-clock]] without connectivity or voice‑activated capabilities.
2. **Position the phone out of arm’s reach** – Place the smartphone on a dresser, shelf, or in a hallway so you must physically get out of bed to use it.
3. **Create a morning buffer** – After the alarm rings, perform a small, non‑phone task (e.g., brush your teeth, make coffee) before checking the phone. This habit reinforces a calmer start to the day.
4. **Keep the clock on a nightstand** – Ensure the alarm clock is within easy reach for waking, but the phone remains distant.

## When to prefer a dumb alarm clock over a smart speaker
- **Avoiding voice‑assistant prompts** – Smart speakers often deliver news briefings, weather updates, or allow you to ask for information, which can pull you back into the digital loop.
- **Limiting accidental screen time** – A dumb clock has no screen, so there’s no visual lure for scrolling.
- **Preserving battery life** – Unlike smart speakers that stay connected to Wi‑Fi, a basic clock uses minimal power and can run for years on a single set of batteries.
