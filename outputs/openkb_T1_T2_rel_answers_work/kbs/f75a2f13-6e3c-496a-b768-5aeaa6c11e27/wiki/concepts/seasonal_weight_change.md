# Seasonal Weight Change

**Definition**: Seasonal weight change refers to the typical, modest shifts in body weight that occur during different times of the year, especially in winter, due to changes in activity, diet, sleep, and fluid balance.

## Key Drivers (as discussed in [[summaries/session-31_2025-09-14]])
- **Reduced physical activity**: Cold weather leads many people to move less, lowering daily caloric expenditure.
- **Comfort‑food consumption**: Higher intake of calorie‑dense, warm foods during colder months can increase energy intake.
- **Sleep pattern alterations**: Changes in bedtime or sleep quality affect hormones that regulate hunger and metabolism.
- **Fluid balance**: Dry indoor air and higher salt intake in winter can cause temporary water retention or loss, making the scale bounce.

## How to Recognize Normal Seasonal Fluctuation
- Weight changes are **small** (usually < 2–3 % of body weight) and **gradual** over weeks.
- The direction of change may **reverse** as seasons shift (e.g., weight may increase again in spring).
- No accompanying concerning symptoms such as persistent fatigue, tachycardia, or digestive issues.

## When to Seek Medical Advice
- Weight change is **significant** (rapid > 5 lb loss/gain) or continues despite returning to normal habits.
- Presence of **red‑flag symptoms** (unexplained fatigue, fast heart rate, swelling, appetite shifts, pain, etc.).
- If weight loss coincides with medication adjustments (e.g., thyroid medication) and you suspect a dosage effect.
