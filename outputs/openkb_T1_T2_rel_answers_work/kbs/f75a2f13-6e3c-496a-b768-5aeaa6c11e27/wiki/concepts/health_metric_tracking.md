# Health Metric Tracking

**Definition**: A lightweight, systematic approach to recording basic health indicators—weight, appetite, energy, sleep, activity, and resting heart rate—to differentiate normal seasonal fluctuations from potentially concerning changes.

## Why It Matters
- Provides concrete evidence for self‑assessment and for discussions with healthcare providers.
- Helps spot trends that may signal underlying conditions (e.g., thyroid dose effects) before symptoms become severe.
- Supports clear, concise communication with clinicians, reducing the need for guess‑work.

## Core Elements to Track (as suggested in [[summaries/session-31_2025-09-14]])
1. **Weight Log** – Record weight weekly (or bi‑weekly) with date.
2. **Appetite & Food Intake** – Note any noticeable increase or decrease compared to your normal baseline.
3. **Energy & Fatigue** – Briefly describe daily energy levels (e.g., "normal", "low", "more jittery").
4. **Sleep Quality** – Track sleep duration and subjective quality (e.g., restful, broken).
5. **Resting Heart Rate (RHR)** – Capture the morning RHR from a smartwatch or manual measurement; compare to personal baseline.
6. **Symptoms** – Jot down any new or unusual signs such as warmth intolerance, shakiness, digestive changes, or mood shifts.

## Practical Tips
- **Keep it Simple**: Use a notebook, spreadsheet, or phone app; consistency beats perfection.
- **Contextual Notes**: Add brief remarks about lifestyle changes (stress, activity level, diet adjustments) that might explain metric shifts.
- **Summarize Periodically**: After 4–6 weeks, create a short paragraph summarizing trends and any concerns to share with a doctor.

## Example Entry
```
Date: 2025-10-01
Weight: 155 lb (down 3 lb since 2025-09-01)
Appetite: Slightly reduced, skipping breakfast most days
Energy: Generally fine, but occasional jitteriness mid‑day
Sleep: 6.5 hrs, woke once
RHR: 68 bpm (baseline 62)
Symptoms: Feeling warmer than usual, mild hand tremor
Notes: Reduced outdoor walks due to colder weather; thyroid dose increased 2025-09-10.
```

By maintaining this basic log, individuals gain a clear picture of whether observed changes fall within normal fluctuation or warrant medical evaluation.
