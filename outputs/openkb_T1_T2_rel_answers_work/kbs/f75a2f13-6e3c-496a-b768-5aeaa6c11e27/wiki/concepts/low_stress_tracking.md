# Low‑Stress Tracking

**Definition**: Low‑Stress Tracking refers to a set of gentle, non‑obsessive habits for observing health and fitness changes over time, focusing on neutral observations rather than numeric judgment.

## Core Principles
- **Neutral checkpoints**: Choose a few simple markers (e.g., clothing fit, energy levels, movement ease) and note them occasionally.
- **Brief, qualitative notes**: Write one‑sentence observations instead of scores or targets.
- **Consistency without rigidity**: Use the same context each week (same time of day, same outfit) but avoid strict schedules.
- **Emotional safety**: Treat data as information, not verdicts; if a marker triggers anxiety, shift focus to other markers.

## Recommended Markers (from [[summaries/session-02_2025-06-15]])
1. **Clothing fit** – Notice whether garments feel looser, snugger, or unchanged.
2. **Energy/Mood** – Record a short line about overall vitality or mood.
3. **Movement ease** – Describe how everyday activities (walking, stairs, usual workout) feel.
4. **Optional weight** – If tracked, log the number once a week at a consistent time and move on, following the approach of [[concepts/neutral_weight_logging]].

These markers collectively form the [[concepts/functional_progress_markers]] that signal gradual physical changes without heavy emphasis on the scale.

## Simple Weekly Template
```
Weekly Check‑In
- Clothes: note fit (looser, snugger, same) – no interpretation.
- Energy: brief comment on overall energy or mood.
- Movement: how the usual walk/stairs/workout felt.
- Weight (optional): number only, treat like checking the weather.
```

## Benefits
- Reduces anxiety and the risk of obsessive weighing.
- Encourages focus on functional improvements and well‑being.
- Provides a sustainable habit that can be maintained long‑term.
