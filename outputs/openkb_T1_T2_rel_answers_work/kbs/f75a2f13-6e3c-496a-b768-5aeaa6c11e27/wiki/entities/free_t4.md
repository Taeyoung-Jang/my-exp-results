# Free Thyroxine (Free T4)

Free T4 is the biologically active portion of the hormone thyroxine circulating in the blood without being bound to proteins. It is commonly measured alongside **TSH** to evaluate thyroid status, especially when adjusting **thyroid medication** dosage.

## Relevance in Session 31 (2025‑09‑14)
- The user noted a recent adjustment of their thyroid medication and subsequent gradual weight loss.
- The assistant suggested checking **Free T4** (and TSH) to determine whether the dose might be slightly high.
- Tracking weight, resting heart rate, and symptoms was recommended before contacting the endocrinologist, with a possible request for repeat labs including **Free T4**.

## Why Free T4 Matters
- Reflects the active hormone available to tissues, providing a direct view of thyroid hormone action.
- Helps differentiate between over‑replacement (elevated Free T4, suppressed TSH) and under‑replacement (low Free T4, elevated TSH).
- Used in conjunction with symptom tracking such as [[concepts/thyroid_dose_effects]] and [[concepts/health_metric_tracking]].

## Typical Clinical Context
- Patients on **thyroid_medication** may have their dose adjusted based on symptom changes and lab results.
- A modest increase in Free T4 can explain subtle signs like increased resting heart rate, warmth intolerance, and slight weight loss, as discussed in the session.
