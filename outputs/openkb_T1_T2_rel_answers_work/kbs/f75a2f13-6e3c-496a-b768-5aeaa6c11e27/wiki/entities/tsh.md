# Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH)

**Type:** other (hormone)

TSH is produced by the pituitary gland and controls the production of thyroid hormones (T3 and T4). It is a primary laboratory marker used to assess thyroid function, especially when adjusting **[[entities/thyroid_medication]]** dosage.

## Relevance in Session 31
- The user reports gradual weight loss after a recent thyroid medication dose change.
- The assistant recommends checking **TSH** (alongside **[[entities/free_t4]]**) to determine if the dose is too high.
- Tracking **resting heart rate** and weight trends, as suggested in **[[concepts/health_metric_tracking]]**, helps decide when to request repeat labs.

## Clinical Context
- Elevated TSH often indicates under‑replacement (hypothyroidism), while suppressed TSH can suggest over‑replacement or hyperthyroidism.
- Small dose adjustments can subtly shift TSH, leading to **[[concepts/thyroid_dose_effects]]** such as mild weight loss, heat intolerance, or increased heart rate.

## Practical Monitoring
- When noticing unexplained weight changes, record weight, appetite, energy, sleep, and resting heart rate.
- Include the date of the medication change and any symptoms in a brief summary for the clinician.
- A concise message to the endocrinologist might reference the recent dose change, the observed weight loss, the altered resting heart rate, and request repeat **TSH** (and **free T4**) labs.
