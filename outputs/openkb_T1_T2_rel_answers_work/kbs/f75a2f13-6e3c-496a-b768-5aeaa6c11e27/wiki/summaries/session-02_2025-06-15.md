# Summary of session-02_2025-06-15

**Main idea:** Provide gentle, low‑pressure ways to monitor fitness and nutrition progress without becoming obsessive.

## Key concepts
- **[[concepts/low_stress_tracking]]** – Emphasizes neutral, occasional checkpoints rather than constant measurement.
- **Functional progress markers** – Using how clothes fit as a subtle indicator of body changes, noting ease of everyday activities (stairs, walking, workouts), and briefly recording daily energy levels, mood, and sleep to see patterns.  
- **[[concepts/neutral_weight_logging]]** – If weight is tracked, treat it like a weather check: same time, no interpretation.
- **Weekly check‑in template** – A simple copy‑paste template for a non‑judgmental weekly note.

## Recommended low‑pressure markers (choose 2–3)
1. **Clothes fit** – Observe whether garments feel looser, snugger, or unchanged.
2. **Energy/Mood** – Write a short line about overall vitality or mood.
3. **Movement ease** – Describe how a regular walk, stairs, or usual workout feels (easier, harder, same).
4. **Optional weight** – Record the number once a week at a consistent time, then move on.

## Simple weekly check‑in template
```
Weekly Check‑In
- Clothes: note fit (looser, snugger, same) – no interpretation.
- Energy: brief comment on overall energy or mood.
- Movement: how the usual walk/stairs/workout felt.
- Weight (optional): number only, treat like checking the weather.
```

## Practical tips
- Keep entries brief (one sentence per marker).
- Use the same time and context each week for consistency.
- View data as observations, not judgments; if it triggers emotion, step back to the other markers.
- Store the template in a notes app for easy copy‑paste.

**Conclusion:** By focusing on a few neutral observations, you can track gradual progress without the stress of constant numbers or self‑criticism.
