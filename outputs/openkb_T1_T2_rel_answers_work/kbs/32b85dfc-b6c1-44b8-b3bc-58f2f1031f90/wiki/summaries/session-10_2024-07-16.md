## Summary of Session 10 (2024‑07‑16)

This conversation covers practical strategies for moving a sleep schedule earlier, focusing on the biological basis of chronotypes and the strongest behavioural levers.

### Key Concepts
- **Circadian rhythm** – an internally generated ~24‑hour clock that is partly genetic, explaining why some people are natural night owls while others are early birds. circadian rhythm
- **Morning light exposure** – the most potent cue for advancing the clock; aim for real outdoor light within the first 30 minutes of waking, for about 10‑20 minutes on a bright day. morning light exposure
- **Gradual wake‑up shift** – move the wake‑time earlier by 15‑30 minutes every few days rather than a large overnight jump. gradual wake‑up adjustment
- **Evening wind‑down** – dim lights, avoid screens, stop caffeine early, and keep bedtime consistent across weekdays and weekends. sleep hygiene
- **Light therapy** – a 10,000‑lux box can substitute for weak winter daylight; use it within 30 minutes of waking for 15‑30 minutes. light therapy
- **Sleep onset handling** – if not sleepy at the intended bedtime, stay awake (engage in a low‑arousal activity) rather than lying in bed awake; go to bed only when drowsy. sleep onset insomnia

### Findings & Recommendations
1. **Biology vs. habit** – chronotype has a genetic component, but habits (light, caffeine, screen use) can shift the clock.
2. **Timing of light** – the sooner after waking, the better; half‑hour window is ideal.
3. **Duration of light** – 10‑20 minutes of outdoor light is sufficient; cloudy days may need a bit longer.
4. **Light‑therapy specifics** – 10,000 lux, placed off‑to‑the‑side, 15‑30 minutes in the first half‑hour after waking.
5. **Adjustment timeline** – small advances noticed after a few days; full shift typically 1‑2 weeks with consistent routine.
6. **Weekend consistency** – keep the same wake‑time for several weeks to reinforce the new rhythm.
7. **Handling awake nights** – avoid forcing sleep; stay up until drowsy, then sleep, while maintaining the fixed wake‑time and morning light.

### Practical Steps
1. Set a target wake‑time earlier by 15 minutes.
2. Within 30 minutes of waking, get outside or use a 10,000‑lux lamp for 15‑30 minutes.
3. Dim lights after sunset, limit screens, and stop caffeine by early afternoon.
4. If not sleepy at the intended bedtime, engage in a quiet activity until drowsy; do not lie awake in bed.
5. Keep the wake‑time constant, even on weekends, for at least 1‑2 weeks.

By combining **consistent earlier wake‑times**, **morning light (natural or therapeutic)**, and **evening sleep‑friendly habits**, most people can shift their bedtime earlier without the misery that accompanies abrupt changes.
