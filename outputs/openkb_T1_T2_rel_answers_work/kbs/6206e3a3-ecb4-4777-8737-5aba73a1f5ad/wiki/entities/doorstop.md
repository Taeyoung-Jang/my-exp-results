# Doorstop

**Type:** Product

**Description:** A small, often rubber or weighted object placed behind a door to keep it ajar, ensuring a clear passage for oxygen tubing and reducing the risk of the door closing on the hose.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-33_2025-03-13]]
- **Purpose in the living‑room setup:** Used as the first quick‑win in the 15‑minute checklist to keep doorways fully open, eliminating partial closures that can catch the tubing.
- **Benefit for tubing management:** By preventing doors from drifting shut, it creates a stable, snag‑free pathway, supporting the [[concepts/tubing-routing]] strategy.
- **Low‑effort implementation:** No installation required; simply wedge the doorstop behind the door hinge side. Suitable for any standard door without permanent modifications.
- **Interaction with other adaptations:** Works alongside [[entities/removable-wall-clips]] that guide the tubing along walls, and complements broader [[concepts/ergonomic-home-adaptation]] efforts to make the home more "lung‑friendly."

## Related Concepts and Entities
- [[concepts/air-quality-management]] – Keeping doors open also aids ventilation, improving overall air quality.
- [[concepts/ergonomic-home-adaptation]] – Doorstops are a simple ergonomic tweak to reduce physical strain.
- [[entities/removable-wall-clips]] – Often used together to create a predictable tubing route.

## Practical Tips
1. Choose a doorstop with a non‑slipping base to stay securely in place.
2. Position it so the door is fully open but not obstructing furniture or foot traffic.
3. Combine with a doorstop on the opposite side if the door tends to swing back.
4. Periodically check that the doorstop remains seated, especially after cleaning or moving furniture.
