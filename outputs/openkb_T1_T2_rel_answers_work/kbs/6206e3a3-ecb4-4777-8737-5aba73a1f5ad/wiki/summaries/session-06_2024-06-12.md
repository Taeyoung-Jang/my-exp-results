- The assistant recommends treating household chores as a dynamic, regularly revisited partnership rather than a static assignment.  
- Core approach: identify each partner’s disliked tasks and current capacity (schedule, energy) → [[concepts/chore_fairness]].  
- Introduce periodic **check‑ins** (weekly or bi‑weekly) framed around feelings rather than fairness metrics to avoid debate → [[concepts/check_in_communication]].  
- Use a simple **trade** or **temporary rebalancing** system: when one partner is overloaded, the other temporarily takes over one or two specific chores, then reverts back → [[concepts/temporary_rebalancing]].  
- Suggested concrete one‑week rebalancing options for a typical split (Laundry/Vacuum ↔ Cooking/Dishes):  
  1. Take over dishes for the week while the partner continues cooking.  
  2. Cook two evenings, letting the partner reheat or prepare simple meals the other nights.  
  3. Keep laundry/vacuuming and add a small kitchen task such as veg prep or lunch packing.  
- Communication phrasing: emphasize support (e.g., “Your week looks full—can I handle a couple of kitchen tasks?”) to keep the tone low‑pressure and collaborative.  
- Emphasis on flexibility: the system should adapt to changing schedules and energy levels without tracking every single task.
