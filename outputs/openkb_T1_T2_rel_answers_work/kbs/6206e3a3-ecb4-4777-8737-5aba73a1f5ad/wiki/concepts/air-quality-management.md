# Air Quality Management

**Definition**: A set of practical, low‑effort interventions aimed at keeping indoor air clean and breathable, especially for people who rely on continuous oxygen therapy.

### Core elements (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-03-13]])

- **Dust control** – Regularly wash bedding, curtains, and removable fabrics; use a vacuum with a sealed HEPA filter for carpet or slow passes on hard floors.
- **Fragrance‑free cleaning** – Choose unscented cleaners and avoid candles or plug‑ins that release volatile compounds.
- **Ventilation** – Open windows briefly when outdoor air quality is good; ensure the kitchen hood runs while cooking, especially frying or high‑heat methods.
- **Air purification** – Place a compact [[entities/hepa-air-purifier]] near the primary seating area to continuously filter airborne particles.
- **Humidity balance** – Keep indoor humidity between 30‑50 % to avoid overly dry or heavy air.

### Low‑dust cleaning routine

1. Vacuum with a HEPA‑rated unit on a slow, steady pass.
2. Wipe surfaces with a slightly damp microfiber cloth rather than a dry one.
3. Run the air purifier on a higher setting during and after cleaning to capture any displaced particles.

### Interaction with other home‑adaptation concepts

- Complements [[concepts/ergonomic-home-adaptation]] by reducing the physical effort needed to maintain a clean environment.
- Supports [[concepts/tubing-routing]] because cleaner air lessens irritation, allowing the user to focus on managing oxygen tubing rather than reacting to dust triggers.

### Quick‑start checklist

- Add a small HEPA air purifier in the living room.
- Set a schedule for weekly fabric washing and monthly deep‑vacuuming.
- Keep a simple window‑opening routine for fresh air exchange.
