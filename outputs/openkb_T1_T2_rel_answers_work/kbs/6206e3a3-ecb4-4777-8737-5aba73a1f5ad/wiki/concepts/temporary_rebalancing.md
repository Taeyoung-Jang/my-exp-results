# Temporary Rebalancing of Household Tasks

**Definition**: A flexible, short‑term adjustment of who does which household chores, intended to alleviate temporary overload for one partner without permanently changing the established division of labor.

**Core Principles**
- **Flexibility**: Adjustments are meant to be temporary, lasting only until the partner’s workload normalises.
- **Low‑Pressure Communication**: Frame the offer as support (e.g., “Your week looks full—can I handle a couple of kitchen tasks?”) to avoid criticism. See [[concepts/check_in_communication]].
- **Focus on Capacity, Not Fairness Scores**: Identify the partner’s current schedule and energy level rather than attempting to tally every task. Relates to [[concepts/chore_fairness]].

**Typical Rebalancing Options** (derived from [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-12]])
1. **Take over dishes for the week** while the partner continues cooking.
2. **Cook two evenings** and let the partner handle simple meals (reheat leftovers or quick breakfasts) the other nights.
3. **Add a small kitchen task** (e.g., vegetable prep, lunch packing) to your usual laundry/vacuum duties, giving the partner a break from the full cooking routine.

**Implementation Steps**
1. **Check‑in**: During a regular, low‑pressure conversation, ask how the partner’s workload feels.
2. **Select a Target Task**: Choose one or two chores that will most reduce the partner’s burden.
3. **Agree on Duration**: Typically a week or until the partner signals it’s okay to revert.
4. **Re‑evaluate**: At the next check‑in, discuss whether the temporary swap was helpful and decide on any further adjustments.

**Benefits**
- Reduces resentment by preventing prolonged imbalance.
- Keeps the overall chore structure stable while providing needed relief.
- Encourages a collaborative mindset rather than a score‑keeping one.
