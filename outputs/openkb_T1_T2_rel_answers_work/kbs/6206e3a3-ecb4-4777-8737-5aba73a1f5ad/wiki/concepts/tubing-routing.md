# Tubing Routing

**Definition**: A set of practical strategies for positioning and securing continuous‑oxygen tubing within a living space so that it does not catch on furniture, doorframes, or flooring, thereby minimising physical strain and breathlessness.

## Overview
People using continuous oxygen often find that everyday obstacles—doorways, corners, and clutter—turn the tubing into a hidden hazard. Effective tubing routing combines ergonomic placement, simple hardware, and routine tidying to create a predictable, low‑effort path.

## Key Strategies (derived from [[summaries/session-33_2025-03-13]])
1. **Clear the direct floor path** – Remove or relocate items that could act as snag points (extra pillows, loose décor).
2. **Run tubing along walls** – Guide the hose close to the baseboard rather than letting it meander across open floor space.
3. **Use low‑profile guides** – Attach [[entities/removable-wall-clips]] at regular intervals to create a gentle “track.”
4. **Secure doorways** – Keep doors fully open with a [[entities/doorstop]] or hold them open; this prevents the tubing from being pulled into a narrowing frame.
5. **Guard corners** – Apply [[entities/corner-guards]] to furniture edges and doorframe corners where the tube tends to snag.
6. **Maintain gentle tension** – Clips should redirect, not tighten; the tubing should have a relaxed curve when you stand, sit, or turn.

## Quick‑Start Checklist (15 min)
- Remove obvious dust‑catchers and place them elsewhere.
- Open doors fully and insert a [[entities/doorstop]].
- Temporarily lay the tubing along the wall, adjusting as you move.

## Expanded Routine (1 hour)
- Attach a few [[entities/removable-wall-clips]]: first clip about an arm’s length from your main seating position, then additional clips at each corner or doorway transition.
- Add soft [[entities/corner-guards]] to any furniture edges that intersect the tube’s path.

## Weekend Projects
- Re‑evaluate room layout to keep high‑traffic pathways clear.
- Consider permanent low‑profile tubing channels or raceways if the space allows.

## Placement Rule of Thumb
- **First clip**: Position roughly an arm’s length (≈ 60 cm) from where the tubing hangs when you are seated; this gives the hose a relaxed curve when you stand.
- **Subsequent clips**: Place at each natural change of direction—doorframes, wall‑to‑wall turns—spaced a few feet apart, ensuring the tube slides smoothly.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of broader [[concepts/ergonomic-home-adaptation]] efforts that aim to reduce physical effort in daily life.
- Complements [[concepts/air-quality-management]] by keeping the tubing away from dust‑prone zones and ensuring cleaner airflow.
- Can be incorporated into a [[concepts/temporary_rebalancing]] plan when a user’s health status or oxygen flow requirements change.
