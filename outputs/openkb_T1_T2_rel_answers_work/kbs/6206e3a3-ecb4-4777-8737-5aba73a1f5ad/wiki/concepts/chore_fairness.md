# Chore Fairness

**Definition**: A dynamic, collaborative method for assigning and adjusting household chores that takes into account each partner's dislikes, current schedule, and energy levels, aiming for a sense of equity over time.

## Core Principles

1. **Identify Preferences and Dislikes**
   - Each partner lists chores they genuinely dislike or find draining.  
   - This creates a baseline of tasks that can be swapped more readily.

2. **Assess Current Capacity**
   - Consider each person’s work schedule, fatigue, and any temporary pressures (e.g., a busy project week).  
   - Capacity can change weekly, so the assignment should be revisited regularly.

3. **Regular Check‑Ins**
   - Schedule brief, low‑pressure conversations (weekly or bi‑weekly) focused on how each partner feels rather than on "fairness metrics."  
   - See also [[concepts/check_in_communication]].

4. **Temporary Rebalancing**
   - When one partner is overloaded, the other temporarily takes over one or two specific chores, then returns to the usual split once the pressure eases.  
   - This is a simple "trade" system rather than a detailed task‑by‑task ledger.  
   - See also [[concepts/temporary_rebalancing]].

## Application in Session‑06 (2024‑06‑12)

The conversation in [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-12]] illustrates Chore Fairness through:
- A focus on **disliked tasks** (e.g., laundry/vacuum vs. cooking/dishes).
- Adjusting responsibilities based on **week‑by‑week energy** and workload.
- Proposing concrete **one‑week rebalancing** options such as taking over dishes, cooking a few nights, or adding a small kitchen task.
- Emphasizing **supportive language** to keep discussions low‑pressure.

## Benefits

- Reduces resentment by preventing long‑term imbalances.
- Keeps the partnership collaborative rather than competitive.
- Requires minimal tracking; the system relies on shared awareness and brief dialogues.
