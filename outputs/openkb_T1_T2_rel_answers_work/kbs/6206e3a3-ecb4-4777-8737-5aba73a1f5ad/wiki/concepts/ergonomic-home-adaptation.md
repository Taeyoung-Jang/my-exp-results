# Ergonomic Home Adaptation

**Ergonomic Home Adaptation** refers to a set of practical, low‑effort modifications to a living space and daily routines that help people who rely on continuous oxygen—or anyone with breathing sensitivity—move, breathe, and live more comfortably. The concept draws on the checklist and tips from [[summaries/session-33_2025-03-13]] and connects closely with [[concepts/air-quality-management]] and [[concepts/tubing-routing]].

## Core Principles
1. **Prioritize low‑effort, high‑impact changes** – Use time‑boxed actions (15 min, 1 hr, weekend) to quickly reduce dust, improve airflow, and streamline tubing paths.
2. **Control the air you breathe** – Regularly launder fabrics, use fragrance‑free cleaners, and place a compact [[entities/hepa-air-purifier]] near the primary seating area.
3. **Create a predictable tubing route** – Keep doors fully open with a [[entities/doorstop]], run the tubing along walls, and secure it with [[entities/removable-wall-clips]] placed a few feet apart, especially at corners and doorframes.
4. **Minimize snag points** – Add soft [[entities/corner-guards]] to furniture edges and shift furniture slightly to avoid pinching the tubing.
5. **Low‑dust cleaning** – On carpet, use a HEPA‑filtered vacuum with slow passes; on hard floors, use a slightly damp microfiber mop while the air purifier runs on a higher setting.

## Practical Checklist (from the source)
- **15‑minute quick wins**: remove unused pillows/blankets, open doors and insert a doorstop, temporarily guide tubing along a wall.
- **1‑hour deeper work**: damp‑wipe surfaces, position the HEPA air purifier, install removable wall clips, adjust furniture spacing.
- **Weekend projects**: replace heavy curtains, add corner guards, streamline overall layout for smooth pathways.

## Why It Matters
For continuous‑oxygen users, even minor obstacles—dust clouds, tight doorways, or tangled tubing—can trigger significant breathlessness and fatigue. By applying ergonomic home adaptation principles, the environment becomes a supportive partner rather than a source of strain, enabling smoother daily routines and better overall well‑being.
