# Check‑In Communication

**Definition**: A low‑pressure, feeling‑oriented dialogue used by partners to review and adjust household chore divisions on a regular basis.

**Key Elements**
- **Frequency**: Schedule brief check‑ins weekly or every couple of weeks to keep the partnership balanced.
- **Framing**: Focus on each person’s current load and emotions rather than asking “Is it fair?” – e.g., “How’s your load this week? Anything you want me to take off your plate?”
- **Tone**: Use supportive language that emphasizes teamwork, such as offering to handle a few tasks temporarily, to avoid turning the conversation into a debate.
- **Outcome**: Identify any temporary overloads and trigger a [[concepts/temporary_rebalancing]] (e.g., a short‑term trade of chores) without needing detailed task tracking.

**Relation to Other Concepts**
- Connects with [[concepts/chore_fairness]] by providing the communication mechanism that keeps perceived fairness dynamic and responsive.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/temporary_rebalancing]] as the trigger for short‑term adjustments.

**Source**: The practice is outlined in the conversation summary [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-12]], where the assistant advises couples to “check in—maybe once a week or once every couple of weeks” and to phrase the discussion around feelings rather than fairness.
