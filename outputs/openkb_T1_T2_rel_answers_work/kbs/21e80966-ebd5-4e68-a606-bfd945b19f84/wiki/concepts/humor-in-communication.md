# Humor in Communication

**Definition**: Humor in communication refers to the strategic use of light, playful language to make interpersonal messages feel friendly, engaging, and approachable while avoiding overt cheesiness or offense.

## Key Elements
- **Tone balance** – Maintaining a casual, upbeat tone without crossing into forced or cringeworthy jokes.
- **Brevity** – Keeping the humorous element concise so the message fits on a single screen.
- **Context awareness** – Tailoring humor to the audience and situation (e.g., a group of friends planning a meetup).

## Application in [[summaries/session-14_2020-06-13]]
In the session dated 2020‑06‑13, the user needed a short, casual text to reschedule a Sunday morning gathering because of a commitment to [[entities/choir]] practice at the [[entities/parish]]. The final version incorporated humor by:
- Describing the conflict as “my Sunday morning got taken over by choir practice,” a playful phrasing that softens the inconvenience.
- Adding a light‑hearted comment: “if that’s way too early for normal humans, I can do 2:00 or 3:00 in the afternoon instead.”
- Keeping the message brief enough to fit on one screen, preserving readability.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/message-drafting]]** – Humor is a stylistic choice within the broader process of crafting concise, effective messages.
- **[[concepts/timing-suggestions]]** – The humorous note pairs with clear time proposals (8:00/8:15 or 2:00/3:00) to aid coordination while keeping the tone light.

## Benefits
1. **Increases receptivity** – Recipients are more likely to respond positively to a friendly, humorous tone.
2. **Reduces perceived inconvenience** – Framing a schedule change with humor can make the adjustment feel less burdensome.
3. **Strengthens social bonds** – Shared laughter, even in text, reinforces camaraderie.

## Practical Tips
- Use everyday language and avoid obscure references.
- Limit jokes to one or two lines to maintain focus on the core information.
- Align humor with the relationship you have with the audience; what works for close friends may not suit formal contacts.
