# Message Drafting

**Definition**: Message Drafting is the process of iteratively crafting short, clear, and appropriately toned communications to convey a specific request or information, often adjusting wording, length, and style to suit the audience and context.

---

## Core Elements
1. **Context Understanding** – Identify the purpose (e.g., rescheduling), constraints (time windows, commitments), and any external factors such as [[entities/choir]] practice at the [[entities/parish]].
2. **Tone Selection** – Choose a tone that matches the relationship (casual, upbeat, humorous). The use of light humor is explored in [[concepts/humor-in-communication]].
3. **Brevity and Clarity** – Keep messages concise while ensuring the key details (who, what, when) are unmistakable.
4. **Timing Suggestions** – Provide explicit time options to facilitate decision‑making; see [[concepts/timing-suggestions]] for the practice of offering specific windows (e.g., 8:00, 8:15, 2:00, 3:00).
5. **Iterative Refinement** – Adjust wording, length, or details based on feedback or evolving needs.

---

## Example from [[summaries/session-14_2020-06-13]]
The following progression illustrates Message Drafting in action:
- **Initial draft** – A brief, polite request to meet earlier.
- **Added timing** – Specific morning windows (8:00 or 8:15) and a cutoff at 9:15.
- **Alternative slot** – Introduced afternoon options (2:00 or 3:00).
- **Tone tweak** – Crafted a more upbeat, slightly funny version while simplifying language.

These steps show how the concept integrates context, tone, brevity, and timing to produce effective messages.

## Practical Tips
- **Start with the core request** before adding optional details.
- **Limit to one or two time options** to avoid overwhelming the recipient.
- **Test readability** – ensure the message fits on a single screen for quick reading.
- **Adjust humor level** based on audience familiarity.
