# Physical Boundary Cue

A **Physical Boundary Cue** is a concrete, short‑duration action that creates a clear demarcation between work and home life. By physically altering the environment, it sends a strong signal to the brain that the workday is over, helping to initiate an [[concepts/evening-shutdown-routine]].

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-03_2024-11-09]])
- **Close the office door** – the primary barrier that separates the work zone from living spaces.
- **Desk reset (≈20 seconds)** – straighten or tidy a few items to make the workspace look finished rather than paused.
- **Consistency** – performing the same steps each evening builds an automatic habit.

## How It Works
1. **Environmental change** – Closing the door physically blocks the work area, reducing visual and auditory cues that can keep the mind in “work mode.”
2. **Psychological cue** – The act of resetting the desk provides a visual cue of completion, reinforcing the mental transition.
3. **Trigger for further rituals** – Once the boundary is set, it naturally leads into other components of a [[concepts/transition-ritual]], such as switching lighting or taking a breath cue.

## Benefits
- **Reduces mental spillover** from work tasks into personal time.
- **Creates a sense of security** by establishing a clear, controllable boundary.
- **Supports habit formation** because the cue is simple, quick, and repeatable.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/evening-shutdown-routine]] – the broader set of steps that follow the boundary cue to fully unwind.
- [[concepts/transition-ritual]] – the collection of environmental, bodily, and comfort cues that together signal the end of the workday.
