# Summary of Session 3 (2024‑11‑09)

**Main contribution** – A concise, printable checklist that creates a mental shutdown routine for people whose work space is part of their home, plus quick‑tip strategies for resisting the urge to reopen the work area.

### Key Ideas & Steps
1. **Physical boundary cue** – Close the office door and spend ~20 seconds tidying the desk. [[concepts/physical-boundary-cue]]
2. **Environmental shift** – Switch to dedicated evening lighting (warm, low‑intensity).  
3. **Body cue** – Take one slow breath or do a shoulder roll to signal “day over.”  
4. **Comfort habit** – Begin a relaxing activity (pour a drink, change into home clothes).  
5. **Optional security check** – Verify doors locked, blinds closed (≤30 seconds).  

### Printable Format (checkboxes)
```
[ ] Close office door and do a 20‑second desk reset
[ ] Switch to evening lighting
[ ] Take one slow breath or shoulder roll
[ ] Start a comfort cue (drink, clothes, etc.)
[ ] Optional: Quick security check (doors locked, blinds closed)
```

### Managing Temptation
- **Pause & note** – When the urge to reopen the door arises, pause 10 seconds, then jot the pending task on a “tomorrow” note instead of acting.  
- The “tomorrow note” satisfies the mental need for acknowledgement without breaking the shutdown flow.  

### Underlying Concepts
- **Evening shutdown routine** – A short, repeatable sequence that signals the brain the workday has ended. [[concepts/evening-shutdown-routine]]
- **Transition ritual** – Combining environmental, physical, and comfort cues to create a clear boundary between work and personal time. [[concepts/transition-ritual]]

These elements can be combined or adapted, but the emphasis is on keeping each step under a minute to maintain habit consistency.
