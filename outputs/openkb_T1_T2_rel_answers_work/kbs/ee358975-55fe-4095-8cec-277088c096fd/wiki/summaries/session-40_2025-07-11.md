## Summary

This document provides a lightweight, action‑oriented plan for staying productive when your office is turned into a construction zone. It focuses on creating a **temporary workspace**, defining **essential vs. non‑essential items**, protecting equipment from dust and noise, and establishing a simple **checklist**, **3–5‑day timeline**, and **daily‑kit** that fits in a backpack. It also outlines an ultra‑simple **box‑tracking** method and a few **contingency** triage steps for common “oh‑no” moments.

### Key Concepts
- **temporary workspace** – Choose a single, reliable spot (home corner, spare room, café) and equip it with a small table, laptop, lighting, and a decent chair.
- **essential items** – Keep daily essentials (laptop, charger, notebook, pens, active files, headphones, security keys) accessible; box and label everything else.
- **dust protection** – Cover stationary office items with plastic sheeting or a sheet; move delicate items out entirely.
- **noise management** – Use noise‑cancelling headphones for calls; fallback to taking meetings in a car or quiet outdoor spot.
- **checklist** – A concise list of items to keep out, items to pack and label, and protection steps.
- **timeline** – Night‑before setup, morning move‑out, and daily actions for the first 3–5 days.
- **daily kit** – Backpack‑sized kit: laptop, charger, compact power strip, headphones/earbuds, notebook, two pens, security badges/keys, small pouch of adapters/cables, and personal comfort items.
- **box tracking** – Number each box, photograph the box with its contents, store photos in a phone album; optional short descriptive labels (e.g., “Tech”, “Files”).
- **contingency planning** – Quick triage for missed cables/documents, dust infiltration, and unexpected contractor access.

### Checklist
- **Keep out & accessible**: laptop, charger, notebook, pens, active files, headphones, security keys, small peripherals.
- **Pack & label**: group remaining items into categories (computer gear, paper/files, decor, misc) and label boxes numerically.
- **Protect at office**: cover remaining items with plastic sheet or old sheet.

### 3–5‑Day Timeline
1. **Tonight** – Set up the home spot, pack non‑essentials, assemble the daily kit in a bag.
2. **Tomorrow morning** – Move essentials out before demolition, cover anything left behind, take quick photos of the layout.
3. **Next 3–5 days** – Use the default workspace consistently, check for dust, keep the daily kit tidy, adjust only if truly needed.

### Daily Kit Packing List (fits in a backpack)
- Laptop & charger
- Compact power strip
- Noise‑cancelling headphones/earbuds
- Notebook & two pens
- Security badges/keys
- Small pouch with adapters & cables
- Personal items (meds, comfort items)

### Box‑Tracking Method
1. Write a large number on each box.
2. Photograph the box with a view of its contents before sealing.
3. Save photos in a phone album titled “Office Boxes.”
4. Optional: add short name tags (e.g., “Tech”, “Files”).

### Contingency / Quick‑Triage Plans
- **Forgotten critical cable/document** – Use a substitute (borrow cable, cloud copy) or briefly peel back plastic, retrieve, and reseal.
- **Dust entering boxes** – Relocate boxes to a higher shelf, tighten flaps, add a second cover (towel/trash bag). If dust is inside, retrieve needed items and reseal the rest.
- **Contractor needs access** – Rapidly move boxes to a car trunk or hallway; sort later when stable.

The overall approach is **minimal, portable, and protected**, allowing you to maintain productivity without turning the situation into a full‑blown project.
