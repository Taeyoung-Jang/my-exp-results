## Overview
A step‑by‑step beginner woodworking guide focused on creating a small side table. It covers project selection, essential [[concepts/basic-tools]], safety practices, a detailed cut list, simple joinery methods, screw sizing, clamp choices, build order, and how to accommodate seasonal wood movement.

## Recommended Tools & Materials
- [[entities/tape-measure]], [[entities/handsaw]], [[entities/drill-driver]] (with assorted bits), clamps, sandpaper, square, pencil
- Quick‑grip clamps (2 medium‑size are sufficient for small projects) – [[entities/quick-grip-clamp]]
- Standard pine lumber – [[entities/pine]]: 1×8 for the top, 1×3 for aprons, 1×2 for legs

## Safety Tips
- Always clamp workpieces securely before cutting or drilling
- Keep hands away from cutting paths; let tools do the work
- Wear eye protection at all times
- Let tools come to a complete stop before setting them aside  

These basics follow [[concepts/woodworking-safety]] guidelines.

## Project Planning & Dimensions
- Target dimensions: ~16" × 16" tabletop, ~20" height
- **Cut list**:
  - Top: 2 × 1×8 boards, each 16" long, glued edge‑to‑edge
  - Legs: 4 × 1×2 boards, each 20" long
  - Aprons: 2 × 1×3 at 13" (long sides), 2 × 1×3 at 11" (short sides)
- Allow a small overhang of the apron under the top for a clean look

## Joinery & Screw Sizing
- Use pilot holes slightly larger than the screw shank to give a modest amount of play, allowing for seasonal wood movement without splitting.
- Attach aprons to legs with 1¼" wood screws driven from the inside face; hide heads for a tidy appearance.
- Secure the tabletop from underneath with 1" screws through the aprons.

## Build Sequence
1. Prepare and glue the tabletop pieces, sand flat.
2. Cut and verify all four legs are equal.
3. Cut apron rails to length.
4. Assemble two “L” corner units (leg + apron) and square them using a carpenter’s square – [[entities/carpenter-square]].
5. Connect the two corners with the remaining aprons, checking for squareness.
6. Position the assembled base, then attach the tabletop from beneath.
7. Final sanding and optional finish.

## Managing Wood Movement
- Oversized pilot holes provide the necessary wiggle room.
- Do not overtighten screws; a snug fit is sufficient.
- This simple allowance replaces the need for specialized hardware in a beginner project.
