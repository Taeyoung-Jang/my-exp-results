# Carpenter Square

A **carpenter square** (also called a framing square) is a metal L‑shaped tool used to verify and mark right angles and to lay out measurements on wood. It is a staple of the **[[concepts/basic-tools]]** set for anyone starting **[[concepts/beginner-woodworking]]**.

## Key Facts from Session 03 (2023‑11‑13)
- Mentioned alongside a tape measure, handsaw, drill/driver, pencil, sandpaper, and quick‑grip clamps as core tools for beginner projects.
- Described as a simple addition that, together with a pencil, enables accurate layout and helps keep pieces square during cuts and assembly.
- Implicitly supports **[[concepts/woodworking-safety]]** by ensuring cuts are made at correct angles, reducing re‑work and the risk of mistakes.

## Typical Uses
- Checking that corner joints are true 90‑degree angles before fastening.
- Marking out board lengths and angles for cuts with a **[[entities/handsaw]]** or power saw.
- Laying out layout lines with a **[[entities/pencil]]** for precise cuts and drill placements.

## Recommended Pairings
- Use with a **[[entities/tape-measure]]** for accurate distance measurements.
- Combine with a **[[entities/drill-driver]]** when pre‑drilling pilot holes at right angles.
- Pair with **[[entities/quick-grip-clamp]]** to hold workpieces steady while checking squareness.
