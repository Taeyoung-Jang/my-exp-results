# Sandpaper

Sandpaper is an abrasive sheet used to smooth and finish wood surfaces. In the **session‑03_2023-11-13** guide it is listed as one of the essential [[concepts/basic-tools]] for anyone starting out in [[concepts/beginner-woodworking]].

## Role in the Project
- After cutting boards with a [[entities/handsaw]] and drilling holes with a [[entities/drill-driver]], sandpaper is used to remove splinters and prepare the surface for assembly and finish.
- It helps achieve a clean, splinter‑free tabletop and leg edges for the beginner side‑table project.

## Recommended Use
- Keep a range of grits (coarse to fine) to tackle rough cuts and final polishing.
- Apply sandpaper by hand or with a sanding block; secure the workpiece with [[entities/quick-grip-clamp]] or a [[entities/carpenter-square]] for stability while sanding.
- Combine with a pencil for marking cut lines and a tape measure for checking dimensions before sanding.

## Connection to Other Tools & Concepts
- Works alongside other basic tools such as the [[entities/tape-measure]], [[entities/pencil]], and [[entities/quick-grip-clamp]] to ensure accurate measurements and safe handling.
- Integral to maintaining safety practices outlined in [[concepts/woodworking-safety]], as smooth surfaces reduce the risk of splinters and injuries.
