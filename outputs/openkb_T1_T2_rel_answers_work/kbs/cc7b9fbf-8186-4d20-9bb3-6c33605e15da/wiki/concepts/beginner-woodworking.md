# Beginner Woodworking

**Beginner woodworking** is the practice of creating simple, functional items—such as shelves, stools, side tables, and benches—using minimal tools and straightforward joinery. It emphasizes learning core skills (measuring, cutting, squaring, sanding) while building confidence through quick, usable results.

## Core Elements

### 1. Project Selection
- Start with small, useful pieces: a basic shelf, a planter box, a simple stool, a side table, or a bench.
- Choose designs that use straight lumber and limit the need for complex joints.

### 2. Essential Tools [[concepts/basic-tools]]
- **Tape measure** ([[entities/tape-measure]]) for accurate dimensions.
- **Handsaw** ([[entities/handsaw]]) for straight cuts.
- **Drill‑driver** ([[entities/drill-driver]]) with appropriate bits for pilot holes and screw driving.
- **Carpenter’s square** ([[entities/carpenter-square]]) to verify right angles.
- **Pencil** ([[entities/pencil]]) for marking cut lines.
- **Quick‑grip clamp** ([[entities/quick-grip-clamp]]) – two medium‑size clamps are sufficient for most beginner projects.
- **Sandpaper** ([[entities/sandpaper]]) for smoothing edges and preparing surfaces.

### 3. Safety Practices [[concepts/woodworking-safety]]
- Always clamp the workpiece securely before cutting or drilling.
- Keep hands away from the cutting path and let the tool do the work.
- Wear eye protection at all times.
- Allow tools to stop completely before setting them down.

### 4. Materials
- **Pine** ([[entities/pine]]) is the preferred wood for beginners: inexpensive, easy to cut, and forgiving.
- Use standard 1×8, 1×3, and 1×2 boards from a big‑box store.

### 5. Simple Joinery
- **Metal brackets** for wall‑mounted shelves – no specialized joinery required.
- **Pocket holes** as a next‑step technique once comfortable with basic screws.
- Direct **screw‑through** connections: 1¼" wood screws for leg‑to‑apron joints, 1" screws for attaching the tabletop.
- Pilot holes slightly oversized to allow modest wood movement without specialized hardware.

### 6. Build Order (Typical for a Side Table)
1. Prepare and glue the tabletop pieces.
2. Cut and verify all legs are equal.
3. Cut apron rails to the required lengths.
4. Assemble two “L” corner units (leg + apron) and square them.
5. Connect the corner units with the remaining aprons, checking for squareness.
6. Attach the tabletop from underneath using screws.
7. Finish with sanding and optional finish.

## Learning Outcomes
- Accurate measurement and layout.
- Safe handling of hand and power tools.
- Recognizing and maintaining right angles.
- Basic screw‑driven joinery and the role of pilot holes.
- Managing wood expansion and contraction through simple construction allowances.
