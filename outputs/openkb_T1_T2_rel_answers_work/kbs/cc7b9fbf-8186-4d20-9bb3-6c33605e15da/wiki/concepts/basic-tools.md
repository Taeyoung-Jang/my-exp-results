# Basic Tools for Woodworking

A concise overview of the core, low‑cost tools a novice woodworker needs to start building functional furniture, as outlined in session 03 2024 11 13. These tools enable accurate measurement, safe cutting, reliable drilling, and basic material handling.

## Core Hand Tools

- **[[entities/tape-measure]]** – for layout and checking dimensions.
- **[[entities/pencil]]** – to mark cut lines and drill points.
- **[[entities/handsaw]]** – a versatile manual saw for straight cuts on 1x lumber.
- **[[entities/drill-driver]]** – drives screws and makes pilot holes; essential for assembling joints.
- **[[entities/quick-grip-clamp]]** – quick‑grip style clamps (two medium‑size are sufficient) keep workpieces steady while cutting or gluing.
- **[[entities/sandpaper]]** – for smoothing edges and preparing surfaces for finish.
- **[[entities/carpenter-square]]** – checks squareness of joints and ensures frames are true.

## Supporting Concepts

- The tool set directly supports [[concepts/beginner-woodworking]], providing the minimum equipment for simple projects like shelves, stools, side tables, and benches.
- Proper use of these tools is a cornerstone of [[concepts/woodworking-safety]], emphasizing clamping, eye protection, and controlled operation.

## Why These Tools?

1. **Measurement & Layout** – A reliable tape measure and pencil let you translate design dimensions onto raw boards accurately.
2. **Cutting** – A handsaw handles the typical 1×8, 1×3, and 1×2 pine stock used in starter projects without requiring power saws.
3. **Joining** – A drill‑driver with appropriate wood‑screw bits creates strong, repeatable connections; pilot holes prevent splitting.
4. **Holding** – Quick‑grip clamps are easy to adjust with one hand, ideal for aligning pieces while the glue or screws set.
5. **Finishing** – Sandpaper smooths edges and prepares the surface for paint, stain, or sealant.
6. **Accuracy** – A carpenter square ensures frames are built square, a critical quality metric for furniture stability.

## Using the Tools in a Beginner Project

When building a simple side table (as described in the session summary), the workflow typically follows:
1. **Measure & Mark** – Use the tape measure and pencil to lay out cuts for the top, legs, and aprons.
2. **Cut** – Saw the pieces to length with the handsaw.
3. **Clamp & Drill** – Clamp an apron to a leg, drill pilot holes, then drive 1¼" screws with the drill‑driver.
4. **Assemble** – Repeat for each corner, checking squareness with the carpenter square.
5. **Attach Top** – Clamp the top, drill pilot holes through the aprons, and secure with 1" screws.
6. **Finish** – Sand all surfaces with sandpaper before applying a finish.

## Safety Checklist

- Clamp every workpiece before cutting or drilling.
- Keep hands clear of the cutting path.
- Wear eye protection throughout.
- Allow tools to stop completely before setting them down.

These basic tools provide a solid foundation for anyone aiming to learn woodworking fundamentals and complete their first functional pieces.
