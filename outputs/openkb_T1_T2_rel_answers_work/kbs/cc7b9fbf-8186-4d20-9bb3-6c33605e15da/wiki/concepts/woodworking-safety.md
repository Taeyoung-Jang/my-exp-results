# Woodworking Safety Practices

**Definition**: A set of essential precautions and habits to minimize risk while working with wood, tools, and equipment.

## Core Safety Guidelines

1. **Secure Workpieces**
   - Always clamp the material firmly before cutting, drilling, or sanding to prevent movement.
2. **Hand Positioning**
   - Keep hands well clear of cutting paths; let the tool do the work without forcing it.
3. **Eye Protection**
   - Wear safety glasses or goggles at all times, even for seemingly harmless tasks.
4. **Tool Control**
   - Allow saws and drills to come to a full stop before setting them down or changing bits.
5. **Avoid Over‑tightening**
   - Screw connections should be snug, not forced, to reduce strain on both the material and the tool.

## Recommended Tools & Gear

- **tape measure**, **pencil**, **handsaw**, **drill driver**, **quick grip clamp**, **sandpaper**, and a **carpenter square** are basic items that support safe, accurate work.
- Protective equipment: safety glasses, hearing protection (for power tools), and a dust mask when sanding.

## Application in Beginner Projects

The safety principles above were emphasized throughout the "session-03_2023-11-13" conversation, especially for
- building a simple side table,
- using basic joinery with screws,
- and handling common tools like the handsaw and drill‑driver.

Following these practices helps new woodworkers avoid accidents and develop disciplined habits early on.
