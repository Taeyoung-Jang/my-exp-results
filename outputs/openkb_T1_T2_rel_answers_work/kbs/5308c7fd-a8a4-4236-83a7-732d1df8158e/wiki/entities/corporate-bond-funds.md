# Corporate Bond Funds

Corporate Bond Funds are pooled investment vehicles that hold **corporate debt securities**. They aim to provide investors with income and some capital appreciation while exposing them to **credit risk** associated with issuing companies.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-15_2023-07-15]]
- Used as the **fixed‑income slice** in simple, long‑term portfolios that also contain [[entities/broad-market-index-funds]] for equity exposure.
- Tend to **move with stocks** during economic downturns because corporate credit risk correlates with broader market sentiment, reducing their defensive value.
- Recommended to **diversify** the bond side by mixing corporate bond funds with [[entities/treasury-securities]] or a [[entities/total-bond-index]] to achieve better downside protection. A typical starting split for the fixed‑income portion is **50 %–66 %** in treasuries/total‑bond funds and the remainder in corporates.
- The allocation decision aligns with [[concepts/asset-allocation-rules]] such as the “100 minus age” guideline and the overall **risk tolerance** of the investor.
- Rebalancing of the portfolio, including the corporate bond portion, follows the [[concepts/rebalancing-strategy]] of either annual/bi‑annual review or when the allocation drifts **>5 percentage points** from the target.
- When considering bond‑side risk, the concept of **bond diversification** (see [[concepts/bond-diversification]]) is highlighted to balance credit exposure against government‑bond stability.
