## Treasury Bill

A **Treasury Bill** (or **T‑bill**) is a short‑term debt security issued by the U.S. Treasury.  They are sold at a discount and mature in **4, 8, or 13 weeks** (sometimes up to 26 weeks), returning the face value at maturity.  Because they are backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government, they are considered one of the safest cash‑equivalent investments.

### Role in Cash‑Bucket Allocation
In the conversation captured in [[summaries/session-38_2025-01-15]], Treasury Bills are recommended as the **secondary tier** of the emergency‑fund bucket after the core liquidity held in a high‑yield savings account.  The guidance suggests:
- Keeping roughly half of the emergency fund in an instantly accessible high‑yield savings account (see [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]]).
- Placing the remainder in a **rolling short‑term Treasury ladder** to capture a slightly higher yield while preserving liquidity.  The suggested ladder uses **4‑week and 8‑week** rungs, which aligns with the [[concepts/short-term-treasury-ladder]] concept.

### Typical Use Cases
- **Emergency fund buffer** – a portion of the 9‑month emergency fund (≈ $28–33k in the example) is allocated to T‑bills for marginally better returns without sacrificing accessibility.
- **Near‑term cash reserve** – funds earmarked for home‑maintenance or known projects can also be parked in short T‑bills, ensuring they earn yield while waiting to be spent.

### Key Characteristics
- **Maturity options:** 4‑week, 8‑week, 13‑week (and occasionally longer).
- **Yield:** Determined at auction; generally slightly higher than a high‑yield savings account for comparable short‑term durations.
- **Liquidity:** Very high; investors can sell on the secondary market, though rolling the ladder minimizes the need for secondary‑market sales.
- **Risk:** Minimal credit risk; interest‑rate risk is limited due to short maturities.

### Relationship to Other Concepts
- Treasury Bills are a core component of the [[concepts/cash-bucket-allocation]] framework, especially when defining the **short‑term cash bucket**.
- Their use influences overall [[concepts/asset-allocation-rules]] by providing a safe, liquid anchor for the portfolio’s cash layer.
- The decision on how much to allocate to T‑bills versus a high‑yield savings account ties into [[concepts/emergency-fund-sizing]] and the user’s risk tolerance.
