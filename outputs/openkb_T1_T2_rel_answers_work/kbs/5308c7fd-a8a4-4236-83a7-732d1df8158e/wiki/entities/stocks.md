## Stocks

**Type:** product

Stocks are equity securities that represent an ownership claim on a corporation’s assets and earnings. In the context of personal finance, they are the primary growth engine of a long‑term investment portfolio.

### Role in the session‑15 portfolio discussion
- The conversation recommends **broad, low‑cost index funds** that track large segments of the equity market as the preferred way to hold stocks. [[entities/broad-market-index-funds]]
- A simple rule for deciding the stock allocation is the *100 minus age* heuristic, which ties the percentage of **stocks** directly to the investor’s time horizon and risk tolerance. [[concepts/asset-allocation-rules]]
- Example allocation mixes described in the session use varying percentages of stocks:
  - Conservative: ~33% stocks → modest drawdowns.
  - Moderate: 50% stocks → moderate drawdowns.
  - Aggressive: ~80% stocks → larger drawdowns.
- Stocks are rebalanced alongside bonds when the portfolio drifts more than about five percentage points from the target. [[concepts/rebalancing-strategy]]

### Interaction with other assets
- The fixed‑income side of the portfolio consists of **corporate bond funds**, **treasury securities**, and a **total bond index** to provide stability. [[entities/corporate-bond-funds]] [[entities/treasury-securities]] [[entities/total-bond-index]]
- Diversifying the bond portion (e.g., adding Treasuries) helps mitigate the risk of stocks and corporate bonds moving together in market downturns. [[concepts/bond-diversification]]
