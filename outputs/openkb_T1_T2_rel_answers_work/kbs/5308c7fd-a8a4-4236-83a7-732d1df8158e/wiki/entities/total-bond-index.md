## Total Bond Index

The **Total Bond Index** is a broad, diversified benchmark that aims to represent the performance of the entire U.S. investment‑grade bond market, including government, corporate, and mortgage‑backed securities. It is commonly used as the basis for low‑cost total‑bond mutual funds or ETFs.

### Key Facts from Session 15 (2023‑07‑15)
- Recommended as a stabilizing component in a mixed stock‑bond portfolio.
- Suggested to make up **50‑66%** of the fixed‑income slice when paired with corporate bonds, providing downside protection and lowering overall portfolio volatility. [[summaries/session-15_2023-07-15]]
- Complements [[entities/corporate-bond-funds]] by adding government‑backed exposure, which tends to hold up better during equity market downturns.

### Related Concepts
- **Bond diversification**: Using a total bond index helps spread credit and duration risk across many issuers, aligning with the principle of [[concepts/bond-diversification]].
- **Asset‑allocation rules**: Incorporating a total bond index fits common allocation heuristics, such as adjusting the equity share based on age or risk tolerance [[concepts/asset-allocation-rules]].
- **Rebalancing strategy**: When the portfolio drifts from its target mix, rebalancing may involve shifting between [[entities/total-bond-index]] and other assets like [[entities/stocks]] or [[entities/treasury-securities]] according to the chosen [[concepts/rebalancing-strategy]].

### Typical Use Cases
- Core holding in a **simple, long‑term portfolio** that seeks growth from equities while relying on the total bond index for stability.
- Replacement for multiple individual bond funds, reducing complexity and expense.
- Basis for a **fixed‑income allocation** that balances income generation (via corporate bonds) with defensive characteristics (via the total bond index and Treasury securities).

### Example Allocation
A suggested split within the fixed‑income portion: 
- **Total Bond Index**: 50‑66% of the bond allocation.
- **Corporate Bond Funds**: Remaining 34‑50% for additional yield.

This mix aims to achieve a smoother ride during market turbulence while keeping the portfolio easy to manage.
