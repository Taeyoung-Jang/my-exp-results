# High‑Yield Savings Account

A **high‑yield savings account (HYSA)** is a low‑risk, interest‑bearing deposit product offered by banks and fintechs. It provides higher returns than traditional checking or regular savings accounts while retaining instant liquidity, making it a common choice for the cash layers of personal finance plans.

## Key Characteristics
- **Liquidity:** Funds can be accessed at any time without penalty, which is essential for emergency‑fund needs.
- **Yield:** Interest rates are substantially above the national average for savings accounts, though still modest compared to market‑linked investments.
- **Safety:** Deposits are typically FDIC‑insured up to $250,000 per institution, preserving principal.
- **Use Cases in [[concepts/cash-bucket-allocation]]:**
  - Core component of the **emergency fund** (see [[concepts/emergency-fund-sizing]]).
  - Holding place for short‑term project and maintenance cash before it is deployed.
  - Acts as the “instant‑access” slice of a **short‑term treasury ladder** strategy (see [[concepts/short-term-treasury-ladder]]) when a portion of the emergency fund is allocated to rolling 4‑ to 8‑week Treasury bills.

## Guidance from [[summaries/session-38_2025-01-15]]
- For a **9‑month emergency fund** of $58,500, the recommendation was to keep **$25–30k** in a HYSA for immediate access.
- The **remaining emergency cash** ($28.5k–33.5k) can be placed in short‑term [[entities/treasury-bill]]s, creating a ladder that maintains liquidity while boosting yield.
- Separate cash buckets for **home‑maintenance** ($8k) and **near‑term projects** ($20k) were also suggested to stay in the HYSA, ensuring the money is ready when needed.

## Typical Allocation Strategy
1. **Instant‑access core:** 50‑55% of the emergency fund in HYSA.
2. **Yield‑enhancing layer:** Remaining emergency cash in a rolling short‑term Treasury bill ladder.
3. **Project/maintenance pools:** Keep dedicated amounts in HYSA to avoid mixing with true emergency reserves.
