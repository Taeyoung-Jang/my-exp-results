# Broad Market Index Funds

**Type:** Product

Broad Market Index Funds are low‑cost, passively managed funds that aim to replicate the performance of a wide‑based equity market index (e.g., total market or S&P 500). In the advice given in [[summaries/session-15_2023-07-15]], they are recommended as the core stock component of a simple, long‑term portfolio because they provide diversified exposure with minimal maintenance.

**Key points from the session**

- Used together with [[entities/corporate-bond-funds]] and other fixed‑income vehicles to form a balanced allocation.
- Paired with the [[concepts/asset-allocation-rules]] such as “100 minus your age” to set the equity share.
- The portfolio’s rebalancing approach follows the [[concepts/rebalancing-strategy]] of annual or semi‑annual adjustments, or when the allocation drifts more than 5 %.
- When combined with a bond side that includes [[entities/treasury-securities]] or a [[entities/total-bond-index]], the overall mix benefits from [[concepts/bond-diversification]].

**Why they are favored**

- Broad market coverage reduces single‑stock risk.
- Low expense ratios keep costs low, improving long‑term returns.
- Passive management means the investor does not need to pick individual stocks, aligning with the goal of a “set‑and‑forget” portfolio.

**Related entities**

- [[entities/stocks]] – the asset class that these funds represent.
- [[entities/corporate-bond-funds]] – the typical fixed‑income counterpart mentioned in the session.
- [[entities/treasury-securities]] and [[entities/total-bond-index]] – alternatives for bond diversification.
