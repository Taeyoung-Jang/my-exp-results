# Summary of session-38_2025-01-15

**Document type:** Conversation (financial planning guidance)

## Core Idea
The assistant outlines a practical framework for allocating cash across four buckets after a recent home purchase:  
1. **Emergency fund** (9‑month coverage),  
2. **Home‑maintenance/repair sinking fund**,  
3. **Near‑term project fund** (12–24 months), and  
4. **Long‑term investing**.  

The advice includes how to calculate essential expenses, how much to keep in a high‑yield savings account versus short‑term Treasury bills, and a sample dollar‑by‑dollar breakdown that totals the user’s $180 k of cash.

## Key Concepts & Findings
- **Essential expense definition** – mortgage (PITI), utilities, groceries, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments; exclude discretionary spending.  
- **Emergency fund sizing** – 6‑month vs 9‑month rule; recommendation leans toward 9‑months for new homeowners. [[concepts/emergency-fund-sizing]]  
- **Liquidity allocation** – keep roughly 50‑55 % of the emergency fund in a [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]] for instant access; the remainder in rolling 4‑ and 8‑week Treasury bills. See [[concepts/short-term-treasury-ladder]] for ladder design.  
- **Home‑maintenance sink** – rule‑of‑thumb ~1 % of home value per year (≈ $6.5k‑$10k for a $650k home); user example uses $8k.  
- **Near‑term project fund** – earmarked cash for known expenses (e.g., appliances, remodels) kept in cash or very short Treasury securities.  
- **Long‑term investing trigger** – any cash not needed for at least 3‑5 years can be moved into diversified growth assets such as [[entities/broad-market-index-funds]] or [[entities/corporate-bond-funds]]. This follows general [[concepts/asset-allocation-rules]].  
- **Phased investment** – optional 6‑12 month dollar‑cost‑averaging schedule vs lump‑sum deployment; choice depends on temperament.

## Actionable Steps
1. Calculate essential monthly expenses (include PITI, utilities, etc.).  
2. Choose emergency fund length (6 vs 9 months) and allocate [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]] vs Treasury bills accordingly.  
3. Set up a maintenance sinking fund (~1 % home value per year).  
4. Identify known projects and allocate a dedicated cash pool.  
5. Move any remaining cash to long‑term investments, either lump‑sum or via a 6‑12 month phased plan.
