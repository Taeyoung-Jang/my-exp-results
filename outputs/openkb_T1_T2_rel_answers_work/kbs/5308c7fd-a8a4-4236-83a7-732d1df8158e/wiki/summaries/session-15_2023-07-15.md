# Summary of Session 15 (2023‑07‑15)

This conversation provides practical advice for building a **simple, long‑term investment portfolio** that balances growth and stability. Key points include:

- **Time horizon** and **risk tolerance** drive the stock‑vs‑bond mix. A longer horizon and comfort with market swings favor a higher equity share.  
- A common **stock allocation rule**: *100 minus your age*, adjustable for aggressiveness. This serves as an anchoring heuristic. See [[concepts/asset-allocation-rules]].
- Recommend using **broad, low‑cost index funds** for both equities and fixed income to keep the portfolio low‑maintenance. Suggested vehicles are [[entities/broad-market-index-funds]] for equities and [[entities/total-bond-index]] or [[entities/treasury-securities]] for bonds.
- Example allocation profiles:
  - **Conservative** – ~33% [[entities/stocks]] / 67% bonds → ~10‑15% drawdown in a bad year.  
  - **Moderate** – 50% [[entities/stocks]] / 50% bonds → ~15‑25% drawdown.  
  - **Aggressive** – ~80% [[entities/stocks]] / 20% bonds → 30%+ drawdown.  
  These drawdown estimates are based on historical bear markets.
- **Rebalancing** strategy: rebalance **once or twice a year** or when the actual allocation drifts **>5%** from the target. This limits drift without excessive tinkering. See [[concepts/rebalancing-strategy]].
- **Corporate bond funds** add credit risk and tend to move with stocks in downturns, reducing their defensive benefit.  
- To improve downside protection, mix corporate bonds with **Treasuries or a total bond index**. A suggested split within the fixed‑income slice is **50‑66%** in [[entities/treasury-securities]]/[[entities/total-bond-index]] and the remainder in [[entities/corporate-bond-funds]]. See [[concepts/bond-diversification]].

Overall, the advice emphasizes a **simple, rule‑based approach**—pick a target equity percentage, use diversified index funds, and rebalance periodically—to create a portfolio that can endure market volatility while staying easy to manage.
