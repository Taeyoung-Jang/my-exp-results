# Bond Diversification

Bond diversification is the practice of spreading fixed‑income investments across different types of bonds—such as government securities, total‑bond index funds, and corporate bond funds—to lower overall portfolio risk and enhance downside protection.

## Why Diversify Bonds?

- **Credit risk mitigation**: Corporate bond funds carry credit risk and tend to move with equities during market downturns, reducing their defensive value. Adding Treasury securities or a total‑bond index introduces assets with lower credit risk that often hold up better when stocks fall.
- **Yield vs. safety balance**: Corporate bonds can provide higher income, while Treasuries and broad bond indexes offer greater stability. A mix allows investors to capture income without sacrificing the portfolio’s stabilizing function.
- **Improved asset‑allocation outcomes**: Diversified bond holdings complement the equity portion, helping the overall asset‑allocation strategy achieve its intended risk‑return profile.

## Implementation Steps
1. **Identify the fixed‑income portion** of your portfolio (e.g., 30 % of total assets).
2. **Split the bond allocation**:
   - **Treasury securities** (or a total‑bond index) – 50‑66 % of the bond slice.
   - **Corporate bond funds** – the remaining 34‑50 %.
3. **Select low‑cost index options** for each component, such as a broad Treasury fund and a total‑bond index fund.
4. **Monitor and rebalance** using the **[[concepts/rebalancing-strategy]]** (e.g., adjust when the bond mix drifts more than 5 % from target).
