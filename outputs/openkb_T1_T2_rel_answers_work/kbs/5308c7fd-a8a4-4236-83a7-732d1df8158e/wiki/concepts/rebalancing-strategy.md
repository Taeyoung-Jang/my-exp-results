# Rebalancing Strategy

**Definition**: A systematic approach for adjusting the weights of assets in an investment portfolio back to their intended target percentages.

## Core Principles
- **Frequency**: Typically performed **once or twice per year**. This cadence balances the need for maintaining the desired risk profile while minimizing transaction costs and tax impacts.
- **Threshold‑based triggers**: Many investors also rebalance when an asset class **drifts more than about 5 percentage points** away from its target allocation. This provides a quantitative rule that can prompt an out‑of‑cycle adjustment.
- **Goal**: Keep the portfolio aligned with the investor’s **risk tolerance and time horizon**, preventing unintended over‑exposure to any single asset class.

## Application in Session 15
In the conversation *session‑15_2023‑07‑15*, the assistant recommended a rebalancing strategy that combines both time‑based and drift‑based rules:
- **Timing**: Rebalance **once or twice a year**.
- **Drift trigger**: Rebalance when the **stock or bond allocation deviates by more than five percentage points** from the target mix.

These guidelines aim to keep the portfolio simple, low‑maintenance, and resilient during market volatility.
