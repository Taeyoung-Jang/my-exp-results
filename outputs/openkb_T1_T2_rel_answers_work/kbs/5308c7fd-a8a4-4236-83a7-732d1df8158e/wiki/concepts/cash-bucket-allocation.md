# Cash Bucket Allocation

Cash Bucket Allocation is a **structured approach** to distributing liquid assets across purpose‑specific buckets that reflect the investor’s time horizon, liquidity needs, and risk tolerance. It builds on the ideas of [[concepts/emergency-fund-sizing]] and [[concepts/short-term-treasury-ladder]] while feeding into broader [[concepts/asset-allocation-rules]].

---

## Typical Buckets (as described in [[summaries/session-38_2025-01-15]])

| Bucket | Purpose | Typical Horizon | Typical Instruments |
|--------|---------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Emergency Fund** | Protect against income disruption and unexpected expenses | 3–12 months (often 6–9 months) | High‑Yield Savings Account (HYSA) + short‑term Treasury bills (4‑week / 8‑week) |
| **Home Maintenance / Repair Sinking Fund** | Cover scheduled and unscheduled home upkeep | 1–3 years (often annual rule of ~1% of home value) | HYSA (primary) and optionally short T‑bills |
| **Near‑Term Project Fund** | Specific known expenses (e.g., appliances, remodels) | 12–24 months | HYSA or very short‑term T‑bills |
| **Long‑Term Investing** | Grow wealth over 3–5+ years | 5+ years | Broad‑market index funds, total‑bond index, other diversified assets |

---

## Liquidity Mix Within the Emergency Bucket

1. **Core instant‑access layer** – roughly 45‑55 % of the emergency total kept in an HYSA for zero‑delay withdrawals.
2. **Rolling short‑term Treasury ladder** – the remainder placed in 4‑week and 8‑week Treasury bills that mature regularly, providing a modest yield boost while preserving liquidity. This mirrors the [[concepts/short-term-treasury-ladder]] pattern.

## Decision Factors

- **Essential expenses**: Include mortgage (PITI), utilities, groceries, transport, insurance, and minimum debt payments. Discretionary items are excluded.
- **Emergency‑fund length**: Choose 6 months for stable income, 9 months (or more) for new homeowners or higher income volatility.
- **Home‑maintenance sizing**: Common rule of thumb ~1 % of home value per year (≈ $6.5k‑$10k for a $650k home). Adjust for age and anticipated repairs.
- **Known project costs**: Sum expected outlays over the next 12–24 months and allocate that amount to a dedicated cash bucket.
- **Investment horizon**: Any cash not needed for at least 3–5 years can be moved to long‑term growth vehicles.

---

## Example Allocation (full $180,000 cash pool)

```
Emergency Fund (9 mo = $58,500)
  • HYSA (instant)                $28,500
  • 4‑week Treasury bills          $15,000
  • 8‑week Treasury bills          $15,000
Maintenance / Repairs (HYSA)       $8,000
Near‑Term Projects (HYSA)          $20,000
Long‑Term Investing                $93,500
Total                              $180,000
```

The example follows the guidance from the conversation and demonstrates how the buckets sum precisely to the available cash.

---

## Practical Steps to Implement Cash Bucket Allocation
1. **Calculate essential monthly expenses** (include PITI, utilities, etc.).
2. **Select emergency‑fund length** (e.g., 9 months) and compute the total amount.
3. **Decide HYSA vs. Treasury split** – keep ~50 % in HYSA, allocate the rest to a rolling 4‑/8‑week ladder.
4. **Set up a maintenance sinking fund** (~1 % of home value per year) in HYSA.
5. **Identify near‑term projects** and park the required cash in HYSA.
6. **Allocate excess cash to long‑term investments**, either as a lump‑sum or via a 6‑12 month dollar‑cost‑averaging schedule.
