# Short-Term Treasury Ladder

A **short‑term Treasury ladder** is a cash‑management technique that spreads emergency‑fund money across a series of very short‑duration Treasury bills (e.g., 4‑week and 8‑week maturities) that mature and are reinvested on a rolling basis. The ladder balances the need for immediate access with a slightly higher return than a plain high‑yield savings account.

---

## Why Use a Treasury Ladder?
- **Liquidity:** At any point, a portion of the ladder is maturing within days to weeks, ensuring cash is available for unexpected expenses.
- **Yield Improvement:** Short‑term Treasury securities typically offer a modestly higher rate than a high‑yield savings account, especially when the market is offering attractive yields.
- **Risk Management:** Treasury bills are backed by the U.S. government, so credit risk is negligible.

## How It Was Applied in [[summaries/session-38_2025-01-15]]
In the conversation, the user had $180 k in cash (high‑yield savings + Treasury bills) and chose a **9‑month emergency fund**. The recommendation was:
- Keep the core emergency cash (≈ $25‑30 k) in a **high‑yield savings account** for instant access.
- Allocate the remaining emergency portion (~$28‑33 k) to a **short‑term Treasury ladder** using equal amounts in 4‑week and 8‑week Treasury bills.
- Roll the bills automatically so that as each matures, the proceeds are either re‑invested into the next rung or moved to the emergency pool if needed.

## Building the Ladder
1. **Determine the emergency‑fund size** (see [[concepts/emergency-fund-sizing]]).  
2. **Set the liquidity split** – typically 50‑55 % in a high‑yield savings account (e.g., [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]]) and the rest in short‑term Treasury bills (e.g., [[entities/treasury-bill]]).
3. **Choose rung lengths** – common choices are 4‑week and 8‑week maturities, which provide frequent turnover without locking cash for too long.
4. **Allocate funds** – for a $58,500 emergency fund, the example used:
   - $28,500 in HYSA (instant access)
   - $15,000 in 4‑week T‑bills
   - $15,000 in 8‑week T‑bills
5. **Automate reinvestment** – set up automatic purchases so that each time a bill matures, the proceeds are used to buy the next scheduled rung.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader **cash‑bucket allocation** strategy (see [[concepts/cash-bucket-allocation]]), where the emergency bucket is split between HYSA and short‑term Treasury ladders.
- Complements **asset‑allocation rules** by ensuring the cash portion of a portfolio is both safe and modestly yield‑enhanced.
- Works alongside **rebalancing strategies**; as the cash portion shrinks or grows, the ladder can be adjusted to maintain the desired liquidity ratio.
