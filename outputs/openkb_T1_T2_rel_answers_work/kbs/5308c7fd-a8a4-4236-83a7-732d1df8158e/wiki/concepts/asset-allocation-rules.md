# Asset Allocation Rules

Asset allocation rules are heuristics that help investors decide how much of a portfolio should be allocated to **stocks** versus **fixed‑income** assets. They provide a simple, repeatable framework for balancing growth potential against stability.

## Core Rules Mentioned in [[summaries/session-15_2023-07-15]]

1. **Age‑Based Rule** – Allocate a percentage to stocks equal to *100 minus your age*. This gives a baseline that can be adjusted up or down depending on personal risk appetite.
2. **Time‑Horizon Rule** – The longer the money can stay invested before it’s needed, the higher the equity portion can be. Investors comfortable with market volatility can lean more heavily toward equities.
3. **Risk‑Tolerance Adjustment** – If an investor is likely to panic during market drops, they should shift toward a higher bond allocation to smooth returns.

These rules serve as anchoring points rather than strict formulas; they are meant to guide the initial target mix before fine‑tuning.

## Example Allocation Profiles

| Profile | Approx. Stock % | Approx. Bond % | Expected Bad‑Year Drawdown |
|---------|----------------|----------------|----------------------------|
| Conservative | 33% | 67% | 10‑15% |
| Moderate | 50% | 50% | 15‑25% |
| Aggressive | 80% | 20% | 30%+ |

The drawdown estimates are based on historical bear‑market performance and illustrate the trade‑off between growth and stability.

## Interaction with [[concepts/bond-diversification]]

When applying asset allocation rules, the **fixed‑income slice** can be further diversified. A mix of **[[entities/treasury-securities]]** and a **[[entities/total-bond-index]]** alongside **[[entities/corporate-bond-funds]]** improves downside protection, especially since corporate bonds can behave similarly to stocks in downturns.

## Connection to [[concepts/rebalancing-strategy]]

After setting an initial allocation using the rules above, investors should periodically **rebalance** to maintain the target mix. A common approach is to rebalance **once or twice a year** or when the actual allocation drifts **more than 5 percentage points** from the target.

## Practical Implementation

- Use **[[entities/broad-market-index-funds]]** for the equity portion to keep costs low and maintain broad market exposure.
- Choose a blend of **[[entities/treasury-securities]]**, **[[entities/total-bond-index]]**, and **[[entities/corporate-bond-funds]]** for the bond portion, guided by the diversification rule.
- Apply the age‑based or time‑horizon rule to set the initial stock‑bond split, then adjust based on personal risk tolerance.
