# Emergency Fund Sizing

**Scope:** This concept describes the methodology for determining the appropriate amount to keep in an emergency fund, drawing on the discussion in [[summaries/session-38_2025-01-15]].

## Why It Matters
An emergency fund acts as a financial safety net that protects all other investment buckets from unexpected cash‑flow shocks. Proper sizing balances **risk tolerance**, **income stability**, and **household cost structure**.

## Core Principles
1. **Identify Essential Monthly Expenses**
   - Include non‑discretionary items: mortgage (principal, interest, taxes, insurance), utilities, groceries, transportation, insurance premiums, and minimum debt payments.
   - Exclude discretionary spending such as dining out, travel, or optional subscriptions.
2. **Choose a Coverage Period**
   - Conventional guidelines suggest **3‑6 months** of essentials for most households.
   - Homeowners often extend to **6‑9 months** because of higher unexpected costs (repair, property tax fluctuations, job changes).
3. **Calculate the Fund Size**
   - `Fund Size = Essential Monthly Expenses × Desired Months of Coverage`.
   - Example from the source: $6,500 × 9 months = **$58,500**.
4. **Allocate Within the Fund**
   - **Immediate‑access portion** (≈ 45‑55 %): keep in a **[[entities/high-yield-savings-account]]** for instant liquidity.
   - **Short‑term yield portion** (≈ 45‑55 %): place in rolling **[[entities/treasury-bill]]** ladder (e.g., 4‑week and 8‑week maturities) to capture a modest yield while preserving liquidity.

## Decision Factors
- **Job stability / income predictability** – more stable income leans toward the lower end of the range.
- **Homeownership status** – new mortgage owners usually opt for the higher (9‑month) level.
- **Personal comfort with risk** – conservative individuals may choose the higher coverage even if income is stable.

## Implementation Tips
1. **Track actual spending** for a few months, strip out non‑essentials, and compute the average essential expense.
2. **Set up automatic transfers** to maintain the target balance as expenses change.
3. **Review annually** or after major life events (e.g., new job, change in household size) and adjust the coverage period accordingly.
