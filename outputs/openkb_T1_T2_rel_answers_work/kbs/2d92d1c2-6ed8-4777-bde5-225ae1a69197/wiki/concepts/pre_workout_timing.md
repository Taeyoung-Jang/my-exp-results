# Pre‑Workout Timing

**Definition**: The practice of consuming a small, easily digestible carbohydrate‑focused snack roughly 30–60 minutes before a morning workout to provide quick‑acting fuel without causing gastrointestinal discomfort.

## Why Timing Matters
- Allows glucose from the snack to enter the bloodstream just as the workout begins, supporting performance and preventing early fatigue.
- Keeps the stomach relatively empty, avoiding the heaviness that can occur with larger meals.
- Works in tandem with an earlier balanced breakfast (see [[concepts/macro_balance]]) to maintain steady energy throughout the morning.

## Typical Window
- **30–60 minutes prior** to the workout.
- Example from [[summaries/session-39_2025-09-15]]: for a 10 a.m. session, aim for a snack at **9:15–9:30 a.m.**.

## Snack Characteristics
- Predominantly simple carbohydrates for rapid absorption.
- Small portion size (e.g., one banana, half a granola bar, a handful of dry cereal).
- May include a tiny amount of protein or fat on strength‑training days, but keep it light.

## Options Suitable for the Timing Window
| Snack | Reason | Shelf‑stable? |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| Banana | High‑glycemic fruit, quick energy | No |
| Half a granola bar | Portable carbs, moderate protein | Yes |
| Handful of dry cereal | Fast carbs, low volume | Yes |
| Small packet of applesauce | Convenient carb source | Yes |
| Nut‑butter squeeze pack + a few crackers | Slight protein/fat for strength days | Yes |
| Oat bar | Balanced carbs, non‑perishable | Yes |

## Integration with Overall Morning Fuel Strategy
1. **Early Breakfast (6–7 a.m.)** – Provide a balanced mix of protein, complex carbs, and healthy fats to set a stable energy baseline.
2. **Pre‑Workout Timing** – Add the quick carb snack 30–60 minutes before exercising to top‑up energy stores.
3. **Post‑Workout** – Follow with a recovery meal containing protein and carbs (outside the scope of this concept).
