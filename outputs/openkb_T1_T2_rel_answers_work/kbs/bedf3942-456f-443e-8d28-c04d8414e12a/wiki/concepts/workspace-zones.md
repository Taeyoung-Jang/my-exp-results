# Workspace Zones

**Workspace zones** are a spatial organization strategy used in hands‑on science labs to divide the classroom into clearly defined areas. By assigning specific functions to each zone, teachers can channel student activity, reduce clutter, and reinforce safety protocols.

## Core Zones
- **Material zone** – where supplies (e.g., specimens, tools) are initially stored and accessed.
- **Mess zone** – the workspace where mixing, cutting, or other messy actions occur. This zone is deliberately isolated so spills stay contained.
- **Cleanup zone** – includes the [[entities/waste-bin]] and [[entities/sink]] where used materials and sharps are disposed of or rinsed.
- **Tool zone** – a sub‑area for items like the [[entities/scalpel]] that are only introduced after safety checks.

## How the Concept Appears in [[summaries/session-09_2025-08-23]]
In the dissection lab script, the teacher creates three zones:
1. **Gathering zone** at the front for the initial reveal.
2. **Demonstration zone** where the safe scalpel set‑down motion is shown.
3. **Cleanup zone** highlighted by pointing to the waste‑bin and sink.
These zones give students a mental map that supports the [[concepts/lab-safety-protocol]] and keeps the activity exciting rather than chaotic.

## Benefits
- **Safety**: Limits where hazardous actions occur, making it easier to monitor compliance with glove and goggles requirements.
- **Clarity**: Students know exactly where to find materials and where to put waste, aligning with the safety checklist.
- **Efficiency**: Reduces time spent searching for supplies or cleaning up unexpected spills, allowing more focus on inquiry.
