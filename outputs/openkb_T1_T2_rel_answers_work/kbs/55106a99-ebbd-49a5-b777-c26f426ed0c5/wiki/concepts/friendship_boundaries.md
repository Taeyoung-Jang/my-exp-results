# Friendship Boundaries

**Definition**: A set of practices and communication approaches that help individuals protect their social energy while maintaining healthy, meaningful friendships.

## Core Principles (derived from [[summaries/session-02_2024-06-15]])
- **Treat social interaction as a finite resource** – recognize that each relationship draws a different amount of energy. [[concepts/social_energy]]
- **Prioritize a core inner circle** – focus regular, deeper contact on a few close friends and keep peripheral contacts lighter. 
- **Communicate your rhythm openly** – share that you are not always quick to reply and that you operate on a slower social pace. This sets expectations without drama.
- **Normalize pauses** – when busy, send brief “thinking of you” messages rather than over‑committing.
- **Offer honest rescheduling** – if you’re low on energy, say, “I want to talk, but can we pick this up tomorrow?” to preserve trust.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/social_energy]] by managing how much energy you allocate to each interaction.
- Supports [[concepts/relationship_maintenance]] through consistent, low‑effort signals (short messages, brief check‑ins) rather than high‑frequency contact.

## Practical Strategies
1. **Set a clear expectation** – e.g., “I’m not always quick to reply, but I enjoy hearing from you.”
2. **Use boundary scripts** – short, friendly phrases for declining frequent invitations or explaining why you seem “hard to pin down.”
3. **Keep communication genuine** – be fully present during the moments you do engage, even if they are brief.
4. **Balance with self‑care** – view boundaries as a form of self‑preservation that prevents burnout while keeping friendships warm.

## Benefits
- Reduces risk of social burnout.
- Increases the quality of interactions with core friends.
- Builds trust through transparent expectations.
- Allows friendships to ebb and flow naturally without feeling rejected.
