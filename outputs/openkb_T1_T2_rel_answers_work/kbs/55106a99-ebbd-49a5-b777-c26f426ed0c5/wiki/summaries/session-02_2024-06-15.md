## Summary

This conversation explores how to manage friendships by treating **social energy** as a finite resource and establishing a sustainable **social rhythm**. Key ideas include:

- **Social Energy Management**: Recognize that each relationship draws varying amounts of energy; monitor feelings of drain or grounding to guide engagement. [[concepts/social_energy]]
- **Small Inner Circle**: Prioritize a core group (e.g., three close friends) for regular, meaningful interaction, while keeping peripheral contacts on a lighter, occasional basis.  
- **Consistent Low‑Effort Signals**: Strengthen bonds through short messages, brief check‑ins, and fully present moments, rather than frequent, high‑frequency contact. [[concepts/relationship_maintenance]]
- **Setting Boundaries**: Communicate personal rhythm openly (e.g., “I’m not always quick to reply”) to set expectations without drama. Normalize pauses and offer honest rescheduling when energy is low. [[concepts/friendship_boundaries]]
- **Boundary Communication Scripts**: Provide ready‑to‑use phrasing for:
  1. Declining frequent invitations while expressing interest.
  2. Explaining “hard to pin down” behavior as a self‑care strategy.

  Example scripts illustrate how to convey limits compassionately, keeping doors open for deeper connections over time.  

- **Burnout Prevention**: Emphasize that a slower social pace helps avoid burnout, ensuring presence and quality when interactions occur.  

Overall, the discussion frames friendship management as a balance between **presence** and **self‑preservation**, offering practical language and a mindset for maintaining healthy, sustainable relationships. [[summaries/session-02_2024-06-15]]
