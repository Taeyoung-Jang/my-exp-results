# Package Pickup Strategy

**Definition**: A logistics approach that directs incoming parcels to fixed carrier locations—such as lockers, store fronts, or hold‑for‑pickup services—rather than a transient residential address, ensuring reliable receipt for people who move frequently.

## Core Elements

1. **Carrier‑specific locations**
   - **Amazon lockers**: Secure, self‑service lockers accessible 24/7 for package delivery and retrieval.
   - **UPS stores**: Retail outlets that accept shipments and allow customers to pick them up in person.
   - **FedEx offices**: Similar to UPS, offering hold‑for‑pickup and package redirection services.
   - Other carrier‑run hubs (postal office holds, local parcel shops) can serve the same purpose.

2. **Advantages**
   - **Address stability**: The pickup point does not change when you relocate, removing the need to update shipping addresses for each move.
   - **Security**: Packages are held in a controlled environment, reducing theft risk compared with unattended door deliveries.
   - **Flexibility**: You can retrieve items on your own schedule, which is useful for short‑term stays.
   - **Compatibility with virtual mailboxes**: While official mail goes to a [[concepts/stable-mail-address]], parcels are routed to carrier locations to avoid forwarding delays.

3. **Implementation Steps**
   - Choose primary carriers you use most (e.g., UPS, FedEx, Amazon).
   - Create an online account with each carrier and set a default delivery address to a nearby store or locker.
   - When ordering, select the carrier’s pickup location rather than a residential address.
   - Upon arrival, carriers typically send a notification (email or app) with a lock code or pick‑up instructions.
   - Collect the package within the carrier’s holding period (usually 7‑14 days).
