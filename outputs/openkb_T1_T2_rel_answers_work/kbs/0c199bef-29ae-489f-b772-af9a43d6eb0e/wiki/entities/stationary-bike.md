# Stationary Bike

A **stationary bike** is an indoor cycling device that provides a low‑impact cardiovascular workout. In the context of the morning‑routine advice from [[summaries/session-32_2024-12-12]], it serves as the central pillar of a light‑exercise habit designed to protect the [[entities/knee]] while still delivering movement.

## Role in the Morning Routine
- Used as the main activity in a **[[concepts/morning-routine]]** that lasts 45–60 minutes.
- Integrated with a **[[concepts/warm-up]]** (leg swings, ankle circles) to prepare the knee.
- Followed by a **[[concepts/green-yellow-red-adjustment]]** check to scale intensity based on knee health.
- Paired with a cool‑down stretch phase that supports recovery and joint health.

## Key Features Highlighted
- **Low resistance, steady cadence** – emphasizes joint lubrication rather than high‑intensity training.
- **Adjustable duration** – green days use the full bike block (20–30 min), yellow days shorten it (15–20 min), red days reduce to a very brief, pain‑free spin (5–10 min).
- **Compatibility with habit formation** – anchored to existing cues (e.g., coffee brewing) to reinforce the routine, aligning with **[[concepts/habit-formation]]** principles.

## Related Concepts & Entities
- [[concepts/green-yellow-red-adjustment]] – framework for adjusting the bike session based on knee condition.
- [[concepts/habit-formation]] – strategy for attaching the bike use to daily triggers.
- [[entities/knee]] – the joint the routine aims to protect.
- [[concepts/warm-up]] – preparatory movements before mounting the bike.

The stationary bike thus functions as a practical, adaptable tool for building a sustainable, joint‑friendly morning habit.
