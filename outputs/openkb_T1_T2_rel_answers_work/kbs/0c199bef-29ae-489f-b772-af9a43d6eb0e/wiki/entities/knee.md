# Knee

The knee is a hinge joint that connects the thigh bone (femur) to the shin bone (tibia) and allows flexion and extension of the lower leg.  It is essential for walking, climbing stairs, and most weight‑bearing activities.

## Relevance in Session 32 (2024‑12‑12)
In the conversation **[[summaries/session-32_2024-12-12]]**, the user’s knee condition directly shapes a **[[concepts/morning-routine]]** that balances light exercise with recovery.  The routine uses a **[[entities/stationary-bike]]** as the primary activity, framed by a **[[concepts/warm-up]]** and a cool‑down stretch.

### Green‑Yellow‑Red Adjustment
The session introduces a **[[concepts/green-yellow-red-adjustment]]** checklist to gauge knee health before each session:
- **Green:** Knee feels smooth, no pain → full routine.
- **Yellow:** Slight stiffness or warmth, improves with gentle motion → shortened bike block.
- **Red:** Sharp pain, swelling, or instability → very brief, low‑intensity activity.

### Habit Formation
The routine is anchored to existing morning cues (e.g., making coffee), supporting **[[concepts/habit-formation]]** by linking the new movement pattern to a stable trigger.

### Mobility Breaks
The warm‑up (leg swings, ankle circles) functions as a **[[concepts/mobility-break]]**, preparing the joint for the stationary‑bike spin and reducing the risk of aggravation.

## Key Takeaways
- Monitoring knee status with the green‑yellow‑red system helps prevent overuse injuries.
- A structured, time‑boxed schedule (45–60 minutes) that includes warm‑up, bike, cool‑down, and shower keeps the habit sustainable.
- Consistency and gentle progression are emphasized over intensity, aligning with broader principles of safe joint health.
