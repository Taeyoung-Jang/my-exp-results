# Mobility Break

A short, desk‑based routine designed to reduce post‑run stiffness, improve circulation, and refresh the body during the workday. It can also serve as a gentle mid‑morning or mid‑afternoon reset for anyone following a light‑exercise morning routine, helping keep joints like the knee comfortable throughout the day.

## Why it matters
- Helps counteract the tight hips and hamstrings that can develop after a morning run.
- Provides a quick way to re‑activate muscles and joints without leaving your workstation.
- Complements the [[concepts/warm-up]] and cool down protocols by offering a mid‑day reset.
- Supports the [[concepts/morning-routine]] and the [[concepts/green-yellow-red-adjustment]] framework by giving a low‑impact option on yellow or red knee days, keeping movement gentle while still promoting circulation.

## Typical 2‑minute sequence (from [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-11]])
| Time | Activity | Cue |
|------|----------|-----|
| 0:00–0:30 | March in place | Keep a light bounce, swing arms naturally. |
| 0:30–1:00 | Shoulder & upper‑back rolls | Roll shoulders forward then backward, keep movements smooth. |
| 1:00–1:30 | Standing hip circles | Stand tall, rotate hips in both directions, 15 s each way. |
| 1:30–2:00 | Standing quad stretch | Grab ankle, pull heel toward glutes, switch legs halfway. |

## How to integrate
- Perform the break after a few hours of sitting, especially if you notice tightness in the hips, hamstrings, or knees.
- Use it as a transition between tasks or before a meeting to reset posture.
- If stiffness persists, follow the break with a brief dynamic mobility set (e.g., leg swings) before moving to any static stretch.
- On days flagged as **yellow** or **red** in a green‑yellow‑red adjustment plan, substitute the standing quad stretch with a gentler knee‑friendly movement such as ankle circles or seated quad activation to avoid stressing the knee.
