# Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV)

The **Department of Motor Vehicles** (DMV) is a state-level government organization responsible for issuing driver licenses, registering vehicles, and maintaining related records. It often serves as the primary point of contact for residents who are relocating to a new state.

---
## Role in Relocation
When moving to a new state, the DMV is typically involved in several key steps:

1. **Driver license transfer** – Most states require an in‑person visit to the DMV to exchange an out‑of‑state license for a new one, often including a vision test. See [[concepts/driver-license-transfer]].
2. **Vehicle registration** – The DMV processes title transfers, issues new plates, and may enforce emissions testing or proof of in‑state insurance. See [[concepts/vehicle-registration]].
3. **Address change** – Updating the address on your driver license is a common trigger for the DMV to forward voter‑registration information to the new state. See [[concepts/address-change]].

The DMV may also provide forms or online portals for voter registration, but updating the license does not automatically enroll you to vote in the new state.

---
## Common Gotchas
- **Deadlines**: Many states impose a 10‑30 day window after moving to complete the license and registration updates.
- **In‑person requirements**: Even if you hold a valid out‑of‑state license, an in‑person visit is often mandatory.
- **Emissions and insurance**: Some jurisdictions require an emissions test and proof of in‑state insurance before finalizing vehicle registration.
- **Interstate data sharing**: The DMV’s voter‑registration data is shared with other states, leading to the cancellation of the old registration once the new address is recorded.
