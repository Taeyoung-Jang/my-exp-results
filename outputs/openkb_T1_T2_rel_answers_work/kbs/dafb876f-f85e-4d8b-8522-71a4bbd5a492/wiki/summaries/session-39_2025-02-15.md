# Summary of session-39_2025-02-15

This document provides a practical checklist and key considerations for anyone relocating to a new U.S. state, covering the sequence of tasks, required documents, common pitfalls, and how to verify voter registration.

---
## Core Checklist (in order of execution)
1. **Forward mail with USPS** – No documents needed, just old and new addresses.  
   *(USPS forwarding)*
2. **Gather essential documents** before the move:
   - Current driver’s license
   - Proof of identity (passport or birth certificate)
   - Social Security number
   - Two proofs of new residence (lease, utility bill, bank statement)
   - Name‑change documentation, if applicable
   - Vehicle title, current registration, and insurance information
3. **Update driver’s license** in the new state (usually in‑person with a vision test). [[concepts/driver-license-transfer]]
4. **Register the vehicle** and obtain new plates; may require:
   - Emissions testing
   - Proof of in‑state insurance
   - Title transfer and payment of any use/sales tax
   [[concepts/vehicle-registration]]
5. **Handle voter registration**:
   - Updating the license does **not** automatically re‑register you in the new state.
   - Submit a new voter‑registration application (often available at the DMV).  

---
## Common Gotchas
- **State‑specific deadlines** (typically 10‑30 days) for license and registration updates.  
- Some states require an **in‑person visit** even for an out‑of‑state license transfer.
- **Emissions testing** and **in‑state insurance** may be prerequisites before vehicle registration.
- **Use/sales tax** on vehicle value may apply even if tax was paid elsewhere.
- Voter rolls are updated via interstate data‑sharing; your old registration will eventually be voided.

---
## Verifying Voter Registration
1. Use the new state’s **voter‑registration lookup** tool (or vote.gov) to see if the DMV submitted your info.
2. If not listed, **register directly** through the state election website – re‑registering will not cause double‑registration issues.
3. Once registered, the former state will cancel the old entry automatically when residency data syncs.
