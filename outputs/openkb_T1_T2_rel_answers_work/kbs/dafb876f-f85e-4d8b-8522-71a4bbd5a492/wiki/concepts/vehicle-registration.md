# Vehicle Registration

Vehicle registration is the bureaucratic process of transferring a vehicle’s title, updating its registration, and obtaining new license plates when a person relocates to a different U.S. state. Proper completion ensures the vehicle is legally operable in the new jurisdiction and avoids penalties.

---

## Core Steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-39_2025-02-15]])
1. **Gather required documents** before visiting the local DMV:
   - Current vehicle title and registration.
   - Proof of in‑state insurance (often required before registration is processed).
   - Proof of new residence (e.g., lease, utility bill) – also needed for the driver‑license update [[concepts/driver-license-transfer]].
   - Emissions testing certificate if the new state mandates it.
2. **Visit the DMV** (or use an online portal where available) to submit the documents, pay any applicable fees, and request new plates.
3. **Pay any state "use" or sales tax** based on the vehicle’s current market value; some states tax the vehicle even if tax was paid elsewhere.
4. **Receive updated registration card and license plates**; attach plates to the vehicle and keep the registration card in the vehicle.

---

## Common Gotchas
- **Emissions testing**: Many states require a recent emissions test before they will issue a new registration. Failure to obtain this test can delay the process.
- **In‑state insurance requirement**: Some states will not complete registration until you provide proof of insurance from a provider licensed in that state.
- **Use/sales tax**: Even if you previously paid tax when purchasing the vehicle, the new state may assess a use tax based on the vehicle’s current value.
- **Deadline awareness**: States typically give a 10‑30 day window after moving to complete registration. Missing the deadline can result in fines.
