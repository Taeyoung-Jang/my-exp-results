## [[concepts/address-change]]

Changing your address after relocating involves a series of coordinated actions to ensure that government records, mail, vehicle registration, and voting status stay accurate. The steps and pitfalls are drawn from the real‑world checklist in [[summaries/session-39_2025-02-15]].

### Core Steps
1. **Forward mail with [[entities/usps]]** – Initiate USPS forwarding before you leave. No documents are needed; simply provide old and new addresses.
2. **Gather proof of new residency** – Two accepted documents (e.g., lease, utility bill, bank statement) are typically required for updating official records.
3. **Update driver’s license** – Most states mandate an in‑person visit to the [[entities/dmv]] for a [[concepts/driver-license-transfer]] and may require a vision test.
4. **Register the vehicle** – Use the gathered title, registration, and insurance to complete a [[concepts/vehicle-registration]]; some states also demand emissions testing or proof of in‑state insurance.
5. **Update voter registration** – Changing the license address does not automatically re‑register you; you must submit a new voter‑registration application, often available at the DMV.

### Common Gotchas
- **State deadlines**: Usually 10‑30 days after moving to update the license and vehicle registration.
- **In‑person requirements**: Even if you hold a valid out‑of‑state license, some states still require a face‑to‑face appointment.
- **Emissions/insurance**: Certain jurisdictions will not issue plates until emissions testing or in‑state insurance proof is provided.
- **Voter‑roll synchronization**: Interstate data‑sharing will eventually cancel your old registration once the new address is recorded.
