# Driver License Transfer

## Overview
Driver license transfer refers to the procedure required when a resident moves to a new U.S. state and must obtain a new, state‑issued driver’s license. The process is typically overseen by the local Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) and often includes an in‑person visit, identity verification, and sometimes a vision test.

## Key Steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-39_2025-02-15]])
1. **Gather Required Documents**
   - Current driver’s license
   - Proof of identity (passport or birth certificate)
   - Social Security number
   - Two proofs of new residence (e.g., lease, utility bill, bank statement)
   - Name‑change documentation, if applicable
2. **Visit the DMV**
   - Most states require an in‑person appointment.
   - Expect a brief vision screening.
   - Submit the documents listed above.
3. **Pay Applicable Fees**
   - Fees vary by state and may include a license issuance fee.
4. **Receive New License**
   - The new license typically arrives on the spot or is mailed within a few weeks.

## Common Gotchas
- **In‑person Requirement**: Even if you hold a valid out‑of‑state license, many states still mandate a face‑to‑face visit.
- **Vision Test**: Some states require a vision test regardless of your driving record.
- **Deadline**: States usually impose a 10‑ to 30‑day window after establishing residency to complete the transfer. See [[concepts/address-change]] for timing considerations.
- **Voter Registration**: Updating the driver’s license does **not** automatically update voter registration in the new state; a separate registration step is needed.

## Interaction with Other Processes
- The driver license transfer often precedes [[concepts/vehicle-registration]] because many states require a valid in‑state license before allowing vehicle title transfer or plate issuance.
- Address changes for mail forwarding are handled through [[entities/usps]] and should be completed before the DMV visit to ensure all correspondence arrives at the new address.
