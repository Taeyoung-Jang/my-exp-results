# Cross‑Contamination Traps

**Definition** – Areas or items in a kitchen that unintentionally retain and transfer food allergens or sensitivities, creating hidden routes for exposure.

## Typical Traps Identified in [[summaries/session-37_2025-01-20]]

| Category | Why It Traps Allergens | Mitigation Tips |
|----------|-----------------------|-----------------|
| **Shared surfaces & tools** | Cutting boards, colanders, baking sheets, and wooden spoons can hold microscopic residues even after visual cleaning. | Use dedicated, non‑porous tools (e.g., stainless‑steel or hard‑plastic) and keep them visually distinct in a [[concepts/allergy-safe-zone]]. |
| **Sponges & cloths** | Traditional kitchen sponges and reusable cloths pick up particles and spread them when reused. | Switch to disposable paper towels or a separate sponge for the safe zone; wash dedicated cloths frequently. |
| **Countertop wiping** | Re‑using the same cloth on counters transfers settled dust and allergen films. | Adopt the [[concepts/damp-wipe-technique]] and use a fresh cloth for each area. |
| **Appliance crevices** | Vents, mixer motor heads, oven fans, and toaster crumb trays collect fine flour dust and residue. | Regularly wipe with a damp cloth; use a [[concepts/hepa-vacuum-use]] for hard‑to‑reach corners. |
| **Pantry shelves & storage** | Flour and powdered ingredients settle on shelves, creating a thin film that can aerosolize. | Perform a slow, top‑to‑bottom damp wipe and let dry before restocking. |
| **Porous materials** | Wood and bamboo absorb oils and proteins, making thorough de‑contamination difficult. | Prefer [[concepts/material-decontamination]]‑friendly materials like stainless‑steel or silicone; replace high‑risk items first. |

## Why Dry Cleaning Fails

Dry sweeping, shaking towels, or using a broom can loft settled particles back into the air, turning a hidden trap into an active source of exposure. The [[concepts/avoid-dry-cleaning]] principle advises always using a slightly damp surface when wiping allergen‑prone zones.

## Connection to Kitchen Reset Practices

The [[concepts/kitchen-reset-checklist]] outlines a systematic approach to eliminate these traps during a kitchen overhaul: start with pantry shelves, introduce dedicated safe‑zone tools, and finish with appliances while avoiding dry cleaning methods.

---

**Key Takeaways**
1. Identify and isolate shared surfaces, sponges, and porous tools.
2. Replace high‑risk items with non‑porous alternatives.
3. Clean using damp wipes and HEPA vacuuming.
4. Follow a structured reset plan to prevent re‑aerosolization.
