# Fermentation Cues

**Definition**: Practical visual and tactile signals bakers use to assess the state of dough during fermentation, helping to distinguish between optimal, under‑, and over‑fermented conditions.

## Why cues matter
During both [[concepts/bulk-fermentation]] and [[concepts/proofing]] the dough undergoes rapid biochemical changes driven by yeast and bacteria. Because timing can vary with temperature, hydration, and flour type, bakers rely on real‑time cues rather than strict clock times to decide when to move to the next step.

## Bulk fermentation cues
| Desired state | How it feels / looks | Typical sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Good** | Dough feels puffier, smoother, and more relaxed; small bubbles are visible under the surface; folds stretch easily. | Gentle expansion, surface sheen, easy stretch. |
| **Under‑fermented** | Dense, tight texture; resists stretching; few or no bubbles. | Tight, heavy dough that feels like it’s fighting you. |
| **Over‑fermented** | Slack or watery edges; dough loses shape when folded; may feel very loose. | Dough spreads, loses tension, looks soupy. |

## Proofing cues
| Desired state | How it feels / looks | Typical sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Good** | Lighter, gently wobbly; a light poke springs back slowly but does not collapse; shaped loaf shows a slight dome. | Soft wobble, slow spring. |
| **Under‑proofed** | Firm, springs back quickly; appears unfinished. | Tight bounce, no give. |
| **Over‑proofed** | Fragile, overly puffy; a poke leaves a dent that doesn’t recover. | Collapsing dent, thin walls. |

## Factors that shift cues
- **Hydration**: High‑hydration dough looks softer and wobblier, so visual "done" cues appear earlier. True over‑fermentation is indicated by a soupy, hard‑to‑handle texture rather than merely wetness.
- **Whole‑grain / rye**: Faster fermentation and weaker gluten give a sticky, velvety feel. Look for a gentle puff and a more settled texture rather than large bubbles. Over‑fermented rye becomes gummy; under‑fermented stays dense and clay‑like.

## Using the cues
1. Observe the dough at regular intervals (every 30 min for bulk, every 15 min for proof).
2. Perform a simple **poke test**: gently press with a fingertip; note the spring‑back speed.
3. Combine visual (bubbles, doming) and tactile (stretch, wobble) information to decide whether to continue fermenting, shape, or bake.
