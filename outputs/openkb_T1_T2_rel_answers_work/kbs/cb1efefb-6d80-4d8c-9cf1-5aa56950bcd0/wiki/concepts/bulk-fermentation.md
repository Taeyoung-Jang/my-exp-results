# Bulk Fermentation

**Type:** Concept

Bulk fermentation is the first, active rise of sourdough after mixing. During this stage the yeast and bacteria in the starter consume sugars from the flour, releasing carbon dioxide that inflates the dough and producing mild acids that add tang and help strengthen the gluten network. Temperature acts as a thermostat: warmer dough speeds up microbial activity, shortening the timeline, while cooler dough slows the process and allows more flavor development.

### Practical cues (from [[summaries/session-02_2024-07-20]])
- Dough feels **puffier**, smoother, and more relaxed.
- Small bubbles become visible just under the surface.
- The dough stretches easily during a series of folds.

### Common failure modes
- **Under‑fermented:** dough remains dense, tight, and resistant to stretching.
- **Over‑fermented:** dough appears slack or watery around the edges and loses shape when folded.

### Relation to other concepts
- See also [[concepts/proofing]] for the later, gentler rise that follows bulk fermentation.
- The visual and tactile signs belong to the broader set of [[concepts/fermentation-cues]].
