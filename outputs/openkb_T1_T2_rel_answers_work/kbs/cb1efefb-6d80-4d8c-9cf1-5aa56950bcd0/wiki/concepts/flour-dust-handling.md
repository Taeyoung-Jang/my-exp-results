# Flour‑Dust Handling

**Definition**: A set of practices for safely removing and containing flour dust in a kitchen so that it does not become airborne and trigger cross‑contamination, especially for allergy‑sensitive environments.

## Why It Matters
Flour dust settles as a thin film on surfaces such as pantry shelves, appliance interiors, and vents. When disturbed, the fine particles can become airborne, increasing the risk of accidental exposure for people with food allergies. Proper handling therefore reduces the chance of hidden allergens re‑entering the air and contaminating the [[concepts/allergy-safe-zone]].

## Core Practices (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-01-20]])
1. **Treat flour dust as a surface film** – it can be removed with a simple wipe; no deep‑cleaning chemicals are required.
2. **Use the [[concepts/damp-wipe-technique]]** – keep cloths slightly damp so dust adheres rather than lofts.
3. **Avoid dry cleaning methods** – no sweeping, dry cloths, or shaking of textiles; see [[concepts/avoid-dry-cleaning]].
4. **Run ovens hot before wiping** – a high‑heat cycle burns off residual particles, after which a damp wipe removes the remaining film.
5. **Employ a HEPA‑rated vacuum** with a hose attachment on low suction for corners and floor edges; avoid bagless vacuums without HEPA filtration. See [[concepts/hepa-vacuum-use]].
6. **Minimize air movement** – keep windows closed and fans off while wiping to prevent dust from being stirred into the air.
7. **Integrate into a broader cleaning plan** – incorporate these steps into the [[concepts/kitchen-reset-checklist]] for a systematic 48‑hour kitchen reset.

## Typical Application Areas
- **Pantry shelves** – top‑to‑bottom damp wipe, allow to dry before restocking.
- **Appliance interiors** – oven, convection oven, stand‑mixers, and toaster interiors after a hot run.
- **Vent covers and fan housings** – remove removable covers, damp‑wipe or vacuum.
- **Countertops and cabinet fronts** – gentle damp wipe, especially around edges where flour can accumulate.

## Practical Tips
- Use paper towels for quick spot cleaning; reserve reusable cloths for larger surfaces.
- After each wipe, ensure the area is fully dry to avoid sticky residues that attract more dust.
- When using a vacuum, select the hose attachment rather than a rotating brush to keep airflow minimal.
- If you need to air out the kitchen, do so **after** all wiping and vacuuming is complete.
