# Material Decontamination for Allergens

## Overview
Material decontamination refers to the process of choosing kitchen surfaces and utensils that can be reliably cleaned of allergen residues, and applying cleaning methods that ensure no trace remains. This concept is central to creating an effective [[concepts/allergy-safe-zone]] and avoiding the pitfalls described in [[concepts/cross-contamination-traps]].

## Material Rankings (from session‑37)
| Material | Decontamination Ease | Allergen Retention Risk |
|----------|----------------------|------------------------|
| **Stainless steel** | Very easy – non‑porous, heat‑tolerant | Low |
| **Hard plastic (smooth)** | Easy – smooth surface, dishwasher safe | Low |
| **Silicone (high‑quality, non‑textured)** | Easy – can be washed thoroughly, but oily allergens may cling if not fully rinsed | Moderate |
| **Wood** | Difficult – porous, can trap proteins and oils | High |
| **Bamboo** | Difficult – similar to wood, porous | High |

The top recommendation is to prioritize **stainless steel** and **smooth hard plastics** for any item that contacts food directly. [[entities/stainless-steel]] surfaces are especially valued for their ability to withstand high‑heat sanitization (e.g., running an oven hot) without degrading.

## Replacement Priorities
1. **Cutting boards** – the single most critical surface; replace with stainless steel or solid plastic.
2. **Toaster** – especially for bread‑related allergens; choose a model with removable crumb trays that can be wiped clean.
3. **Utensils with crevices** – colanders, spatulas with detachable heads, and wooden spoons should be swapped for silicone or stainless alternatives.
4. **Other high‑contact items** – any tool that repeatedly contacts allergen‑containing foods (e.g., mixers, whisk attachments).

## Cleaning Techniques
- Use the **damp‑wipe technique**: keep cloths slightly damp to capture particles rather than loft them into the air. See [[concepts/damp-wipe-technique]].
- Avoid dry cloths, sweeping, or shaking towels, as outlined in [[concepts/avoid-dry-cleaning]].
- For fixed appliances (oven, stand mixer), run a hot cycle first to burn off residue, then wipe with a damp cloth.
- Employ a **HEPA vacuum** with a hose attachment for corners and shelves; avoid bagless models without HEPA filtration. Refer to [[concepts/hepa-vacuum-use]].

## Integration with Kitchen Practices
Material decontamination works hand‑in‑hand with the broader [[concepts/kitchen-reset-checklist]] for a 48‑hour kitchen reset. By selecting the right materials and applying the proper cleaning methods, you reduce the need for extensive redesign while maintaining a safe zone.
