# Avoid Dry Cleaning in Allergen‑Sensitive Kitchens

## Definition
Avoid using dry cleaning methods—such as dry cloths, brooms, or sweeping—that can loft allergen particles into the air. Instead, rely on damp wiping and controlled vacuuming to keep surfaces clean without re‑aerosolizing residues.

## Why It Matters
- **Cross‑contamination traps** like cutting boards, sponges, and countertops often hold invisible allergen films. When these surfaces are brushed with a dry cloth, the particles become airborne and can settle back onto food or the newly created allergy‑safe zone. [[concepts/cross-contamination-traps]]
- Flour dust, a common trigger for many food allergies, behaves like a fine powder. Dry cleaning can stir this dust, increasing inhalation risk and redepositing it on clean surfaces. [[concepts/flour-dust-handling]]

## Recommended Practices
1. **Damp‑Wipe Technique** – Keep wiping cloths slightly damp so that particles adhere to the cloth rather than floating away. This method is central to safe kitchen cleaning. [[concepts/damp-wipe-technique]]
2. **HEPA Vacuum Use** – When vacuuming, use a low‑suction HEPA‑filtered hose attachment. Avoid bagless vacuums without HEPA filters, as they can release captured allergens back into the air. [[concepts/hepa-vacuum-use]]
3. **No Fans or Open Windows During Cleaning** – Keep ventilation off while wiping to prevent disturbed particles from being pushed around the room.
4. **Integrate into a Kitchen Reset Checklist** – The avoidance of dry cleaning is a core step in any systematic kitchen reset aimed at establishing an allergy‑safe zone. [[concepts/kitchen-reset-checklist]]

## Material Considerations
Choosing surfaces that are easy to decontaminate—such as stainless steel or smooth hard plastics—reduces reliance on aggressive cleaning methods. While not a direct cleaning method, material choice supports the overall goal of keeping allergens in check. [[concepts/material-decontamination]]
