## HEPA Vacuum Use

**Definition:** A cleaning method that employs a vacuum equipped with a HEPA filter and a hose attachment, operated on low suction, to capture fine allergen particles (such as flour dust) without redistributing them into the air.

### Why it matters
- Flour dust and other microscopic allergen residues can become airborne during dry sweeping or regular vacuuming, increasing cross‑contamination risk.
- A HEPA filter traps 99.97 % of particles down to 0.3 µm, preventing them from re‑entering the kitchen environment.
- Using the hose attachment allows targeted cleaning of corners, appliance crevices, and pantry shelves where dust settles.

### Recommended equipment
- Vacuum with a certified HEPA filter (avoid bagless models lacking HEPA).
- Hose or narrow nozzle attachment for precision work.
- Low‑suction setting to minimize air turbulence.

### Best‑practice guidelines
1. **Keep surfaces damp** – combine with the [[concepts/damp-wipe-technique]]; wipe surfaces first, then vacuum remaining settled particles.
2. **Avoid dry cloths or brooms** – aligns with [[concepts/avoid-dry-cleaning]].
3. **Use only the hose attachment** – prevents blowing dust around.
4. **Vacuum in a single direction** – start from high points (top shelves) moving downward.
5. **Limit suction power** – high suction can stir up fine particles; low power keeps airflow gentle.
6. **Maintain the filter** – replace or clean the HEPA filter according to manufacturer recommendations.

The [[concepts/kitchen-reset-checklist]] for session‑37 recommends a HEPA vacuum for:
- Cleaning pantry shelves after a damp wipe.
