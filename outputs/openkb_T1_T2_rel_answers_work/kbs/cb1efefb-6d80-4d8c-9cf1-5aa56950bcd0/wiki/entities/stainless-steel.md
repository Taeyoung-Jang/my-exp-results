# Stainless Steel

Stainless steel is a corrosion‑resistant metal alloy known for its **non‑porous surface**, making it one of the easiest materials to fully decontaminate. In [[summaries/session-37_2025-01-20]] it is highlighted as the top recommendation for allergy‑safe kitchen upgrades.

## Key Points from Session 37
- **Material choice**: Listed under [[concepts/material-decontamination]] as the safest option for cutting boards, utensil sets, and other high‑contact items because it does not retain oils or protein residues.
- **Cross‑contamination reduction**: Replacing porous items (wood, bamboo) with stainless steel helps eliminate hidden allergen reservoirs identified in [[concepts/cross-contamination-traps]].
- **Cleaning method**: Works best with the [[concepts/damp-wipe-technique]] and can be safely cleaned using a HEPA vacuum (see [[concepts/hepa-vacuum-use]]) without risk of lingering particles.
- **Role in kitchen reset**: Included in the step‑by‑step plan of the [[concepts/kitchen-reset-checklist]] as a priority replacement item (cutting board, utensil set, toaster parts).
- **Comparison**: While [[entities/silicone-utensils]] are also easy to clean, stainless steel is preferred for its durability and complete lack of pores.

## Practical Use
- **Cutting boards**: Switch to a stainless steel board to prevent allergen buildup.
- **Utensils**: Choose stainless steel spatulas, spoons, and tongs for routine cooking.
- **Appliance parts**: When possible, select stainless steel components (e.g., toaster trays) to maintain a clean environment.

By integrating stainless steel into the kitchen, you create a more reliable barrier against accidental allergen exposure and simplify regular cleaning routines.
