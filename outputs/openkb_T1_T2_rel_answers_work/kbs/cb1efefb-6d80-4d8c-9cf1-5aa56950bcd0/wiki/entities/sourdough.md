# Sourdough

Sourdough is a **product**—a loaf of bread that relies on a symbiotic culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria to leaven and flavor the dough. The document *session-02_2024-07-20* provides a concise, non‑technical guide for home bakers, focusing on the practical aspects of its fermentation.

## Key points from the guide

- **Bulk fermentation** – The dough’s initial rise where the microbes consume sugars, produce CO₂, and generate mild acidity that strengthens gluten. Temperature controls the speed of this stage. See [[concepts/bulk-fermentation]].
- **Proofing** – The final, gentler rise that lets the shaped loaf settle and develop a light lift before baking. Microbial activity continues at a slower pace. See [[concepts/proofing]].
- **Fermentation cues** – Practical, tactile signs to judge readiness:
  - During bulk: puffier, smoother dough with surface bubbles; under‑fermented dough feels dense, over‑fermented dough looks slack or watery. 
  - During proof: light wobbliness, slow spring‑back; under‑proofed dough springs back too quickly, over‑proofed dough leaves a dent that doesn’t recover. See [[concepts/fermentation-cues]].
- **Hydration effects** – High‑hydration dough feels softer and more wobbly, which can be mistaken for being “done” early. True over‑fermentation leads to a soupy, hard‑to‑handle texture.
- **Whole‑grain and rye variations** – These flours ferment faster but produce a sticky, less‑elastic dough. Look for a gentle puff and a velvety feel rather than large bubbles; over‑fermented rye becomes gummy, under‑fermented stays dense.
