# Summary of session-02_2024-07-20

**Topic:** Simple, non‑technical explanation of sourdough fermentation for a friendly handout.

## Core ideas
- **Bulk fermentation** – yeast and bacteria consume sugars, produce CO₂ and mild acids that develop flavor and strengthen gluten. Temperature speeds or slows this activity. [[concepts/bulk-fermentation]] yeast, lactic acid bacteria, acidity, temperature.
- **Proofing** – the final, gentler rise where the dough relaxes and the shape sets. Microbial activity continues at a slower pace. [[concepts/proofing]]
- **Practical cues** – visual and tactile signs for both stages (puffiness, surface bubbles, wobbliness, spring‑back) and typical failure modes (over‑ vs under‑fermentation). [[concepts/fermentation-cues]] over‑fermentation, under‑fermentation.
- **Hydration effects** – high‑hydration dough feels softer and more wobbly, can appear “ready” earlier; true over‑fermentation leads to soupy texture. Hydration.
- **Whole‑grain / rye doughs** – ferment faster, feel sticky due to weaker gluten; look for gentle puff, velvety feel rather than large bubbles. Over‑fermented rye becomes gummy, under‑fermented stays dense. Whole‑grain dough, rye dough.
