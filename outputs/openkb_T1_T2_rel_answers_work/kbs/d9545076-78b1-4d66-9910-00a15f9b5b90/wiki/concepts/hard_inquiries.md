# Hard Inquiries

A **hard inquiry** (or hard pull) occurs when a lender or creditor checks your credit report as part of a decision to extend credit. Unlike soft inquiries, hard inquiries are recorded on your credit file and can cause a small, short‑term dip in your credit score.

## How Hard Inquiries Affect Scores
- Each hard inquiry typically reduces a score by a few points.
- The impact diminishes over time and generally falls off after 12 months; the inquiry remains on the report for up to 24 months.
- Multiple inquiries of the same type (e.g., auto‑loan rate shopping) that occur within a short window are often **grouped** and counted as a single inquiry by most scoring models.

## Grouping Rules for Auto Loans
- When you shop for an auto refinance, lenders’ inquiries are treated as a single event if they happen within roughly a **14‑day window**. This is referenced in the [[summaries/session-09_2023-05-12]] discussion.
- This grouping prevents your score from being hit multiple times during rate shopping.

## Practical Guidance from Session 09
- Apply for a new travel rewards credit card **first**, as its hard inquiry is assessed independently and has a modest impact.
- Wait **4–6 weeks** after the card inquiry before beginning auto‑loan rate shopping to let the initial inquiry settle.
- Keep your **credit utilization** low after opening the new card to avoid additional score drops (see [[concepts/credit_utilization]]).

## Best Practices
1. **Plan the timing** of credit applications to minimize overlapping inquiries.
2. **Concentrate auto‑loan shopping** into a short period (≈14 days) to benefit from inquiry grouping.
3. **Monitor your credit reports** after each inquiry to confirm the expected impact.
4. **Avoid large balances** on newly opened cards before the statement closes, as utilization can compound the effect of a hard inquiry.
