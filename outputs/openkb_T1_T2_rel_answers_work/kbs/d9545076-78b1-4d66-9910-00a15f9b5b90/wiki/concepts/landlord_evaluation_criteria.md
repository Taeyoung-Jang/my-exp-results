# Landlord Evaluation Criteria

Landlords evaluate prospective tenants on a range of indicators that together predict the likelihood of on‑time rent payments and responsible property care. The primary criteria highlighted in [[summaries/session-31_2024-11-12]] are:

## Core Assessment Areas

1. **Income & Employment Stability**
   - Verifiable steady income (pay stubs, employer contact).
   - Length of tenure with current employer.
2. **Credit History**
   - Overall credit score and any recent negative marks.
   - Credit utilization patterns (how much of available credit is used). [[concepts/credit_utilization]]
3. **Rent‑Payment Track Record**
   - History of paying rent on time.
   - Any past evictions or lease violations.
4. **References**
   - Written or verbal references from previous landlords or property managers.
   - Employer references confirming reliability.
5. **Background Checks**
   - Basic criminal and public‑record screening.
   - Verification of identity (photo ID).
6. **Additional Signals**
   - Demonstrated savings or ability to cover a higher security deposit.
   - Willingness to sign a longer lease or move in quickly.

## How This Ties Into Application Strengthening

Understanding these criteria helps renters tailor their documentation and communication. For example, providing a concise “renter intro” note, offering a larger security deposit, or using a guarantor service can directly address perceived gaps in the above areas. See [[concepts/rental_application_strengthening]] for tactics to improve each criterion.

## Interaction With Guarantor Services

When credit history is weak, tenants often supplement the evaluation with third‑party guarantors. The acceptability of such services is governed by the landlord’s own policies, which should be clarified during the application process. For an overview of common guarantor providers and their costs, refer to [[concepts/guarantor_services_overview]].
