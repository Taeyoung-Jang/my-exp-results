# Credit Utilization

Credit utilization is the ratio of credit card balances to their credit limits. It is a key factor in credit scoring models; lower utilization generally supports a higher score.

## Why it matters in the session-09 plan

In the **session-09** conversation, the user is advised to keep utilization extremely low after opening a new travel rewards card. The strategy includes:
- Paying the bulk of a large purchase immediately after it posts.
- Making a final payment a day or two before the **statement closing date** so that the balance reported to credit bureaus is near zero (or a tiny amount like $10).
- Avoiding large purchases on the new card until after the auto‑refinance inquiry window to prevent a temporary spike in utilization.

These actions help prevent a noticeable dip in the credit score when lenders pull the report for the auto loan.

## Practical tips extracted from the source
- **Timing of payments:** Multiple payments during the month are encouraged; there is no downside to paying down the balance often.
- **Reported balance goal:** Leaving a small balance (e.g., $10) is fine; reporting zero is equally effective for score stability.
- **Utilization target:** Aim for a utilization well below the typical 30% threshold, ideally under 5% for the new account during the critical period.
