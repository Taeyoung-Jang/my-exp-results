# Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD)

Meibomian Gland Dysfunction (MGD) occurs when the meibomian glands in the eyelids produce abnormal or insufficient oil, disrupting the lipid layer of the tear film. This causes increased tear evaporation, leading to symptoms of dry eye such as gritty, burning, or blurry vision.

## Causes & Risk Factors
- Age‑related gland changes
- Poor lid hygiene
- Environmental factors (dry air, wind)
- Contact lens wear
- History of refractive surgery ([[entities/refractive-surgery]]) which can affect corneal nerves and tear production

## Typical Symptoms
- Intermittent or persistent eye dryness
- Burning or itching sensation
- Blurred vision that improves with blinking
- Redness of the eyelid margins

## Diagnosis
Clinicians assess gland expressibility, oil quality, and lid margin inflammation during slit‑lamp examination. Meibography can visualize gland loss.

## Management Strategies
| Strategy | Details |
|---|---|
| **Warm compress therapy** | Apply a comfortably warm compress (e.g., a [[entities/warm-compress-mask]]) for **5–10 minutes** daily to melt hardened meibum and improve gland secretion. See also [[concepts/warm_compress_therapy]]. |
| **Lid hygiene** | Gently clean the lid margins with a mild, non‑foaming [[entities/lid-cleanser]] to remove debris and bacterial buildup. |
| **Artificial tears** | Use preservative‑free [[entities/artificial-tears]] several times daily to supplement the aqueous layer. |
| **Night‑time lubricants** | Consider a gel or ointment at bedtime to protect the ocular surface while sleeping; see [[concepts/gel_vs_ointment_nighttime_lubricants]]. |
| **Environmental control** | In dry indoor settings a small [[entities/humidifier]] can reduce evaporative loss. |
| **Lifestyle adjustments** | Follow the [[concepts/20-20-20-rule]] and practice good [[concepts/screen-ergonomics]] to reduce visual strain that can exacerbate dryness. |

- Sudden increase in symptom severity
- Persistent eye pain, redness, or photophobia
- Noticeable visual acuity loss
- Unilateral symptoms or signs of infection
