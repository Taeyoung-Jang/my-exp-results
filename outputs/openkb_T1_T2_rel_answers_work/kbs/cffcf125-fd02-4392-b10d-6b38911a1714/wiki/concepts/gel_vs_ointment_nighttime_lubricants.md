# Gel vs Ointment Nighttime Lubricants

**Type:** Concept

## Overview
Gel and ointment lubricants are the two main categories of nighttime eye drops used to alleviate dry‑eye symptoms while you sleep. Both aim to increase tear film stability, but they differ in viscosity, duration of action, and visual side‑effects.

---

## Key Differences
| Feature | Night Gel (e.g., [[entities/night-gel-lubricant]]) | Night Ointment (e.g., [[entities/night-ointment-lubricant]]) |
|---|---|---|
| Viscosity | Moderate – semi‑fluid, spreads easily | High – thick, semi‑solid |
| Duration of lubrication | 4‑6 hours (covers most of the night) | 8‑12 hours (provides a longer barrier) |
| Visual blur after application | Mild, usually clears within 5‑10 minutes | Significant blur that can persist into the morning |
| Ideal use case | Occasional nighttime dryness, patients who need clear vision shortly after waking | Severe nighttime dryness, incomplete eyelid closure, or when a longer‑lasting barrier is desired |
| Sensitivity to heat | Stable at room temperature | May become overly soft if stored warm; keep cool |

---

## When to Choose a Gel
- You experience **moderate dryness** at night but still need to see clearly soon after waking (e.g., for early‑morning reading or driving). 
- Your lids close fully and you do **not** have significant lagophthalmos.
- You prefer a product that **washes out quickly** with a splash of water in the morning.

## When to Choose an Ointment
- You wake up with **sticky, gritty eyes** or notice that your eyelids do not fully close during sleep.
- You have **severe dry‑eye** or meibomian gland dysfunction that requires a longer‑lasting protective layer.
- Blurred vision in the first few minutes after waking is acceptable.

---

## Practical Tips (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-15]])
1. **Start with a gel** as a safe middle ground; switch to an ointment if nightly dryness persists.
2. Apply the product **after a warm compress** (see [[concepts/warm_compress_therapy]]) to maximize meibomian gland function.
3. Use **preservative‑free artificial tears** ([[entities/artificial-tears]]) earlier in the day to reduce overall drop burden.
4. Monitor for **warning signs** such as sharp pain or sudden visual loss – see [[concepts/dry_eye_warning_signs]].
5. If you have had **refractive surgery**, ask your clinician about corneal nerve recovery (see [[concepts/corneal_nerve_recovery_post_refractive_surgery]]) before committing to a long‑term ointment regimen.

## Summary
- **Gel**: lighter, short‑term blur, good for moderate dryness and quick morning vision.
- **Ointment**: thicker, longer‑lasting, ideal for severe dryness or lid‑closure issues, but causes prolonged blur.
- Choose based on severity, eyelid closure, and personal tolerance for morning blur, and reassess after any eye surgery or changes in symptoms.
