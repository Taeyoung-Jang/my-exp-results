# Dry Eye Warning Signs

**Type:** Concept

Dry eye is usually a chronic irritation that can be managed with lifestyle tweaks and over‑the‑counter lubricants, but certain symptoms signal that the condition may be escalating or that an underlying problem needs urgent attention. Recognizing these warning signs helps patients decide when to seek prompt eye‑care rather than continue self‑management.

## Primary Warning Signs
| Sign | Why it matters | Typical action |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| **Sharp, sudden eye pain** | May indicate corneal abrasion, ulcer, or acute inflammation. | Contact an eye professional immediately (same‑day or emergency). |
| **Significant or worsening redness** that does not improve with lubricants | Could reflect infectious conjunctivitis, severe meibomian gland dysfunction, or inflammation. | Schedule a prompt appointment. |
| **Photophobia (light sensitivity)** beyond normal dryness discomfort | Often a sign of corneal epithelial compromise or surface inflammation. | Seek evaluation soon. |
| **Sudden decrease in visual acuity** or blurry vision that does **not** clear with a blink | May indicate corneal edema, a tear‑film breakup, or a more serious ocular surface issue. | Obtain urgent assessment. |
| **Unilateral symptoms** (one eye markedly worse, pain, discharge, or vision change) | Asymmetry can point to infection, a foreign body, or early post‑surgical complication. | Book an appointment promptly. |
| **Persistent gritty or foreign‑body sensation despite frequent drops** | Suggests underlying gland dysfunction or nerve damage that needs professional treatment. | Review with an eye‑care provider. |
| **Excessive tearing (epiphora) or discharge** | May be a reflex response to severe surface irritation or blockage of drainage pathways. | Get examined. |
