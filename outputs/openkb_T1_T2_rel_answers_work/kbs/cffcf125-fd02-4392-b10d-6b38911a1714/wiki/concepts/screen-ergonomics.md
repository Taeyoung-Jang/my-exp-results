## Screen Ergonomics

Screen ergonomics refers to the arrangement of your workstation and viewing habits that minimise visual fatigue and support comfortable, sustainable use of digital displays.

### Core Recommendations (from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]])
- **Viewing distance**: Position the monitor about an arm’s length away – roughly 60–70 cm – which aligns with the natural resting focus of the eyes.
- **Vertical placement**: Keep the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level so the eyes look down a small angle, reducing eyelid strain.
- **Posture**: Sit upright with shoulders relaxed; the screen should be directly in front of you to avoid neck rotation.
- **Lighting**: Use soft, even ambient light and minimise glare on the screen. Good lighting works together with ergonomics to lower the need for squinting. See [[concepts/lighting-for-vision]].
- **Regular breaks**: Apply the *20‑20‑20* rule – every ~20 minutes, look at something ~20 feet away for ~20 seconds – to relax the focusing muscles. [[concepts/20-20-20-rule]]
- **Moisture management**: Blink deliberately and consider a humidifier or artificial tears if eyes feel dry. [[entities/humidifier]] [[entities/artificial-tears]]

### Interaction with Other Concepts
- **Blue‑light glasses**: While some users wear them, they do not replace ergonomic adjustments for strain reduction. [[concepts/blue-light-glasses]]
- **Vision symptoms**: Persistent headaches or fatigue may indicate that ergonomic settings or the prescription need adjustment. [[concepts/vision-symptoms]]
- **Optometric exam**: When setting up an ergonomic workstation, it can be useful to ask the eye‑care professional to evaluate vision at the typical screen distance. [[concepts/optometric-exam]]

### Benefits
- Decreased eye fatigue and headache incidence
- Lower risk of developing long‑term visual discomfort
- Improved posture and reduced neck/shoulder strain

Implementing these ergonomic practices creates a healthier visual environment for daily screen use.
