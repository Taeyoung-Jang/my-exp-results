# Warm Compress Therapy

Warm compress therapy involves applying a comfortably warm (but not hot) source to the eyelids for a short period, typically **5–10 minutes**. The heat softens the secretions of the **meibomian glands**, helping them release oil that stabilizes the tear film and reduces evaporative dry‑eye symptoms.

## How It Works
- **Heat effect:** Gentle warmth melts the waxy component of meibum, making it easier for the glands to express oil onto the lid margin.
- **Tear‑film improvement:** A healthier lipid layer slows tear evaporation, leading to less ocular surface irritation.
- **Frequency:** Most people find **once‑daily** applications sufficient; it can be done in the **morning** before lubricating drops.

## Practical Tips (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-15]])
- Use a **microwavable warm‑compress mask** (or a re‑warmed washcloth) that retains steady heat.
- Ensure the temperature is **comfortably warm** to the fingertip; if it feels hot, let it cool before placing on the lids.
- Apply the mask for **5–10 minutes**; longer sessions do not provide additional benefit and may cause lid irritation.
- Perform the routine **daily** unless your eye‑care professional advises otherwise.

## Safety Considerations
- Avoid temperatures that cause a burning sensation; excessive heat can inflame the skin and ocular surface.
- If the lids appear **red or sore** after a session, reduce the heat or frequency.
- Do **not** use the compress on eyes with active infection, inflammation, or recent ocular surgery without professional guidance.

## When to Use Warm Compress Therapy
- **Meibomian gland dysfunction** (MGD) – see [[concepts/meibomian_gland_dysfunction]].
- **Dry‑eye symptoms** that worsen throughout the day, especially in environments with low humidity.
- As a complementary step to **preservative‑free artificial tears** (see [[entities/artificial-tears]]) and nightly lubricants (see [[concepts/gel_vs_ointment_nighttime_lubricants]]).

## Summary
Warm compress therapy is a simple, low‑risk, and effective home‑care method for improving meibomian gland output and stabilizing the tear film. When performed correctly—5–10 minutes of comfortably warm heat once daily—it can markedly lessen dry‑eye discomfort without the need for expensive equipment.
