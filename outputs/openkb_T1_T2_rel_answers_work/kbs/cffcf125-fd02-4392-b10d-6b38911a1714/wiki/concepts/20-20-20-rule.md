# 20‑20‑20 Rule

**Definition**: The 20‑20‑20 rule is a practical eye‑care habit that suggests taking a brief visual break every 20 minutes by looking at an object about 20 feet (6 meters) away for roughly 20 seconds. The pause relaxes the ciliary muscles responsible for focusing, helping to prevent fatigue from prolonged near‑work such as screen use.

**Source Context**: In the conversation *session‑13_2024‑06‑15*, the assistant recommends this rule as the "simplest" way to give eyes a reset during long screen sessions. The user is advised that the exact numbers are flexible – the key is a regular short break.

**Why It Works**:
- Near‑focus tasks (reading, computer work) keep the eye’s lens in a contracted state, causing strain over time.
- Looking at a distant point allows the lens to relax, reducing accommodative stress.
- The brief pause also encourages natural blinking, which combats dryness.

**Practical Tips**:
1. Set a timer or use software reminders to prompt the break.
2. Choose a spot with a clear view of something ~20 feet away (a wall, window, or distant object).
3. Use the pause to also stretch, adjust posture, or take a sip of water.
4. If 20 seconds feels too long, a shorter glance still offers benefit; consistency matters most.
