# Summary of session-38_2025-02-15

**Document type:** Conversation – user asks for practical dry‑eye management after an eye exam and refractive surgery.

## Key Points
- **Daily routine**
  - **Morning:** 5–10 min warm compress (microwavable [[entities/warm-compress-mask]] or re‑warmed washcloth). Follow with 1 drop of [[entities/artificial-tears]] per eye; repeat later if needed.
  - **Evening:** Gentle lid wash with a mild, non‑foaming [[entities/lid-cleanser]]. Use a gel **or** ointment if you wake up with dry or sticky eyes (gel = short‑term blur, ointment = longer‑lasting blur into morning). See [[concepts/gel_vs_ointment_nighttime_lubricants]] for details.
  - **Frequency:** [[entities/artificial-tears]] 2–4×/day are safe long‑term; using them every 1–2 hours on screen‑heavy or travel days is also acceptable, but a shift to constant hourly use signals a need for professional review.
  - **Warm compress safety:** Should feel comfortably warm, never hot; 5‑10 min daily is fine. Cool if it feels hot to finger/eyelid. Excessive heat can irritate lids.

- **Shopping list** (product types, not brands)
  1. **Warm‑compress mask** – microwave‑able, retains gentle heat.
  2. **Preservative‑free artificial tears** – single‑use vials or preservative‑free bottles ([[entities/artificial-tears]]).
  3. **Night‑time gel or ointment** – choose based on dryness severity and tolerance for morning blur (see [[concepts/gel_vs_ointment_nighttime_lubricants]]; [[entities/night-gel-lubricant]] and [[entities/night-ointment-lubricant]]).
  4. **Small humidifier** – optional, useful in dry indoor climates ([[entities/humidifier]]).
  
  **Avoid** drops with redness‑relieving agents, strong preservatives (e.g., benzalkonium chloride), or daily‑use medicated formulas unless prescribed.

- **Follow‑up questions for optometrist**
  1. How would you grade my dryness (mild/moderate) and what is the recommended drop schedule?
  2. Are my meibomian glands and oil layer healthy? ([[concepts/meibomian_gland_dysfunction]])
  3. Have my corneal nerves fully recovered after [[entities/refractive-surgery]]? ([[concepts/corneal_nerve_recovery_post_refractive_surgery]])
  4. Is my dryness typical for the postoperative stage I’m in?
  5. Any signs of flap‑related or corneal‑surface complications that would alter the plan?
  6. When should I seek urgent care (sharp pain, redness, light sensitivity, vision change, unilateral symptoms)? ([[concepts/dry_eye_warning_signs]])

- **Safety notes**
  - Frequent preservative‑free drops do **not** cause toxicity, but a sudden increase in baseline frequency may indicate worsening pathology.
  - Gel vs. ointment decision: start with gel; switch to ointment if nightly dryness persists or lids don’t close fully.
  - Watch for warning signs (sharp pain, worsening redness, photophobia, sudden visual drop, unilateral symptoms) to prompt earlier appointment.
