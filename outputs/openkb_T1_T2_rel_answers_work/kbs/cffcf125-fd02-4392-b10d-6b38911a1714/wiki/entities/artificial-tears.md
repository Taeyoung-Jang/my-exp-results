## Artificial Tears

Artificial tears are over‑the‑counter lubricating eye drops designed to moisten the ocular surface and relieve dryness, especially during prolonged visual tasks such as screen work. They are a core recommendation in both [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]] and [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-15]], where they are highlighted as a safe, preservative‑free option for daily dry‑eye management.

### Key Points from the Sessions
- **Use case:** Helpful for people who experience dry or tired eyes after many hours at a computer, reading small text, or after refractive surgery. In the later session, preservative‑free formulations were emphasized as safe for frequent (2–4×/day) and even hourly use on demanding days.
- **Frequency & safety:** Preservative‑free drops can be used multiple times daily without toxicity. A sudden need for drops every hour on most days may signal worsening dryness and merit a professional check‑up.
- **Complementary measures:** Can be combined with frequent blinking, a **[[entities/humidifier]]**, proper lighting, and a **[[entities/warm-compress-mask]]** for optimal surface moisture. For nighttime relief, a **[[entities/night-gel-lubricant]]** or **[[entities/night-ointment-lubricant]]** may be added.
- **Relation to eye‑strain and dry‑eye concepts:** While not a fix for focusing effort, artificial tears address the *dryness* component of eye strain, one of the symptoms outlined in [[concepts/vision-symptoms]]. They also support the health of the meibomian glands discussed in [[concepts/meibomian_gland_dysfunction]].
- **No direct impact on prescription:** They do not replace the need for an updated prescription or a dedicated computer lens; they simply improve surface moisture.

### Related Concepts and Entities
- **Screen ergonomics:** Proper screen distance and posture (see [[concepts/screen-ergonomics]]) reduce the need for frequent artificial‑tear use.
- **Lighting:** Adequate, even illumination (see [[concepts/lighting-for-vision]]) helps prevent excessive squinting and dryness.
- **Warm compress therapy:** A daily **[[entities/warm-compress-mask]]** used for 5–10 minutes can improve meibomian gland function, enhancing the effectiveness of artificial tears (see [[concepts/warm_compress_therapy]]).
- **Night‑time lubricants:** Use a gel for mild nighttime dryness or an ointment if dryness is severe; gels cause brief blur, ointments may blur into the morning (see [[concepts/gel_vs_ointment_nighttime_lubricants]]).
- **Refractive surgery considerations:** After procedures like LASIK, artificial tears are especially important while corneal nerves recover (see [[concepts/corneal_nerve_recovery_post_refractive_surgery]]).

**When to consider:** If you notice persistent dry‑eye symptoms despite regular artificial‑tear use, discuss a comprehensive eye exam with an optometrist to rule out underlying issues such as meibomian gland dysfunction or postoperative complications.
