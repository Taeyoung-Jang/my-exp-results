## Refractive Surgery

**Type:** Event

Refractive surgery refers to a group of ophthalmic procedures that reshape the cornea to correct refractive errors such as myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism. Common techniques include LASIK, PRK, and SMILE. The goal is to reduce dependence on glasses or contact lenses.

### Relevance to Session 38 (2025-02-15)
- The user mentions having undergone refractive surgery and asks about dry‑eye management specific to that history.
- Key concerns after refractive surgery include:
  - **Corneal nerve recovery** – often incomplete initially, leading to reduced tear production and increased dryness. See [[concepts/corneal_nerve_recovery_post_refractive_surgery]].
  - **Meibomian gland function** – can be affected, contributing to evaporative dry eye. See [[concepts/meibomian_gland_dysfunction]].
  - **Dry‑eye warning signs** that may require earlier follow‑up. See [[concepts/dry_eye_warning_signs]].
- Suggested follow‑up questions in the session include asking the optometrist about nerve recovery, typical postoperative dryness, and any flap‑related complications.
