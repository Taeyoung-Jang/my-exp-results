# Humidifier

A **humidifier** is a household appliance that increases the humidity level of indoor air. In eye‑care it is suggested as a simple, optional method to reduce eye dryness and fatigue, especially in dry indoor environments or when heating/air‑conditioning lowers moisture.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]]
- The assistant advises using a humidifier (or a similar moisture‑adding device) alongside regular breaks and blinking to help keep eyes comfortable.
- It is presented as one of several low‑effort strategies for managing eye strain, alongside proper lighting, screen ergonomics, and the 20‑20‑20 rule.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-15]]
- Recommended as an **optional** aid for dry‑eye management, particularly when the home air is dry or when HVAC systems remove moisture.
- Can be combined with a daily warm compress and preservative‑free artificial tears for a more comprehensive routine.
- Not essential for everyone; its benefit is most noticeable for people who experience increased dryness in climate‑controlled environments or after refractive surgery.

## Practical advice
1. **Placement:** Keep the humidifier near your workstation but not directly on the desk to avoid water damage.
2. **Maintenance:** Clean the unit regularly to prevent mold or mineral buildup, which could affect air quality.
3. **Usage:** Aim for indoor relative humidity of about 40‑60 %; higher levels may promote dust mites. Activate it especially in dry seasons or when using heating/air‑conditioning.
4. **Combine with other habits:** Pair humidifier use with the 20‑20‑20 rule, regular blinking, proper lighting, and a warm compress routine for maximum benefit.
5. **When to consider:** If you notice persistent dryness after refractive surgery or in climate‑controlled rooms, adding a humidifier can help stabilize tear film stability.
