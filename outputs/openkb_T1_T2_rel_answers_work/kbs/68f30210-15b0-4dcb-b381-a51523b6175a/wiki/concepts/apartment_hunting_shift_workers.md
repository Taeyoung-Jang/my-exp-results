# Apartment Hunting for Shift Workers

**Definition** – A systematic approach to locating and evaluating rental units that meet the unique needs of workers on rotating night‑shift schedules, focusing on daytime quiet, blackout capability, HVAC performance, and convenient commute.

## Core Principles
- **Consistency of sleep environment** is essential; prioritize units that allow a stable [[concepts/anchor_sleep]] regardless of shift changes.
- Evaluate **daytime noise** comprehensively because night‑shift workers must sleep during daylight hours. See [[concepts/daytime_noise_mitigation]] for detailed mitigation tactics.
- Ensure the building permits **blackout solutions** and has robust **air‑conditioning** to keep the bedroom cool during daytime heat.
- Proximity to the workplace reduces commute fatigue; aim for a 15‑20 minute drive.
- Budget constraints (e.g., $1,400/month all‑in) must be factored in early.

## Key Evaluation Criteria
| Category | What to Ask / Check | Why It Matters |
|----------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Noise** | Quiet during daytime? Any regular sources (landscaping, construction, pool, playground)? | Direct impact on ability to achieve [[concepts/anchor_sleep]]. |
| **Light** | Are blackout curtains allowed? Do windows support tension rods or hardware? | Controls daylight exposure for daytime sleep. |
| **Temperature** | Strength of AC? Does the bedroom receive direct sun? | Prevents overheating that disrupts sleep. |
| **Location in Building** | Unit position (corner, top floor, away from leasing office, pool, mailroom, elevators)? | Reduces ambient noise and foot traffic. |
| **Parking / Traffic** | Is there heavy vehicle or foot traffic near the unit? | Parking lot noise can disturb sleep through windows. |
| **Cost & Move‑In** | Total monthly cost with fees, earliest move‑in date? | Ensures the unit fits budget and timeline. |

## Outreach Text Template (6‑8 Questions)
```
Hi, I work night shift and need to confirm a few things before touring.
1. Is the unit quiet during daytime hours? Any regular noise sources?
2. Are blackout curtains allowed, and can the windows accommodate tension rods?
3. How strong is the AC, and does the bedroom get direct sun/heat?
4. Where is the unit in the building (near high‑traffic areas like pools or mailrooms?)
5. What’s the total monthly cost with fees, and the earliest move‑in date?
Thanks—happy to tour ASAP if it fits.
```

## Day‑Sleep Tour Checklist (10 Items)
1. Hallway noise level (doors, elevators).  
2. Outside noise with windows closed (traffic, construction).  
3. Bedroom sun exposure during the day.  
4. Window frame support for blackout curtains or rods.  
5. AC output in the bedroom (temperature drop).  
6. How quickly the room cools after AC is on.  
7. Nearby loud sources (dumpsters, playgrounds, dog areas).  
8. Proximity to elevators or stairs (foot traffic).  
9. Parking‑lot noise audible from the bedroom.  
10. Wall thickness – tap for echo indicating thin walls.
