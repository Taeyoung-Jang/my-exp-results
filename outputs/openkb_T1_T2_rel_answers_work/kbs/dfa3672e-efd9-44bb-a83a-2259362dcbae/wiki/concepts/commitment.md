# Commitment

**Definition**: Commitment is a promise made in good faith that signals respect for oneself and for the people to whom the promise is directed. It involves an intention to follow through, while allowing for honest reassessment when circumstances change.

---

## Core Elements (as discussed in [[summaries/session-13_2023-07-17]] and expanded in [[summaries/session-38_2024-07-17]])

1. **Respectful Intent** – A commitment demonstrates respect for the other party and for one’s own values. It is not a rigid contract but a signal of seriousness.
2. **Good‑Faith Basis** – The promise is grounded in an honest appraisal of one’s ability and willingness at the time of making it.
3. **Flexibility with Integrity** – When new information or priorities emerge, the responsible response is early, transparent communication rather than silent withdrawal. This maintains [[concepts/integrity]].
4. **Impact on Trust** – Consistently honoring high‑stakes commitments builds [[concepts/trust]]. Repeated casual breaches erode that trust.
5. **Adaptive Scheduling** – Commitments can be organized within a weekly planning framework that distinguishes *fixed* from *flexible* tasks, creating intentional "white space" to absorb unexpected changes. [[concepts/weekly-planning]] [[concepts/white-space]]
6. **Decision‑Checklist Reassessment** – A brief decision checklist helps decide whether an unexpected opportunity should reshape a commitment, ensuring the change is purposeful and communicated. [[concepts/decision-checklist]]

---

## How Commitment Relates to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/integrity]]** – Integrity governs the moral quality of how a commitment is kept or renegotiated. It emphasizes honesty about *why* a change occurs.
- **[[concepts/trust]]** – Trust is the social capital accrued when commitments are reliably honored. Breaking promises without clear communication diminishes trust.
- **[[concepts/weekly-planning]]** – A structured weekly plan helps surface which promises are truly fixed and which can be flexibly shifted, supporting realistic commitment management.
- **[[concepts/white-space]]** – Deliberate open blocks in a schedule provide the breathing room needed to reassess commitments without feeling overwhelmed.
- **[[concepts/decision-checklist]]** – A concise set of questions guides quick, principled decisions about reshuffling commitments when new opportunities arise.

---

## Practical Guidance

- **Set Expectation Levels**: Use clear language to indicate whether a promise is firm (“Count on me”) or tentative (“I’m likely in, but will confirm later”).
- **Communicate Early**: If circumstances shift, inform the affected party promptly, explain the reason, and take ownership of any inconvenience.
- **Assess Stakes**: Evaluate the importance of the original promise to the other person before deciding to alter plans.
- **Use a Weekly Template**: List fixed commitments first, then allocate flexible tasks into open blocks with built‑in white space. This visual layout makes it easier to see where adjustments are possible.
- **Apply the Decision Checklist**: When an unexpected opportunity appears, run through the 1‑minute checklist (e.g., does it matter more than the current task? Will moving the original task cause real consequences?).
- **Rescheduling Safeguard**: If a bumped flexible task is important (like exercise or a personal project), schedule it within a defined window (e.g., 48 hours) to prevent it from disappearing.
