## Integrity

Integrity refers to the quality of acting in honest alignment with one's stated commitments, especially when circumstances change. It is not about never altering plans, but about being transparent about *why* a change occurs and communicating that change responsibly.

Key points drawn from [[summaries/session-13_2023-07-17]]:
- A promise is a *commitment to act in good faith*; integrity means treating that promise seriously.
- When priorities shift, the responsible response is **early, honest communication** rather than silently ignoring the original agreement.
- Integrity involves evaluating the **stakes** of the original commitment (how much it matters to the other party) against personal priorities.
- Clear expectation‑setting (e.g., “I’m likely in, but will confirm later”) helps distinguish **casual** from **serious** promises, preserving trust.

### Relationship to other concepts
- [[concepts/commitment]] provides the foundation; integrity is the *ethical execution* of that commitment.
- Maintaining [[concepts/trust]] relies on consistent integrity; broken promises without communication erode trust.

### Practical application
1. **Assess stakes** – Determine how important the promise is to the other person.
2. **Weigh personal priority** – Ask if the new opportunity aligns with your values or is merely tempting.
3. **Communicate** – If you decide to change course, use language that acknowledges the original promise and offers responsibility, e.g., “I know I said I’d be there, and I’m sorry to change plans…”.

By balancing respect for commitments with honest, transparent communication, integrity supports both personal well‑being and healthy relationships.
