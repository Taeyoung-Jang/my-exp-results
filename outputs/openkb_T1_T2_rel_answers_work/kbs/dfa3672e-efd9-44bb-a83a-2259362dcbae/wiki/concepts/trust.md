# Trust

**Definition**: Trust is the confidence that a person will act in good faith, honor commitments, and meet the expectations set by their promises.

## Relationship to Commitment and Integrity

In the conversation from [[summaries/session-13_2023-07-17]], trust is presented as the core outcome of reliable [[concepts/commitment]] and authentic [[concepts/integrity]]. When a promise is made, the other party’s trust hinges on two main behaviors:

1. **Consistent Follow‑Through** – Keeping the word when the stakes are high reinforces the belief that future promises will also be kept.
2. **Transparent Communication** – If circumstances change, early and honest communication preserves trust by showing respect for the other person’s needs and plans.

## Building and Maintaining Trust

- **Assess the stakes**: Evaluate how important the original commitment is to the other person. High‑stakes promises demand stricter adherence.
- **Set clear expectations**: Use language that reflects the level of certainty (e.g., “I’ll confirm later” vs. “Count on me”). This prevents misunderstandings that can erode trust.
- **Own the impact of changes**: When a promise must be altered, acknowledge the effect, apologize, and offer to mitigate any inconvenience. This approach demonstrates integrity and helps retain trust.

## Trust in Decision‑Making

The document emphasizes that trust is not a static attribute but a dynamic one that can be strengthened or weakened by each interaction. Choosing to honor a promise, especially when it matters to the other person, reinforces trust. Conversely, repeatedly breaking firm promises, even with notice, can degrade trust over time.

## Key Takeaways

- Trust is built through reliable [[concepts/commitment]] and honest [[concepts/integrity]].
- Early, transparent communication when plans shift is essential to maintain trust.
- Clear expectation‑setting helps both parties understand the level of commitment, protecting trust from being unintentionally broken.
