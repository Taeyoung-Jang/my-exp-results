# Summary of Session 38 (2024‑07‑17)

**Main contribution**: A practical framework for weekly planning that balances *commitments* with *flexible* tasks, incorporates intentional [[concepts/white-space]], and provides a quick decision checklist for handling unexpected opportunities.

## Key Concepts
- **Weekly Planning as a Guide** – Treat the week as a flexible outline rather than a rigid script. [[concepts/weekly-planning]]
- **White Space** – Deliberate open blocks (e.g., 30‑minute gaps) that absorb surprises and prevent overwhelm. [[concepts/white-space]]
- **Commitments vs Flexible Tasks** –  
  - **Commitments**: obligations tied to other people or hard deadlines (meetings, appointments, promised events). [[concepts/commitment]]  
  - **Flexible tasks**: activities that can move without breaking obligations (exercise, personal projects, errands).  
- **Decision Checklist (1‑minute)** – A short set of questions to evaluate whether an unexpected opportunity merits reshuffling the schedule. [[concepts/decision-checklist]]
- **Rescheduling Safeguard** – If a flexible but important task (e.g., exercise) is bumped, it must be re‑scheduled within 48 hours to avoid drift.

```
Decision Checklist (1 minute)

2. Will moving the original task cause real consequences or just a small shift?
3. Does this opportunity give me energy, progress, or connection I want?
4. Do I know exactly where the bumped task will go?
5. Does the reshuffle still leave my week feeling manageable?

Safeguard Rule
If I bump exercise or a personal project, I must reschedule it within 48 hours.
```

## How to Apply
1. **Start each week** by listing all commitments first.  
2. **Create 1‑2 open blocks** (white space) for flexible tasks.  
3. **When an unexpected event appears**, run the decision checklist.  
4. **If you bump a flexible‑important task**, immediately assign a new slot within the 48‑hour window.

This approach keeps the structure steady while allowing guilt‑free pivots, ensuring important but non‑urgent activities don’t disappear.
