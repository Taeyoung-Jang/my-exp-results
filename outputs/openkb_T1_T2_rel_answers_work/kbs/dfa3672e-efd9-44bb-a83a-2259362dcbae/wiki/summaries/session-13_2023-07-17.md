## Summary

- **Commitment as respect**: A promise is framed as a *[[concepts/commitment]] to act in good faith* and a sign of respect for oneself and others. Keeping your word doesn’t require rigid adherence to the original plan, but it does require treating the promise seriously.

- **When circumstances change**: The responsible response is *early, honest communication* and ownership of any impact. This maintains [[concepts/trust]] and shows [[concepts/integrity]] without disappearing.

- **Casual vs. serious promises**: Distinguish the level of certainty up front. Use clear language such as “I’m likely in, but will confirm later” for lower‑stakes commitments and “Count on me” for firm ones. Setting these expectations creates appropriate boundaries and avoids being perceived as flaky.

- **Decision framework**:
  1. **Assess stakes** – How important is the original commitment to the other party?
  2. **Weigh personal priority** – Does the new opportunity genuinely align with your values or is it merely tempting?
  3. **Consider long‑term trust** – Repeatedly breaking firm promises erodes [[concepts/trust]].
  4. **Choose action** – Honor high‑stakes promises; otherwise, back out with transparent communication.

- **Sample language for backing out**: “I know I said I’d be there, and I’m sorry to change plans. Something came up that I want to take, but I didn’t want to leave you hanging, so I’m telling you as soon as I can. If this puts you in a bind, let me know and I’ll figure it out.” This phrasing demonstrates honesty, responsibility, and offers a chance to mitigate any negative impact.

- **Key takeaways**: Integrity is *not* about never changing plans; it’s about being honest about *why* you change them and communicating respectfully. Clear expectation‑setting and thoughtful evaluation of each promise help balance reliability with personal well‑being.
