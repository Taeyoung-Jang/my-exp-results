# Summary of Session 35 (2024-12-16)

This document provides a concise, neutral one‑pager that municipal leaders can read aloud (≈2 minutes) to explain practical policies for stabilising the tax base while preserving services. Six concrete options are presented, each with a specific example and a brief trade‑off note, followed by a short “common concerns & straightforward responses” segment.

## Policy Options (with examples and trade‑offs)

1. **Modest infill development** – e.g., permit a pair of two‑family homes on streets that already have water, sewer, and school bus routes.  
   *Trade‑off:* Slightly higher neighborhood activity, but new revenue with minimal extra service costs.  
   [[concepts/infill-development]]

2. **Streamlined permitting** – e.g., adopt a standard 30‑day review timeline for small accessory‑unit applications.  
   *Trade‑off:* Town must keep standards consistent and transparent.  
   [[concepts/permit-streamlining]]

3. **Routine infrastructure maintenance** – e.g., replace a small culvert before failure to avoid a costly emergency repair.  
   *Trade‑off:* Requires predictable annual spending.  
   [[concepts/infrastructure-maintenance]]

4. **Fee alignment with service costs** – e.g., adjust inspection fees to fully cover staff time on‑site.  
   *Trade‑off:* Some applicants face higher upfront costs, but the general tax base is protected.  
   [[concepts/service-fee-adjustment]]

5. **Land‑use planning linked to fiscal projections** – e.g., model how a proposed subdivision would affect school enrollment and road wear before approval.  
   *Trade‑off:* More upfront analysis needed.  
   [[concepts/land-use-fiscal-planning]]

6. **Encouraging mixed‑use development** – e.g., allow a small neighborhood‑scale commercial space (café or repair shop) on an existing busy corridor.  
   *Trade‑off:* Increased local activity, but diversified revenue reduces reliance on residential taxes.  
   [[concepts/mixed-use-development]]

## Common Concerns & Straightforward Responses

- **Impact on schools, roads, or services** – Respond with town‑specific data (enrollment trends, traffic counts, service‑cost analyses) to show the limited scale of change.  
  [[concepts/common-concerns]]
- **Neighborhood character** – Clarify the precise scale and location of any development to demonstrate minimal aesthetic or density shifts.  
  [[concepts/common-concerns]]
- **Streamlining weakening oversight** – Explain that clearer standards increase predictability without reducing scrutiny.  
  [[concepts/common-concerns]]

The overall message emphasizes that there is no “free” option; each lever involves a **trade‑off** between growth, cost control, and service expectations.  
[[concepts/tradeoffs]]
