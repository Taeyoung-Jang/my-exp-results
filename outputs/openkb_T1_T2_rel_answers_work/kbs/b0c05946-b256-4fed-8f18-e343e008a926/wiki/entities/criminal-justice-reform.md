# Criminal Justice Reform (type: other)

Criminal Justice Reform refers to a broad set of initiatives, policies, and social movements that seek to change how criminal law is applied, how courts operate, and how law‑enforcement and correctional institutions function. The goal is to create a more just, equitable, and effective system by addressing issues such as mass incarceration, racial disparities, excessive sentencing, and policing practices.

## Key Points from [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-15]]
- **Stability Argument:** The conversation notes that appeals to “stability” are sometimes invoked in criminal‑justice debates to caution against rapid reforms that could unintentionally affect public safety.
- **Legitimate Caution vs. Power Preservation:** It distinguishes between genuine risk‑assessment (e.g., concerns about unintended consequences) and uses of stability that protect entrenched punitive practices, linking to the broader theme of [[concepts/power-preservation]].
- **Evaluation Signals:** The document suggests checking who bears the risk of the status quo, whether the argument acknowledges the problem, and if concrete evidence is offered—principles that align with [[concepts/risk-assessment]].
- **Cross‑Domain Comparison:** The discussion places criminal‑justice reform alongside other policy areas—civil rights, climate policy, and healthcare—highlighting how stability arguments can play out similarly across domains.
