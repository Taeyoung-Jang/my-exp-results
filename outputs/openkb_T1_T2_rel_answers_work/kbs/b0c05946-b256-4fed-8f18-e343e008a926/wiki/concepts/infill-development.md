# Infill Development

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** Modest infill development adds housing within existing infrastructure to broaden the tax base.

---

Infill development refers to building new residences or mixed‑use projects on parcels that are already served by municipal utilities, roads, and schools.  The goal is to generate additional property‑tax revenue without the high incremental cost of extending services to new, outlying areas.

### Key Features
- **Location within existing service zones** – Projects are sited where water, sewer, electricity, and schooling are already in place, minimizing new capital outlays.
- **Modest scale** – Typical examples include a pair of two‑family homes or accessory‑unit conversions rather than large, high‑density complexes.
- **Revenue‑cost balance** – By filling gaps in already‑served neighborhoods, the town can collect extra taxes while keeping service costs relatively flat.

### Example from Session 35
In the 2024‑12‑16 discussion, a concrete illustration was provided: *allowing a couple of two‑family homes on streets that already have water, sewer, and school bus routes*.  This example shows how a small infill project can add tax revenue with little to no additional infrastructure spending.

### Trade‑offs
- **Increased neighborhood activity** – More residents can mean slightly higher traffic and demand on schools, but the impact is usually limited due to the modest scale.
- **Community perception** – Some residents worry about changes to neighborhood character; clear communication about the limited scope helps mitigate this concern.
