## Power Preservation

**Definition**
Power Preservation refers to the strategic deployment of *stability* arguments to protect and perpetuate existing power structures, often masking self‑interest as prudent risk management.

**How It Operates**
- **Framing Risk:** Presents potential changes as vague or overly risky, emphasizing uncertainty rather than concrete evidence.
- **Burden Shifting:** Argues that the costs of change will be borne by the broader public while those defending the status quo face little personal risk.
- **Obfuscation of Problems:** Downplays or denies the existence of systemic harms that reform seeks to address, thereby justifying inaction.
- **Appeal to Tradition:** Invokes the longevity and experience of institutions as inherent merit, suggesting that long‑standing systems are inherently superior.

|--------|---------------------------|
| **Civil Rights** | Calls for “stability” to avoid social unrest, effectively delaying needed legal equality for the [[entities/civil-rights-movement]]. |
| **Climate Policy** | Economic disruption narratives used by vested interests to stall the transition advocated by [[entities/climate-change-activism]]. |

| **Criminal Justice** | Emphasizes public safety fears to keep punitive practices that disadvantage marginalized groups, relevant to [[entities/criminal-justice-reform]]. |

**Distinguishing Legitimate Caution from Power Preservation**
- **Specificity of risk:** Legitimate [[concepts/risk-assessment]] cites concrete, evidence‑based hazards; power preservation relies on vague, generic threats.
- **Acknowledgement of the problem:** Genuine caution admits the issue exists and proposes incremental solutions; power preservation often ignores or downplays the problem.
- **Stakeholder exposure:** When those making the argument also bear the potential downsides, the stance is more credible. Power preservation typically benefits a narrow elite while placing risks on the wider public.
- **Long‑term viability:** If the argument would still hold after 5–10 years of unchanged outcomes, it leans toward genuine risk management rather than protecting entrenched interests.
