# Risk Assessment

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A systematic process of identifying, evaluating, and weighing the potential risks and benefits of maintaining the status quo versus implementing change.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Identify Specific Risks**  
   - Pinpoint concrete, plausible hazards rather than vague unease.  
   - Example from the source: concerns about energy‑system reliability when shifting climate policy.

2. **Evaluate Evidence**  
   - Use data, precedent, and expert analysis to judge the likelihood and severity of each risk.

3. **Assess Stakeholder Impact**  
   - Determine who bears the risk of inaction versus change.  
   - The source emphasizes checking whether the arguer’s constituency also faces the claimed risk.

4. **Compare Against Status‑Quo Harms**  
   - Weigh the identified risks against the ongoing harms of the current system.
   - The conversation notes that when status‑quo harms outweigh potential risks, the stability argument loses force.

5. **Explore Incremental Paths**  
   - If risks are real, propose measured, evidence‑based adjustments rather than blanket inaction.
   - This mirrors the checklist from the source that looks for an alternative path forward.

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/stability-in-politics]]** – Stability arguments are often framed as risk assessments; the quality of the assessment determines whether the argument is genuine caution or a cover for power preservation.
- **[[concepts/power-preservation]]** – Poor or selective risk assessments can be used to shield entrenched interests.

---

## Practical Application (from session‑14_2023‑06‑15)

The source provides a checklist for evaluating stability arguments in real time. The checklist is essentially a rapid risk‑assessment tool:

1. What specific risk is cited?  
2. Is the underlying problem acknowledged?  
3. Is an incremental pathway offered?  
4. Who bears the risk?  
5. Would the caution still make sense after 5‑10 years?

Applying this systematic approach helps distinguish between legitimate risk management and obstruction.

---

## Example Domains Mentioned

- **Civil rights**, **climate policy**, **healthcare**, and **criminal‑justice reform** all involve risk‑assessment debates about how quickly to implement change.
