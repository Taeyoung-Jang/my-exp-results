## Land‑Use Fiscal Planning

Land‑Use Fiscal Planning is the systematic integration of long‑term land‑use scenarios with municipal fiscal projections. By estimating how proposed developments will affect revenue streams (property taxes, fees) and service costs (schools, roads, utilities), towns can choose patterns that keep the tax base stable while preserving service levels.

### How it appears in practice
In the **session‑35_2024-12-16** discussion, the town considered modeling the impact of a proposed subdivision on school enrollment and road wear before approving it. This analysis would reveal whether the development’s tax contribution outweighs the additional costs of student transportation, traffic mitigation, and infrastructure wear. The approach supports decisions that **balance costs and revenue**, helping to avoid unexpected tax hikes.

### Key Steps
1. **Data Collection** – Gather current enrollment trends, traffic counts, utility usage, and cost‑per‑unit service data.
2. **Scenario Modeling** – Simulate various development layouts (density, mix of residential/commercial) to forecast fiscal outcomes.
3. **Decision Criteria** – Set thresholds for acceptable cost‑revenue ratios; if a scenario exceeds limits, adjust design or defer.
4. **Ongoing Review** – Update models regularly as actual costs and revenues materialize.

### Trade‑offs
The primary trade‑off is the need for **more upfront analysis** and possibly longer review timelines. However, this investment can prevent larger fiscal surprises later, aligning with the broader concept of [[concepts/tradeoffs]].
