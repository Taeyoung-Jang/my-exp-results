# Tiny Rituals

**Definition**: Tiny rituals are short, repeatable cues—often lasting 1–10 minutes—that signal the brain to shift from active mode to a relaxed, wind‑down state. They rely on consistency and a simple anchor to become automatic, making personal time possible even in fragmented evenings.

**Why they matter**
- They require minimal time, so they fit into unpredictable windows (e.g., after a baby feed or during a nap‑trap).
- By pairing a cue with a brief activity, they create a habit loop that reduces decision fatigue and the urge to scroll.
- Tiny rituals can be rotated to keep variety while preserving the core habit of a quick reset.

**Core elements**
1. **Anchor cue** – a single, consistent trigger such as dimming the lights, placing the phone face‑down, or lighting a small lamp. This aligns with [[concepts/anchor_habits]] and supports [[concepts/habit_consistency]].
2. **Micro‑activity** – a brief, low‑effort action (e.g., a grounding breath, a stretch, a sip of tea). Examples from [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-14]] include:
   - a one‑minute grounding pause: feet flat, shoulders relaxed, three slow breaths ([[concepts/grounding_pause]]);
   - a micro‑comfort ritual: rubbing lotion on hands or splashing warm water on the face ([[concepts/micro_comfort_ritual]]);
   - a quick stretch or neck roll (part of a [[concepts/mini_moods]] body‑reset).
3. **Default action** – keep a non‑digital object (book, calming object, comfy socks) within arm’s reach to replace the impulse to reach for the phone ([[concepts/default_action]]).
4. **If/then cues** – embed the ritual in everyday moments (e.g., "If the baby finally goes down, then place the phone face‑down and take a breath"). This leverages [[concepts/if_then_cues]] to make the ritual automatic.
5. **Phone placement** – positioning the phone out of immediate reach and using settings like Do Not Disturb adds friction to scrolling while keeping you reachable ([[concepts/phone_placement]]).

**Typical Tiny Rituals from the source**
- **Quiet‑light ritual**: dim lights, make a warm drink, sit quietly for 5 minutes.
- **Body‑reset**: gentle stretch, deep breathing, let the mind drift (3–5 minutes).
- **Micro‑reading**: read 2–3 pages of a gentle book (5 minutes).
- **Sensory reset**: step to a window or balcony for fresh air, then wash face (2–3 minutes).

**Integration with broader evening practices**
Tiny rituals complement larger [[concepts/evening_routine]] structures by providing modular, low‑commitment entry points. When combined with consistent anchors and the habit‑forming strategies above, they help parents and busy individuals reclaim brief, restorative moments each night.
