# If‑Then Cues

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A habit‑building strategy that pairs a recognizable situation (the *if* part) with a predefined micro‑action (the *then* part) to trigger a quick wind‑down or grounding routine.

---

## Core Idea
If‑Then Cues create mental shortcuts that automate the start of a calming habit. By embedding the cue in everyday moments—such as the baby finally settling or finishing a feed—the brain learns to associate the situation with a brief, restorative action, reducing reliance on willpower.

---

## Key Elements (from [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-14]])
1. **Specific Situations** – Identify moments that naturally occur in the evening routine, e.g.:
   - Baby goes down
   - Nap‑trapped state
   - Finished a feed
   - About to crawl into bed
2. **Paired Micro‑Action** – Choose a tiny, low‑effort activity (1–10 seconds) such as:
   - One slow breath and phone placed face‑down
   - Closing eyes for ten seconds, relaxing the jaw
   - Shoulder roll and three breaths
   - Docking the phone and enabling night mode
3. **Consistency** – Repeating the same cue‑action pair builds an automatic response, linking to [[concepts/habit_consistency]] and [[concepts/anchor_habits]].

---

## Example If‑Then Statements
- **If** the baby finally goes down, **then** take one slow breath, put the phone face‑down, and stretch the neck.
- **If** you’re nap‑trapped, **then** close your eyes for ten seconds, relax your jaw, or listen to a soothing audio with the phone locked.
- **If** you’ve just finished a feed, **then** pause shoulders, breathe three times before any other task.
- **If** you’re about to crawl into bed, **then** dock the phone out of arm’s reach and switch to night mode.

These statements act as *anchors* that cue the brain into a brief grounding state, supporting the broader [[concepts/evening_routine]] and [[concepts/tiny_rituals]].

## Practical Tips for Implementation
1. **Identify 3–4 high‑frequency evening moments** that interrupt your flow.
2. **Select a micro‑action** that takes ≤10 seconds and aligns with your preferences.
3. **Write the cue‑action pair** in plain language and place a reminder (e.g., sticky note) near the relevant environment.
4. **Pair with phone settings** (Do Not Disturb, blank home screen) to reduce temptation.
5. **Track consistency** for at least two weeks to let the cue become automatic.

---

## Benefits
- Reduces decision fatigue and the urge to scroll.
- Provides quick mental reset during unpredictable windows.
- Strengthens habit loops through clear, repeatable cues.
- Enhances overall evening calmness, supporting better sleep.
