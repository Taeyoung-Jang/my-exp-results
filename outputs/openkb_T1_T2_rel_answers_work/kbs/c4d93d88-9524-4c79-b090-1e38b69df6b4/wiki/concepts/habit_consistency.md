# Habit Consistency

**Definition**: The ability to perform core evening‑routine actions reliably, even when the day becomes chaotic or energy levels fluctuate.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]])
1. **Scale‑down, don’t skip** – When time or energy is limited, shrink each habit to a “bare‑minimum” version rather than abandoning it. Examples include:
   - 30 seconds of shoulder rolls instead of a full stretch.
   - One‑line note for tomorrow‑prep instead of a full to‑do list.
   - Turning down lights or washing the face as the calming cue.
2. **Pair with a cue** – Attach the habit to a fixed trigger (e.g., after dinner, after a shower) so it becomes automatic.
3. **Micro‑win mindset** – Treat any completed micro‑action as a success; this keeps the habit chain intact and sustains motivation.
4. **Flexibility across energy states** – Use the same anchor habits but adjust intensity:
   - Low‑energy: ultra‑short versions.
   - High‑energy: slightly longer versions while still staying within the 60‑120 minute window.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- [[concepts/anchor_habits]]: Consistency is built around a small set of anchor habits that are easy to repeat.
- [[concepts/evening_routine]]: A consistent routine reinforces the overall evening routine structure and reduces decision fatigue.

## Practical Tips
- **Pre‑set a minimal time block** (e.g., a 5‑minute timer) and commit to completing the habit within that window.
- **Use visual reminders** such as a sticky note on the bedroom door that lists the three anchor habits.
- **Log a quick check‑in** after each night (e.g., a simple ✅ or ❌) to keep awareness of streaks.
- **Allow “micro‑fallbacks”**: if you can’t stretch, do deep breaths; if you can’t write a note, say tomorrow’s priority out loud.

By embracing these strategies, the core evening routine stays resilient, providing the restorative benefits intended without turning into an additional source of stress.
