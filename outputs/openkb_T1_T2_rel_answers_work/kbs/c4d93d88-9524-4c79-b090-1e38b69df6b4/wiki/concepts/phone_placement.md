# Phone Placement

**Definition**: Phone placement is the intentional arrangement of a mobile device in physical space to create friction against mindless scrolling, while still allowing essential communication.

## Why it matters
- Reduces the automatic impulse to reach for the phone, especially during fragile wind‑down moments.
- Keeps the device accessible for important calls or messages, preserving reachability.
- Supports the broader goal of building sustainable [[concepts/tiny_rituals]] and [[concepts/anchor_habits]].

## Core principles from [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-14]]
1. **Consistent spot** – Place the phone on a shelf, charging stand, or opposite side of the couch so reaching for it requires a deliberate motion.
2. **Out‑of‑arm‑reach** – The location should be just far enough that you must stand up or walk a short distance, breaking the habit loop.
3. **Face‑down orientation** – During wind‑down, set the phone face‑down; the screen is hidden, reducing visual temptation.
4. **Dedicated docking** – When preparing for bed, dock the phone in a fixed spot and enable night mode to dim notifications.

## Practical setup steps
1. Choose a **home‑base** for everyday use (e.g., nightstand charging dock).
2. For evening wind‑down, move the phone to a **secondary spot** (e.g., a shelf across the room) and place it face‑down.
3. Activate **Do Not Disturb** with exceptions for essential contacts, ensuring you stay reachable.
4. Turn on **night mode** or a low‑brightness setting once the phone is docked.
5. Optionally, hide scrolling‑heavy apps in a folder labeled “Later” to add friction.

## Benefits
- **Reduced scrolling**: Physical distance and hidden screen create natural friction.
- **Improved sleep hygiene**: Night‑time phone placement limits blue‑light exposure.
- **Clear mental cue**: The phone’s location becomes a visual reminder that it’s time for a personal wind‑down.
