# Evening Routine

An **evening routine** is a short, intentional sequence of low‑stress activities performed after work or school to transition the mind and body toward rest.  The goal is to feel a gentle sense of accomplishment while avoiding overstimulation from screens or chores.

## Core Characteristics
- **Duration:** Typically 60–120 minutes, but can be scaled down to a 10‑minute “bare minimum” version on low‑energy nights.
- **Structure:** Combines three anchor habits – a physical reset, a brief tomorrow‑prep, and a calming cue – with optional creative or restorative elements.
- **Flexibility:** Routines can be adjusted for low‑energy vs. high‑energy days by shrinking or expanding each component.

## Common Templates (from [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]])
1. **Quiet Restorative** – Warm drink → tiny tidy → gentle stretch/shower → light reading.
2. **Creative Flow** – Music cue → quick space reset → 20‑40 min hobby (journaling, sketching, music).
3. **Active + Prep** – Short walk or light workout → shower → 5‑min tomorrow prep → unwind (show, podcast, book).

Each template follows the same underlying pattern of a **physical reset**, a **productive micro‑task**, and a **calming cue**.  The routine can be shortened by eliminating optional steps (e.g., skipping the tidy or reducing reading time) while preserving the anchor habits.

## Anchor Habits and Their Role
- **Physical reset** (e.g., stretch, walk) releases tension and signals the body that the day is ending.
- **Tomorrow‑prep** (a quick note or packing an item) unloads mental clutter.
- **Calming cue** (dimming lights, tea, shower) creates a sensory signal that promotes relaxation.

These anchors are discussed in more depth under [[concepts/anchor_habits]].

## Maintaining Consistency
Even on chaotic days, the routine survives by **scaling down** rather than skipping altogether – a principle highlighted in [[concepts/habit_consistency]].  For example, a 30‑second stretch, a one‑line note, and turning down the lights can preserve the habit chain.
