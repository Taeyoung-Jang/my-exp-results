# Mini‑Moods

**Definition**: A set of 3–4 interchangeable, time‑boxed wind‑down activities designed for the evening. Each mini‑mood lasts 5–10 minutes (or even as short as a minute) and can be swapped depending on the amount of free time available.

**Key characteristics**
- **Rotatable**: Parents can choose among several options (e.g., quiet‑light ritual, quick body reset, micro‑reading, sensory reset) to keep the habit fresh and avoid decision fatigue.
- **Micro‑scale**: Emphasizes brevity; the routine is intentionally brief so it sticks even when exhaustion tempts scrolling.
- **Cue‑driven**: Each mini‑mood is activated by a simple anchor cue (placing the phone face‑down, dimming a lamp, etc.) that triggers the habit [[concepts/anchor_habits]].
- **Consistency**: Repeating the same cue before any mini‑mood helps build habit consistency [[concepts/habit_consistency]].
- **Integration with other concepts**:
  - Uses **tiny rituals** to signal transition into wind‑down time [[concepts/tiny_rituals]].
  - Can include a **grounding pause** (feet flat, three breaths) as a starter [[concepts/grounding_pause]].
  - May involve a **micro comfort ritual** such as rubbing lotion or splashing warm water [[concepts/micro_comfort_ritual]].
  - Pairs with a **default action** (book, calming object) to replace the impulse to scroll [[concepts/default_action]].
  - Structured with **if/then cues** for common moments (baby down, nap‑trapped, post‑feed, bedtime) [[concepts/if_then_cues]].
  - Suggests strategic **phone placement** to create friction for mindless scrolling [[concepts/phone_placement]].

**Example mini‑moods from the source**
1. **Quiet‑light ritual** – Dim lights, make a warm drink, sit quietly without multitasking.
2. **Quick body reset** – Gentle stretches, deep breathing, allowing the mind to drift.
3. **Micro‑reading** – Two‑three pages of light reading, nothing work‑related.
4. **Sensory reset** – Step onto a balcony or open a window for fresh air, then wash the face.

These options are deliberately short so they can be performed in unpredictable windows, such as a one‑minute break while the baby sleeps or a brief pause after a feeding.

**Practical implementation**
- Choose a single **anchor cue** that always starts the mini‑mood (e.g., place the phone face‑down). 
- Keep the chosen activity under ten minutes to lower the barrier to start.
- Rotate the mini‑moods throughout the week to maintain novelty.
- Pair each mini‑mood with an **if/then** statement for seamless integration into daily life.
