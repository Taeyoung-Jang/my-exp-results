## Grounding Pause

**Definition**: A micro‑pause lasting about a minute that uses simple body positioning and breathing to signal a shift from active mode to relaxation mode. It is designed for unpredictable, short windows of time.

**Core steps**
1. Place your feet flat on the floor.
2. Relax your shoulders and drop any tension in your neck.
3. Take three slow, deep breaths, inhaling through the nose and exhaling through the mouth.
4. Optionally, close your eyes for a few seconds to deepen the sense of grounding.

**Purpose**
- Provides an instant mental reset without requiring equipment or a dedicated space.
- Helps break the autopilot loop that leads to mindless scrolling.
- Serves as a *tiny ritual* that can be anchored to a cue (e.g., baby finally goes down) and integrated into an *if/then* habit loop.

**Use case from [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-14]]**
- The session suggests the Grounding Pause as one of several *mini moods* for parents with fragmented evenings. It is presented as a “one‑minute grounding pause” that can be performed even while nap‑trapped or after a feed.

**Related concepts**
- Part of a broader set of [[concepts/tiny_rituals]] that focus on ultra‑short, repeatable actions.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/mini_moods]] by offering a quick, low‑effort option when time is scarce.
- Can be embedded in [[concepts/if_then_cues]] such as “If the baby finally goes down, then do a grounding pause before any other activity.”
- Supports the development of [[concepts/anchor_habits]] by providing a consistent cue (e.g., placing feet flat) that triggers the pause.
- May be paired with a [[concepts/default_action]] (e.g., keeping a small cushion or a breathing reminder nearby) to replace the impulse to reach for a phone.

**Implementation tips**
- Keep a visual reminder (post‑it, phone wallpaper) of the steps so the pause can be initiated without thinking.
- Pair the pause with a simple environmental cue, such as dimming the lights or placing the phone face‑down, to reinforce the habit.
- Track success by noting how often the pause is used in a week; consistency builds *habit consistency* over time.
