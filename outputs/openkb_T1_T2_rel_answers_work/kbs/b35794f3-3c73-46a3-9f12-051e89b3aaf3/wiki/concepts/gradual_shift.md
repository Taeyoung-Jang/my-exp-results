# Gradual Shift of Sleep Schedule

**Concept Overview**

The *gradual shift* approach involves moving your wake‑up time in modest steps—typically **15–30 minutes per day**—until you reach the desired earlier schedule. This incremental adjustment mirrors the natural adaptation process of the **[[concepts/circadian_rhythm]]**, reducing the feeling of jet‑lag‑like fatigue that can accompany larger, abrupt changes.

**Key Details from Source**
- Most people can reliably shift their internal clock by **15–30 minutes each day** without excessive grogginess.
- With strong **[[concepts/sleep_hygiene]]** practices—especially bright morning light—some individuals can manage up to **1 hour per day**, but beyond that the shift becomes uncomfortable and harder to sustain.
- The gradual method is recommended over a “big jump” because it minimizes sleep pressure spikes, keeps bedtime consistent, and supports long‑term adherence.

**Practical Steps**
1. **Set a target wake‑up time** and calculate the daily increment (e.g., 15 min earlier each morning).
2. **Maintain consistent wake‑up times** even on weekends, allowing at most a 1‑hour drift to preserve progress (**[[concepts/weekend_consistency]]**).
3. **Expose yourself to bright light** within the first 10‑15 minutes of waking to reinforce the new timing.
4. **Keep evening light low** and limit screen exposure at least an hour before bed, supporting overall **[[concepts/sleep_hygiene]]**.
5. **Monitor how you feel**; if you experience excessive fatigue, pause the shift for a day before continuing.

**Related Concepts**
- **[[concepts/circadian_rhythm]]** – the biological clock that the gradual shift aims to realign.
- **[[concepts/sleep_hygiene]]** – essential habits (light exposure, screen limits) that enhance the effectiveness of a gradual shift.
- **[[concepts/weekend_consistency]]** – maintaining similar wake‑up times on weekends to avoid undoing progress.
