# Sleep Hygiene and Caffeine Timing

**Definition**: Coordinating daily habits, particularly the timing of caffeine consumption, to support good [[concepts/sleep_hygiene]] and promote consistent, restorative sleep.

## Core Idea
Caffeine remains active for several hours; consuming it too late can interfere with falling asleep and reduce sleep quality. Aligning caffeine intake with a personal sleep schedule is a key component of a [[concepts/healthy_caffeine_routine]] and works together with broader sleep‑hygiene practices such as a regular wake‑up time, morning light exposure, and an evening wind‑down routine.

## Recommended Cutoff
- Stop caffeine approximately **eight hours before bedtime**. For a typical 10:30 pm bedtime, this means a **cutoff around 1–2 pm**.
- This guideline is highlighted in the taper plan from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]], where a 1 pm cutoff is suggested to protect sleep.

## Practical Implementation
1. **Identify your usual bedtime** and count back eight hours to set a daily cutoff.
2. **Track main caffeine sources** (e.g., [[entities/drip_coffee]], [[entities/latte]], [[entities/green_tea]], [[entities/energy_drink]]) and ensure none are consumed after the cutoff.
3. Use a simple “cup count” method rather than precise milligram tracking to stay aware of daily intake.
4. **Complement with sleep‑hygiene actions**:
   * Keep a consistent wake‑up time every day, even on weekends.
   * Get at least ten minutes of natural morning light (or use a [[entities/bright_light_lamp]]) soon after waking.
   * Dim lights and stop screens at least an hour before bed to reduce evening light exposure.
   * If a larger shift is needed, apply a [[concepts/gradual_shift]] of 15–30 minutes per day rather than a sudden jump.

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- The cutoff rule feeds directly into [[concepts/caffeine_cutoff]], which formalizes the time‑based limit.
- When reducing caffeine, a gradual **[[concepts/taper_plan]]** (half‑caff, smaller servings, decaf swaps) helps avoid withdrawal symptoms, tying into [[concepts/headache_management]].
- Consistent wake‑up times and light exposure reinforce the body's [[concepts/circadian_rhythm]], making the caffeine cutoff more effective.
- Maintaining a stable schedule on weekends supports [[concepts/weekend_consistency]], preventing the gains from being undone.
- The overall approach ties into a broader [[concepts/healthy_caffeine_routine]] that balances alertness with sleep quality.

## Benefits
- **Improved sleep onset**: Less difficulty falling asleep.
- **Higher sleep efficiency**: Fewer awakenings and deeper sleep stages.
- **Reduced next‑day caffeine dependence**: Regular timing can lower the overall daily caffeine requirement.
- **More stable circadian rhythm**: Consistent habits help the internal clock stay aligned, making mornings feel easier.
