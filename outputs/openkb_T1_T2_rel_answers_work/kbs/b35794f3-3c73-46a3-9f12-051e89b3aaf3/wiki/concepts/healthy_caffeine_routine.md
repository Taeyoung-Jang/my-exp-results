# Healthy Caffeine Routine

A **healthy caffeine routine** is a set of habits that let you enjoy caffeine’s benefits—alertness, mood lift, and ritual pleasure—while avoiding negative effects on sleep, anxiety, digestion, and dependence.

## Core Elements

1. **Appropriate Daily Dose**
   - Most adults tolerate **300–400 mg** of caffeine per day, but individual sensitivity varies.  
   - The routine should keep intake within a personal comfort zone, adjusting for factors like sleep quality, stress, and how jittery you feel.

2. **Timing and Cut‑off**
   - Caffeine’s half‑life is 5–6 hours, so stopping intake about **eight hours before bedtime** helps protect sleep.  
   - For a typical 10:30 pm bedtime, a cut‑off around **1 pm–2 pm** is recommended. See [[concepts/caffeine_cutoff]].

3. **Monitoring Signs of Overuse**
   - Common indicators include shakiness, anxiety, racing heart, headaches, difficulty falling asleep, and a growing need for caffeine just to feel normal.
   - If you notice these, consider reducing dose or adjusting timing.

4. **Gradual Tapering**
   - Reducing caffeine slowly—using half‑caff drinks, smaller servings, or swapping to decaf—minimizes withdrawal headaches.  
   - A structured week‑by‑week taper plan (see [[concepts/taper_plan]]) can keep the routine sustainable.

5. **Sleep Hygiene Integration**
   - Align caffeine habits with other sleep‑supportive practices: consistent wake‑time, limiting screen exposure, and creating a relaxing bedtime routine.  
   - Proper timing of caffeine complements overall [[concepts/sleep_hygiene]].

## Practical Tips from Session 13

- **Morning Anchor:** Keep a regular 12 oz drip coffee (~100 mg) as the primary daily dose.  
- **Mid‑Morning Adjustment:** Switch the second coffee or latte to half‑caff or a smaller size to cut ~50 mg.  
- **Afternoon Shift:** Move any green tea (~25 mg) to after lunch, then replace it with decaf or herbal tea to eliminate late‑day caffeine.  
- **Weekend Energy Drinks:** Limit to before noon or replace with a small coffee; an energy drink can contain 80 mg or more.

These steps illustrate how a **healthy caffeine routine** can be personalized while still preserving the enjoyable morning coffee ritual.
