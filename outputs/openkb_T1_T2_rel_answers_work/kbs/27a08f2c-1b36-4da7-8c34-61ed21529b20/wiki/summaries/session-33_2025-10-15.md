# Summary of session-33_2025-10-15

**Main contribution**: A concrete, plug‑and‑play schedule for a 7 pm – 7 am night shift that aligns light exposure, meal timing, short movement breaks, and a low‑stimulus “crash stack” for the 4–5 am dip, plus a gradual “partial flip” strategy for days off.

## Core Routine (7 pm – 7 am)
- **Bright‑light kickoff** (7:00‑7:20): stay in the brightest available light to signal “morning.” [[concepts/light-exposure]]
- **Main meal** (7:30‑8:00): treat as breakfast, protein‑rich, moderate carbs. *(meal timing)*
- **Movement breaks**: 9:30, midnight, 2:00 am – 2‑minute walk/stretch. [[concepts/movement-breaks]]
- **Mid‑shift lighting**: keep environment bright through 2 am, then gradually soften after 3 am.
- **Snack** (2:30‑3:00): light protein‑carb snack.
- **4–5 am crash mitigation** – two stacks (see below).
- **Pre‑end bite** (6:00): tiny snack (yogurt/fruit) to avoid hunger.
- **Commute wind‑down**: low lights, sunglasses if daylight, no screens, quiet. *(shift wind‑down)*

## 4–5 am Crash Stacks
### Stack A – Bright‑light available
1. **Bright light** – 30 s eye adjustment in a brighter area. 
2. **Movement** – 60‑90 s gentle walk or stretch. 
3. **Cold water sip** – 1‑2 oz. 
4. **Breathing** – 3 slow breaths (~30 s). 
5. Return to normal lighting.

### Stack B – Dim area only
1. **Movement first** – 90 s slow lap or stretch. 
2. **Cold water sip** – 1‑2 oz. 
3. **Breathing** – 3 slow breaths (~30 s). 
4. **Micro‑snack** (optional) – a few nuts or a small cheese piece. 
5. Resume work.

Both stacks aim to raise alertness just enough to finish the shift without triggering a strong “daytime” signal that would hinder sleep later. [[concepts/crash-mitigation]]

## Days‑off Partial‑Flip Plan (usual sleep 9 am – 4 pm)
1. **Day 1**: Shift sleep earlier to 7 am – 2 pm (‑2 h). 
2. **Day 2**: Aim for midnight/1 am bedtime, wake 8‑9 am. 
3. **Day 3**: Return to a later night‑shift‑compatible window (e.g., 3 am – 10/11 am) to ease back into the next shift. 

The goal is a gradual drift rather than a full reversal, preserving sleep stability while allowing daytime activities. *(partial flip schedule)*
