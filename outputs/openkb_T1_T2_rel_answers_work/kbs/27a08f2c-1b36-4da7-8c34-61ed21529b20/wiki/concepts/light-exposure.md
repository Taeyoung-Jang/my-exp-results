# Light Exposure

**Definition**: The strategic use of bright and dim lighting to signal wakefulness or rest, helping night‑shift workers align their circadian rhythms with unconventional work hours.

## Why It Matters for Night Shifts
- **Alertness cue**: Bright light in the first hour of a night shift (e.g., 7:00‑7:20) tells the body it is "daytime," boosting alertness without caffeine.
- **Gradual wind‑down**: Dimming lights after the midpoint of the shift (around 3 am) eases the transition toward sleep once the shift ends.
- **Commute protection**: Wearing sunglasses or keeping lights low on the way home prevents morning sunlight from resetting the internal clock.

## Practical Steps (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-10-15]])
1. **Kick‑off bright light** – Spend 10‑20 minutes in the brightest available area as soon as you start the shift.
2. **Maintain brightness** – Keep the work environment well‑lit through the middle of the night (10 pm‑2 am) to sustain daytime signaling.
3. **Taper lighting** – After a light snack at ~3 am, reduce glare and use warmer, softer lighting to begin the evening phase.
4. **Home transition** – On the commute, use low‑intensity lighting or sunglasses; once home, keep lights dim and avoid screens.

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/movement-breaks]]**: Short walks or stretches complement light exposure by providing additional physiological cues for alertness.
- **[[concepts/crash-mitigation]]**: When the 4‑5 am energy dip hits, a brief re‑exposure to brighter light (if possible) can be part of the reset stack.

## Benefits
- Reduces reliance on stimulants.
- Improves sleep quality after the shift.
- Supports a consistent routine that the body can learn to anticipate.
