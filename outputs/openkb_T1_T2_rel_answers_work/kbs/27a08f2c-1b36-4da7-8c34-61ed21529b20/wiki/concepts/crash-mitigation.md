# Crash Mitigation

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Practical, low‑stimulus strategies for overcoming the mid‑night energy dip on night shifts while preserving the ability to fall asleep after the shift ends.

---

## Core Idea
Crash mitigation refers to a brief, controlled “reset” that raises alertness enough to get through a low‑energy window (typically the 4–5 am period on a night shift) without triggering a strong physiological signal that the body should stay awake. The goal is a **gentle nudge**, not a caffeine‑style spike, so the post‑shift wind‑down remains effective.

---

## Techniques from [[summaries/session-33_2025-10-15]]
The source provides two concrete stacks that can be applied depending on the lighting conditions at the moment of the dip.

### Stack A – Bright‑light Available
1. **Bright‑light exposure** – 30 seconds in a brighter area to cue alertness.  
2. **Movement** – 60‑90 seconds of gentle walking or stretching.  
3. **Cold water** – 1‑2 oz sip.  
4. **Breathing** – Three slow breaths (~30 seconds).  
5. Return to normal lighting.

### Stack B – Dim Area Only
1. **Movement first** – 90 seconds of slow lap or stretch.  
2. **Cold water** – 1‑2 oz sip.  
3. **Breathing** – Three slow breaths (~30 seconds).  
4. **Micro‑snack (optional)** – a few nuts or a small piece of cheese.  
5. Resume work.

Both stacks keep stimulation modest: they avoid caffeine, intense exercise, or bright screens that could delay sleep onset.

## Practical Implementation Tips
- **Timing:** Deploy the stack as soon as the dip is felt (usually 4‑5 am).  
- **Duration:** Keep the total reset under two minutes; longer periods risk raising arousal too much.  
- **Environment:** If possible, use a lamp or step outside for a few seconds of daylight; otherwise rely on movement and hydration.  
- **Food:** Only a tiny protein snack if you’re hungry; avoid carbs or sugar that can cause a post‑snack crash.

---

## Benefits
- Maintains alertness for the remaining shift hours.  
- Prevents reliance on caffeine or stimulants.  
- Preserves the ability to wind down quickly after the shift, supporting restorative sleep.
