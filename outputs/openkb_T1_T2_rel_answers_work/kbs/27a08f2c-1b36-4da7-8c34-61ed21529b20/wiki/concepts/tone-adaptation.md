# Tone Adaptation

**Definition**: Tone adaptation is the practice of modifying the wording, phrasing, and overall voice of a message to align with a desired audience perception—ranging from neutral and formal to upbeat and casual.

## How it appears in the source document
In *session-06_2025-06-15*, the user asked for two versions of a forum announcement:
- A **neutral tone** version that stays factual and low‑energy.
- An **upbeat tone** version that adds enthusiasm and positive framing.

The assistant demonstrated tone adaptation by:
1. Changing introductory phrasing (e.g., "The latest update just rolled out" vs. "The update is live").
2. Varying word choice such as "nice tweaks" (upbeat) versus "a few small changes" (neutral).
3. Adjusting the level of excitement in the call‑to‑action while keeping the core information identical.

## Why it matters
- **Audience alignment**: Different community sub‑groups may respond better to a more formal or more energetic voice.
- **Clarity vs. excitement**: A neutral tone often maximizes clarity, while an upbeat tone can increase engagement.
- **Reusability**: Providing multiple tone options lets the original poster choose the style that best fits their community culture.
