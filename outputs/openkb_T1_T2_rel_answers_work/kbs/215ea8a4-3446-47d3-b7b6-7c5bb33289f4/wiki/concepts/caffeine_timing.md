# Caffeine Timing

**Definition**: The practice of aligning caffeine consumption with the body's internal clock to minimise disruption of sleep quality and daytime alertness.

## Core Principles
- **Avoid late‑day caffeine** – Consuming caffeine after the early afternoon can interfere with the natural decline of alertness needed for bedtime.
- **Shift intake earlier** – Prefer caffeine in the morning or before the early afternoon to take advantage of the body’s heightened metabolic clearance during the day.
- **Mind the window** – A practical rule is to stop caffeine intake by **2 pm** (or at least 6‑8 hours before the typical bedtime) to protect sleep onset.

## Why Timing Matters
Caffeine acts as a stimulant that can delay the onset of the **[[concepts/circadian_rhythm]]**‑driven sleep pressure. When taken too late, it shifts the internal clock, leading to difficulty falling asleep and reduced sleep quality. Proper timing supports a **[[concepts/consistent-sleep-schedule]]** and reinforces the benefits of other sleep hygiene practices such as **[[concepts/morning_light]]** exposure.

## Practical Tips from Session‑33
- After establishing a stable wake‑time, evaluate caffeine habits.
- Cut or move any late‑day caffeine (e.g., afternoon coffee, energy drinks) to the morning hours.
- If additional optimisation is needed, experiment with a stricter cutoff (e.g., no caffeine after 12 pm) and monitor sleep quality.
- Use the timing strategy alongside other keystone habits to maximise overall sleep health.
