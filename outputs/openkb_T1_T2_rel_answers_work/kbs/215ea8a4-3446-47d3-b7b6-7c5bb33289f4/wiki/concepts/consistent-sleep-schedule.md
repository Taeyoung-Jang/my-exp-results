# Consistent Sleep Schedule

**Definition**
A consistent sleep schedule is the practice of going to bed and waking up at roughly the same times each day, including weekends, to reinforce the body's internal [[concepts/circadian_rhythm]].

**Key Points from [[summaries/session-15_2023-01-18]]**
- Keeping a regular wake‑time within about **one hour** of the usual schedule—even on weekends—prevents large phase shifts that feel like jet lag.
- When a late night forces a shorter sleep, the recommendation is to still rise close to the normal time (within ~1 hour) and, if needed, use a brief **early‑afternoon nap** of 20‑30 minutes to mitigate fatigue without pushing bedtime later.
- For individuals with chronic insomnia, the priority remains consistency; even after a poor night, maintain the typical wake‑time window to protect bedtime rhythm.

**Key Points from [[summaries/session-33_2024-01-19]]**
- Tightening wake‑time consistency to a **15–20‑minute** window (no more than ±30 minutes on weekends) yields noticeable gains in alertness and daily smoothness.
- After stabilising wake‑time, increasing **morning light** exposure further strengthens the circadian cue.
- Fine‑tuning **caffeine timing**—avoiding late‑day caffeine—can be addressed once wake‑time and light are stable.
- If a late night occurs, it is better to get up near the usual time and, if necessary, take a short nap of **10–25 minutes** early in the afternoon rather than sleeping in for hours.
- When a nap isn’t possible, brief bright‑light exposure, a short walk, or 10 minutes outdoors serve as effective “second‑best” strategies.

**Why It Matters**
- Stabilises the circadian clock, leading to faster sleep onset and deeper, more restorative sleep.
- Reduces the need for compensatory naps or “sleep‑in” periods that can disrupt the next night’s sleep.
- Works synergistically with other sleep hygiene practices such as a [[concepts/screen-curfew]] and, when needed, a controlled early‑afternoon nap.

**Practical Recommendations**
1. Choose a target bedtime and wake‑time you can realistically maintain every day.
2. Aim for a wake‑time window of **±15–20 minutes** on weekdays and no more than **±30 minutes** on weekends.
3. After an occasional late night, keep the wake‑time close to the usual schedule and consider a short, early‑afternoon nap of **10–25 minutes** if daytime sleepiness persists.
4. Prioritise morning exposure to natural light to reinforce the schedule.
5. Limit caffeine intake to earlier in the day; avoid consumption after early afternoon.
6. If a nap isn’t feasible, use brief bright‑light exposure, a short walk, or 10 minutes outdoors as a backup strategy.
