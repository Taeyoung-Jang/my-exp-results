# Circadian Rhythm

**Definition**: The circadian rhythm is the ~24‑hour internal biological clock that synchronises physiological processes, hormone release, sleep‑wake timing, and behaviour to the day‑night cycle.

---

## Core Components
- **Central pacemaker**: The suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the hypothalamus receives light signals from the eyes and coordinates peripheral clocks throughout the body.
- **Zeitgebers** (time‑givers): External cues such as **morning light**, meals, activity, and social schedules that entrain the rhythm.
- **Sleep‑wake regulation**: Aligns sleep propensity with night time and alertness with day time.

---

## Relevance to Session‑33 (2024‑01‑19)
In the conversation, the assistant highlighted the circadian rhythm as the reason why **wake‑time consistency** and **morning light** are the most effective first steps for improving sleep quality. By anchoring the wake time and exposing the eyes to bright light soon after rising, the SCN receives a strong signal, stabilising the internal clock and making later habits (caffeine timing, nap scheduling) easier to implement.

---

## Practical Applications
- **Wake‑time consistency**: Aim to rise at the same clock‑time daily (see [[concepts/consistent-sleep-schedule]]). A narrow 15–20 minute window reduces internal drift.
- **Morning light exposure**: Bright light within the first hour after waking reinforces the SCN signal and advances the rhythm (see [[concepts/morning_light]]).
- **Caffeine timing**: Consuming caffeine later in the day can shift the rhythm or delay sleep onset; aligning caffeine intake with earlier hours supports a stable rhythm (see [[concepts/caffeine_timing]]).
- **Nap timing**: Short naps early in the afternoon avoid interfering with the evening buildup of sleep pressure, preserving the rhythm (see [[concepts/screen-curfew]] for related advice on limiting evening light exposure).
