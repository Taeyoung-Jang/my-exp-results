# Screen Curfew

**Definition**: A *screen curfew* is the practice of stopping the use of electronic screens (smartphones, tablets, computers, TVs) at least one hour before intended sleep time to reduce blue‑light exposure and mental stimulation, thereby supporting the natural onset of sleep.

---

## Why It Matters

The conversation in [[summaries/session-15_2023-01-18]] highlights screen curfew as one of the two most impactful habits for improving sleep quality. Blue‑light emitted by screens suppresses melatonin production, delays circadian rhythms, and keeps the brain in a heightened state of alertness. Cutting off screens early helps the body transition toward rest.

---

## Practical Recommendations

1. **Set a firm cutoff time** – Aim to stop using all bright screens at least **60 minutes** before your planned bedtime.
2. **Replace screen time with calming activities** – Read a physical book, stretch, meditate, or dim the lights to signal the evening wind‑down.
3. **Use technical aids if needed** – Enable night‑mode filters or blue‑light‑blocking glasses if you must use a device briefly.
4. **Maintain consistency** – Apply the curfew every night, including weekends, to reinforce the habit.

---

## Interaction with Other Sleep Strategies

- **Consistent Sleep Schedule**: A regular bedtime supports the curfew’s effectiveness; aligning both habits stabilizes the circadian rhythm. See [[concepts/consistent-sleep-schedule]].
- **Early‑Afternoon Nap**: When a late night occurs, a brief nap (20‑30 min) can mitigate sleep debt without compromising the night’s sleep, provided the screen curfew is still observed before the next bedtime. See [[concepts/early-afternoon-nap]].

---

## Benefits Summarized

- Faster sleep onset
- Improved sleep depth and continuity
- Reduced nighttime awakenings
- Alignment of circadian rhythm with natural darkness
