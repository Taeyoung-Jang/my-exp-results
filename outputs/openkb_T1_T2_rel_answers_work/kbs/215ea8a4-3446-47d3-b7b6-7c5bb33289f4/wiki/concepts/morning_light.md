# Morning Light Exposure

**Definition**: Exposure to bright natural light in the early morning that helps synchronize the internal clock, supporting alertness and a stable sleep‑wake schedule.

**Key Points from [[summaries/session-33_2024-01-19]]**
- Acts as a keystone for establishing a [[concepts/consistent-sleep-schedule]].
- Reinforces the [[concepts/circadian_rhythm]] by providing a strong zeitgeber (time cue) shortly after waking.
- Recommended after solidifying wake‑time consistency; increasing morning light can further sharpen alertness.
- Works synergistically with other habits such as limiting late‑day caffeine ([[concepts/caffeine_timing]]) and maintaining a regular bedtime.

**Practical Recommendations**
1. **Timing**: Aim for light exposure within the first hour after waking.
2. **Duration**: 20‑30 minutes of outdoor light or a bright‑light box (~10,000 lux) is effective.
3. **Consistency**: Pair with a regular wake‑time (within a 15‑20 minute window) to maximize benefits.
4. **Complementary Strategies**: Combine with a modest [[concepts/screen-curfew]] in the evening to avoid competing light cues.

**Why It Matters**
Morning light provides the strongest cue for resetting the biological clock, making it easier to keep a consistent sleep schedule and improve overall sleep quality.
