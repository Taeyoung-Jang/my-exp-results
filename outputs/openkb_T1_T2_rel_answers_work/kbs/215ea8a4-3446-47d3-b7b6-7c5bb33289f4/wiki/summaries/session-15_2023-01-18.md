# Summary of session-15_2023-01-18

**Main Points**
- Two core habits for better sleep: a **consistent sleep schedule** and a **screen curfew** at least an hour before bed. [[concepts/consistent-sleep-schedule]] [[concepts/screen-curfew]]
- Weekend wake‑up times can deviate by ~1 hour without major disruption; larger shifts (2‑3 h) act like jet‑lag.  
- After a late night, keep wake‑time close to the usual schedule (within ~1 hour). If needed, a short **early‑afternoon nap** (20‑30 min) helps without delaying bedtime. [[concepts/early-afternoon-nap]]
- Nap timing: before mid‑afternoon and limited to the 20‑30 min “sweet spot” to avoid deep‑sleep inertia and bedtime push‑back.
- **Sleep‑debt** management: treat a rough night as damage‑control, not an opportunity to oversleep; small adjustments preserve rhythm.
- For **chronic insomnia**, consistency outweighs attempting to “catch up.”
  - Keep morning wake‑time within ~1 hour of the norm.
  - Prioritize protecting the bedtime rhythm; skip naps if they interfere with falling asleep at night.
  - Occasionally a brief, early nap is acceptable if it does not shift bedtime.

**Practical Recommendations**
1. Set a regular bedtime and wake‑time; aim for ≤1 h variance on weekends.
2. Turn off screens ≥1 h before sleep; use dim lighting and calming activities.
3. If you stay up late, still rise near your usual time; consider a 20‑30 min nap before 3 p.m.
4. With insomnia, limit or avoid daytime sleep to protect nighttime sleep onset.

These guidelines emphasize **rhythm over total sleep quantity**, helping both typical sleepers and those with chronic insomnia maintain a stable circadian pattern.
