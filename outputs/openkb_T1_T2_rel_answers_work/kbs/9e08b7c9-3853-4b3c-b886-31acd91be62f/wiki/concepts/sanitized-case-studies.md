# Sanitized Case Studies

**Definition**: A sanitized case study is a public‑facing write‑up that describes an engineering problem, the approach taken, and the measurable outcome while deliberately removing any confidential code, architecture diagrams, logs, or product‑specific identifiers.

---

## Why they matter
- Recruiters need *observable* evidence of impact, but many engineers work on private repositories.
- Providing a concise, high‑level narrative lets hiring teams verify competence without risking IP leakage.
- They serve as a bridge between private work and the *observable‑artifacts* that recruiters look for.

---

## Core elements of a sanitized case study
1. **Context** – Briefly describe the domain (e.g., a high‑traffic API, an internal automation tool) without naming the product or internal services.
2. **Challenge** – State the specific engineering problem (latency, reliability, manual‑process overhead) in generic terms.
3. **Approach** – Summarize the technical actions (profiling, caching, refactoring, building a service layer) without exposing code snippets or internal architecture details.
4. **Outcome** – Highlight measurable impact using placeholders or aggregate numbers (e.g., "improved response times by X%", "reduced manual effort by Y hours per week").
5. **Proof of work** – Link to public artifacts such as a small open‑source tool, a sanitized diagram, or a hosted write‑up, noting that full details are available on request.

---

## How to present them on résumé / LinkedIn
- Add a line under a "Projects" or "Selected work" section: *“Sanitized case studies and public technical notes available on request”*.
- Provide a short URL to a personal site or portfolio that hosts the redacted documents.
- Use language that frames the material as a *standard industry practice* for engineers with private codebases.
