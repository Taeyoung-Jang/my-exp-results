# Observable Artifacts

**Definition**: Observable artifacts are tangible, shareable pieces of an engineer’s work—such as pull‑request links, code snippets, diagrams, short write‑ups, or before‑and‑after system descriptions—that can be examined by recruiters to verify impact without revealing proprietary details.

## Why they matter
In the context of private‑repo environments, recruiters need evidence they can mentally verify. Observable artifacts provide that evidence by offering *observable* proof rather than vague claims. They help bridge the gap between confidential work and the need for demonstrable competence.

## Types of observable artifacts
- **PR or commit links** (when public) that showcase problem‑solving approach.
- **Sanitized case studies** that describe the challenge, approach, and measurable outcome while omitting sensitive code or architecture details. See [[concepts/sanitized-case-studies]].
- **Short technical write‑ups or blog posts** that explain a pattern or solution.
- **Diagrams or before‑and‑after screenshots** with redacted identifiers, illustrating system improvements.
- **Public side‑project samples** (e.g., small tools, scripts) that reflect coding style.

## Connection to measurable impact
Observable artifacts often include quantitative results—e.g., latency reduced by X % or manual work cut by Y hours—linking directly to [[concepts/measurable-impact]]. This combination of concrete evidence and metrics makes the artifact compelling to recruiters.

## How to present them
- List them under a “Projects” or “Technical Portfolio” section on a résumé or LinkedIn profile.
- Use phrasing such as “Selected technical write‑ups and public code samples available at <link>.”
- Ensure any screenshots are clearly redacted to avoid disclosing confidential information.
