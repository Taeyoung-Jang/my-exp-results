# Front‑Desk Onboarding Strategies

**Overview** – A focused set of practices that help new front‑desk employees quickly reach confidence while minimizing stress during their first weeks on the job. The approach draws on insights from [[summaries/session-33_2024-11-09]] and aligns with broader hospitality concepts such as [[concepts/front-desk-skills]] and [[concepts/emotional-resilience-hospitality]].

## Core Elements

1. **Shadow Experienced Staff**
   - Observe check‑ins, guest handling, and peak‑time rhythms.
   - Note how senior team members prioritize in‑person guests versus phone calls.

2. **Master the Property‑Management System**
   - Dedicate short practice sessions after each shift to navigate the [[entities/property-management-system]].
   - Confidence with the PMS reduces bottlenecks when the desk gets busy.

3. **Map Back‑of‑House Contacts**
   - Identify the go‑to housekeeper, maintenance technician, and any other support roles.
   - Keeping a mental (or written) list speeds problem resolution.

4. **Ask Micro‑Questions Early**
   - Raise small clarifications as soon as they arise rather than letting confusion accumulate.
   - This habit builds a culture of curiosity and prevents larger errors.

## Supporting Concepts

- The tactics are a concrete expression of [[concepts/onboarding-strategies-front-desk]].
- They reinforce the development of [[concepts/front-desk-skills]] such as calm under pressure, clear communication, and anticipation.
- Building emotional resilience early on connects to [[concepts/emotional-resilience-hospitality]], helping staff handle guest stress without burnout.

## Quick Checklist for the First Two Weeks

- [ ] Shadow at least two seasoned front‑desk agents during a full shift.
- [ ] Log 10‑minute PMS drills after each day.
- [ ] Write down key internal contacts and their typical response times.
- [ ] Keep a notebook of questions that arise; review with a supervisor daily.

By following these strategies, new front‑desk team members can shorten the learning curve, stay calm during rushes, and lay a solid foundation for long‑term success in hospitality.
