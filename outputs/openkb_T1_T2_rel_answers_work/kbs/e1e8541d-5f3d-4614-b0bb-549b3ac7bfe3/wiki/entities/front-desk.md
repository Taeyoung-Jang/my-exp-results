# Front Desk

The **Front Desk** is the primary guest-facing hub in a hotel where staff manage check‑ins, check‑outs, reservations, inquiries, and a range of operational tasks. It acts as the communication nexus between guests and back‑of‑house services such as housekeeping, maintenance, and the property‑management system.

## Core Functions
- **Guest interaction** – Greeting arrivals, handling departures, answering questions, and resolving issues in person and via phone.
- **Reservation management** – Using the property‑management system to create, modify, and cancel bookings.
- **Coordination** – Relaying requests to housekeeping, maintenance, and other departments.
- **Financial transactions** – Processing payments, refunds, incidentals, and authorizing cards.
- **Security & privacy** – Verifying guest identity and safeguarding personal information.

## Insights from [[summaries/session-33_2024-11-09]]
- **Pacing & surprise** – New staff often underestimate the rapid pace and emotional load of front‑desk work, which includes sudden rushes and guests’ travel‑related stress.
- **Key skills** – Success relies on calm under pressure, clear and kind communication, anticipation, reading guests, and humor. (See [[concepts/front-desk-skills]])
- **Onboarding tips** – Early weeks should focus on shadowing experienced staff, mastering the property‑management system, learning back‑of‑house contacts, and asking micro‑questions. (See [[concepts/onboarding-strategies-front-desk]])
- **Multitasking approach** – Prioritize face‑to‑face guests, use brief hold scripts for callers, and provide quick acknowledgment phrases to buy time.
- **Common mistakes** – Errors often stem from acting too quickly: incorrect refunds, missed room‑move updates, improper incidentals handling, and privacy breaches. A two‑second pause and verbal confirmation of details are recommended habits. (See common front desk mistakes)
