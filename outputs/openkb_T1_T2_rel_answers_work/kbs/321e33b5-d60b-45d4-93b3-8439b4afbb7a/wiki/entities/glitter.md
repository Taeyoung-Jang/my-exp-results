## Glitter

Glitter is a fine, reflective particle used in crafts and decorative projects. In the context of a **school fundraiser** (see [[entities/school-fundraiser]]) it often creates a persistent mess that can adhere to clothing, skin, and surfaces.

### Key facts from [[summaries/session-31_2025-06-15]]
- **Messy potential**: When used without proper containment, glitter spreads to volunteers' sleeves and personal items, turning a clean workspace into a glitter‑filled zone.
- **Containment strategy**: The document proposes a dedicated **messy quarantine area** at the start of the [[concepts/assembly_line_workflow]], where any glitter‑prone tasks (unpacking, sealing decorative pieces) are performed before items enter the clean zone.
- **Supplies for control**:
  - Large trays and shallow boxes to limit stray particles.
  - **Baby wipes** (see [[entities/baby-wipes]]) placed at each station for quick clean‑ups.
  - Painter’s tape to mark flow lanes, preventing cross‑traffic.
- **Roles**: A **floater** (see [[concepts/floater_role]]) monitors the line, restocks supplies, and promptly clears any stray glitter to keep the line moving.
- **Cleanup**: After the fundraiser, a 10‑minute [[concepts/cleanup_plan]] is executed, with the floater collecting loose glitter, volunteers wiping stations, and the quarantine area sealed to trap any remaining particles.

### Practical implications
- Use pre‑sealed decorative elements to eliminate loose glitter before they reach volunteers.
- Provide ample trash bins and wipes at each station.
- Train volunteers on the importance of staying within their assigned zones to prevent cross‑contamination.

By following the workflow and cleanup guidelines outlined in the session summary, organizers can minimize glitter fallout and keep participants clean and focused on the fundraising goals.
