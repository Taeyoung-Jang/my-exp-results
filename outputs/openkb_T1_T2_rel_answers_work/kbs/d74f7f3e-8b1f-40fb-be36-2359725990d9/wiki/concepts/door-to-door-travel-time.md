# Door‑to‑Door Travel Time

Door‑to‑Door Travel Time measures the complete elapsed time a traveler experiences from the moment they leave their starting point until they reach their final destination, accounting for all ancillary activities such as getting to and from terminals, security procedures, boarding, and any post‑arrival logistics.

## Why it matters
Accurately estimating door‑to‑door time helps travelers choose the most efficient mode of transport for a given trip, especially when the scheduled travel time alone can be misleading. It is a core element of [[concepts/travel-mode-comparison]], enabling quick, consistent comparisons across air, rail, and road options.

## Typical time‑add‑ons per mode (from [[summaries/session-06_2022-07-02]])
- **Air travel** – add roughly three hours total:
  1. ~1 hour to reach the airport and check‑in.
  2. ~1 hour for early arrival, security screening, and boarding.
  3. ~1 hour for taxi‑out, deplaning, baggage claim, and travel from the arrival airport.
- **Train travel** – add about 15‑30 minutes at the start and 15‑30 minutes at the end (≈ 30‑60 minutes total).
- **Driving** – add a small buffer for traffic, typically 15‑30 minutes for short trips.

These additive components are examples of [[concepts/time-add-ons]] that transform a scheduled travel duration into a realistic door‑to‑door estimate.

## Using the concept
1. Take the scheduled travel time for each option.
2. Apply the appropriate time‑add‑ons for the mode.
3. Compare the resulting totals against personal constraints (e.g., a five‑hour cutoff) to decide the preferred mode.

By standardising these calculations, travelers can make informed decisions without needing detailed itinerary analysis each time.
