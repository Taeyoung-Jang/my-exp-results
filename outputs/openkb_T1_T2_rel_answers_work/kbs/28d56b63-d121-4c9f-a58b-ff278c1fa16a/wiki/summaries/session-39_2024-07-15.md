## Overview
This conversation discusses why air can feel *crisp* or *heavy* and provides practical, low‑tech ways to sense and estimate humidity, temperature, pressure, and sea‑salt aerosols during walks, especially comparing coastal and inland environments.

## Factors influencing perceived air heaviness
- **Humidity**: High moisture slows sweat evaporation, making air feel dense. [[concepts/humidity-perception]]
- **Temperature**: Warm air holds more moisture; cooler air feels sharper.
- **Air pressure**: Low pressure can create a “stuffed” sensation.
- **Aerosols**: Sea‑salt particles add a subtle texture to coastal air. [[concepts/coastal-aerosols]]

## Coastal vs. inland humidity
- **Coastal air**: Constant sea‑breeze brings moisture and fine sea‑salt spray, giving a slightly sharper, fresher edge. [[entities/ocean]] [[entities/sea-salt]]
- **Inland humid air**: Often contains plant, soil, or urban particles, producing a softer, muggy feel after storms.
- Both feel heavy, but the *composition* of aerosols changes the sensory experience.

## Simple sensory cues (no instruments)
- **Skin**: Stickiness or a faint dampness indicates high humidity.
- **Hair**: Frizz or waviness signals moisture levels.
- **Breath**: Cool, “biting” air suggests dryness; warm, heavy breath suggests humidity.
- **Scent travel**: Stronger, farther‑reaching ocean smells point to moist, salty air; earthy or leafy scents indicate inland humidity.

## Low‑tech measurement methods
- **Thermometer‑hygrometer**: Pocket‑size devices give temperature and relative humidity readings; correlate numbers with personal sensations. [[entities/thermometer-hygrometer]]
- **DIY salt aerosol check**: Wipe a clean glass or phone screen before a walk; a faint tacky film afterward hints at sea‑salt deposition. Observe metal objects (keys, bike chain) for early dullness or rust as an indirect salt indicator. [[concepts/low-tech-environment-monitoring]]

## Practical at‑home comparison plan
1. Walk daily for a week, noting sensory cues (skin, hair, breath, scents).  
2. Record temperature and humidity from a cheap hygrometer.  
3. Perform the glass‑screen or metal‑object check to gauge salt presence on coastal days.  
4. Compare coastal vs. inland days to see how composition and humidity level affect perceived air density.  

These steps let you build an intuitive sense of air conditions without relying on weather apps, while also gathering simple quantitative data for personal comparison.
