## Overview
- Explains how **altitude**, **humidity**, **temperature**, and **air quality** alter breathing, cooking, and exercise performance.  
- Offers practical adaptation methods for high‑altitude, dry, humid, and polluted environments.  
- Links to concepts: altitude adaptation, humidity, temperature, air quality, hydration, electrolyte balance.

## Acclimatization Timelines
- **Descent to sea level**: immediate boost in aerobic capacity; negligible adjustment period.  
- **Return to altitude**: 2–7 days for noticeable adaptation; full physiological shift may take several weeks.  
- **Warning signs** of over‑exertion: headaches, unusual fatigue, dizziness, elevated resting heart rate, or being winded during low‑intensity activities.  
- Related concept: acclimatization.

## Training Recommendations (first 3–5 days back at ~8,000 ft)
- Reduce **mileage/intensity** by ~⅓.  
- Prioritize easy, conversational runs; avoid intervals.  
- Optional **strides** after day 2; resume true intervals after day 5.  
- Keep heart rate in the **easy zone** or an **RPE** of ~3–4 ("I could chat").  
- Concept links: training periodization, altitude training, physiological adaptation.

## Hydration & Electrolyte Plan
- Dry air increases fluid loss via respiration.  
- Aim for steady water intake: roughly one standard bottle (≈0.5 L) every 1–2 hours while awake.  
- Add a modest electrolyte supplement **once or twice daily**; adjust based on thirst, urine color, and headache presence.  
- Monitor: light‑colored urine and absence of headache indicate adequate hydration.  
- Related concepts: hydration, electrolyte balance.

## Sample 3–5‑Day Return‑to‑Altitude Running Plan (8,000 ft)
| Day | Run Type | Mileage change | Effort | Notes |
|-----|----------|----------------|--------|-------|
| 1‑2 | Easy runs | –30 % to –40 % of normal | HR in easy zone, RPE 3‑4 | No intervals |
| 3‑4 | Easy runs + short strides | –30 % volume, strides ≤15 s | Same HR cap, add 4‑5 strides per run | Observe recovery |
| 5   | Resume normal volume if symptom‑free | Return to baseline | HR still in easy zone for warm‑up, then normal | Re‑introduce moderate intervals |

---
*Key takeaways*: gradual reduction of load, vigilant monitoring of physiological cues, and proactive hydration/electrolyte intake smooth the transition between sea‑level and high‑altitude environments.
