# Ocean

The **Ocean** is the vast body of saline water that borders continents and shapes the climate of adjacent land areas. In the context of [[summaries/session-39_2024-07-15]], the ocean is highlighted as a primary source of **humidity** and **coastal aerosols**, which affect how air feels during walks near the shoreline.

## Key Points from the Session
- **Humidity Source**: Sea breezes constantly lift moisture from the ocean surface, increasing relative humidity and making the air feel denser and “thicker.” This ties directly to [[concepts/humidity-perception]].
- **Coastal Aerosols**: Fine particles of sea‑salt emitted from breaking waves create a subtle texture in the air, referenced as [[concepts/coastal-aerosols]].
- **Sensory Effects**: The combination of high humidity and salt‑laden aerosols leads to stickier skin, frizzier hair, and a sharper scent carry‑over, all of which are described in the conversation.
- **Low‑Tech Monitoring**: The session suggests using a pocket [[entities/thermometer-hygrometer]] and simple DIY checks (e.g., inspecting a glass surface) to gauge the ocean’s influence on local air quality, linking to [[concepts/low-tech-environment-monitoring]].

## Related Entities
- [[entities/sea-salt]] – the mineral component carried in the coastal air.
- [[entities/coastline]] – the land‑water interface where the ocean’s effects are most noticeable.
