# Coastline

A coastline is the geographic boundary where land meets the ocean. In the context of [[summaries/session-39_2024-07-15]], the coastline influences the perceived heaviness of air through several mechanisms:

- **Humidity perception**: Proximity to the ocean raises ambient humidity, making the air feel denser and affecting sweat evaporation. See [[concepts/humidity-perception]].
- **Coastal aerosols**: Sea‑salt particles and fine spray are carried inland by sea breezes, adding a subtle texture to the air. See [[concepts/coastal-aerosols]].
- **Low‑tech monitoring**: Walkers can use a pocket [[entities/thermometer-hygrometer]] to measure temperature and relative humidity, correlating sensor readings with the sensory cues experienced along the coast. See [[concepts/low-tech-environment-monitoring]].

Other related entities include the [[entities/ocean]], the primary source of moisture and salt, and [[entities/sea-salt]], which contributes to the unique feel of coastal air.

The document suggests simple observational methods—skin dampness, hair frizz, and scent travel—to gauge how the coastline’s maritime influence alters air quality without relying on digital weather apps.
