# Summary of Session 12 (2019‑04‑14)

- **Goal:** Design an “ideal” education path for undecided undergraduates that balances curiosity, practical skills, and job stability.  
- **Core Philosophy:** Build a **flexible curriculum** that develops transferable thinking habits while allowing early real‑world testing. [[concepts/flexible-curriculum]]

## Common Core (non‑negotiable)
- Math (Calculus or Statistics)  
- Intro Programming  
- Statistics  
- Writing/Communication  
- One Lab Science  
- Applied/Project‑based course by the end of Year 2  

These courses provide **core skills** for both policy/econ and data/tech pathways. [[concepts/core-skills]]

## Two Specialization Tracks
- **Policy/Econ‑Leaning Path** – Emphasizes micro/macro economics, econometrics, quantitative analysis, and public‑policy electives.  
- **Data/Tech‑Leaning Path** – Emphasizes data structures, algorithms, AI/ML, and applied software development.  

Both tracks interleave the common core with track‑specific courses in a semester‑by‑semester table.

## Decision Checkpoints (End of Year 2)
1. Completion of an intermediate course that remains intellectually engaging.  
2. Ability to name 2‑3 realistic job paths linked to the potential major.  
3. Completion of at least one real‑world experience (internship, research, or project).  

## High‑Leverage Minors/Certificates
- Data Analytics – strengthens quantitative literacy for both tracks. [[entities/data-analytics]]  
- Computer Science Fundamentals – provides technical depth useful in policy, economics, and tech roles. [[entities/computer-science-fundamentals]]  
- Public Policy or Public Interest Technology – adds institutional context while remaining compatible with data‑heavy work. [[entities/public-policy]]  

These minors balance practical job‑market value with flexibility, letting students shift between policy‑oriented roles and technical or analytical roles without restarting the path. [[concepts/high-leverage-minors]]

## Place‑Out Strategies
- If placed out of Intro Programming, substitute a more applied coding course (e.g., Data Structures or a Python‑focused applied programming class).  
- If placed out of Calculus, move directly into Statistics, Probability, or Econometrics to maintain progression.  

## Practical Experience Emphasis
Early summer internships, research assistantships, or self‑driven projects are recommended each year to test fit and inform major decisions.
