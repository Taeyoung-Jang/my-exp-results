# Flexible Routine

**Definition**
A *flexible routine* is a lightweight framework of recurring habits that gives the day a predictable shape without rigidly fixing every hour. It balances consistency (e.g., a morning ritual) with openness for spontaneous activities, helping individuals navigate life transitions and avoid the stress of overly strict schedules.

**Key Elements**
- **Anchor activities**: Small, repeatable actions such as a brief breakfast, a short walk, or a 10‑minute planning window that signal the start of the day.
- **Thematic blocks**: Instead of exact time‑slots, assign a theme (e.g., *deep work* in the morning, *admin* in the afternoon) to guide focus while allowing task fluidity.
- **Evening reset**: A decompression period followed by a simple, optional task and a restorative wind‑down. The reset can be scaled to low, medium, or high energy levels.
- **Visible micro‑goals**: One to two non‑negotiable tasks per day that provide a sense of progress without overwhelming the schedule.

**Practical Tips**
1. **Start small** – pick one anchor habit (e.g., a 5‑minute morning stretch) and build from there.
2. **Use themes, not time‑boxes** – label parts of the day ("focus", "admin", "creative") and let task choice flow within each theme.
3. **Adjust daily** – refer to the [[concepts/weekday_reset]] scale to match your current energy level.
4. **Review weekly** – spend Saturday morning reviewing what worked and tweaking the routine for the upcoming week.

By embedding these elements, a flexible routine provides the stability needed for transitions while preserving the freedom to adapt to changing energy and priorities.
