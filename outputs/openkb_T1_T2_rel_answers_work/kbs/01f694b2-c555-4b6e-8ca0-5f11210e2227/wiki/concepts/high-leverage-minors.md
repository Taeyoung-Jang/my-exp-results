# High‑Leverage Minors

**Definition**: Academic minors or certificate programs that provide broadly applicable, market‑valued skills, enabling students to pivot between fields such as public policy, economics, data science, and technology without needing to restart their degree.

---

## Why They Matter

High‑leverage minors act as **skill bridges** in a [[concepts/flexible-curriculum]] by:
- Reinforcing the [[concepts/core-skills]] of quantitative analysis, programming, and communication.
- Allowing students to keep doors open while they experiment with different majors.
- Enhancing employability by adding concrete, resume‑ready competencies.

---

## Recommended High‑Leverage Minors (as highlighted in [[summaries/session-12_2019-04-14]])

| Minor / Certificate | Primary Benefit | How It Connects to Both Tracks |
|---------------------|----------------|--------------------------------|
| **Data Analytics** (entity: [[entities/data-analytics]]) | Strengthens quantitative literacy, data cleaning, visualization, and basic modeling. | Directly useful for policy/econ research (e.g., labor‑market analysis) and for tech roles involving data pipelines. |
| **Computer Science Fundamentals** (entity: [[entities/computer-science-fundamentals]]) | Provides a solid grounding in programming, algorithms, and software design. | Enables technical proficiency for data‑heavy policy work and equips econ students with coding skills for computational modeling. |
| **Public Policy / Public Interest Technology** (entity: [[entities/public-policy]]) | Offers institutional knowledge, policy analysis frameworks, and exposure to real‑world governance challenges. | Complements technical expertise by teaching how to apply data‑driven solutions to societal problems. |

---

## How to Choose a Minor

1. **Assess your current skill gaps** – If you lack quantitative tools, prioritize **Data Analytics**; if you need programming depth, choose **Computer Science Fundamentals**.
2. **Consider career intent** – For roles at the intersection of technology and governance, the **Public Policy** minor adds essential context.
3. **Check program compatibility** – Ensure the minor fits within the common core schedule and does not overload any semester.

---

## Integration with a Flexible Curriculum

When mapping a four‑year plan, place the minor courses after the shared **core** (Math, Intro Programming, Statistics, Writing/Communication, Lab Science). This timing maximizes synergy:
- Early **core** courses build the foundation.
- Minor courses then expand that foundation into **high‑leverage** territory, supporting either a **policy/econ‑leaning** or a **data/tech‑leaning** track.

---

## Practical Example

A student following the **policy/econ‑leaning** path might take:
- Year 1 core courses (Math, Intro Programming, etc.)
- Year 2 minor: **Data Analytics** (statistical software, data visualization)
- Year 3–4: policy electives and a capstone that uses the analytics skills.

A student on the **data/tech‑leaning** path could reverse the order, using the **Computer Science Fundamentals** minor to deepen programming before taking advanced AI/ML classes.

---

## Outcome

Completing a high‑leverage minor equips graduates with **portable competencies** that enhance both employability and the ability to shift career directions, aligning with the flexible, skill‑first approach advocated in the source conversation.
