## Burnout Management

**Definition**: Burnout Management refers to a set of proactive and reactive practices that help individuals recognize, mitigate, and recover from chronic stress and exhaustion, especially when major life changes disrupt established routines.

### Core Principles
- **Early Detection**: Pay attention to signs such as persistent fatigue, reduced motivation, and irritability.
- **Energy‑Based Adjustments**: Scale down tasks to match current energy levels rather than pushing through fatigue.
- **Micro‑Resets**: Incorporate short, purposeful breaks that restore mental bandwidth.
- **Flexible Structure**: Maintain a light rhythm (e.g., a morning ritual and an evening reset) that can be easily adjusted.
- **Prioritization of Small Wins**: Focus on one or two non‑negotiable tasks per day to build momentum without overwhelm.

### Practices Highlighted in [[summaries/session-35_2024-04-14]]
1. **Shrink Daily Goals**
   - When feeling burned out, reduce the evening routine to the *low‑energy* option: a brief decompression, an easy meal, and a micro‑task (e.g., taking out the trash).
2. **Adjust Task Order**
   - Swap a demanding activity for something energizing at the start of the day or after the commute to reset motivation.
3. **Flexible Routine**
   - Use [[concepts/flexible_routine]] to keep the day’s structure predictable yet adaptable, allowing for quick scaling up or down.
4. **Tiered Weekday Reset**
   - Adopt the three‑level [[concepts/weekday_reset]] approach (low, medium, high energy) to match evening activities with current stamina.
5. **Environment Shifts**
   - Change physical surroundings (e.g., work in a different room or take a short walk) to break monotony and refresh focus.

### Connection to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/flexible_routine]]**: Provides the overarching framework that makes burnout‑preventive adjustments easy to implement.
- **[[concepts/weekday_reset]]**: Offers concrete, energy‑graded evening routines that act as regular recovery windows.
- **[[concepts/high-leverage-minors]]**: Identifies small, high‑impact actions (like a 5‑minute tidy) that can dramatically improve wellbeing without large time investment.

### Actionable Checklist
- **Morning**: Keep a 10‑minute personal ritual (breakfast, walk, planning).
- **During Day**: Monitor energy; if fatigue spikes, insert a brief stretch or change of scenery.
- **Evening**: Choose a reset level based on energy:
  - *Low*: Decompress, easy meal, micro‑task.
  - *Medium*: Simple dinner, tidy one area, 30‑minute hobby.
  - *High*: Full‑cooked meal, larger chore, focused personal time.
- **Weekly Review**: On a designated planning day, assess which reset level was used most and adjust upcoming goals accordingly.
