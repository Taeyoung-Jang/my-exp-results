# Core Skills

**Definition**: Core Skills are the foundational, transferable abilities that enable students to adapt across disciplines, solve problems, and communicate effectively in varied professional contexts.

---

## Why Core Skills Matter

The *session-12_2019-04-14* discussion emphasizes that undecided undergraduates need a curriculum built around a set of core competencies. These competencies:
- Provide a **flexible curriculum** foundation that can support multiple specialization tracks (e.g., policy/econ or data/tech) [[concepts/flexible-curriculum]].
- Increase employability by ensuring graduates have practical, market‑ready abilities regardless of their eventual major.
- Reduce the risk of early over‑specialization, a common pitfall highlighted in the conversation.

## Core Skills Identified in the Source Document

| Skill | Typical Course(s) | Role in the Curriculum |
|-------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Quantitative Literacy** (Math, Statistics) | Calculus, Statistics, Probability, Econometrics | Enables data‑driven decision‑making; essential for both policy/econ and data/tech tracks. |
| **Programming Fundamentals** | Intro Programming, Data Structures | Provides technical fluency needed for modern workplaces and for analytical work across fields. |
| **Written & Oral Communication** | Writing/Communication, Communication‑heavy classes | Critical for articulating ideas, producing reports, and collaborating across disciplines. |
| **Scientific/Experimental Thinking** | Lab Science, Applied/Project‑based courses | Develops hypothesis testing, data collection, and empirical analysis skills. |
| **Project‑Based Learning** | Applied/Project‑based course (by end of Year 2) | Gives hands‑on experience, bridges theory with real‑world practice, and informs major‑selection checkpoints. |

Each semester’s schedule marks the relevant courses as **Core**, ensuring consistent development of these abilities throughout the first two years.

## Takeaways

1. **Invest early** in math, statistics, programming, and communication to create a versatile skill base.
2. **Leverage project‑based courses** to apply those skills in real contexts before committing to a major.
3. **Use Core Skills as a decision filter** during the Year 2 checkpoints: if a skill area feels engaging and useful, it signals a promising direction for specialization.
