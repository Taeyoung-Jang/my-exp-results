**Core recommendation**: Choose a single [[concepts/ecosystem]] (iCloud or Google) as the [[concepts/source_of_truth]] and let its built‑in sync services handle calendars, contacts, notes, and photos across phone, laptop, and tablet.

**Google‑centric workflow for iPhone + Windows**  
- Add a [[entities/google]] account on the [[entities/iphone]], enable only **Contacts** & **Calendars**.  
- Set Google as the default contacts account to avoid iCloud duplication.  
- Use web or desktop apps on [[entities/windows]]; everything syncs via Google.  
- Turn off iCloud contacts/calendar syncing to prevent duplicate databases.

**Photo handling**  
- [[entities/google_photos]] works on the [[entities/iphone]] but does not integrate into the native Photos app, creating a separate local camera‑roll library.  
- [[entities/icloud_photos]] offers tighter integration (Live Photos, original quality) but ties you to Apple’s ecosystem.

**Migrating existing iCloud data to Google**  
1. Export contacts (vCard) from iCloud.com → import to Google Contacts.  
2. Export iCloud calendars → import to Google Calendar.  
3. Verify data on the [[entities/iphone]], then disable iCloud contacts/calendar sync.  

**When a cross‑platform system is already in place**  
- Prefer aligning iCloud data with the IT‑provided system instead of a separate Google migration to avoid duplicate sources of truth.  
- Disable iCloud syncing for contacts/calendar after migration to the new system.  

### Key concepts  
- **Source of truth** – central account that holds the master copy of data.  
- **[[concepts/ecosystem]]** – iCloud vs. Google, each offering native services for calendars, contacts, notes, and photos.  
- **[[concepts/duplicate_avoidance]]** – disabling competing sync services and setting default accounts.  
- **[[concepts/cross_platform_sync]]** – using Google (or the IT‑provided) services to bridge iPhone, Windows, and Android devices.
