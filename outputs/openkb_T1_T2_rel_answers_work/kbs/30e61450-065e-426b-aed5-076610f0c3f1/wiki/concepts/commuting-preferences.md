# Commuting Preferences

**Definition** – A structured way to capture an individual’s daily routine, mood drivers, and lifestyle constraints in order to recommend the most suitable commuting mode.

---

## Core Questionnaire
The concept originates from a 12‑question checklist (see [[summaries/session-34_2025-04-11]]) that probes five high‑level dimensions:

1. **Structure & Predictability** – How much routine and timetable certainty the person wants.
2. **Physical Activity & Outdoor Preference** – Desired level of movement during the commute.
3. **Social Context** – Comfort with being alone, surrounded by strangers, or seeking community.
4. **Tolerance for Delays & Noise** – Stress response to uncertainty, crowding, and ambient sound.
5. **Practical Priorities** – Door‑to‑door convenience, cost sensitivity, pace, and eco‑motivation.

Each question offers three answer options (A, B, C) that map onto a spectrum from **high control/solitude (A)** through **moderate flexibility (B)** to **high activity/social/eco orientation (C)**.

---

## How Answers Translate to Modes
The pattern of A/B/C selections feeds into the [[concepts/mode-recommendation-matrix]]:

* Predominantly **A** → favors **[[entities/personal-car]]** (private driving) for control and privacy.
* Mixed **B** responses → points to **[[entities/ride-share]]** or on‑demand services that keep convenience while reducing driving effort.
* Predominantly **C** → aligns with active options such as biking, walking, or **[[entities/public-transit]]** for variety and eco‑benefits.

---

## Integration with Fallback Strategies
When the primary recommendation is unavailable (e.g., car breakdown), the concept dovetails with [[concepts/fallback-commuting-strategies]] to provide a hierarchy of secondary options (backup and last‑resort plans).

---

## Practical Uses
- **Personal coaching** – Guides users through the checklist to discover their commuting personality.
- **Mobility planning** – Helps urban designers categorize resident needs for infrastructure investment.
- **Product design** – Informs developers of ride‑share or transit apps about user segmentation.
