# Summary of session‑34_2025‑04‑11

**Main contribution** – A concise, 12‑question checklist that maps personality and lifestyle factors to commuting mode recommendations, plus a personalized “default + backup + last‑resort” plan with scenario examples.

## Key concepts
- **Commuting preference questionnaire** – captures structure, activity level, solitude, tolerance for delays, convenience, noise sensitivity, predictability, cost, pace, eco‑motivation, and social desire. [[concepts/commuting-preferences]]
- **Mode recommendation matrix** – interprets answer patterns (dominant A, B, C) to suggest driving, public transit, biking/walking, or ride‑share. [[concepts/mode-recommendation-matrix]]
- **Mood & routine impact** – highlights how each mode aligns with personal mood, stress levels, and daily rhythm. *(no wikilink available)*
- **Fallback commuting strategies** – defines a hierarchy (default → backup → last‑resort) and applies it to real‑world conditions (bad weather, car unavailable, exhaustion). fallback commuting‑strategies

## Findings
1. **Answer pattern for a driver‑preferring user** – Mostly **A** choices (structured, solitary, predictable, convenient) with neutral **B** on activity and eco‑motivation.
2. **Default plan** – [[entities/personal-car]] driving, matching high control and privacy needs.
3. **Backup plan** – [[entities/ride-share]]/on‑demand services, preserving door‑to‑door convenience and personal space without the need to drive.
4. **Last‑resort plan** – [[entities/public-transit]], used only when neither personal car nor ride‑share is available.

## Example scenarios
| Scenario | Chosen plan | Reasoning |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| Bad weather | **Ride‑share** | Keeps personal bubble and avoids exposure, while retaining convenience. |
| Car in the shop | **Ride‑share** | Mirrors the default experience without schedule constraints. |
| Exhausted after work | **Ride‑share** | Provides privacy and minimal effort, avoiding the cognitive load of driving. |
