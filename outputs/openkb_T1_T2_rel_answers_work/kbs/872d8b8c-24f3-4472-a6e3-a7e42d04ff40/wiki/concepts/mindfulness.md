# Mindfulness

**Definition**: Mindfulness is the practice of deliberately paying attention to present‑moment experience with an attitude of openness and non‑judgment.

## Core Principles
- **Attention to the present** – Focusing on breath, bodily sensations, thoughts, or external sounds as they arise.
- **Non‑reactive awareness** – Noticing experiences without immediately trying to change them.
- **Gentle curiosity** – Approaching each moment with a simple, inquisitive stance.

## Practical Guidance from [[summaries/session-04_2022-06-15]]
- **Start tiny**: Anchor a single mindful breath to an existing habit (e.g., while drinking coffee) to avoid feeling like an extra task.
- **Micro‑check‑ins**: Sprinkle brief moments of awareness throughout the day to reinforce the habit.
- **Chunking vs. single block**: Beginners can split a 20‑minute practice into two 10‑minute sessions or keep it as one continuous block, depending on what feels repeatable.
- **Structured routines**:
  - **Body‑scan routine** – 5 min of settling and breath, 10 min of a systematic body‑scan from feet to face, and 5 min returning to breath.
  - **Breath‑focused routine** – Continuous breath attention, expanding to ambient sounds in the final minutes.
- **Busy‑mind adaptation** – Use breath counting (1‑10) and simple mental labeling (e.g., “thinking,” “feeling,” “hearing”) to give the mind a scaffold.
- **Morning habit** – The body‑scan version eases the transition from sleep to alertness and works well as a daily morning ritual.
- **Restless‑morning tweak** – Speed up the body‑scan, add soft sensory labels, and insert brief breath‑counting to settle heightened restlessness.
