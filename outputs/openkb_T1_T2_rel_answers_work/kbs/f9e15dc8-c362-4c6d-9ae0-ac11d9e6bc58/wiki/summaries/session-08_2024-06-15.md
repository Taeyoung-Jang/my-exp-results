# Summary of session‑08_2024‑06‑15

## Overview
A joint‑friendly workout program focused on protecting the knees while building strength. It outlines a **3‑day strength routine** emphasizing hips/glutes and upper body, **2 low‑impact cardio days**, knee‑safe warm‑up & cool‑down protocols, a **Monday‑to‑Sunday schedule**, and simple **progression rules** for 4‑6 weeks.

## Strength Plan (3 Days)
- **Day 1 – Hip & Upper Body**: hip thrusts, supported Romanian deadlifts, seated cable rows, chest press, side‑lying hip abductions. Knee‑friendly substitution: back‑supported glute bridges.
- **Day 2 – Upper Body + Posterior Chain**: lat pulldowns, dumbbell overhead press, incline rows, band pull‑aparts, reverse hypers/back extensions. Substitutions: seated machines for any knee‑loading setup.
- **Day 3 – Hips & Glutes**: single‑leg glute bridges, cable kickbacks, lying hamstring curls, chest‑supported rows, biceps/triceps work. Substitutions: stability‑ball curls if hamstring curls irritate the knee.

## Warm‑up & Cool‑down (5–10 min each)
- Warm‑ups use gentle hip mobility, core activation, band work, and shoulder breathing drills—no knee loading.
- Cool‑downs include stretch sequences for hips, chest, back, hamstrings, and glutes, performed slowly and pain‑free.

## Low‑Impact Cardio (2 Days)
- Primary options: stationary bike (light resistance), swimming, deep‑water running.
- Alternatives: elliptical (if knee tolerates) – **rowing omitted** per request.

## Weekly Schedule (Mon–Sun)
| Day | Activity |
|-----|----------|
| **Mon** | Day 1 Strength |
| **Tue** | Cardio (bike / swim) |
| **Wed** | Day 2 Strength |
| **Thu** | Rest or mobility |
| **Fri** | Day 3 Strength |
| **Sat** | Cardio (bike / swim / elliptical) |
| **Sun** | Full rest or gentle stretching |

## Progression Rules (4‑6 weeks)
1. **Initial Phase (Weeks 1‑2)** – Light weights, aim for upper rep range only if movement feels smooth.
2. **Weight Increment** – When an exercise feels easy for two consecutive sessions and the knee is stable, add a small load (≈1–5 lb or next plate).
3. **Repetition Increment** – If weight increase feels too aggressive, add 1‑2 reps per set instead.
4. **Micro‑Progression** – Keep changes modest; avoid sudden jumps.
5. **Regression** – If knee pain appears, revert to previous comfortable weight, reduce range of motion, or use a substitution.
