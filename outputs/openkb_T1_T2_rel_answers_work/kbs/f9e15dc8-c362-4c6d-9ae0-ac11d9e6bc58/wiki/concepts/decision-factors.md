# Decision Factors for Choosing Movement Activities

**Definition** – The set of personal, physical, and contextual criteria that guide a person in picking a movement‑based hobby or exercise modality.

## Core Factors (as identified in [[summaries/session-36_2025-03-16]])

1. **Desired emotional effect** – Do you want to feel energized, relaxed, or expressive? This initial feeling cue points toward dance, yoga, or a casual fitness class respectively. [[concepts/decision-factors]]
2. **Physical goals** – Strength, flexibility, stamina, or stress‑relief. Clarifying which attribute you aim to develop narrows the options.
3. **Schedule availability** – Number of days per week, length of each session, travel time, and ability to fit activity without feeling rushed. Links to [[concepts/midday-habit-planning]] for structuring short‑duration midday sessions.
4. **Recovery profile** – How your body feels after a session (e.g., “happily tired” vs. sore) and how long it takes to bounce back. Connects to [[concepts/recovery-assessment]].
5. **Social and community preference** – Importance of group energy, class atmosphere, or solo practice.
6. **Variety vs. focus** – Whether you prefer one main activity with supportive extras or rotating activities for freshness. See [[concepts/balance-vs-variety]].
7. **Mental refreshment** – Does a single activity provide sufficient mental reset, or do you need a contrasting modality for balance?
8. **Perceived scheduling pressure** – Anticipated stress of adding another activity to an existing routine.

## Applying the Factors
- Start by answering the **emotional effect** question to surface the most natural fit.
- Map your **physical goals** onto the typical benefits of each activity (e.g., dance → coordination & cardio; yoga → flexibility & calm; fitness class → strength & stamina).
- Use the **schedule** factor to determine feasible days and session length, ensuring no back‑to‑back high‑intensity slots.
- Conduct a quick **recovery check** after a trial week to see if soreness or fatigue interferes with subsequent activities.
- Evaluate **social preference** and **variety vs. focus** to decide whether you’ll keep the activity as a primary focus or rotate it weekly.

## Outcomes
When these decision factors are systematically considered, you can design a balanced routine—such as the three‑day weekly plan (yoga, dance, strength) suggested in the source conversation—that maximizes enjoyment, progress, and sustainability.
