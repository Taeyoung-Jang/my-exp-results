# Friction Removal

## Definition
Friction removal refers to the practice of spotting and eliminating tiny obstacles or inconveniences in someone else’s day, doing so in a way that feels natural and unobtrusive.

## Why It Matters
- It creates a sense of smooth, **low‑key kindness** without drawing attention to the giver. [[concepts/low-key-kindness]]
- When repeated, these micro‑acts become a **quiet habit**, integrating generosity into everyday routines. [[concepts/quiet-habit]]
- By reducing minor friction, the gesture often sparks a subtle “pay‑it‑forward” or **chain reaction**, encouraging the recipient to do the same for others.

## Typical Examples (from [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-26]])
- Holding a door a beat longer for the person behind you.
- Covering a coffee, parking meter, or the first round of fries and asking the recipient not to repay.
- Picking up an extra snack and leaving it on a coworker’s desk without note.
- Offering to grab an errand you’re already doing for yourself.
- Providing a quick “I’ve got you” when someone is juggling bags.

## Practical Tips for Applying Friction Removal
1. **Stay observant** – Look for moments where a tiny inconvenience appears (e.g., a missing cup, a short line, a heavy bag).
2. **Keep it low‑effort** – The action should require little extra time or resources on your part.
3. **Be silent about it** – Avoid drawing attention; let the benefit speak for itself.
4. **Encourage forward‑paying subtly** – If asked about repayment, use gentle language that respects the other’s comfort while still promoting the idea of passing the kindness onward.
