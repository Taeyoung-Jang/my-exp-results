# Relationship‑Based Budgeting for Gifts

**Concept definition**: A budgeting approach that tailors the amount and type of a gift to the closeness of the giver‑recipient relationship and the giver’s financial comfort, rather than adhering to a fixed monetary rule.

## Core Principles
1. **Assess relationship depth** – Identify whether the recipient is a close family member, a good friend, an acquaintance, or a more distant contact.
2. **Set a comfort ceiling** – Determine the maximum amount you can comfortably spend without strain.
3. **Align gift value with relationship tier** – Choose a gift that feels generous for the relationship tier while staying within your comfort ceiling.
4. **Prioritize thoughtfulness** – The perceived value often stems from personal relevance or effort, not just price.

## Guidance from [[summaries/session-01_2023-12-26]]
- For a **typical mid‑range wedding**, a single guest generally spends "low‑to‑mid hundreds" (e.g., $100–$200) and a couple gives slightly more, but not a strict double.
- The conversation emphasizes that there is *no universal rule*; instead, the amount should reflect how close you are to the couple (or birthday person) and what you can afford.
- Travel costs can justify scaling the gift down, as the act of attending already represents a contribution.

## Application Scenarios
### Weddings
- **Acquaintance or distant guest**: modest, thoughtful item or a cash gift that fits your budget.
- **Close friend/family**: larger or more personalized gift, still respecting your financial limits.

### Birthdays (generalized)
- Follow the same relationship tiering: closer friends may receive higher‑value or more personal gifts, while casual acquaintances receive modest, considerate presents.

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- This budgeting method is part of broader **[[concepts/gift-giving-etiquette]]**, which examines social expectations around gifting.
- When travel influences cost, the approach dovetails with **[[concepts/travel-adjustments-for-gifts]]**, allowing the giver to adjust expectations based on incurred expenses.

## Practical Tips
- Write down the relationship tier before shopping to keep budgeting focused.
- Remember that *thoughtfulness often outweighs price*; a handwritten note or a DIY element can increase perceived value.
- Avoid the "matching" mindset; focus on *closeness + affordability* rather than tracking reciprocal amounts.
