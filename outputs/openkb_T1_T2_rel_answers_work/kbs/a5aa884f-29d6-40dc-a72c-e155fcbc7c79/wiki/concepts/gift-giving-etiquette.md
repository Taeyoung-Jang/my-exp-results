# Gift Giving Etiquette

Gift‑giving etiquette captures the informal rules that help people decide **what** to give, **how much**, and **why**. It balances personal sentiment with social expectations and varies by the nature of the event and the closeness of the relationship.

## Core Principles

1. **No universal price rule** – There is no fixed amount that applies to all occasions. Instead, focus on the *relationship* and cultural norms.
2. **Relationship‑based budgeting** – Use the depth of your connection with the recipient to set a comfortable spending range. See [[concepts/relationship-based-budgeting]] for a systematic approach.
3. **Event context matters** – Different events (e.g., a birthday vs. a wedding) carry distinct expectations.
   - *Birthdays*: Often more personalized, with flexibility in monetary value.
   - *Weddings*: Guests consider the overall formality and may reference typical ranges, but the “cover your plate” myth is discouraged.
4. **Travel adjustments** – If attending a distant event, it is acceptable to scale the gift down, recognizing that travel costs already represent a contribution. See [[concepts/travel-adjustments-for-gifts]].
5. **Avoid the matching ledger** – Tracking past gift values creates unnecessary pressure; prioritize thoughtfulness and affordability over exact reciprocity.

## Practical Guidance for Weddings

- **Typical budget range**: For a single guest, low‑to‑mid hundreds (e.g., $100–$200); for a couple, a modest step up, not a strict double.
- **Travel considerations**: Reduce the gift amount proportionally if you incur significant travel expenses.
- **Thoughtful over transactional**: A modest, well‑chosen gift or cash contribution that aligns with your budget is socially acceptable.

## Practical Guidance for Birthdays

- Tailor the gift to the recipient’s interests and your closeness.
- Monetary gifts are fine, but personalized items often carry more perceived value.

## Social Expectations

People generally appreciate gifts that convey goodwill rather than precise monetary equivalence. Most hosts/families are more concerned that guests attend and partake in the celebration than that each gift matches a specific dollar amount.
