## Summary of session‑01 (2023‑12‑26)

This conversation explores **gift‑giving etiquette** for birthdays and weddings, focusing on how to decide an *appropriate* spending level and the interplay between personal sentiment and social expectations.

### Key Points
- **No universal price rule** – Gift amounts are influenced by relationship closeness, cultural norms, and the event’s formality rather than a fixed dollar figure.
- The popular “cover your plate” idea is not a reliable guideline; most couples are not tracking whether gifts match the cost of the meal.
- **Relationship‑based budgeting**:
  - *Close friends/family*: higher, more personal gifts.
  - *Acquaintances or distant guests*: modest yet thoughtful items or cash gifts that fit the giver’s budget. See [[concepts/relationship-based-budgeting]].
- **Typical mid‑range wedding ranges**:
  - Single guest: low‑to‑mid hundreds (e.g., $100–$200).
  - Couple: a modest step up, not a strict double.
- **Travel considerations** – If you incur travel expenses, it is socially acceptable to scale the gift down, as the effort to attend is already a contribution. See [[concepts/travel-adjustments-for-gifts]].
- **Matching mindset** – Tracking past gift values creates unnecessary pressure; gifts should be based on *closeness + affordability* rather than a ledger of reciprocity.

### Practical Takeaways
1. Assess your **relationship level** with the couple and choose a gift that feels genuine and affordable.
2. Adjust for **travel costs** by reducing the monetary component if needed.
3. Focus on the **thoughtfulness** of the gift rather than striving to match past exchanges.
