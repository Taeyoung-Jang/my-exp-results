# Evening Routine Phases

The **Evening Routine Phases** concept describes a simple, repeatable structure for winding down the day. It divides the post‑kids‑care portion of an evening into four consecutive blocks:

1. **Reset** – a brief mental shift (2‑10 min) after the children are settled, allowing the parent to transition out of "parent mode."
2. **Prep for tomorrow** – quick preparations that smooth the next morning (e.g., laying out clothes, packing a bag). Typical duration is 5‑20 min.
3. **One small chore** – a single, bounded household task (e.g., clearing a counter, starting a laundry load). Duration varies from 5‑25 min.
4. **Downtime** – protected personal time before bedtime, ranging from 15 min to over an hour depending on energy level.

The sequence remains constant; only the length of each block flexes to match the individual’s energy and any spill‑over from the children’s routine. This ordering creates a predictable flow that reduces stress and prevents the evening from feeling chaotic.

## How it connects to other concepts
- **[[concepts/flexible-time-blocking]]** – The phases embody flexible time‑blocking: block lengths adapt while the order stays fixed.
- **[[concepts/parent-anchor-scheduling]]** – The children’s routine acts as a fixed anchor (5:30 – 8:30 pm). The phases are arranged around that anchor, providing structure before and after the anchor.
