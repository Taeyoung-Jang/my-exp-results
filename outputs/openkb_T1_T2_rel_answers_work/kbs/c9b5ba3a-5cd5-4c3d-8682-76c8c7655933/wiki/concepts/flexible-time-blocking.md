# Flexible Time Blocking

**Definition**: Flexible Time Blocking is the practice of scaling the duration of scheduled activity blocks up or down, while preserving their sequential order, to maintain a predictable flow despite variable energy levels or external interruptions.

---

## Core Principles
- **Maintain Sequence**: The order of activities (e.g., reset → prep → chore → downtime) stays constant; only the length of each segment changes.
- **Scale to Energy**: When you feel exhausted, shrink each block to the minimum needed (e.g., a 2‑minute reset, 5‑minute prep). When you have more energy, expand blocks for deeper engagement.
- **Absorb Variability**: If an external factor (such as a delayed kids’ routine) cuts into the schedule, reduce each subsequent block proportionally rather than skipping steps.
- **Predictability over Exact Timing**: Predictable sequencing reduces decision fatigue, even if the clock times shift.

---

## Application in Evening Routines
The concept is illustrated in the evening‑routine framework from [[summaries/session-14_2025-03-16]]:
- **Reset** – a brief mental transition after the kids are settled.
- **Prep for Tomorrow** – quick tasks that smooth the morning.
- **One Small Chore** – a bounded household task.
- **Downtime** – protected personal time before sleep.

By applying Flexible Time Blocking, a parent can:
1. **Shrink** each block when the kids’ routine runs late (e.g., 2‑min reset, 5‑min prep, 5‑min chore) and let the remaining time flow into downtime.
2. **Expand** blocks on higher‑energy evenings, allowing more thorough preparation or a larger chore.

## Benefits
- **Reduced Stress**: Predictable order prevents feeling rushed even when time is scarce.
- **Adaptability**: Allows the same routine to fit a wide range of daily circumstances.
- **Efficiency**: Minimizes wasted time deciding what to do next; you simply adjust the length of the next block.
