## Nighttime Food Avoidance

**Definition**: A set of dietary recommendations for the evening hours that aim to prevent foods and beverages that can disrupt sleep quality or digestion.

### Overview
During the late‑day period, certain foods and drinks can interfere with the body’s natural wind‑down process, leading to restless sleep or difficulty falling asleep. The concept is informed by the advice given in [[summaries/session-40_2025-09-13]], which outlines specific categories of items to limit after dinner.

### Foods & Beverages to Avoid
| Category | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| **Heavy, greasy, fried foods** | Slow digestion keeps the stomach active, causing discomfort and keeping the nervous system alert. |
| **High‑sugar snacks** | Rapid glucose spikes trigger an energy surge, followed by a crash that can disturb sleep cycles. |
| **Very spicy dishes** | Increase core body temperature and may cause heartburn, both of which hinder the ability to fall asleep. |
| **Caffeinated drinks (coffee, tea, soda) after late afternoon** | Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors, delaying the onset of sleepiness. |
| **Large portions close to bedtime** | Overloading the digestive system can lead to reflux or restless sleep. |

### Rationale
- **Digestive Load**: A lighter stomach allows the parasympathetic nervous system to dominate, promoting relaxation.
- **Thermoregulation**: Spicy foods raise internal temperature, counteracting the natural cooling that prepares the body for sleep.
- **Neurochemical Balance**: Sugar and caffeine impact neurotransmitters (e.g., dopamine, adenosine) that regulate alertness.

### Practical Tips
1. **Plan Light Evening Meals**: Focus on easily digestible proteins, whole grains, and vegetables prepared earlier in the day (see [[concepts/make-ahead-meal-planning]]).
2. **Choose Gentle Snacks**: If a snack is needed, opt for a small serving of yogurt, fruit, or a handful of nuts.
3. **Hydrate Wisely**: Stick to water or herbal teas without caffeine.
4. **Set a Cut‑off Time**: Aim to finish the last substantial bite at least 60‑90 minutes before bedtime.
5. **Integrate with Evening Routine**: Pair food avoidance with calming activities such as those described in [[concepts/evening-wind-down-routine]].
