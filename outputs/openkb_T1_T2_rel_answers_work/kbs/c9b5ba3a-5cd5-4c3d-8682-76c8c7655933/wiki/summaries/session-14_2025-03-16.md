# Summary of session-14_2025-03-16

**Main idea**: A flexible evening‑routine framework built on four ordered phases – reset, prep for tomorrow, one small chore, downtime – designed to fit around a fixed kids‑care window (5:30 – 8:30 pm) and to keep evenings predictable and low‑stress.

## Core framework
1. **Reset** – a brief mental‑shift right after the kids are settled (2‑10 min).  
2. **Prep for tomorrow** – quick tasks that smooth the next morning (e.g., lay out clothes, pack a bag) (5‑20 min).  
3. **One small chore** – a single, bounded household task (e.g., clear dishes, start a laundry load, fold a few items) (5‑25 min).  
4. **Downtime** – protected personal time before winding down for sleep (15‑90 min, depending on energy level).

The order stays constant; block lengths expand or contract based on energy and any spill‑over from the kids’ routine.

## Sample schedules (kids’ routine 5:30–8:30 pm, bedtime 10:30 pm)
| Energy level | Reset | Prep | Chore | Downtime |
|--------------|-------|------|-------|----------|
| **Exhausted** | 8:30‑8:35 (5 min) | 8:35‑8:45 (10 min) – basic prep | 8:45‑9:00 (15 min) – tiny chore | 9:00‑10:15 (1 h 15 min) – calm activity |
| **More energy** | 8:30‑8:40 (10 min) | 8:40‑9:00 (20 min) – expanded prep (tidy entry, pack lunches) | 9:00‑9:25 (25 min) – larger chore (kitchen reset, fold laundry) | 9:25‑10:15 (50 min) – richer downtime |

## Handling late kids’ routine
*If the kids’ routine runs past 8:30,* shrink each block proportionally (e.g., 2‑min reset, 5‑min prep, 5‑min chore) while **preserving the sequence**. Downtime then absorbs any remaining time, maintaining predictability without skipping steps.
