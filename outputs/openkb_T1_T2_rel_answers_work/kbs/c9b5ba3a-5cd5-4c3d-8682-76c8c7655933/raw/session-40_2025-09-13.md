**User:** I’m trying to plan a better weeknight routine and could use some ideas. Now that my evenings are usually free, I’ve started signing up for weeknight yoga classes around 7 and can stay out a bit later without rushing home. Any suggestions for simple dinner prep or snack options that won’t feel heavy before class, plus a couple low-effort ways to wind down afterward so I’m not wired at bedtime?

**Assistant:** You could treat dinner more like a light, steady pre‑class snack and a simple post‑class meal. Something easy to digest a couple of hours before yoga works well—think a small portion of protein with something fresh, like a little yogurt with fruit, a slice of whole‑grain toast with nut butter, or a quick veggie-and-hummus plate. Just enough to keep you from getting hungry but not so much that it sits heavily.

After class, you can do something warm and simple so your body settles down: a small bowl of soup, scrambled eggs with greens, or even a microwave‑friendly grain bowl you assemble ahead of time. Keeping it warm and light tends to help the transition to bedtime.

For winding down, try a short “bridge” routine that signals your brain it’s time to switch gears. A warm shower or a few minutes of stretching in dim light usually works well. If you’re still a little keyed up, giving yourself ten minutes with a book or a calming playlist can help your nervous system shift into sleep mode without feeling like another task.

**User:** Could you give me a few more specific make-ahead options I can prep on Sunday that work as a light pre-yoga bite and also as an easy post-class dinner? Ideally stuff that keeps 3–4 days and doesn’t take a ton of cooking.

**Assistant:** You could prep a couple of versatile “base” foods on Sunday that you can mix and match all week. For example, a batch of roasted vegetables and a pot of quinoa or farro keep well for several days. Before yoga, you can take a small scoop of the grains with a few veggies and a drizzle of olive oil or a spoon of hummus so it stays light. After class, you can warm a bigger portion, add a soft-boiled egg, rotisserie chicken, or a handful of greens, and it becomes a satisfying but gentle dinner.

Another easy option is making a simple lentil or vegetable soup that doesn’t rely on heavy cream. You can sip a small cup before class if you need something, then have a proper bowl afterward with a piece of bread or a little cheese.

Overnight oats also work surprisingly well in this role. Keep them plain on Sunday, then portion out as you go. Before yoga, a few spoonfuls with fruit keeps you steady; after class, you can add nuts or a bit of yogurt to make it more filling.

If you want something that feels fresher, you can prep a big container of chopped veggies and a simple protein like baked tofu or shredded chicken. Before yoga, grab a small handful with dip; later, toss it into a quick wrap or warm it up with rice.

All of these hold up for three to four days and don’t take more than a little roasting, simmering, or assembling up front.

**User:** If I’m doing yoga at 7, what timing would you recommend for the pre-class bite vs the post-class meal? I’m usually home around 8:30–9, and I don’t want to eat too late but I also don’t sleep well if I’m hungry.

**Assistant:** If you’re practicing at 7, try having your light bite around 5:30–6. That gives you enough fuel without leaving you too full once class starts. Then treat the 8:30–9 window as a quick, warm, easy-to-digest meal—something small but real so you don’t go to bed hungry. Most people do fine eating within an hour of sleep as long as the food is light, so a modest portion is usually all you need.

**User:** That makes sense. Can you suggest a few specific “small but real” post-yoga meals that take ~10 minutes when I get home (ideally using the make-ahead stuff), and also a couple things to avoid at night that tend to mess with sleep?

**Assistant:** You could turn your make‑ahead pieces into quick little bowls or skillet meals. Warm some of your roasted veggies and grains, stir in a scrambled egg or a handful of pre‑cooked chicken or tofu, and add a spoon of something saucy like pesto or tahini so it feels like a real meal. A cup of your prepped soup with a piece of toast or a little cheese is another easy one. If you’re keeping chopped veggies and protein in the fridge, you can toss them into a quick sauté with a splash of broth or soy sauce and have something ready in minutes.

The main things to skip late at night are heavy, greasy foods that linger in your stomach and anything super sugary that gives you a quick energy spike. Very spicy meals can also keep you awake because they raise your body temperature a bit. Light, warm, and simple tends to help your body settle down instead of gearing back up.
