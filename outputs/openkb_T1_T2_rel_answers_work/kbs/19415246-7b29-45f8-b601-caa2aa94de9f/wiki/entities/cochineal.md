# Cochineal (product)

**Type:** product

**Description:**
Cochineal is a natural red dye obtained from the dried bodies of female *Dactylopius* insects. It has been a prized colorant in Andean textile traditions for centuries, providing deep, rich reds that are difficult to replicate with synthetic dyes.

## Key Facts from session 03 2024 06 24
- In the session‑03 discussion, cochineal is highlighted as a hallmark of **natural dyes** used in authentic Andean ponchos.
- The dye’s tonal variation and uneven fading patterns are cited as reliable indicators for **[[concepts/textile-authentication]]**.
- Red hues derived from cochineal are contrasted with synthetic reds, which tend to appear uniformly bright and lack the subtle depth of traditional pieces.

## Role in Textile Authentication
- When examining a garment, the presence of cochineal‑derived reds—showing soft tonal shifts and natural weathering—supports a conclusion of **hand‑woven authenticity** (see [[concepts/handwoven-characteristics]]).
- Inspectors use dye analysis as part of a broader checklist to differentiate traditional Andean wool ponchos from tourist‑market replicas.

## Cultural Significance
- Red pigments from cochineal often carry cultural meaning, featuring in ceremonial garments and community‑identifying motifs across the **southern Peruvian highlands** and other Andean regions.
- Respectful handling of cochineal‑dyed textiles aligns with **[[concepts/cultural-sensitivity-andine-textiles]]**, emphasizing the importance of provenance and community permission before wearing such pieces.
