## Southern Peruvian Highlands

The Southern Peruvian Highlands refer to the rugged, mountainous areas in the south of Peru, encompassing parts of the Andean foothills and high plateau. This region is celebrated for its longstanding traditions of hand‑woven wool garments, especially ponchos, that feature distinctive weave structures, natural dyes, and community‑specific motifs.

### Textile significance
- **Hand‑woven characteristics**: The highlands are a prime source of textiles that exhibit the traits described in [[concepts/handwoven-characteristics]], such as irregular hand‑spun wool, subtle weave tension variations, and integral or braided selvedges.
- **Natural dyes**: Traditional colors often derive from cochineal reds and indigo blues, linking the area to the entities [[entities/cochineal]] and [[entities/indigo]].
- **Cultural context**: Certain patterns and color palettes are tied to specific villages or ayllus, making cultural sensitivity important. See [[concepts/cultural-sensitivity-andine-textiles]] for guidance on respectful appreciation.
