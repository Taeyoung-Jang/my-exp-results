# Indigo (product)

Indigo is a deep‑blue dye obtained from the leaves of the indigo plant. In the Andes it has been used for centuries to color wool and cotton fabrics, producing a characteristic rich, slightly uneven hue that distinguishes natural dye work from synthetic coloration.

## Role in textile authentication
Indigo is frequently cited in [[concepts/textile-authentication]] as a marker of authentic, hand‑woven garments. Natural indigo shows tonal variation within a single color band and a soft fading pattern that differs from the uniform, bright saturation of synthetic blues. When evaluating Andean ponchos, the presence of indigo‑derived blues—especially when paired with other natural dyes such as cochineal—supports a traditional provenance.

## Hand‑woven characteristics
The use of indigo often aligns with specific weave structures noted in [[concepts/handwoven-characteristics]], such as plain weave ground fabrics and tapestry‑woven decorative panels. Traditional backstrap‑loom pieces may feature indigo‑dyed borders or geometric motifs that are integral to the cultural identity of regions like the [[entities/southern-peruvian-highlands]] and the [[entities/central-bolivian-altiplano]].

## Cultural sensitivity
Because indigo coloration can be tied to community‑specific symbolism, handling and displaying indigo‑dyed textiles should respect the cultural context outlined in [[concepts/cultural-sensitivity-andine-textiles]]. Certain patterns or color combinations may hold ceremonial meaning and are not intended for casual wear.
