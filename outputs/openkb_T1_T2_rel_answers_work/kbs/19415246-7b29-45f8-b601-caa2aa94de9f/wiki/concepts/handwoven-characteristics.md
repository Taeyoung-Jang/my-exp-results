# Handwoven Characteristics

Handwoven characteristics describe the physical and visual cues that indicate a textile was produced manually on a traditional loom rather than by modern industrial processes.  These traits are especially important for identifying authentic Andean garments such as wool ponchos.

## Primary Indicators

| Indicator | What to Observe | Typical Hand‑woven Traits |
|-----------|----------------|---------------------------|
| **Fiber type** | Feel the yarn strands. | Hand‑spun wool (or alpaca, cotton) shows slight variations in thickness and twist; machine‑spun yarn is uniformly smooth. |
| **Weave irregularities** | Hold the fabric to light and examine tension. | Small wavering in the weave, non‑perfect alignment of pattern joins, and uneven tension across the cloth. |
| **Edge finishes** | Inspect selvedges, neck slit, and garment borders. | Integral selvedge created on the loom, braided selvedges, hand‑wrapped neck slits, or hand‑sewn blanket‑stitch edges. Machine overlock edges suggest a replica. |
| **Dye quality** | Look for tonal depth and variation. | Natural dyes (e.g., cochineal, indigo) produce a soft, uneven color with subtle tonal shifts and uneven sun‑fading; synthetic dyes appear flat and uniformly saturated. |
| **Repair evidence** | Check for mended areas. | Repairs made with compatible yarn and traditional stitching methods signal age and authenticity; lack of repairs often indicates a newer commercial item. |

## Supporting Details from Session‑03 (2023‑06‑24)

The conversation in [[summaries/session-03_2023-06-24]] outlines a step‑by‑step inspection checklist that directly references these characteristics.  It recommends:
- Running fingers along warp and weft to detect hand‑spun irregularities.
- Observing the weave for tiny wavering under light.
- Photographing selvedges and neck openings to capture edge finishes.
- Noting dye softness, especially the depth of cochineal reds and natural indigo blues.
- Recording any compatible‑yarn repairs as evidence of traditional use.

## Cultural Context

Understanding handwoven traits also ties to [[concepts/cultural-sensitivity-andine-textiles]], because many weave structures, motifs, and finish styles are linked to specific Andean communities.  Misidentifying a replica as authentic can lead to cultural appropriation, while correctly recognizing traditional features supports ethical collecting and purchasing.
