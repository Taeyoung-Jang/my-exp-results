# Textile Authentication

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** Systematic approaches to distinguish genuine traditional Andean textiles from later commercial reproductions.

## Overview
Textile authentication combines visual, tactile, and contextual analysis to assess whether a woven garment—particularly Andean ponchos—was produced using historic hand‑woven methods and natural materials, or whether it is a modern tourist‑market replica. The process draws on knowledge of fiber properties, weave structures, edge finishes, dye behavior, and cultural significance.

## Key Indicators
- **Fiber type**: Hand‑spun wool shows irregular thickness and subtle texture, while machine‑spun yarn is uniformly smooth.
- **Weave irregularities**: Hand‑woven cloth exhibits minor tension wavering and non‑perfect pattern joins, characteristic of [[concepts/handwoven-characteristics]].
- **Edge and selvedge**: Integral or braided selvedges and hand‑wrapped neck slits signal traditional production; overlock or machine stitching points to a replica.
- **Dye characteristics**: Natural dyes (e.g., cochineal reds, indigo blues) give a soft, uneven tonal depth, whereas synthetic dyes appear flat and uniformly saturated.
- **Repair evidence**: Older pieces often contain compatible yarn repairs performed by hand; newer items typically lack such wear.

## Hands‑on Checklist (derived from [[summaries/session-03_2023-06-24]])
1. **Silhouette & drape** – Observe overall shape before handling.
2. **Fiber feel** – Run fingers along warp/weft to detect hand‑spun irregularities.
3. **Weave inspection** – Hold to light; look for slight tension variation.
4. **Selvedge & neckline** – Photograph edges; note whether they are integral, braided, or machine‑stitched.
5. **Pattern & joins** – Assess alignment and consistency of motifs.
6. **Dye assessment** – Look for tonal variation and uneven fading patterns.
7. **Repairs & wear** – Document any hand‑sewn mends.
8. **Measurements** – Record length, width, neck opening.
9. **Provenance** – Capture known origin, community, or acquisition details.

## Cultural Sensitivity
Authentication should respect the cultural meanings embedded in motifs and construction techniques. Certain designs are ceremonial or community‑identifying; wearing such pieces without permission can be disrespectful. For guidance on respectful handling, see [[concepts/cultural-sensitivity-andine-textiles]].
