# Independence Readiness

**Definition** – The state in which a potential solo consultant feels prepared to start a one‑person business because they have a clear problem they solve, a defined target market, and a compelling value proposition that can be communicated succinctly.

## Core Elements

1. **Problem Clarity** – Knowing exactly *what* messy or ambiguous issue you will address. In the source conversation, the assistant stresses that “the people who feel ‘ready’ usually have a pretty clear sense of what problem they solve, who they solve it for, and why those people would pick them over alternatives.”
2. **Target Audience** – Identifying a specific segment of clients who need that problem solved. Broad, vague audiences lead to traction issues.
3. **Unique Value Promise** – Articulating a concise, differentiated offer (often a *clear‑offer*) that can be described in a single paragraph.
4. **Minimum Viable Service** – Selecting one bounded service (e.g., a project definition sprint) that can be delivered quickly, allowing proof of concept before scaling.
5. **Pricing Confidence** – Choosing a pricing model that aligns with the service’s ambiguity while avoiding under‑pricing (see [[concepts/pricing-models]]).

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/clear-offer]]** – Independence readiness is achieved when the entrepreneur can craft a single, well‑scoped offer that communicates the solution and scope.
- **[[concepts/career-decision]]** – Deciding to become independent is a career decision that should be based on the readiness criteria rather than external pressures.
- **[[concepts/low-risk-experiment]]** – Starting with a tight, low‑commitment engagement (e.g., a 1‑2‑week sprint) serves as a low‑risk experiment to validate readiness and market demand.
- **[[concepts/pricing-models]]** – Selecting an appropriate pricing structure (fixed fee, retainer, etc.) is part of the readiness checklist because it signals confidence and sustainability.

## Practical Checklist (derived from [[summaries/session-37_2025-03-14]])

- [ ] Can you state the problem you solve in one sentence?
- [ ] Have you identified a specific client segment who would pay for that solution?
- [ ] Do you have a *clear‑offer* with a tight scope and defined outcome?
- [ ] Have you chosen a pricing model that matches the ambiguity of the work?
- [ ] Are you prepared to launch outreach immediately, rather than waiting for perfect branding or legal setup?

## Why It Matters

Readiness reduces the tendency to over‑engineer branding, legal structures, or service menus before there is any paying client. It shifts focus to *validation through delivery*, enabling faster learning, cash flow, and the ability to iterate on the offer.
