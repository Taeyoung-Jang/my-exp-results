# Career Decision

**Definition**: A career decision is the process of choosing between continuing in a current role where one already has mastery and pursuing a “next level” role that may involve more ambiguity, leadership, or new responsibilities.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-15_2024-06-15]])

1. **Self‑Observation** – Assess daily emotional states: does the current work energize or drain you?
2. **Concrete Visualization** – Imagine the next‑level role in specific terms (problems, pace, responsibility) rather than as an abstract promotion.
3. **Value Alignment** – Match the choice against personal values such as mastery, growth, stability, autonomy, or impact.
4. **Low‑Risk Experimentation** – Test aspects of the higher role through short projects, shadowing, or temporary leadership tasks to gather real feedback before committing. See [[concepts/low-risk-experiment]].
5. **Signal Recognition** – Identify positive stretch signals (steady alertness, curiosity, satisfying fatigue) versus negative ones (dread, irritability, masking frustration).

## Related Concepts

- **[[concepts/depth-over-management]]** – When the decision leans toward deepening expertise rather than moving into people‑management.
- **[[concepts/low-risk-experiment]]** – A method for safely sampling higher‑level responsibilities.

## Practical Application

- Create a personal rubric based on the above elements to rate the current role vs. the imagined next level.
- Conduct one or two low‑risk experiments (e.g., lead a small cross‑team project) and record the emotional and performance signals.
- Re‑evaluate the decision after gathering data, ensuring the final choice aligns with core values rather than external expectations.
