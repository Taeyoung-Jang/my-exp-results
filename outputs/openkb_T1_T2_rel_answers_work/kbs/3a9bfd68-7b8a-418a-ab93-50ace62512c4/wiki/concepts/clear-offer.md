# Clear Offer

A **Clear Offer** is a single, tightly scoped service proposition that a solo consultant presents to a potential client. It defines **what** will be delivered, **for whom**, and **within what time or budget limits**, making the value proposition instantly understandable.

---

## Why a Clear Offer Matters

- It signals **[[concepts/independence-readiness]]**: readiness is less about paperwork and more about having a concrete, marketable service.
- It reduces the risk of over‑engineering or under‑pricing, acting as a **[[concepts/low-risk-experiment]]** for a new solo practice.
- It provides a clear anchor for choosing a **[[concepts/pricing-models]]** that matches the project's ambiguity.

---

## Core Elements of a Clear Offer

1. **Problem Statement** – A brief description of the specific messy or ambiguous situation you will address.
2. **Scope Boundary** – Explicit limits (e.g., one initiative, a 1‑2 week sprint, a fixed number of interviews).
3. **Deliverable** – The concrete output (e.g., a actionable briefing, a workflow map, a decision‑making framework).
4. **Pricing Structure** – Fixed fee, short‑term retainer, or project‑based fee aligned with the scope.
5. **Next‑Step Call‑to‑Action** – A simple request (e.g., “send me the messiest part”) that lets the prospect test fit quickly.

These examples all share the Clear Offer pattern: a single promise, a tight time or activity boundary, and a straightforward price.

---

## Crafting Your Own Clear Offer

1. **Identify the mess you love** – what ambiguous problems excite you?
2. **Define the container** – how many weeks, how many stakeholders, what concrete output?
3. **Choose a pricing model** – match fixed fee for tight scopes, retainer for ongoing ambiguity work.
4. **Write a one‑paragraph pitch** – include problem, scope, deliverable, and a simple “send me the messiest part” CTA.
5. **Test quickly** – use the CTA to get a yes/no response within a day, turning the offer into a low‑risk experiment.
