# Low‑Risk Experiment

A **Low‑Risk Experiment** is a short, bounded activity that lets an individual sample aspects of a higher‑level role without the full commitment or risk of a permanent transition. The goal is to gather concrete personal data—emotional and performance signals—so that career decisions can be made based on lived experience rather than speculation or external expectations.

## Core Characteristics
- **Time‑bounded**: The experiment runs for a defined period (e.g., a few weeks) and then is evaluated.
- **Low stakes**: It does not jeopardize current responsibilities or career standing.
- **Focused scope**: Targets a specific facet of the desired role (e.g., leading a small project, shadowing a manager, mentoring a teammate).
- **Reflective feedback**: The participant tracks feelings such as steady alertness, curiosity, or dread, and measures observable outcomes.

## Examples from [[summaries/session-15_2024-06-15]]
1. **Volunteer for a short project** that involves more uncertainty or cross‑team coordination than usual work. The participant takes on a slice of the project, not the whole thing, and notes how the experience feels.
2. **Shadow a senior colleague** in the target role, attending meetings and observing decision‑making processes.
3. **Temporary leadership moments** such as mentoring a newer teammate or guiding a small group through a decision, providing a taste of people‑management responsibilities.

These activities are framed as *experiments* rather than *trials*, emphasizing learning over performance evaluation.

## Why It Matters for [[concepts/career-decision]]
Low‑Risk Experiments provide concrete data that help answer the central question of a career decision: *Does this next‑level work feel like a natural extension of what I enjoy, or does it feel like a costume?* By isolating the emotional and functional signals of a stretch role, individuals can align their choices with personal values and avoid being swayed by external expectations.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/depth-over-management]]** – When a professional prefers depth rather than people‑management, low‑risk experiments can focus on technical stretch opportunities (e.g., tackling a complex reliability issue) instead of leadership tasks.
- **[[concepts/career-decision]]** – Low‑Risk Experiments act as a decision‑support tool, feeding real‑world experience into the evaluation framework.

## Conducting a Low‑Risk Experiment
1. **Define a clear objective**: Identify the exact skill or responsibility you want to test.
2. **Set boundaries**: Determine duration, scope, and success criteria.
3. **Execute with minimal disruption**: Choose a project or shadowing opportunity that fits within existing workloads.
4. **Reflect and record**: Note emotional responses (alertness vs. dread) and measurable outcomes (e.g., reduced time to resolve a problem).
5. **Evaluate**: Compare observations against your values and longer‑term career goals.

By following this structured approach, professionals can make more informed, values‑aligned career moves without succumbing to external pressure.
