# Summary of session‑37 (2025‑03‑14)

**Main focus** – How a solo consultant can decide when they’re *ready* to go independent and choose a single, well‑defined offering that targets ambiguous, “messy” projects.

## Key concepts
- [[concepts/independence-readiness]]: Readiness hinges more on clear problem definition, target market, and unique value than on legal paperwork.  
- early decisions: Service focus, pricing model, client acquisition strategy, and legal setup are the first pivotal choices.  
- mistakes for new consultants: Over‑engineering branding/legal structures, delaying outreach, under‑pricing, and being too broad.  
- [[concepts/clear-offer]]: One concrete promise (e.g., a short‑term sprint) that can be described in a single paragraph.  
- messy projects: Ambiguous, poorly scoped initiatives that many avoid but present premium value.  
- [[concepts/pricing-models]]: Fixed fee, day rate, project‑based fee, short retainer – each matched to scope uncertainty.

## Recommended offer types for messy work
1. **project definition sprint** – 1‑2 weeks to turn a vague initiative into a concrete plan with goals, milestones, and decision points (fixed fee, $1.5‑4 k).  
2. **workflow untangling** – 3‑week engagement mapping current state and delivering targeted fixes (project‑based fee, $2.5‑6 k).  
3. **decision‑making accelerator** – 1‑month lightweight retainer to unblock decisions and define criteria (retainer, $2‑5 k/month).  
4. **founder’s clarity partner** – Bi‑weekly sessions with a founder to structure thinking and prioritize (retainer, $1‑3 k/month).

The assistant advises selecting the **project definition sprint** as the starter offer because it is fast, contained, and easy for founders to say “yes”.

## Pitch paragraph (single‑paragraph offer)
> “If you’ve got a vague initiative you need to move on but don’t have time to untangle, I run a one‑to‑two‑week project definition sprint where I take your fuzzy idea, interview the right people, map constraints, and turn it into a clear plan with goals, milestones, and decision points. The scope is tight: one initiative, fixed time window, and a concrete briefing you can execute immediately. If you want to see whether your project is a fit, just send me a short note describing the messiest part—you’ll get an honest yes/no within a day.”

## Pitch variants (tone)
- **Direct** – concise, action‑oriented.  
- **Warm** – friendly, empathetic.  
- **Operator / No‑nonsense** – blunt, focused on efficiency.

## Outreach assets
- Subject line: “Quick project‑clarity sprint?”  
- Short DM: “Good meeting you last weekend. If you’ve got a vague project that needs shaping, I run a one‑to‑two‑week sprint that turns fuzzy ideas into clear, executable plans. If you want to see if yours is a fit, just tell me the part that feels messiest.”

## Takeaways
1. Define a **single, bounded offer** before worrying about branding or legal form.  
2. Choose a pricing structure that aligns with the ambiguity of the work (project‑based fee or short retainer works best).  
3. Validate fit quickly via a brief “messiest part” description and respond within a day.  
4. Use tone‑specific pitch variants and a concise outreach message to secure the first 2‑3 clients without a large audience build‑up.
