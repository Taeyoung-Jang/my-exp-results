# Summary of session-15_2024-06-15

## Core Ideas

- **Evaluating career direction** – Compare staying in a role you excel at versus pursuing a “next level” by assessing personal feelings, concrete role envisioning, and alignment with personal values. [[concepts/career-decision]]
- **Low‑risk experimentation** – Test‑drive higher‑level responsibilities through short‑term projects, shadowing, or temporary leadership moments without committing fully. [[concepts/low-risk-experiment]]
- **Signals of a good stretch** – Look for steady alertness, curiosity, and satisfying fatigue versus dread, irritability, and energy spent masking frustration.
- **Depth‑focused growth** – For those who prefer mastery over management, stretch opportunities should deepen expertise, improve system reliability, or increase influence without formal leadership. [[concepts/depth-over-management]]

## Practical Project Examples

1. **System reliability fixes** – Target flaky services or deployment bottlenecks.  
2. **Internal tooling** – Streamline dev setups, scripts, or test harnesses.  
3. **Observability enhancements** – Consolidate logs, dashboards, and tracing for clearer diagnosis.  
4. **Documentation revamp** – Capture tacit knowledge to reduce repetitive work.  

These projects showcase impact through craftsmanship rather than hierarchy.

## Concrete Proposal: Observability & Alert‑Noise Reduction

- **Objective**: Reduce noisy or low‑value alerts and provide clearer diagnostics for on‑call engineers.  
- **Scope**: Focus on the most active services and the highest‑frequency alerts.  
- **Success Metrics**:
  - Decrease in total alert volume (target X% reduction).  
  - Reduction in mean time to understand an incident.  
  - Fewer unnecessary escalations.  
- **Timeline**: 4‑6 weeks for an initial cleanup, with iterative refinements thereafter.  
- **Benefits**: Smoother on‑call experience, faster incident resolution, and lower cognitive load for the team, especially newer members.

## Takeaway

By aligning career choices with personal values, using low‑commitment experiments to gauge fit, and selecting depth‑oriented projects—like the outlined observability improvement—engineers can grow impactfully without pursuing traditional promotion pathways.
