# High‑Interest Debt Repayment

**Definition** – A focused approach to reducing balances that carry the highest interest rates, typically 8 % APR or above, in order to prevent interest from eroding savings and to free cash for other financial goals.

---

## Why Prioritize High‑Interest Debt?
- **Cost impact:** Interest accrues faster than most investment returns, especially on credit‑card balances (e.g., 19 % APR on [[entities/credit-card-debt]]). 
- **Stability boost:** Paying down costly debt creates a financial safety net that makes it easier to maintain an [[concepts/emergency-fund]] and to allocate money toward long‑term goals like a [[concepts/down-payment-savings]] plan.
- **Rule of thumb:** Debt at **≈8 % APR or higher** is classified as high‑interest and should be tackled before lower‑rate obligations such as a 5 % student loan.

---

## Core Steps (as illustrated in [[summaries/session-07_2023-05-14]])
1. **Identify the debt** – List all obligations and flag any with an APR ≥ 8 % (e.g., the $6,000 credit‑card balance).
2. **Allocate a dominant share of surplus** – In the example, $1,200 of monthly surplus is split with **≈58 % ($700)** directed to the high‑interest credit‑card until it is cleared.
3. **Maintain a minimal emergency buffer** – Keep a small safety net (2‑week buffer) while the debt repayment proceeds, preventing reliance on the same high‑cost credit.
4. **Adjust as milestones are met** – Once the credit‑card is paid off, redirect the freed cash to other goals (e.g., home down‑payment) while continuing to build the emergency fund.

---

## Interaction with Other Financial Concepts
- **[[concepts/budgeting]]** provides the surplus needed to allocate toward debt repayment.
- A modest **[[concepts/emergency-fund]]** protects against setbacks during the repayment phase.
- After high‑interest debt is eliminated, the cash flow can be re‑assigned to **[[concepts/down-payment-savings]]** or other medium‑term goals.

---

## Practical Tips
- **Automate payments** to the high‑interest balance to avoid missed contributions.
- **Avoid new high‑rate debt** while paying down existing balances.
- **Re‑evaluate interest rates** periodically; refinance if a lower‑rate option becomes available (though still higher than typical investment returns).
