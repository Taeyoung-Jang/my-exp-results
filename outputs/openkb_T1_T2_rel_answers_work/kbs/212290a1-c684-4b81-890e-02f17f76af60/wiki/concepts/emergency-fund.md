## Emergency Fund

The **emergency fund** is a liquid cash reserve designed to cover unforeseen expenses without forcing reliance on high‑interest debt. It acts as the first line of defense in personal finance, ensuring that short‑term shocks (e.g., car repairs, medical bills) do not derail longer‑term goals such as a [[concepts/down-payment-savings]] or debt reduction.

### Key Features
- **Liquidity:** Must be easily accessible (e.g., savings account, money‑market fund).
- **Size Targets:** Often expressed in months of essential expenses. In the *session‑07* playbook, two milestones are used:
  1. **2‑week buffer** – a minimal safety net that allows rapid progress on other priorities.
  2. **1‑month buffer** – a more robust cushion before shifting full focus to debt repayment or savings.
- **Prioritization Order:** The emergency fund is built *in parallel* with high‑interest debt repayment (e.g., [[entities/credit-card-debt]]) but remains a “must‑do” element in the overall [[concepts/budgeting]] strategy.

### Interaction with Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/budgeting]]:** The emergency fund size determines the amount of surplus that can be allocated to other goals; budgeting helps identify the surplus to fuel the fund.
- **[[concepts/high-interest-debt-repayment]]:** While paying down high‑interest debt is critical, a minimal emergency buffer prevents new debt from accumulating during the repayment phase.
- **[[concepts/down-payment-savings]]:** Once a sufficient buffer is in place, additional surplus can be redirected to the down‑payment fund for a home purchase.

### Practical Implementation (from [[summaries/session-07_2023-05-14]])
- **Initial Allocation:** $200 of a $1,200 monthly surplus went toward building the emergency fund, targeting a 2‑week buffer.
- **Adjustment After Milestone:** When the 2‑week buffer was reached, that $200 was reallocated to the home‑down‑payment fund while the 1‑month buffer continued to be built at a slower pace.
- **Post‑Debt Phase:** After the [[entities/credit-card-debt]] was cleared, the emergency fund contribution could be increased again to reach the traditional 3‑month target.

### Why It Matters
A well‑funded emergency reserve reduces financial stress, protects credit scores by avoiding new debt, and provides flexibility to pursue larger goals (e.g., buying a home) with confidence.
