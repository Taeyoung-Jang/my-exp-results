# Integrating Outdoor Time into Daily Routines

**Definition** – A set of practical tactics that embed short periods of natural light exposure into the normal flow of work, travel, and leisure, helping to support circadian health without requiring dedicated “extra” time.

## Why it matters
- Even a few minutes of outdoor light, especially in the **morning**, can provide a strong cue for the internal clock, improving mood and sleep regulation. See [[concepts/morning-light-benefits]].
- Direct sunlight is more effective than shade, but any exposure to open sky contributes to the light dose.

## Core tactics (derived from [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-15]])
- **Commute integration** – Walk part of the route, bike, or get off public transport a stop early.
- **Parking farther away** – Adds a short walk in daylight before entering the building.
- **Coffee or snack break outdoors** – Use a nearby bench, patio, or park.
- **Post‑lunch loop** – A quick 5‑minute walk around the block after eating.
- **Sunny workspace** – Position a desk near a window that receives direct light; keep curtains open first thing in the morning.
- **Micro‑breaks** – If only 2‑3 minute slots are possible, still step outside; consistency outweighs exact duration.

## Timing guidance
- Aim for **10–15 minutes** of outdoor exposure, preferably **morning** or around **midday**. If only a few minutes are feasible, prioritize the **first break of the day**.
- One slightly longer stint (>5 min) is marginally more effective than several very brief bursts, but any exposure is beneficial.

## Interaction with bright‑light therapy
- When outdoor exposure is unreliable (e.g., winter, indoor‑heavy mornings), a **bright‑light lamp** delivering ~10 000 lux can serve as a reliable substitute. See [[concepts/bright-light-therapy]].
- Using both together has no downside: the lamp provides a baseline cue, and outdoor moments act as a bonus.

## Summary
Integrating outdoor time is about **leveraging existing routines** to capture natural light, thereby supporting circadian rhythms with minimal disruption to daily life. This concept is illustrated in the practical advice from [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-15]].
