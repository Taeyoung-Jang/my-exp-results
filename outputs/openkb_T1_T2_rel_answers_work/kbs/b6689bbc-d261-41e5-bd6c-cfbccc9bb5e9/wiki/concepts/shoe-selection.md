# Shoe Selection

**Definition:** Choosing footwear that provides adequate cushioning, support, and fit to protect the feet during increased walking activity.

## Core Principles
- **Prioritize shoes before other interventions.** A well‑chosen shoe reduces foot fatigue and mitigates injury risk.
- **Fit and comfort matter immediately.** No break‑in period should be required; the shoe should feel comfortable the moment it is put on.
- **Support specific foot mechanics.** Identify overpronation or instability and select neutral or stability shoes accordingly.

## Checklist (from [[summaries/session-32_2024-12-15]])
1. **Instant comfort** – no rubbing or need for a break‑in phase.
2. **Heel fit** – snug but not tight; no slipping while walking.
3. **Midsole cushioning** – sufficient to absorb sidewalk impact.
4. **Flex point** – shoe should bend at the ball of the foot, not in the mid‑sole.
5. **Toe room** – about a thumb’s width of space in front of the toes.
6. **Stability** – foot should not collapse inward (overpronation) or roll outward during stance.

## Determining Need for Insoles
- **General foot fatigue or whole‑foot pressure** → likely resolved with a better shoe.
- **Localized pain** (arch, heel, inner ankle) → consider targeted insoles.
- **Trial method:** switch to a supportive shoe for a week; if discomfort persists, add insoles.

## Quick In‑Store Tests
- **Wear pattern of old shoes**: interior heel/forefoot wear suggests overpronation → choose a stability shoe; even or exterior wear indicates a neutral gait.
- **Walk‑around test**: notice ankle rolling inward during a short walk; excessive inward roll signals need for stability.
- **Balance check**: stand on one foot; wobble or arch collapse points to insufficient support.
