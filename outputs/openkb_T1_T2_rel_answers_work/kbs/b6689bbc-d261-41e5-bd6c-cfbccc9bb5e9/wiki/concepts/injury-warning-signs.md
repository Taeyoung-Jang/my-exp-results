# Injury Warning Signs

**Definition**: Observable symptoms that suggest a walking routine may be causing tissue stress or injury, prompting a reduction in activity or professional evaluation.

## Typical vs. Concerning Symptoms
| Symptom | Normal (Transient) | Warning Sign |
|---|---|---|
| Fatigue | Mild soreness that eases after a day | Sharp or pinpoint pain during or after walking |
| Stiffness | Light next‑day stiffness | Persistent swelling that does not subside |
| Sensation | General tiredness | Numbness, tingling, or loss of feeling in the foot/leg |
| Gait | Slight adjustment due to tiredness | Noticeable change in walking pattern (e.g., limping, over‑pronation) |
| Duration | Discomfort resolves within 24‑48 h | Pain lasting several days or worsening over time |

## Action Steps When Warning Signs Appear
1. **Stop or reduce** the walking session and allow the area to rest.
2. **Assess footwear** – ensure shoes provide adequate support and cushioning; see [[concepts/shoe-selection]].
3. **Apply basic care** – ice, elevation, and gentle compression if swelling is present.
4. **Seek professional help** if any warning sign persists beyond a few days, intensifies, or is accompanied by numbness or gait changes.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Proper **[[concepts/walking-ergonomics]]** (pace, warm‑up, stretch) can minimize the occurrence of warning signs.
- Integrating walking into daily life should be done gradually, as discussed in [[concepts/integrating-outdoor-time]].
