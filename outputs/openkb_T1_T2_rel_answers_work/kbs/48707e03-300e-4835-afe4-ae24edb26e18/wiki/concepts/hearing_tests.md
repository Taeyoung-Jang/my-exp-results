# Hearing Tests

**Definition**: Systematic audiologic procedures used to measure the ear's ability to detect sounds and to understand speech, both in quiet and in noisy environments.

## Core Components
1. **Pure‑tone audiometry** – Determines the softest audible tones across frequencies, establishing the presence and degree of hearing loss.
2. **Word / sentence recognition in quiet** – Evaluates how clearly speech is perceived when presented at comfortable listening levels.
3. **Speech‑in‑noise assessment** – Presents speech stimuli with background noise to find the signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) needed for accurate comprehension. Common formal tests include [[entities/quicksin]] and other standardized protocols.
4. **Informal whispered‑sentence‑in‑noise tasks** – Live‑voice, non‑standardized checks often used for real‑world fitness‑for‑duty decisions, as described in [[summaries/session-37_2024-06-12]].

## Interpretation of Results
- Scores are compared to **age‑matched normative data**.  Results may be expressed as a **percentage correct**, an **SNR‑50 value**, or descriptive phrases such as *"within normal limits"*.
- The **final impression** section, typically authored by an [[entities/audiologist]], indicates whether the individual meets criteria for **clearance** or **fit for duty** based on these metrics.

## Clinical Relevance
- Adequate performance (e.g., SNR ≤ +2 dB) suggests the person can follow conversations in typical noisy settings like meetings or conferences.
- Poor performance may lead to recommendations for [[concepts/assistive_listening_devices]], hearing‑aid fittings, or environmental accommodations.
