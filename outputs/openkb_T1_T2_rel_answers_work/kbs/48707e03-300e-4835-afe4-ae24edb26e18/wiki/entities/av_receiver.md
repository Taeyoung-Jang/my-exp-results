# AV Receiver

An **AV Receiver** (often abbreviated AVR) is a central hub that processes audio and video signals for a home theater system. It amplifies speaker outputs, switches video sources, and provides a range of audio processing features.

## Role in session‑13_2023‑06‑12
The document [[summaries/session-13_2023-06-12]] recommends upgrading to an AVR paired with a dedicated [[entities/center_speaker]] as the most effective way to improve [[concepts/audio_clarity]] and dialogue intelligibility.

## Key Features Highlighted
- **Individual speaker room‑correction (EQ)** – Allows each channel, including the center, to be calibrated to the listening environment without flattening the high end.
- **Dialogue lift** – Raises the perceived height of speech, useful when the center speaker sits low.
- **Independent center‑channel level control** – Essential for fine‑tuning speech volume relative to the rest of the mix.
- **Support for dynamic range compression** – Helps keep dialogue consistent without crushing the impact of music or effects; ties into [[concepts/dialogue_enhancement]].
- Optional **center spread** – Widens the front soundstage but is not required for clarity.

## Comparison to Soundbars
While a high‑quality [[entities/soundbar]] can provide decent sound, the document notes that a true AVR with a separate center driver offers superior control and natural dialogue reproduction. If a soundbar is used, the recommendation is to choose one with a real center driver rather than virtual dialogue modes.

## Integration with Center Speakers
The AVR should be paired with a well‑behaved center speaker. The guide discusses two main types:
- A vertically oriented or **coaxial** design ([[entities/coaxial_center]]) for off‑axis consistency.
- A 3‑way horizontal design that avoids the lobing problems of an MTM configuration ([[entities/mtm_center]]).

By providing the necessary amplification, processing, and connectivity, an AV Receiver is the cornerstone for achieving the dialogue clarity goals outlined in the session.
