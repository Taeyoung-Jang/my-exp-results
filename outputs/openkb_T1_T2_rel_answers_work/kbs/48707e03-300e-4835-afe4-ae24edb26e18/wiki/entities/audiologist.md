# Audiologist

**Type:** other

## Overview
An audiologist is a licensed professional specializing in the evaluation, diagnosis, and management of hearing and balance disorders. In the context of [[summaries/session-37_2024-06-12]], the audiologist conducted a series of auditory tests to determine the user’s ability to understand speech in noisy environments and subsequently issued a fitness‑for‑duty clearance.

## Role in Session‑37 (2024‑06‑12)
- Performed **pure‑tone audiometry** to establish basic hearing thresholds.
- Administered **word and sentence recognition** testing in quiet conditions.
- Conducted a **speech‑in‑noise assessment** using an informal whispered‑sentence‑in‑noise task, a real‑world variant of standardized tests such as [[entities/quicksin]].
- Documented the methodology, results, and normative comparisons in the report.
- Provided a final impression stating the user was *cleared* to resume duties on the [[entities/conference_interpreting_roster]].

## Evaluations Performed
| Test | Purpose | Typical Metric |
|------|---------|----------------|
| [[concepts/hearing_tests]] (pure‑tone) | Detect the softest audible tones across frequencies | Thresholds in dB HL |
| Word/Sentence Recognition (quiet) | Assess clarity of speech when sufficiently loud | Percent correct |
| [[concepts/speech_in_noise_assessment]] (whispered sentences + background noise) | Determine the signal‑to‑noise ratio needed for accurate speech perception | SNR‑50 or percent correct; compared to age‑matched norms |

The informal whispered‑sentence test is not a formal standardized protocol, but it is often employed for **fitness‑for‑duty** decisions when real‑world communication demands are high.

## Clearance Decision
The audiologist’s clearance language referenced:
- *Performance within normal limits* for speech‑in‑noise.
- *Adequate word recognition* in quiet.
- A statement that the user meets the hearing requirements for the **conference interpreting roster**.

When results fall outside normative ranges, the audiologist may recommend **assistive listening devices** (see [[concepts/assistive_listening_devices]]) or hearing‑aid solutions to improve speech perception in challenging acoustic settings.
