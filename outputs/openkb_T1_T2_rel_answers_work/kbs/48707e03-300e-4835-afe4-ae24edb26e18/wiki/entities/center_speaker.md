## Center Speaker

A **center speaker** is the front‑channel speaker in a home‑theater system that primarily handles dialogue and on‑screen voices.  In the context of the *session‑13_2023‑06‑12* discussion, the center speaker is highlighted as the most effective upgrade for improving [[concepts/audio_clarity]] and overall speech intelligibility.

### Why It Matters
- Provides a dedicated channel for speech, allowing independent level adjustments and more natural placement of voices on the screen.
- Works best with an [[entities/av_receiver]] that offers independent center‑channel level control and optional [[concepts/dialogue_enhancement]] or dynamic range compression settings.

### Design Recommendations from the Session
- **Avoid horizontal MTM designs** because they can produce lobing issues that degrade off‑axis clarity.  See [[entities/mtm_center]] for an example of a design to avoid.
- Prefer a **3‑way horizontal center** with a dedicated midrange, or better yet a **coaxial/"concentric" center** for point‑source behavior that maintains clarity across a wider listening area.  Refer to [[entities/coaxial_center]].
- Cabinet size is secondary to a smooth, even frequency response in the vocal range.

### Placement & Installation
- When mounted **below the TV**, angle the speaker upward so the tweeter aims toward ear height.
- Keep the speaker as close to the screen as possible and mount it on a solid surface to reduce vibrations.
- Align the front edge with the shelf (don’t recess) to avoid boxy resonance.

### Interaction with Other Products
- If a soundbar is used instead of a full AVR system, select one with a **real center driver** rather than virtual dialogue modes.  See [[entities/soundbar]] for the alternative approach.
