# Coaxial Center Speaker

A coaxial (or "concentric") center speaker places the tweeter directly in line with the mid‑woofer, creating a true point‑source that delivers consistent speech intelligibility even when listeners sit off‑center.

## Key Characteristics
- **Point‑source layout** – Keeps the vocal frequency response even across a wide seating area, avoiding the lobing issues of horizontal MTM designs.
- **Dialogue focus** – Works well with [[concepts/audio_clarity]] and [[concepts/dialogue_enhancement]] settings to keep speech clear without excessive processing.
- **Compatibility** – Pairs effectively with an [[entities/av_receiver]] that provides independent center‑channel level control and mild room‑correction.

## Recommended Use Cases
- **Off‑center listening** – Ideal for rooms where viewers may sit a seat or two away from the screen’s central axis.
- **AVR‑centric setups** – When upgrading from a [[entities/soundbar]] to a full [[entities/av_receiver]] system, a coaxial center often yields the most natural dialogue.
- **Complementary speaker choices** – Use alongside a quality [[entities/center_speaker]] (horizontal or 3‑way) if a point‑source design is not feasible; avoid [[entities/mtm_center]] which can cause voice smearing off‑axis.

## Placement Tips
- Position the speaker as close to the TV screen as possible, preferably on a sturdy shelf to reduce vibration.
- Angle the unit upward so the tweeter points toward ear height when seated.
- Ensure the front of the cabinet is flush with the shelf to minimize boxy reflections.
