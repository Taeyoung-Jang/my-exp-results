# Soundbar

A **soundbar** is a single‑channel speaker system designed to sit in front of a TV and reproduce its audio.  In the context of *session‑13_2023‑06‑12*, the soundbar is discussed as a budget‑friendly alternative to a full‑featured [[entities/av_receiver]] with a dedicated [[entities/center_speaker]].

## Key points from the document

- **Dialogue clarity:**  When a soundbar is the chosen solution, the recommendation is to select one that includes a **real center driver** rather than relying on virtual *dialogue‑enhancement* modes.  A true center driver helps keep speech intelligible without making other sounds thin or processed.  See [[concepts/audio_clarity]] and [[concepts/dialogue_enhancement]].

- **Processing settings:**  Light use of *dialogue‑enhancement*, modest *dynamic range compression*, and avoidance of aggressive *night mode* are advised.  Heavy processing can make the overall mix sound “mushy” or unnatural.

- **Comparison to AVR + center:**  While a dedicated AVR with a separate [[entities/center_speaker]] (or even a [[entities/coaxial_center]]) generally provides superior dialogue anchoring, a well‑chosen soundbar can still deliver satisfactory performance for users who prefer a simpler setup.

- **Placement considerations:**  The soundbar should be placed directly below or in front of the TV, ideally centered with the screen, and kept clear of obstructions to ensure an even soundstage.
