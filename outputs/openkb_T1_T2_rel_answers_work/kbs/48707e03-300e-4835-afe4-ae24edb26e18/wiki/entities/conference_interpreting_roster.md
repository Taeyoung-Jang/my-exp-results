# Conference Interpreting Roster

**Type:** Event (professional qualification list)

## Overview
The Conference Interpreting Roster is a compiled list of individuals who have been deemed qualified to provide live interpretation services at conferences, meetings, and other large‑scale events. Inclusion on the roster typically requires meeting specific auditory and performance standards, especially for roles where accurate speech perception in noisy environments is critical.

## Relevance to Session 37 (2024‑06‑12)
- During the audiology follow‑up described in [[summaries/session-37_2024-06-12]], the user sought clearance to rejoin this roster.
- The audiologist performed a **speech‑in‑noise assessment** (including an informal whispered‑sentence task) and documented the results in the report.
- The final impression in the report used language such as “cleared for return to conference interpreting roster,” indicating that the user's auditory performance met the functional criteria required for the roster.

## Key Auditory Criteria
- **Pure‑tone thresholds** and **word recognition** scores were evaluated as part of standard [[concepts/hearing_tests]].
- Specific **speech‑in‑noise performance** (often expressed as an SNR needed for 50 % accuracy) was compared to age‑matched norms, aligning with [[concepts/speech_in_noise_assessment]].
- Successful clearance generally implies that the interpreter can understand spoken content in typical conference acoustics, which often involve background chatter and reverberation.

## Related Entities & Concepts
- Performed by an [[entities/audiologist]].
- May reference the [[entities/quicksin]] test or similar standardized speech‑in‑noise measures.
- Clearance decisions can lead to recommendations for **assistive listening devices** (see [[concepts/assistive_listening_devices]]) if needed for future performance.

## Practical Implications
- Inclusion on the roster enables the interpreter to accept assignments that require high levels of **audio clarity** and **dialogue enhancement** in challenging acoustic settings.
- If speech‑in‑noise performance were insufficient, the interpreter might be advised to use hearing aids or other assistive technologies before re‑qualification.
