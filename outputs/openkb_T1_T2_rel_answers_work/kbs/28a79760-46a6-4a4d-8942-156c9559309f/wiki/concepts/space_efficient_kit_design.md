# Space‑Efficient Kit Design

**Definition**: A planning approach that selects the highest‑value emergency items and arranges them to fit within limited storage volumes (e.g., a 30–40 L bin) without sacrificing essential functionality.

## Core Principles
1. **Prioritize high‑impact items** – focus on water, multi‑purpose lighting, power, nutrition, medical, and documentation.
2. **Use compact, dual‑purpose gear** – e.g., a lantern that doubles as a charger, or a multitool that replaces several separate tools.
3. **Standardize container sizes** – choose uniform bottles, pouches, and sealed envelopes to stack efficiently.
4. **Separate “stay‑home” vs. “grab‑and‑go”** – keep a sealed emergency bin for shelter‑in‑place and a lightweight go‑bag for rapid evacuation.
5. **Label and compartmentalize** – small zip‑bag pouches or clear bags make inventory checks quick and prevent accidental over‑packing.

## Practical Layout (from [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-02]])
### Emergency Bin (30–40 L)
- **Water**: two 1‑gallon jugs (Reliance) or a few 1‑L bottles (Smartwater).
- **Lighting**: one LED lantern (Black Diamond Moji or Goal Zero Crush Light) and a compact headlamp (Petzl Tikkina).
- **Power**: 10‑20 k mAh power bank (Anker) with charging cable.
- **Food**: 4–6 shelf‑stable meals you actually eat (canned, pouches, etc.).
- **Medications**: a few days’ supply in a labeled pouch.
- **First‑Aid**: slim kit (MyMedic Solo or Adventure Medical 0.7).
- **Tools**: multitool + roll of tape.
- **Documents**: sealed envelope with copies of IDs, insurance, contacts.

### Go‑Bag (small backpack)
- IDs and insurance copies.
- Phone charger + tiny power bank (Anker Nano‑style).
- One water bottle + calorie‑dense snacks.
- One change of clothes in a zip‑bag.
- Small toiletries kit.
- One day of essential meds.
- Tiny flashlight (Olight i3T).

## Maintenance (see [[concepts/maintenance_schedule]])
- Perform a bi‑annual check (every 6 months) to rotate food, test power banks, and update documents.
- Align checks with existing chores (e.g., daylight‑saving time change) to reduce friction.

## Reducing Clutter (related to [[concepts/emergency_preparedness_basics]])
- Treat the bin as a sealed “capsule”; only store items not used daily.
- Store daily‑use items (extra batteries, tape, tools) elsewhere to avoid frequent opening.
- Use labeled pouches to instantly spot misplaced objects.
