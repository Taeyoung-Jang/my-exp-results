## Simple Two‑Section Notebook Layout

**Definition**: A concise, single‑page structure for a pocket notebook that separates everyday tasks from spoken‑communication preparation, using clear headings and minimal formatting.

### Core Elements (as described in [[summaries/session-40_2025-07-24]])
1. **Date header** – top line for the day's date.
2. **Today’s focus & progress checkbox** – a one‑line focus statement followed by a `[ ]` box to mark completion. This supports the habit of tracking progress and aligns with [[concepts/focus-and-progress-tracking]].
3. **To‑dos section** – a short list for errands, appointments, or other actionable items.
4. **What I want to say section** – space for drafting a phone call, meeting remarks, or any verbal communication you need to organize.

The layout is deliberately linear:
```
Date: ____________
Today’s focus: ____________________   [ ]
 
To‑dos:
____________________
____________________
____________________

What I want to say:
____________________
____________________
____________________
```

### Example Page (from the source)
```
Date: March 16
Today’s focus: Keep things steady and simple   [ ]
 
To‑dos:
Pick up prescription
Post office for the package
Refill cat food

What I want to say:
Call Dr. Rivera — describe the dizziness, note mornings are hardest, ask if medication timing could help
```

### Why It Works
- **Cognitive off‑loading** – Writing tasks and speech outlines externalizes thoughts, which research links to the [[concepts/cognitive-benefits-of-writing]].
- **Simplicity** – Only two sections keep the page from becoming a complex system, reducing friction for daily use.
- **Flexibility** – When a section fills up, you simply turn the page and continue, maintaining the same structure.

### Connections to Other Concepts
- The layout is a concrete application of the broader [[concepts/simple-notebook-layout]] idea, focusing specifically on a two‑section split.
- The inclusion of a focus line and checkbox ties the design to [[concepts/focus-and-progress-tracking]], encouraging a sense of accomplishment.

### Usage Tips
- Keep the notebook constantly on hand (e.g., in a pocket) to capture thoughts immediately.
- Review both sections once a day to keep tasks and spoken plans current.
- If you run out of space, add a small arrow (→) at the bottom and continue on the next page; the page remains a snapshot, not a rigid contract.
