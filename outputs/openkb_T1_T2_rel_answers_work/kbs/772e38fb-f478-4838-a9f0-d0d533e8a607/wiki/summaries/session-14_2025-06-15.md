# Summary of session‑14_2025‑06‑15  

**Date:** 2025‑06‑15 14:30  

## Core contribution  
A concise, actionable framework for deciding whether to try a new restaurant, cafe, or activity, including a 5‑question checklist, polite “bail” scripts, and strategies to overcome the habit of sticking to familiar spots.  

## Quick‑decision checklist (5 yes/no questions) [[concepts/quick-decision-checklist]]  
1. **Mood match:** Is this place aligned with the mood I’m in?  
2. **Social proof:** Do the latest photos or comments look generally acceptable?  
3. **Cost/effort:** Is the price or effort level right for me right now?  
4. **Curiosity spark:** Do I feel at least a little curious or excited about trying it?  
5. **Acceptable okay‑ness:** If it’s just “okay,” am I still willing to give it a short try?  

## Graceful “bail” scripts [[concepts/polite-bail-scripts]]  
- **Restaurant host:** “Thanks so much. I just realized this isn’t quite what I was looking for today, but I appreciate your time.”  
- **Meeting a friend:** “Hey, this spot isn’t really the vibe I expected. Want to switch to somewhere nearby instead?”  
- **Solo outing:** “This isn’t quite what I had in mind today, so I’m going to head out and try another place.”  

## Nudging toward new experiences [[concepts/risk-reduction-for-new-places]]  
- Treat new places as **tiny trials** rather than full commitments.  
- Pair the new venue with a **familiar anchor** (e.g., a reliable coffee, a favorite book, a short‑time limit).  
- Reframe the visit as an **experiment**: observe, enjoy briefly, and decide without pressure.
