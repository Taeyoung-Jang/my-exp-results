## Privacy and Safety in Neighbor Interactions

**Definition** – This concept covers the considerations and best practices for protecting personal information and maintaining personal safety while establishing casual relationships with building neighbors.

### Key Points from [[summaries/session-38_2024-06-15]]
- **Phone number sharing:** Including a phone number on a neighbor note is optional. The source advises that if you feel any discomfort, omit the number and share it later with trusted neighbors.
- **Note placement:** Posting a brief, handwritten note on your own door is recommended over slipping notes under doors or posting in common areas, reducing the risk of unwanted attention.
- **Tone and content:** Use low‑key phrasing that invites brief, optional interaction, avoiding any language that could be interpreted as a request for ongoing contact.

### Practical Recommendations
1. **Assess comfort level** before disclosing contact details. If you are uncertain, keep the note simple and omit personal phone numbers.
2. **Use low‑pressure language** – phrases like “feel free to say hi” or “no pressure at all” signal openness without demanding a response.
3. **Keep physical cues neutral** – a handwritten note placed slightly off‑center on the door looks personal, not like a directive sign.
4. **Prefer in‑person cues** for deeper connections; the initial note serves merely as a friendly introduction.

### When to Share Contact Info
- Only after you have gauged the neighbor’s trustworthiness or established a rapport.
- Limit the purpose of the number (e.g., “for building‑related matters”) to set clear expectations.
- Consider alternative contact methods, such as a building message board or email, if available.

By following these guidelines, you can foster a sense of community while safeguarding your personal information and comfort.
