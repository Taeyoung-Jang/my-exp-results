# Upper/Lower Split

**Definition**: A [[concepts/upper-lower-split]] is a strength‑training schedule that alternates dedicated upper‑body days with lower‑body days, typically performed four times per week (two upper, two lower). This layout balances workload, optimizes recovery, and provides a simple structure that supports long‑term consistency.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]])
- **Frequency**: Four sessions per week – Upper 1, Lower 1, Upper 2, Lower 2.
- **Session Structure**:
  - 1 main lift (e.g., bench press, squat, overhead press, deadlift).
  - 2–3 supportive movements targeting complementary muscle groups.
  - 1 optional “feel‑good” exercise that can be omitted without penalty.
- **Volume**: 6–8 working sets per major lift, usually split into 2–3 sets of 5‑10 reps. Accessory work is 2‑3 sets of 8‑15 reps.
- **Progression**: Follow a simple [[concepts/progression-strategies]] approach – add reps each week until the top of the range, then increase load and restart at the lower end.
- **Auto‑regulation**: Adjust weight or reps on a day‑to‑day basis based on warm‑up difficulty; no rigid deload required each session.
- **Deloading**: Every 6–8 weeks (or when motivation/joint comfort declines), halve the number of working sets while keeping load constant for a recovery week.

## Benefits for Sustainability
- **Predictability**: Fixed day types reduce decision fatigue.
- **Flexibility**: Easy swap options for each movement if equipment is unavailable.
- **Recovery**: Upper and lower sessions are separated, allowing muscle groups adequate rest.
- **Scalability**: Can be intensified by adding sets, reps, or load, or simplified by trimming volume.

## Typical Exercise Choices (example week)
- **Upper 1**: Barbell bench press → dumbbell bench / push‑ups; cable row → dumbbell row.
- **Lower 1**: Back squat → goblet squat / hack‑squat; Romanian deadlift → dumbbell RDL.
- **Upper 2**: Overhead press → dumbbells / machine press; pull‑ups → lat pulldown.
- **Lower 2**: Conventional deadlift → trap‑bar / kettlebell; leg press → front squat / step‑ups.

## When to Adapt the Split
- **Stalled Progress**: If main lifts plateau for 6‑8 weeks, tweak reps, volume, or accessory selection.
- **Exercise Discomfort**: Replace movements that cause pain or dread.
- **Time Constraints**: Reduce sets or shorten sessions to stay within schedule.
- **Life Changes**: Shift to a different split (e.g., push/pull) if weekly availability changes.
