# Full‑Body Training Patterns

**Description**: A set of foundational movement patterns—squat, hinge, push, pull, and core—that together provide balanced, efficient stimulus for the whole body in a single training session.

---

## Overview

Full‑body training patterns are the building blocks of time‑efficient strength programs, especially when training frequency is limited to two sessions per week. By organizing each workout around the major biomechanical patterns, you can ensure that every major muscle group receives adequate stimulus without needing a long, split‑routine.

The core patterns are:

| Pattern | Primary Muscles | Typical Exercise Examples |
|---------|----------------|--------------------------|
| **Squat** | Quadriceps, glutes, core stabilizers | Back squat, goblet squat, leg press, split‑squat |
| **Hinge** | Hamstrings, glutes, lower back | Romanian deadlift, kettlebell swing, hip‑hinge cable pull |
| **Push** | Chest, shoulders, triceps | Bench press, dumbbell floor press, neutral‑grip dumbbell press |
| **Pull** | Upper back, lats, biceps | Cable row, band row, lat pull‑down, assisted pull‑up |
| **Core** | Abdominals, spinal stabilizers | Plank, dead‑bug, scapular squeezes |

These patterns map directly to the **Superset Training** approach used in the [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-15]] template, where each superset pairs a lower‑body pattern with an upper‑body pattern to keep the workout dense and within a 45‑minute window.

---

## Practical Application

### Two‑Day Weekly Schedule

- **Day 1 (Tuesday)** – Emphasize **Squat + Pull** followed by **Hinge + Push**.  
- **Day 2 (Saturday)** – Start with **Hinge + Pull** then move to **Squat/Lunge + Push** (using a shoulder‑friendly press).

Both days use the same **Superset Training** structure (see [[concepts/superset-training]]) to alternate exercises, limiting rest to ~30 seconds between moves and ~60 seconds between rounds.  

### Set & Rep Scheme

- **Sets**: 2‑set option for a shorter 35‑minute session; 3‑set option fills the full 45‑minute block.
- **Reps**: 6‑10 controlled reps per exercise, staying in the strength‑hypertrophy zone.
- **Rest**: 30 seconds between the paired exercises, 60 seconds between each superset round.

### Progression

Progress follows simple **Progression Strategies** like weekly load increments, rep additions, or tempo pauses (see [[concepts/progression-strategies]]). The goal is a steady, manageable increase without extending session length.

---

## Benefits

1. **Time Efficiency** – By hitting all patterns in two sessions, total weekly training time stays under 2 hours.
2. **Balanced Development** – Reduces the risk of over‑emphasizing any single muscle group.
3. **Scalable** – Patterns are easy to adapt to different loads, equipment, and skill levels.
4. **Recovery‑Friendly** – Light cardio or mobility on off‑days supports recovery without adding significant fatigue.
