## Checkout Script for Declining Loyalty Offers

A **checkout script** is a short, polite set of phrases shoppers can use when cashiers ask for a phone number, email, or enrollment in a store’s loyalty or rewards program. The goal is to decline the offer quickly, maintain a courteous tone, and still receive any publicly‑available sale price.

### Why Use a Script?
- **Privacy protection** – Avoid giving stores data about your purchases.
- **Time efficiency** – A ready‑made line prevents a drawn‑out back‑and‑forth.
- **Consistent savings** – You still capture the same discounts that are visible in the store’s weekly ad or on‑shelf price tags, without needing an account.

### Core Script (from [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]])
1. **Primary line** – “No thanks, I’m skipping the rewards program, but I’m good to go as is.”
2. **If the cashier pushes** – “I prefer not to use loyalty accounts, but I’ll take any public sale price.”

### Alternate One‑Liners
- “I’m skipping accounts today, but I’m ready to check out.”
- “No loyalty number for me, thanks—just ring me up.”
- “I don’t use rewards programs, but I’ll take any standard sale price.”

These variations allow you to adapt to different cashier attitudes while staying firm and polite.

- Supports [[concepts/low-effort-savings]]: By declining loyalty enrollment, shoppers rely on simpler, low‑effort tactics like weekly ad scanning and unit‑price comparison.
- Ties into the broader **checkout experience** and privacy considerations.
