# Cash‑Back Card Evaluation

**Definition**: A systematic approach to determine whether the rewards earned from a cash‑back credit card outweigh its annual fee and any behavioral costs, ensuring the card adds net value to personal finances.

## Core Evaluation Steps
1. **Estimate Annual Rewards**
   - Identify your typical spend in each bonus or rotating category.
   - Multiply spend by the card’s cash‑back rate for that category.
   - Sum the rewards across all applicable categories.
2. **Subtract Fixed Costs**
   - Deduct the card’s annual fee from the total estimated rewards.
3. **Behavioral Check**
   - Confirm the spend patterns used in the calculation match your existing behavior; do *not* assume you will change habits to meet bonus thresholds.
4. **Decision Threshold**
   - If the net reward (rewards – fee) exceeds the fee by a comfortable margin (e.g., >10 % of the fee), the card is likely worth keeping.
   - If the net reward merely breaks even or is lower, consider switching to a fee‑free card or a card with a better alignment to your spending.

## Quick “Is It Worth It?” Formula
```
Net Value = Σ(Spend_category × Rate_category) – Annual_Fee
```
- Use real‑world spend, not projected spend aimed at hitting max bonuses.
- For rotating categories, include only those you already spend in naturally.

## Practical Tips
- **Keep it simple**: Avoid cards that require extensive tracking of multiple rotating categories.
- **Align with natural spend**: Choose a card whose bonus categories mirror your regular purchases (e.g., groceries, gas, dining).
- **Monitor annually**: Re‑evaluate each year as spend patterns or card offers change.
