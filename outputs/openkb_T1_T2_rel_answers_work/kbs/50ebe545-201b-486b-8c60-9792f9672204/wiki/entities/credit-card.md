# Credit Card (product)

## Overview
A **credit card** is a payment product that lets users borrow funds from a financial institution to make purchases, with the obligation to repay the balance later. Many credit cards offer **cash‑back** or other rewards that can be leveraged within a personal finance system.

## Role in Budgeting (Session 31)
- In the discussed budgeting workflow, a credit card that **auto‑categorizes transactions** and syncs to a budgeting dashboard is the central hub for tracking spending.
- By routing most purchases through a single credit card, the user simplifies category management and enables a low‑effort weekly review.

## Cash‑Back Evaluation
- The decision framework follows [[concepts/cash-back-evaluation]]: estimate annual rewards based on typical spend, subtract any annual fee, and keep the card only if net rewards comfortably exceed the fee.
- Rotating bonus categories are only counted if they align with existing spending patterns, avoiding the trap of changing habits just to earn points.

## Auto‑Categorization & Dashboard Sync
- The card’s integration with a **budgeting app** (see [[entities/budgeting-app]]) provides automatic transaction import and category assignment, supporting the [[concepts/simple-budgeting]] approach.
- Users are advised to keep spending categories minimal (e.g., groceries, dining, transportation, housing, shopping, entertainment) and rely on the card’s feed to maintain consistency.

## Privacy & Security Practices
- After linking the credit card to the budgeting tool, it’s crucial to review the bank’s data‑sharing permissions, ensuring only the necessary transaction data is shared.
- Follow [[concepts/privacy-best-practices]]: enable two‑factor authentication, audit connected third‑party services, and disable optional location or marketing data collection.
