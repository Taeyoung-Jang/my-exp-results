# Time Blocking

**Definition**: Time blocking is a planning technique that divides the day into discrete, pre‑assigned blocks of time for specific activities. By committing each portion of the day to a defined purpose, it reduces decision fatigue, creates clear cues for habit activation, and helps maintain a balanced routine.

## Key Elements from [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-13]]
- **Exact start/end times** – The schedule begins at 4:45 am and ends at 9:00 pm, with explicit buffers for transition (e.g., 7:15‑9:00 am buffer before work).  
- **Training integration** – Early‑morning training blocks (hard or easy sessions) are booked first, ensuring they never clash with work or social commitments.
- **Commute handling** – On Tuesdays and Thursdays, the block includes a 35‑45 min drive, an office‑side shower/change, and a realistic eating window.
- **Themed afternoons** – Each weekday carries a single focus (chore, social, admin, errand) that occupies a defined block, preventing the overload that typically follows a 9‑5 job.
- **Evening wind‑down** – A consistent 9:00 pm bedtime is enforced by reserving the final block for phone‑free relaxation.

## How It Supports Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/habit-formation]]** – By pairing a cue (the time block) with a routine (training, work, meal prep), the method reinforces automatic habits.
- **[[concepts/theme-based-scheduling]]** – The “themed afternoon” approach is a subset of time blocking, grouping similar low‑effort tasks into a single block each day.
- **[[concepts/meal-prep-basics]]** – Fixed meal‑prep windows (e.g., breakfast after training, dinner after chore block) align with blocked time, making nutrition management effortless.

## Benefits
1. **Predictability** – Knowing exactly what will happen at each hour reduces mental load.
2. **Boundary protection** – Clear start/end times for work, training, and leisure keep personal time sacred.
3. **Efficiency** – Buffer periods absorb overruns and transition friction.
4. **Flexibility within structure** – While blocks are fixed, the activities inside (e.g., which protein to cook) can vary, preventing monotony.

## Implementation Tips
- **Create a visual grid** (digital calendar or printed sheet) with colour‑coded blocks.
- **Include small buffers** (10‑15 minutes) between high‑intensity activities.
- **Review weekly** to adjust block lengths based on real‑world constraints.
- **Pair blocks with cues** (e.g., alarm at block start) to trigger the intended habit.
