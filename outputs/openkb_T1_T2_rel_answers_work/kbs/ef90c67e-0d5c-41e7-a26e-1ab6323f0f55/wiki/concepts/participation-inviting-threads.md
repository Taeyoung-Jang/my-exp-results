# Participation‑Inviting Threads

**Definition**: Structured discussion prompts designed to encourage broad participation, especially from quieter members, without feeling like spam.

## Core Characteristics
- **Low entry barrier**: Simple, personal questions that anyone can answer (e.g., favorite rediscovered game, memorable sound effect).
- **Open‑ended**: Leaves room for anecdotes, recommendations, and varied perspectives.
- **Community‑building focus**: Aims to reinforce the welcoming vibe described in [[concepts/community-health-indicators]].
- **Non‑expert oriented**: Does not require deep technical knowledge, reducing intimidation for newcomers.

## Example Prompts from [[summaries/session-13_2024-07-12]]
1. *“What unexpected game did you rediscover recently?”*
2. *“Share a quirky sound effect or manual illustration that still sticks with you.”*
3. *“Which games feel cozy on old hardware?”*
4. *“What hidden gem did you find because you rented everything at the local shop?”*
5. *“Tell us a childhood misunderstanding you had about a classic title.”*

These prompts provide clear entry points and encourage storytelling, which helps keep conversations alive and inclusive.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **Community Health Indicators**: Participation‑inviting threads are a practical tactic for achieving the *newcomer‑friendly* and *thread‑quality* indicators in [[concepts/community-health-indicators]].
- **Gatekeeping Mitigation**: By fostering a positive, inclusive tone from the start, these threads can pre‑empt gatekeeping behavior, complementing strategies outlined in [[concepts/gatekeeping-mitigation]].

## Implementation Tips
- Post the thread in a visible forum section (e.g., "General Discussions").
- Pin or highlight the thread temporarily to attract attention.
- Respond promptly and warmly to early replies to model the desired tone.
- Avoid overly frequent posting of the same prompt to prevent perception of spam.

## Where to Share
- Dedicated retro gaming forums.
- Community Discord channels (e.g., [[entities/discord]]) and subreddit sidebars (e.g., [[entities/reddit]]) where allowed.
