# Summary of session-12_2024-06-15

**Date:** 2024‑06‑15  

### Core Idea
The conversation explores how people can balance *play‑it‑safe* versus *take‑the‑leap* choices when facing uncertainty, offering a concrete checklist and concrete “stop‑rule” examples for common life decisions.

### Key Concepts
- **Calculated risk vs. gamble** – A calculated risk is backed by an understanding of downsides and realistic upside probabilities; a gamble lacks this grounding. [[concepts/calculated_risk]]
- **Small‑steps approach** – Break larger uncertain actions into low‑commitment trials and pre‑define checkpoints where you reassess. [[concepts/small_steps]]
- **Stop‑rules / checkpoints** – Predetermined resource or outcome thresholds that trigger a pause and re‑evaluation. [[concepts/stop_rules]]
- **Decision checklist** – A short, repeatable set of questions to run before committing to an uncertain opportunity.

### Practical Checklist (copy‑paste)
```
• What’s the realistic upside for me?
• What’s the worst plausible downside?
• Can I handle that downside without major disruption?
• Do I have enough information to make this choice?
• Is there a smaller, low‑stakes version I can try first?
• What would make this a clear “yes” for me?
• What would make this a clear “no”?
• What specific point will I stop and reassess if things aren’t improving?
```

### Stop‑Rule Examples
1. **New hobby**  
   - Give it four sessions; if dread remains, stop.  
   - Limit starter budget; pause if additional gear is needed before confirming interest.  
   - No spark of enjoyment after one month → stop.

2. **Job change**  
   - Require at least two fitting roles within six weeks; otherwise pause search.  
   - If interview processes stall repeatedly with no feedback, reassess.  
   - If job‑search stress harms daily functioning, pause until stable.

3. **Unfamiliar trip**  
   - Cost cap: pause if expenses exceed set limit.  
   - Secure safe lodging by a deadline; if not, stop planning.  
   - Identify at least three exciting activities; lacking them signals reconsideration.

### Takeaways
- Use the checklist to quickly gauge whether an uncertain opportunity aligns with personal risk tolerance.  
- Define clear stop‑rules to protect against sunk‑cost bias and to maintain control over resource expenditure.  
- Repeated low‑stakes exposure builds familiarity with uncertainty, making larger leaps feel less intimidating.
