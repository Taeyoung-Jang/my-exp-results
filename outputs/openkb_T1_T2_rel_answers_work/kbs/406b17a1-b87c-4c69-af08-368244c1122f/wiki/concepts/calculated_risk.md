# Calculated Risk

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A decision made with known odds and manageable downside, distinct from a gamble.

---

## Definition
Calculated risk refers to a choice where the decision‑maker has a reasonable understanding of both potential upside and downside, and the downside is tolerable or can be mitigated. It contrasts with a gamble, which lacks clear information about probabilities or consequences.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]])
1. **Understanding possible downsides** – The individual can articulate what the worst plausible outcome looks like.
2. **Realistic upside** – There is a credible expectation that the positive outcome is achievable, not just wishful thinking.
3. **Mitigation or absorption** – The downside can be absorbed without major disruption to the person’s life or resources.

## How it differs from a gamble
| Calculated Risk | Gamble |
|----------------|--------|
| Informed assessment of odds | No clear odds or data |
| Identifiable mitigation strategies | No clear mitigation |
| Decision based on reasoned judgment | Decision based on hope or impulse |

## Relationship to other concepts
- **[[concepts/small_steps]]** – Using low‑commitment experiments helps turn a potentially uncertain venture into a series of calculated risks.
- **[[concepts/stop_rules]]** – Pre‑defined checkpoints act as safety nets, reinforcing the “manageable downside” component of a calculated risk.

## Practical Application
The summary from **session‑12 (2024‑06‑15)** provides a practical checklist for assessing calculated risk:

- *What’s the realistic upside?*
- *What’s the worst plausible downside?*
- *Can I handle that downside without major disruption?*

If these questions can be answered satisfactorily, the situation likely qualifies as a calculated risk.
