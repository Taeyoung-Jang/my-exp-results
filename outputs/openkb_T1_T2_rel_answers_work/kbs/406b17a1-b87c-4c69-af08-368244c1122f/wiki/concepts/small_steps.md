# Small Steps Approach

**Type:** Concept

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]]

## Definition
The Small Steps Approach is a strategy for managing uncertainty by decomposing a larger, potentially risky undertaking into a series of low‑stakes, incremental actions. Each step is designed to be affordable in terms of time, money, or emotional energy, and includes a predefined checkpoint—often called a *stop‑rule*—where progress is evaluated and a decision is made whether to continue, adjust, or stop.

## Core Principles
1. **Incremental commitment** – Start with the smallest viable version of the activity (e.g., a single hobby session, a short informational interview, a brief research trip).
2. **Pre‑set checkpoints** – Before beginning, decide what measurable sign of progress or resource limit will trigger a reassessment. This aligns with the broader concept of [[concepts/stop_rules]].
3. **Feedback loop** – Use the outcome of each step to gather information, refine expectations, and reduce the unknowns for the next iteration.
4. **Risk calibration** – By limiting exposure at each stage, the approach helps individuals differentiate between a **calculated risk** (where odds and impacts are understood) and a gamble. See [[concepts/calculated_risk]].

## Benefits
- **Reduced fear of failure** – Small, reversible steps lower emotional stakes.
- **Data‑driven decisions** – Each checkpoint provides concrete evidence to inform the next move.
- **Skill building** – Incremental practice builds competence and confidence before full commitment.
- **Sunk‑cost protection** – Clear stop‑rules prevent escalation of commitment when results are poor.

## Example Application (from the source session)
- **New hobby:** Commit to four practice sessions; if dread persists, stop.
- **Job change:** Conduct a limited number of informational interviews before deeper investment.
- **Unfamiliar trip:** Book only baseline accommodation and a single activity; reassess after confirming safety and interest.

## How to Implement
1. **Identify the goal** – What larger outcome are you aiming for?
2. **Design the minimal viable step** – What is the smallest action that still provides useful feedback?
3. **Set a stop‑rule** – Define a concrete threshold (time, cost, satisfaction signal) that will trigger a pause.
4. **Execute and observe** – Carry out the step, record observations, and compare against expectations.
5. **Decide** – At the checkpoint, either proceed to the next increment, adjust the plan, or abandon the effort.
