## Foundation vs. Flexible Decision Framework

The **Foundation vs. Flexible Decision Framework** is a practical tool for balancing stability and spontaneity. It categorizes every decision or activity into one of two buckets:

1. **Foundation items** – aspects of life that have long‑term consequences or affect others who depend on you. Typical examples include:
   - Finances
   - Sleep and health
   - Work responsibilities and deadlines
   - Commitments to other people
   Protecting these pillars prevents cascading stress and preserves core wellbeing. 
2. **Flexible items** – low‑impact choices that mainly affect your immediate experience and can be undone easily (e.g., trying a new café, taking a different route home). These are safe zones for experimentation and are the essence of [[concepts/spontaneity_practice]].

### How the framework works
- **Identify** the nature of the decision. Ask: *If this goes wrong, will I experience lasting stress or will someone else be affected?* This mirrors the logic of [[concepts/calculated_risk]].
- **Apply a stop rule** when the activity falls under the foundation bucket. The stop rule is a concrete boundary that says, *"Do not proceed if it threatens a foundation item."* See also [[concepts/stop_rules]].
- **Proceed with flexible choices** using small, low‑stakes experiments ([[concepts/low_stakes_experiments]]) to expand your comfort with spontaneity.

### Connection to other concepts
- The framework is essentially a concrete instantiation of [[concepts/foundation_flexible]], providing explicit criteria for what counts as foundation versus flexible.
- It encourages **small steps** ([[concepts/small_steps]]) by starting with trivial, reversible actions, gradually enlarging the flexible zone.
- By treating spontaneity as a skill ([[concepts/spontaneity_practice]]), the model gives a structured path to practice while respecting essential boundaries.
