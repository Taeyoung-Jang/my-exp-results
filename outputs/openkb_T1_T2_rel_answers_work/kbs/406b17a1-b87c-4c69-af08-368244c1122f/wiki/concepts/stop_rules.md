# Stop Rules

**Definition**: Stop rules are pre‑set resource or outcome thresholds that, when reached, signal you should pause an activity, reassess progress, and decide whether to continue, adjust, or abandon it.

---

## Why Stop Rules Matter
When dealing with uncertainty, it’s easy to fall into *sunk‑cost bias*—continuing because you’ve already invested time, money, or effort. Stop rules give you an objective exit point, protecting you from over‑commitment while still allowing you to pursue worthwhile opportunities.

---

## Core Elements of a Stop Rule
1. **Resource Limit** – a maximum amount of time, money, or energy you’re willing to spend before evaluating results.
2. **Result Signal** – a specific, measurable indicator of progress (e.g., enjoyment level, skill improvement, feedback) that must be observed by a certain deadline.
3. **Decision Point** – a clear moment when you compare actual outcomes to the predefined expectations and decide to continue, tweak, or stop.

---

## Examples from Session 12 (2024‑06‑15)
- **New hobby**: give the hobby four sessions; if dread persists, stop. Limit the starter budget and pause if additional gear is needed before confirming interest. If no spark of enjoyment appears after one month, stop.
- **Job change**: require at least two suitable roles within six weeks; otherwise pause the search. Halt if interview processes repeatedly stall without feedback. Stop if the search harms day‑to‑day functioning.
- **Unfamiliar trip**: set a cost cap; pause planning if exceeded. Require safe lodging by a deadline; stop if not secured. Identify at least three exciting activities; reconsider the trip if fewer are found.

## How to Create Your Own Stop Rules
1. **Define Success Metrics** – What minimal sign of progress would make you feel the effort is worthwhile?
2. **Set Quantitative Limits** – Decide on maximum time, money, or other resources you’ll allocate before checking the metrics.
3. **Choose a Review Date** – Mark a calendar date or number of iterations when you’ll evaluate the metrics.
4. **Document the Rule** – Write it down in a simple format (e.g., “If after 4 sessions I still dread the hobby, I will stop.”).
5. **Apply and Reflect** – Follow the rule, and after each pause, reflect on what you learned and whether to adjust the rule for future attempts.
