# Low‑Stakes Experiments

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A strategy of deliberately choosing tiny, low‑consequence actions to train the habit of spontaneity while keeping overall risk minimal.

## Overview
Low‑stakes experiments are micro‑behaviors that involve little or no real cost, such as taking a different route home, trying a new café without checking reviews, or saying yes to a last‑minute social invitation that does not affect sleep, finances, or work commitments. By repeatedly exposing oneself to harmless uncertainty, the brain learns that "unplanned" does not automatically equal "risky."

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader [[concepts/spontaneity_practice]] that treats spontaneity as a skill.
- Operates within the "flexible" side of the [[concepts/foundation_flexible]] framework, avoiding the "foundation" pillars of sleep, money, health, and work.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/stop_rules]] that define what can be experimented with safely.
- Often implemented as a series of [[concepts/small_steps]] that gradually increase comfort with uncertainty.
- Can be evaluated using [[concepts/calculated_risk]] to ensure the experiment stays low‑impact.

## Practical Guidance (from [[summaries/session-32_2025-03-14]])
1. **Pick actions with minimal consequences** – e.g., a new lunchtime walk, a spontaneous dessert stop, or a short nighttime drive.
2. **Keep the stakes low** – the worst‑case outcome should be mild inconvenience, not a missed deadline or financial loss.
3. **Repeat regularly** – consistency rewires the brain’s risk perception, turning spontaneity into a habit.

## Benefits
- Expands personal flexibility without jeopardizing core responsibilities.
- Generates memorable stories and experiences.
- Builds confidence to later take on larger, more uncertain opportunities.
