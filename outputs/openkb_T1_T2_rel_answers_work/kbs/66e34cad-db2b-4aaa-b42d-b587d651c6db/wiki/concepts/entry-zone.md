## Entry Zone

**Definition**: An *entry zone* is a deliberately defined micro‑space at the doorway of a small living area that signals a transition from the outside world to the home environment. It provides a visual cue and a practical spot for everyday items (keys, mail, coat) without requiring additional furniture.

### Why It Matters
- Helps a studio feel larger by establishing a mental boundary.
- Reduces clutter by giving mail and accessories a dedicated place.
- Supports low‑effort daily routines such as the [[concepts/welcome-home-ritual]] and the [[concepts/low-energy-routine]] described in [[summaries/session-31_2025-04-14]].

### No‑Cost Strategies (from session‑31)
1. **Tape Boundary** – Use painter’s tape or washi tape on the floor to outline a small “entry square.” The visual line creates a psychological edge.
2. **Box‑Lid Tray** – Repurpose a shallow box lid or an existing kitchen tray as a mail/key holder placed on the taped area.
3. **Adhesive Hook** – Install a removable wall hook for coats or bags; this turns a wall spot into a functional latch point.
4. **Re‑position Existing Light** – Angle a lamp or swivel a chair so it faces inward at the entry, reinforcing the sense of a distinct zone.
5. **Mini Rug (if available)** – A small rug can serve as a floor‑level boundary, separating the entry from the rest of the studio.

### Quick Implementation Checklist
- [ ] Choose a tape color and mark a 2‑ft square at the door threshold.
- [ ] Place a box lid or tray inside the taped square.
- [ ] Hang a hook nearby for coats or bags.
- [ ] Adjust an existing lamp to illuminate the zone.
- [ ] Optional: lay down a small rug that fits within the taped area.

### Benefits in Practice
- **Visual Clarity** – The tape line instantly signals “this is where you drop things.”
- **Reduced Mental Load** – A designated spot means you stop searching for keys or mail after a long shift.
- **Enhanced Comfort** – Pairing the zone with a warm lamp ties into the [[concepts/welcome-home-ritual]], making the entry feel inviting.
