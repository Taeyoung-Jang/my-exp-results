# Real‑Time Communication

## Definition
Real‑time communication is the practice of delivering up‑to‑the‑minute service information to transit riders through multiple channels (PA announcements, digital displays, mobile apps, and on‑site signage) so that riders can make informed decisions during disruptions.

## Role in Disruption Management
Effective real‑time communication is a core element of [[concepts/public-transit-disruption-management]]. It reduces uncertainty, mitigates crowding, and supports the agency’s gate‑closure playbook by ensuring that every update is visible, consistent, and timestamped.

## Recommended Cadence
The source document [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]] proposes a concrete update schedule during gate closures:
- **Minute 0:** Staff announce the start of metering, the reason for the hold, and the time of the next update.
- **Minute 3:** First status check; if no change, announce “No new update yet; next check in five minutes.”
- **Every 5 minutes thereafter:** Provide a refreshed estimate (or explicitly state that an estimate is unavailable) with a timestamp.
- **Digital displays:** Auto‑refresh every 2 minutes; when data is stale, show “Awaiting next estimate” rather than an outdated ETA.

## Message Templates
| Situation | Template |
|-----------|----------|
| Unknown ETA | “Service is temporarily paused due to construction activity ahead. We do not have an estimated arrival time yet. We will provide another update at **[time]**.” |
| Shuttle buses replacing service | “Train service is suspended between **[stations]**. Shuttle buses are running every few minutes from **[location]**. Please follow staff directions and posted signs.” |
| Single‑tracking with longer headways | “Trains are sharing one track between **[stations]**. Expect trains every 15 to 20 minutes in both directions. Please allow extra travel time.” |

## Benefits for Riders
- **Predictability:** Riders can set personal bail‑out thresholds (see [[concepts/rider-contingency-planning]]) based on clear delay information.
- **Reduced Stress:** Frequent, honest updates—even “we still don’t know”—are less frustrating than silence.
- **Efficient Crowd Management:** Staff can direct riders promptly when they know the latest status.

## Benefits for Agencies
- **Lower Complaints:** Transparent communication curtails escalation to 311 or rider‑union channels.
- **Operational Flexibility:** Real‑time data allows agencies to adjust shuttle frequencies or re‑allocate staff on the fly.
- **Better Perception:** Consistent messaging demonstrates professionalism during long‑term projects.
