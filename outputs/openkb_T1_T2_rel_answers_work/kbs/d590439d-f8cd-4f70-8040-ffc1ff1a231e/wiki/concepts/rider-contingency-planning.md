# Rider Contingency Planning

**Definition**: A set of pre‑trip and on‑site strategies that individual riders use to maintain reliable travel when public transit service is irregular or unavailable.

---

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]])

1. **Pre‑trip preparation**
   - Enable push notifications in a preferred **[[entities/transit-app]]**.
   - Set a personal bail‑out threshold (e.g., 10 min delay for flexible jobs, 0‑5 min for zero‑tolerance positions).
   - Save at least one **alternate route** (frequent bus, **[[entities/bike-share]]**, or **[[entities/ride-hail]]**) in the app and test it once.
   - Pack essentials: charged phone, headphones, transit card with a small buffer balance.

2. **Decision‑tree logic**
   - **Flexible schedule**: wait one delay cycle; if delay exceeds threshold, switch to the alternate route.
   - **Zero‑tolerance schedule**: skip the train entirely if any *major delay* or *service paused* notice appears.

3. **On‑site actions**
   - When the threshold is reached, leave the station immediately.
   - Follow staff directions to **[[entities/shuttle-bus-service]]** stops or to the nearest bike‑share dock.
   - Use real‑time displays or the **[[entities/transit-app]]** for updated estimates; if screens show “awaiting next estimate,” treat it as a cue to bail.

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- Part of a broader **[[concepts/public-transit-disruption-management]]** framework that includes agency‑side tactics.
- Relies heavily on effective **[[concepts/real-time-communication]]** to give riders timely data for the decision tree.

---

## Practical Tools

- **Threshold selector** – a simple numeric field in a notes app to define “max acceptable delay.”
- **Backup route list** – pre‑loaded in the **[[entities/transit-app]]**, with icons for **[[entities/bike-share]]** and **[[entities/ride-hail]]** options.
- **Essentials checklist** – phone charged, headphones, loaded transit card, app login.

---

## Why It Matters

Rider contingency planning reduces stress, prevents unnecessary crowding at stations, and improves overall system reliability by smoothing demand spikes during disruptions. By making individual decisions predictable, agencies can better allocate resources, and riders can maintain confidence in the transit network.
