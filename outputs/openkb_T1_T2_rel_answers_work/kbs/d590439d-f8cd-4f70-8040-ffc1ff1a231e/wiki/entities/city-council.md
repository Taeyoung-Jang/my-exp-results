# City Council

**Type:** organization

**Overview**
The City Council is the elected legislative body responsible for setting policy, allocating budget, and overseeing municipal services, including public transit systems.

**Connection to [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]]**
- The session‑33 document proposes a **weekly summary template** that riders can send directly to the City Council to highlight recurring issues such as unstaffed gate closures, stale real‑time estimates, and missing shuttle‑bus signage.
- It recommends using the Council as a conduit for escalation after filing reports with [[entities/311]] and the transit agency.
- The suggested email includes an “ask list” (staffing at gates, five‑minute update cadence, clear shuttle signage) aimed at prompting Council action or oversight.

**Relevant Concepts**
- [[concepts/public-transit-disruption-management]] – The Council can influence agency practices through funding and policy directives.
- [[concepts/real-time-communication]] – Council oversight may require agencies to adopt stricter update cadences.
- [[concepts/rider-contingency-planning]] – Advocacy encourages the Council to support rider‑focused contingency resources.

**Related Entities**
- Works alongside the [[entities/transit-board]] and may respond to complaints filed via [[entities/311]].
- May coordinate with rider advocacy groups such as the [[entities/rider-union]].
- Can influence the provision of [[entities/shuttle-bus-service]] and integration with [[entities/bike-share]] or [[entities/ride-hail]] options.

**Typical Actions**
- Review weekly rider‑submitted summaries and case numbers.
- Hold hearings or request reports from the transit agency.
- Allocate budget for additional staffing, signage, and real‑time system upgrades.
- Communicate policy changes back to the public through official channels.
