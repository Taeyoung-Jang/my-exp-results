# Shuttle Bus Service

**Type:** product

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]]

## Overview
Shuttle Bus Service is a **replacement transit product** deployed by agencies to maintain passenger mobility when rail lines are temporarily out of service due to construction, signal upgrades, or other disruptions. It is positioned as a core component of [[concepts/public-transit-disruption-management]] and is designed to provide frequent, predictable service that mimics the affected rail corridor.

## Key Facts from Session 33 (2024‑06‑15)
- **Purpose:** Acts as a stand‑in for suspended train service, offering riders an alternative while gates are closed or single‑tracking is in effect.
- **Frequency:** Recommended to run “every few minutes,” with clear headways communicated via signage and announcements.
- **Communication:** Must be announced through the same [[concepts/real-time-communication]] channels used for rail updates (PA announcements, digital displays, whiteboards). Templates include:
  - *“Train service is suspended between [stations]. Shuttle buses are running every few minutes from [location].”*
- **Visibility:** Physical signage at station gates, turnstiles, and curbside stops should list shuttle routes, stops, and expected wait times. Updated whiteboards help riders see the current pattern.
- **Integration with rider tools:** Riders are encouraged to monitor shuttle status via their [[entities/transit-app]] (push notifications, live maps) as part of a broader [[concepts/rider-contingency-planning]] strategy.
- **Operational Cadence:** Agencies should synchronize shuttle dispatch with a five‑minute update cadence, ensuring that any changes to shuttle frequency or routing are reflected promptly.

## Role in Rider Planning
Shuttle Bus Service is a primary element of the **Plan B** checklist for commuters:
- Serves as the first alternate when train delays exceed personal bail‑out thresholds (e.g., 10–15 minutes).
- Requires riders to have the shuttle stop location and schedule pre‑loaded in their [[entities/transit-app]].
- Complements other contingencies such as bike‑share or ride‑hail options.

## Advocacy and Feedback
When shuttle service performance is inadequate (e.g., missing signage, irregular frequency), riders can document the issue and file reports through [[entities/311]] or agency complaint systems. Aggregated weekly summaries can be sent to the [[entities/city-council]] or the [[entities/transit-board]] to push for improvements.
