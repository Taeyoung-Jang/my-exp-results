**User:** I’m trying to do a bit of a closet reset for spring and I’m not sure what to keep versus donate—especially outerwear. I still have that heavy wool coat stored in my wardrobe. Any tips on how to store it properly (so it doesn’t get moth damage or lose shape) and whether it’s worth keeping if I only wear it a few times each winter?

**Assistant:** If you still like the coat and it fits your style, there’s no harm in keeping a quality wool piece even if you only reach for it a few times a season. Classic wool coats age well and are expensive to replace, so unless it feels outdated or you actively avoid wearing it, it’s usually worth hanging onto.

For storing it, the biggest things are shape, cleanliness, and moth prevention. Give it a proper clean before putting it away, because moths are attracted to body oils and tiny food particles more than the wool itself. Hang it on a sturdy, wide‑shouldered hanger so the weight doesn’t distort the shoulders. Slip it into a breathable garment bag rather than plastic, since wool needs airflow to stay fresh. You can tuck a bit of cedar or lavender inside the closet for natural moth deterrence.

If you find yourself never choosing it even on the coldest days, that’s a good sign it might be time to let it go. Otherwise, with good storage, it can last for many more winters.

**User:** That helps—thanks. When you say “proper clean,” do you mean dry clean every time before storing, or is airing/spot-cleaning enough? Also, what’s the best way to use cedar (blocks, hangers, sachets), and how often do I need to replace or refresh it?

**Assistant:** You don’t need to dry clean it every year unless it’s actually dirty. If it still looks fresh and doesn’t have any odors or stains, a good brush with a fabric brush and a day of airing it out is usually enough. If you wore it a lot or spilled anything on it, then a dry clean before storage is worth it.

For cedar, anything that gives off scent works: blocks, sachets, even cedar hangers. Blocks last the longest, sachets are gentler, and hangers are nice if you want something set-and-forget. Over time the scent fades, so once or twice a year you can lightly sand the surface of blocks or replace sachets to keep them effective. You don’t need a ton—just enough nearby that moths get the hint.

**User:** Got it. One more thing: should I store the coat hanging in the closet or folded in a storage bin? My closet gets a bit humid in late spring/summer—any tips for preventing mildew or that musty smell while it’s stored?

**Assistant:** Hanging is usually better for a structured wool coat because it keeps the shoulders and drape intact. If your closet gets humid, though, you can still hang it as long as you use a breathable garment bag and add something to control moisture, like a small silica‑gel or charcoal dehumidifier pouch nearby. Just avoid sealed plastic bags—they trap moisture and cause that musty smell.

If humidity is really high, folding it loosely and storing it in a fabric storage box with the same moisture absorbers is fine too; just refold it once or twice over the summer to avoid a deep crease.

Either way, keep it clean, dry, and able to breathe, and you won’t get mildew.

**User:** If I hang it, do you have a specific type of breathable garment bag you recommend (cotton vs. non-woven), and should I leave a little space around it for airflow? Also, how often should I check on it over the summer to make sure there’s no moisture or moth activity?

**Assistant:** A simple cotton or canvas garment bag is usually the safest bet because it breathes well and doesn’t trap humidity. Non‑woven bags are okay too, but cotton tends to feel sturdier and vents a little better. Leaving a bit of space around the coat helps—just an inch or two so air can move and the fabric isn’t pressed against other items.

You don’t need to check it constantly. A quick look every month or so is enough: make sure it still feels dry, your moisture absorber hasn’t filled up, and there’s no sign of nibbling or little moth casings. If everything looks normal the first couple of checks, it’ll probably stay that way all season.
