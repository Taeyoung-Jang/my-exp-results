# Winter Outerwear Storage

**Concept**: Effective methods for keeping winter coats in good condition while they are out of season.

---

## Core Principles
1. **Clean Before Storing**
   - Ensure each coat is freshly washed or dry‑cleaned to avoid stains set‑in and odors.
2. **Support the Shape**
   - Hang coats on sturdy hangers that support the shoulders; this prevents sagging and maintains the cut.
3. **Ventilation**
   - Store in a space with decent airflow. Avoid airtight plastic containers that trap moisture.
4. **Breathable Protection**
   - Use a breathable garment bag (e.g., a cotton or mesh cover) to shield the coat while still allowing air circulation. See [[entities/garment-bag]].

## Practical Steps
- **Select a Storage Area**: Choose a closet shelf, wardrobe, or dedicated storage room that stays dry and has moderate temperature.
- **Organise by Type**: Group similar outerwear together (e.g., parkas, insulated jackets) to make retrieval easier and to avoid crushing delicate fabrics.
- **Avoid Over‑Packing**: Give each coat room; tightly packed garments can develop creases and lose insulation efficiency.

## Why It Matters
Proper storage prolongs the life of winter coats, keeps them smelling fresh, and preserves their insulating properties for the next season. This aligns with broader [[concepts/closet-decluttering]] practices that aim to maintain a functional, streamlined wardrobe.

## Relation to Coat Selection
When deciding how many coats to keep—covered in [[concepts/coat-selection-strategy]]—the storage approach ensures that each retained piece remains ready for use, reducing the temptation to acquire replacements due to poor condition.
