# Burnout Prevention

Burnout prevention encompasses practical tactics that help individuals maintain energy, focus, and well‑being when working extended or irregular schedules. The concept is especially relevant for alternative work structures such as compressed weeks or four‑ten days, as discussed in [[summaries/session-33_2025-09-12]].

## Core Strategies

1. **Segment the day and insert brief mental breaks** – Breaking a long workday into natural segments reduces continuous cognitive load and creates micro‑recovery moments.
2. **Front‑load high‑energy tasks** – Schedule demanding or high‑focus work during personal peak‑energy periods, leaving lighter tasks for later.
3. **Establish a clear end‑of‑day cutoff ritual** – A consistent routine (e.g., a short walk, shower, or desk tidy‑up) signals transition out of work mode and protects evening time.
4. **Keep evenings simple** – Opt for easy meals, low‑effort downtime, and avoid adding new commitments that could extend the work mindset.
5. **Maintain basic health habits** – Consistent sleep, hydration, and short physical movement breaks support resilience throughout longer days.

## Related Concepts

- **[[concepts/alternative_work_schedules]]** – Provides the context (compressed weeks, flexible hours) where burnout prevention tactics are most needed.
- **[[concepts/boundary_setting]]** – Involves defining clear limits (e.g., “no laptop after 5 pm”) to prevent work from spilling into personal time.
- **[[concepts/buffer-zones]]** – Refers to intentional time buffers (e.g., a 15‑minute transition block) that help smooth the shift between work segments.
- **rest‑protection** – Encompasses protecting dedicated rest periods, such as treating Fridays as an “off” day.
- **time‑blocking** – A scheduling technique that allocates specific blocks for focused work, breaks, and personal activities, reinforcing the other strategies.

## Practical Example from Session 33

In the four‑ten schedule described in [[summaries/session-33_2025-09-12]], the assistant recommends:

- Breaking the 10‑hour day into several segments with short resets.
- Using a consistent shutdown ritual (walk, shower, tidy desk) to cue evening time.
- Avoiding late‑day meetings and keeping evenings low‑effort.
- Protecting Friday mornings with a clear identity and status indicators.

These recommendations illustrate how burnout prevention integrates with **boundary setting**, **buffer‑zones**, and **time‑blocking** to create a sustainable work rhythm.
