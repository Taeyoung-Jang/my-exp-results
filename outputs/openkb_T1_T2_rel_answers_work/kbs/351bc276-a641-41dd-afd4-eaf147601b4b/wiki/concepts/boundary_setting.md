## Boundary Setting

Boundary setting refers to the intentional creation of clear limits around when and how you are available for work, especially in flexible or non‑traditional schedules.  It helps maintain personal time, reduce stress, and prevent work from spilling into off‑hours.

### Why it matters
- **Protects personal time** – ensures evenings, weekends, or designated off‑days remain free for rest or hobbies.
- **Reduces burnout** – by preventing continuous work overload, it supports [[concepts/burnout_prevention]].
- **Improves coordination** – clear expectations make it easier for teammates to plan meetings without assuming constant availability.

### Core practices (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-09-12]])
1. **Communicate explicitly** – announce your schedule in a meeting and repeat it consistently.
2. **Use status indicators** – set chat status (e.g., “Out on Fridays”) and block calendar time.
3. **Enforce hard rules** – only check work messages on designated days if truly urgent; otherwise respond the next workday.
4. **Create buffer zones** – keep short periods before and after work blocks free for transition activities, supporting [[concepts/buffer-zones]].
5. **Leverage time‑blocking** – schedule protected downtime (e.g., Friday mornings) as you would a meeting, linking to [[concepts/time-blocking]].

### Linking to related concepts
- **[[concepts/burnout_prevention]]** – boundaries help avoid fatigue from extended workdays.
- **[[concepts/time-blocking]]** – structuring protected periods reinforces boundaries.
- **[[concepts/buffer-zones]]** – short transition windows reduce cognitive load when switching contexts.
- **[[concepts/rest-protection]]** – safeguarding rest periods is a direct outcome of strong boundaries.
- **[[concepts/alternative_work_schedules]]** – flexible schedules increase the need for clear boundary-setting practices.

### Practical tips
- **Set a clear identity for off‑days** (e.g., treat Friday as a personal day) and communicate it to coworkers.
- **Maintain consistent rituals** at the end of each workday to signal the transition, such as a walk or tidying your workspace.
- **Document the rule**: "No laptop unless urgent" and stick to it.
- **Model the behavior** – consistently honoring your own boundaries trains others to respect them.
