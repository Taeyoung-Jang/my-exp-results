# Summary of session-05_2025-06-12

**Main idea**: A practical approach to weekly planning that emphasizes *loose time blocks* and *buffer zones* to keep schedules adaptable, especially when most free time is on weekends.

## Key concepts
- **Weekly time‑blocking** – grouping activities into broad periods (e.g., "Saturday AM focus") instead of hour‑by‑hour slots. [[concepts/time-blocking]]
- **Buffers** – short, intentional gaps between blocks that absorb spillover and prevent cascade delays. [[concepts/buffer-zones]]
- **Focus block** – a dedicated high‑energy window for deep work, suggested for Saturday morning (≈9‑12).
- **Chores block** – a lighter‑intensity period for routine tasks, placed in Saturday afternoon (≈1‑4).
- **Social block** – a flexible evening slot for social activities, kept separate from productive windows (e.g., 6‑9 PM).
- **Rest protection** – reserving Sunday evening (post‑3 PM) as untouchable downtime to recharge for the week. [[concepts/rest-protection]]
- **Weekend template** – a repeatable schedule structure that balances productivity, obligations, and recovery.

## Findings / recommendations
- **Flexibility over rigidity**: By using blocks and buffers, the schedule can absorb unexpected extensions without derailing the entire weekend.
- **Energy‑aligned placement**: Schedule the most demanding tasks when personal energy peaks (e.g., Saturday morning) and reserve low‑energy times for lighter activities or rest.
- **Clear boundaries**: Protecting Sunday evening creates a reliable recharge period, improving readiness for the upcoming week. [[concepts/rest-protection]]
- **Customizable**: Users can shift the social block to either Saturday evening or Sunday midday based on preference, while keeping the overall shape intact.
