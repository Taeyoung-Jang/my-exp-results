# Summary of Session 33 (2025‑09‑12)

## Alternative workweek structures
- **Compressed week** (e.g., four long days) – extra full day off, but longer days can cause fatigue. See [[concepts/alternative_work_schedules]] for the broader family of schedules.  
- **Four‑day workweek with reduced hours** – gentler reduction, limited applicability.  
- **Flexible hours** – align work with personal energy peaks, possible coordination issues.  
- **Split shifts** – morning and evening blocks with a midday break; can fragment the day.  
- **Core‑hours** – mandatory overlap window plus flexible surrounding time.  
- **Results‑Only Work Environment (ROWE)** – time‑agnostic, high autonomy, requires strong trust.

## Common pros and cons
- **Pros:** increased control, better alignment with natural rhythms, longer continuous personal time.  
- **Cons:** coordination challenges, longer/fragmented days, risk of boundary erosion.

## Burnout mitigation for a 4‑10 schedule
- Segment the day and insert brief mental resets; short breaks act as [[concepts/buffer-zones]].  
- Front‑load demanding tasks to high‑energy periods.  
- Establish a clear end‑of‑day cutoff ritual (e.g., walk, shower, tidy desk).  
- Keep evenings simple: easy meals, low‑effort downtime.  
- Avoid late‑day meetings; maintain consistent sleep and hydration.  
- All of these practices support [[concepts/burnout_prevention]].

## Protecting the “off” Friday
- Define a clear identity (rest, hobbies, social plans) – akin to [[concepts/rest-protection]].  
- Block calendar time and set chat status (e.g., “Out on Fridays”) to signal unavailability.  
- Pre‑move small errands to weekday pockets to prevent pile‑up.  
- Enforce a hard rule: no laptop unless urgent, and respond the next workday.

## Communicating boundaries
- Announce the Friday‑off policy in a team meeting and repeat consistently – this is an example of [[concepts/boundary_setting]].  
- Use status indicators and calendar blocks for passive reinforcement.  
- Respond to any unexpected pings after the day ends without apology; consistency trains expectations.
