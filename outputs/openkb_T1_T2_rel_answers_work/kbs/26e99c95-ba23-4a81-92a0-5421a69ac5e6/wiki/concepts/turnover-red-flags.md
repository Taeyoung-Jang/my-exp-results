# Turnover Red Flags

## Definition
A set of observable indicators that staff turnover at a daycare or nanny‑share may be unusually high or problematic, suggesting instability that could affect a child’s experience.

## Why It Matters
Consistent caregivers are crucial for children’s sense of security and developmental continuity. Frequent, unexplained changes can lead to gaps in routine, reduced quality of care, and parental anxiety.

## Typical Turnover vs. Red‑Flag Patterns
- **Normal turnover**: 1‑2 staff changes per year in a small centre, or a few in a larger centre; often explained by seasonal schedules or career moves.
- **Red‑flag turnover**: Multiple departures within a short period, especially of lead teachers, with vague or defensive explanations; inability or unwillingness to let parents observe staff interactions; high turnover that coincides with other operational issues (chaotic environment, understaffing).

## Specific Red‑Flag Indicators
- **Unexplained high turnover** – many teachers leaving without clear reasons.
- **Vague or defensive answers** when asked about safety or staffing policies.
- **Hesitation to allow drop‑ins or tours** – staff seem reluctant to be observed.
- **Chaotic or understaffed appearance** – crowded rooms, high child‑to‑staff ratios.
- **Focus on “rules” over emotional support** – indicating possible staff burnout.
- **Inconsistent communication** about staffing changes or replacements.

## How to Ask About Turnover Politely
Use neutral, stability‑focused language, e.g.: 
> “I know childcare jobs can have a lot of movement. What’s your staff stability been like over the past year, especially for the classroom my child would be in?”

This phrasing signals interest rather than suspicion and invites a transparent response.
