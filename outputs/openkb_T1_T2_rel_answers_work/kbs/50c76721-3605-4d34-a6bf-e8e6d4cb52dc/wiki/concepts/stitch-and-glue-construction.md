# Stitch-and-Glue Construction

**Definition**: A boat‑building technique that joins pre‑shaped plywood panels with epoxy fillets and reinforces the hull with fiberglass cloth, creating a strong, lightweight, and relatively easy-to‑build vessel.

---

## Why Choose Stitch‑and‑Glue?
- **Simplicity** – Requires only basic tools (drill, clamps, epoxy) and minimal woodworking skill.
- **Durability** – Epoxy fillets and fiberglass provide a waterproof, impact‑resistant skin.
- **Forgiving for Beginners** – The method tolerates small alignment errors and is well‑suited for lake‑use kayaks.
- **Speed** – Faster than strip‑planking or skin‑on‑frame when following an efficient workflow.

## Core Steps (as highlighted in [[summaries/session-05_2025-05-24]])
1. **Panel Preparation**
   - Cut plywood to the boat’s hull shape.
   - Rough‑sand edges and pre‑drill stitch‑holes.
2. **Stitching**
   - Use zip ties or wire to temporarily hold panels in the correct geometry.
   - Fair the shape and adjust until the hull is smooth.
3. **Epoxy Filleting**
   - Apply continuous fillets of [[entities/epoxy-resin]] along all interior seams.
   - Ensure fillets are smooth and not overly thick to avoid later sanding issues.
4. **Curing Management**
   - Follow best practices in [[concepts/epoxy-curing-management]]: work in stable temperature, allow full cure before moving to the next step.
5. **Glass Reinforcement**
   - Wet‑out [[entities/fiberglass-cloth]] thoroughly and lay it over the exterior of the hull.
   - Remove air bubbles and ensure full saturation.
6. **Waterproofing & Seam Sealing**
   - After the glass cures, apply additional epoxy coats and smooth them for a watertight finish, referencing [[concepts/waterproofing-and-seam-sealing]].
7. **Finishing**
   - Lightly sand, apply a finish coat, and install fittings.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Thick or Lumpy Fillets** – Lead to excessive sanding; keep fillets even.
- **Insufficient Wet‑out** – Dry spots in the fiberglass allow water ingress.
- **Temperature Swings** – Can cause amine blush or uneven cure; schedule epoxy work during steady‑temp windows (see [[concepts/epoxy-curing-management]]).
- **Skipping Edge Sealing** – All exposed plywood edges must be sealed before the hull is closed.

## Suitable Projects
- Small human‑powered craft such as a **[[entities/kayak]]** for lake use.
- Simple dinghies or pedal‑powered boats where speed of build is valued.
