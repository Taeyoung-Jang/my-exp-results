# Waterproofing and Seam Sealing

**Definition**: The set of practices that protect a wooden boat’s hull from water ingress by properly sealing exposed wood edges, applying smooth epoxy fillets, and ensuring fully‑wet fiberglass reinforcement.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Edge sealing**
   - Every exposed plywood edge must be coated with epoxy (or a compatible sealant) before panels are joined. This prevents capillary action that draws water into the wood.
2. **Epoxy fillets**
   - Apply a **smooth, continuous fillet** of epoxy along each interior seam. The fillet should be thick enough to bridge gaps but free of lumps or voids, as uneven fillets lead to sanding re‑work and potential weak points.
3. **Fiberglass wet‑out**
   - Lay **fiberglass cloth** over the fillets and ensure it is **fully saturated** with resin. Dry spots or bubbles compromise the barrier and can cause cracking under stress.
4. **Surface preparation**
   - After each epoxy cure, lightly scuff the surface to promote adhesion for the next layer. Clean of dust, wax, or amine blush before proceeding.

---

## Why It Matters for Stitch‑and‑Glue Builds

The **stitch‑and‑glue** method relies heavily on epoxy joints for structural integrity. Proper waterproofing and seam sealing:
- Enhances durability for regular lake use.
- Reduces long‑term maintenance by preventing rot.
- Provides a smooth hull finish that improves water flow and handling.

---

## Practical Steps (derived from [[summaries/session-05_2025-05-24]])

1. **Prepare panels** – Fair and tack the hull shape before any epoxy is mixed.
2. **Apply fillets** – Use a mixing syringe or spatula to place a uniform bead along each seam; let it cure fully.
3. **Scuff & clean** – Lightly sand cured fillets, wipe with a clean cloth.
4. **Glassing** – Lay **[[entities/fiberglass-cloth]]** over the fillet, wet out with **[[entities/epoxy-resin]]** in a temperature‑stable window (see [[concepts/epoxy-curing-management]]).
5. **Final seal** – After the glass cures, apply a thin finish coat for added protection and aesthetic polish.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- Skipping edge sealing leads to hidden water pathways.
- Over‑thick or lumpy fillets require extensive sanding.
- Inadequate resin saturation creates weak spots that can delaminate.
- Ignoring amine blush (a by‑product of epoxy curing) reduces bond strength; wash it off with warm water before subsequent coats.
