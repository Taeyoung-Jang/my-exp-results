# Epoxy Curing Management

**Definition**: The systematic planning and control of epoxy resin curing conditions—temperature, humidity, and timing—to ensure reliable bond quality, avoid amine blush, and achieve consistent mechanical properties.

## Why it matters
- Epoxy is the primary adhesive and sealant in [[concepts/stitch-and-glue-construction]] and in the [[concepts/waterproofing-and-seam-sealing]] of small wooden boats such as a [[entities/kayak]].
- Improper cure can lead to bubbles, weak fillets, surface blush, and ultimately water ingress or structural failure.

## Core principles from [[summaries/session-05_2025-05-24]]
1. **Stable temperature windows** – Perform filleting, glass wet‑out, and fill‑coat work when the garage temperature is steady or gently rising (e.g., early evening or mid‑morning). This reduces thermal gradients that cause air entrapment.
2. **Humidity control** – Cool, humid conditions promote amine blush on the surface of cured epoxy. Aim for the warmest, driest part of the day for each curing step.
3. **Full cure before the next step** – Allow each epoxy layer (fillet, glass, reinforcement tape, fill coat) to reach its full cure time before sanding, scuffing, or applying the next coat. This prevents re‑work caused by tacky surfaces.
4. **Surface preparation** – Light scuff after each cure to ensure proper adhesion of subsequent layers.

## Practical workflow
| Stage | Recommended actions |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Fillet application** | Mix epoxy at room temperature, apply smooth fillets, let cure fully (per manufacturer’s time) before any sanding. |
| **Glassing** | After fillet cure, lightly scuff, then wet‑out [[entities/fiberglass-cloth]] in a single, draft‑free session. Keep ambient temperature steady; avoid applying over a blush‑covered surface. |
| **Reinforcement tapes & fill coats** | Once glass cures, repeat scuff‑and‑wet cycle. Schedule these steps during the same temperature window to maintain consistency. |
| **Final finish** | After final sand, apply a protective coat; ensure no residual blush remains.

## Dealing with amine blush
- **Identification**: A waxy, slightly oily film on cured epoxy, often lighter in color.
- **Fast remediation**: Wash with warm water and a non‑abrasive scrub pad, rinse, dry, and lightly scuff before the next epoxy application.
- **Prevention**: Keep the work area warm (>20 °C) and avoid high humidity during the cure period.

## Summary checklist
- ☐ Check garage temperature stability before each epoxy step.
- ☐ Schedule work during rising‑temp windows.
- ☐ Verify full cure time per [[entities/epoxy-resin]] data sheet.
- ☐ Inspect for blush; wash if present.
- ☐ Lightly scuff before applying subsequent layers.

By systematically managing these variables, builders can avoid common time‑killers and achieve a durable, waterproof hull.
