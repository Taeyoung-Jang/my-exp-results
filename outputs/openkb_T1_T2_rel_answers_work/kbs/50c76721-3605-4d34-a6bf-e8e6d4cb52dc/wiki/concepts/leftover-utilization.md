# Leftover Utilization

**Leftover utilization** is the practice of planning meals and routines so that cooked ingredients are deliberately reused in subsequent dishes, minimizing food waste and saving time.

---
## Core Principles
1. **Ingredient Consolidation** – Choose a limited set of versatile proteins, vegetables, and pantry staples that can be transformed into several distinct meals.
2. **Batch Cooking** – Prepare a core component (e.g., roasted chicken, roasted vegetables, cooked rice) in a single session and store it for easy reheating.
3. **Cross‑Meal Pairing** – Re‑combine the same cooked items with different sauces, grains, or cooking methods to create new recipes.
4. **Efficient Reheating** – Use gentle reheating techniques (covered microwave with a splash of water, or stovetop with broth) to keep proteins moist.

---
## How It Was Applied in [[summaries/session-35_2025-07-24]]
- **Batch‑cooked chicken**: One pound of boneless chicken thighs was roasted on Friday and again on Sunday, providing protein for four dinners.
- **Roasted vegetables**: A whole head of broccoli and two bell peppers were roasted once and then divided among a bowl, a stir‑fry, and a fried‑rice skillet.
- **Unified grains**: Pre‑cooked rice (already on hand) served as the base for three meals, while pasta was used for a single pesto dish.
- **Sauce diversity**: A small jar of [[entities/pesto]] and a bottle of [[entities/soy-sauce]] / [[entities/teriyaki-sauce]] gave each dinner a distinct flavor profile without needing new ingredients.
- **Lunch reuse**: Leftover roasted chicken and vegetables were repurposed into salads, rice bowls, and an egg‑pesto scramble.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Integrated with [[concepts/meal-prep]]: The weekend schedule (Friday prep, Sunday prep) is a classic meal‑prep workflow that relies heavily on leftover utilization.
- Supports a smooth [[concepts/weekend-routine]] by front‑loading cooking tasks, allowing low‑effort weekday dinners.

---
## Practical Tips
- **Portion control**: Cook just enough of each ingredient to cover the planned meals, reducing excess.
- **Storage**: Keep cooked protein and vegetables in airtight containers; separate sauces to prevent sogginess.
- **Flavor refresh**: Add a fresh squeeze of lemon, a drizzle of [[entities/olive-oil]], or a pinch of herbs before serving to keep leftovers tasting new.

## Example Meal Flow
| Day | Meal | Core leftovers used |
|-----|------|----------------------|
| Mon | Roasted chicken bowl | Friday‑roasted chicken, veg |
| Tue | Pesto pasta | Small amount of leftover chicken, spinach |
| Wed | Chicken stir‑fry | Sunday‑cooked chicken, raw or leftover veg |
| Thu | Fried‑rice skillet | Remaining chicken, veg, egg |
| Lunches | Salads, rice bowls, egg scramble | Various leftover portions |

By designing a menu around a handful of shared ingredients, the plan achieves minimal waste, quick weeknight cooking, and consistent nutrition.
