# Epoxy Resin

Epoxy resin is a two‑component adhesive and coating system widely used in marine woodworking. In the context of the **session‑05_2025‑05‑24** conversation, it serves several critical functions:

- **Fillet creation**: shaping smooth, strong epoxy fillets at panel seams before glassing.
- **Fiberglass wet‑out**: saturating [[entities/fiberglass-cloth]] to form a waterproof skin.
- **Curing management**: timing mixes and applying coats according to [[concepts/epoxy-curing-management]] to avoid issues like amine blush.
- **Waterproofing and seam sealing**: part of the overall strategy described in [[concepts/waterproofing-and-seam-sealing]].

### Key facts from the document
1. **Temperature stability** – Epoxy work should be scheduled during periods of steady or gently rising temperature (early evening or mid‑morning) to ensure proper cure and minimize bubbles.
2. **Amine blush mitigation** – When blush appears, the recommended quick remedy is a warm‑water wash, light scrubbing, rinsing, drying, and a brief scuff before the next coat.
3. **Sequential workflow** – Apply and fully cure fillets first, sand lightly, then proceed to glassing, reinforcement tapes, fill coats, and final finish. Each stage must be clean and fully cured to prevent rework.
4. **Interaction with construction method** – The epoxy resin is a core material for the [[concepts/stitch-and-glue-construction]] approach, providing the adhesive bond and structural reinforcement that make the method both simple and durable.
