## Fiberglass Cloth

Fiberglass cloth is a woven fabric made from fine glass fibers, commonly used in boat building to provide structural strength and waterproofing when saturated with epoxy resin.

### Key facts from [[summaries/session-05_2025-05-24]]
- **Purpose**: Reinforces the hull of a wooden kayak, creating a durable, water‑tight skin.
- **Application steps**: After epoxy fillets cure, the cloth is wet‑out in a clean, stable‑temperature session, ensuring full saturation without dry spots.
- **Quality tip**: The summary emphasizes that *"fully saturate fiberglass cloth; avoid dry spots"* as essential for long‑term durability.
- **Integration with epoxy**: Works in conjunction with [[entities/epoxy-resin]]; proper curing management is crucial to prevent issues like amine blush, as detailed in [[concepts/epoxy-curing-management]].

### Related concepts
- The use of fiberglass cloth aligns with best practices for [[concepts/waterproofing-and-seam-sealing]].
- It is a recommended step in the [[concepts/stitch-and-glue-construction]] workflow for building forgiving, lake‑ready kayaks.

### Practical notes
- Ensure the cloth lies flat and is fully wetted before the epoxy cures.
- After curing, lightly scuff the surface before applying additional coats or finishes.
- Store cloth in a dry environment to maintain its strength.
