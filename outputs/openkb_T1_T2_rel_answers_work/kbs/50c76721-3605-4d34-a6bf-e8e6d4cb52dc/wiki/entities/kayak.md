# Kayak

A kayak is a narrow, lightweight, human‑powered watercraft designed for paddling or pedaling, commonly used on lakes and calm rivers. It is popular among DIY builders because it can be constructed with relatively simple techniques while still delivering a durable, safe vessel.

## Typical Construction Methods
- **Stitch‑and‑glue construction** – preferred for first‑time builders due to its simplicity, strength, and forgiving nature. See [[concepts/stitch-and-glue-construction]].
- Skin‑on‑frame and strip‑planking are alternatives but require more skill and time.

## Guidance from Session 05 (2025‑05‑24)
The session advises focusing on hull shape, material selection, and structural stress handling before adding features. It emphasizes the importance of:
- Sealing every exposed plywood edge and applying smooth epoxy fillets to ensure waterproofing and seam strength – see [[concepts/waterproofing-and-seam-sealing]].
- Using fully saturated fiberglass cloth to reinforce seams. The recommended materials are [[entities/epoxy-resin]] and [[entities/fiberglass-cloth]].
- Managing epoxy work (fillets, glass wet‑out, fill coats) in steady or slowly rising temperature windows to avoid amine blush. For curing best practices, refer to [[concepts/epoxy-curing-management]].

## Material Considerations
- **Epoxy resin** – provides the primary bonding and sealing agent; must be applied in proper temperature and humidity conditions.
- **Fiberglass cloth** – used to reinforce fillets and hull panels, ensuring durability under repeated stress.

## Waterproofing & Seam Sealing
Key steps highlighted:
1. Seal all plywood edges before assembly.
2. Apply continuous, smooth epoxy fillets before glassing.
3. Fully wet‑out the fiberglass cloth, avoiding dry spots.

## Epoxy Curing Management
- Schedule epoxy work during periods of stable or rising temperature (early evening or mid‑morning).
- If amine blush appears, wash it off with warm water, scrub, rinse, dry, and lightly scuff before the next coat.
