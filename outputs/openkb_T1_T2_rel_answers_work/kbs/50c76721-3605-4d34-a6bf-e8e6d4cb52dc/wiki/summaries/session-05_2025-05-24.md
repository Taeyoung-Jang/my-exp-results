# Summary of Session 5 (2025‑05‑24)

## Core focus
- Choosing a build method for a first‑time human‑powered **[[entities/kayak]]** → recommendation: **[[concepts/stitch-and-glue-construction]]** for simplicity, durability, and forgiving construction.  
- Early priorities: hull shape, material selection, structural stress handling, and ergonomics.

## Construction method comparison
- **[[concepts/stitch-and-glue-construction]]**: easy, strong, good for lake use.  
- Skin‑on‑frame: lighter but requires more skill.  
- Strip planking: aesthetically pleasing but time‑intensive.

## Critical waterproofing & seam‑sealing steps
- Seal every exposed plywood edge.  
- Apply smooth, continuous epoxy fillets before glassing; use **[[entities/epoxy-resin]]**.  
- Fully saturate **[[entities/fiberglass-cloth]]**; avoid dry spots.

## High‑risk mistakes & recommended order
1. Fair panels and tack before mixing epoxy.  
2. Shape and cure fillets; let cure fully → sand lightly.  
3. Wet‑out fiberglass in a clean, stable‑temperature session.  
4. After cure, add reinforcement tapes, fill coats, final sanding, and finish.

## Epoxy scheduling in a temperature‑fluctuating garage
- Perform epoxy work during periods of steady or slowly rising temperature (early evening or mid‑morning). See **[[concepts/epoxy-curing-management]]**.  
- Keep humidity low to prevent amine blush (plain‑text).  
- If blush appears: wash with warm water, scrub with a soft pad, rinse, dry, then scuff before the next coat.

## Quick blush remediation
- Warm‑water wash + soft pad, rinse, dry, scuff → restores bond.

## Additional design considerations
- Ergonomic seating and balanced paddle/pedal position.  
- Stable hull geometry for forgiving handling.

## Takeaway
Follow a sequential curing process, maintain clean surfaces, and schedule epoxy work around stable temperatures to avoid rework and build a safe, durable, enjoyable kayak.
