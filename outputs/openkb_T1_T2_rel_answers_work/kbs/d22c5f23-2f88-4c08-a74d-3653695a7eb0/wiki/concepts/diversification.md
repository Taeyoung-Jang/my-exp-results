# Diversification

**Description:** Spreading investments across many assets to reduce risk.

---

## Overview
Diversification is the practice of allocating capital among a wide variety of investment vehicles, asset classes, sectors, and geographic regions. By not putting all of your money into a single security or a narrow set of holdings, you lower the impact that any one poor‑performing investment can have on the overall portfolio.

## Why it matters for beginners
In the conversation from [[summaries/session-08_2021-06-15]], diversification is presented as the first concrete step for novice investors. It allows a new investor to:
- Gain exposure to the broader market without having to pick individual winners.
- Reduce volatility compared to holding a single stock or a concentrated group.
- Build a solid foundation that can be managed with low effort, especially when using broad market index funds or ETFs.

## Common vehicles for diversification
- **Broad market index funds / ETFs** – e.g., total‑stock‑market or total‑world‑stock funds that hold hundreds or thousands of securities in a single purchase.
- **Robo‑advisors** – automated platforms that construct diversified portfolios using a mix of stock and bond ETFs based on your risk tolerance.
- **Bond ETFs** – add a non‑equity component to smooth returns and further spread risk.

## How it connects to other concepts
- [[concepts/risk-tolerance]] – The degree of diversification you choose (e.g., more bonds vs. stocks) should align with how much risk you are comfortable taking.
- [[concepts/rebalancing]] – Even a diversified portfolio can drift from its target mix over time; periodic rebalancing restores the intended allocation.
- [[concepts/scoring-rubric]] – A personal risk‑scoring rubric can help decide how aggressive or conservative your diversification guardrails should be.

## Using a scoring rubric to set diversification guardrails
The discussion in [[summaries/session-32_2023-06-15]] introduces a 0–5 scoring rubric across seven factors (time horizon, liquidity, drawdown tolerance, concentration, income stability, emergency fund, behavior risk). Summing the scores (max 35) yields posture bands:
- **Conservative:** 0–14
- **Moderate:** 15–24
- **Aggressive:** 25–35

These posture bands can guide the size of each diversification bucket:
1. **Speculative single names** – 0–10 % of total assets.
2. **Sector / theme funds** – 10–30 % of total assets.
3. **Broad index / value core** – 60–90 % of total assets.

The principle is to keep a stable core that forms the bulk of the portfolio while allowing a capped slice for higher‑risk, high‑potential positions. The rubric helps ensure the chosen guardrails match the investor’s overall risk posture.

## Practical steps for a starter portfolio
1. **Choose a core diversified vehicle** such as a total market index ETF.
2. **Add a complementary bond ETF** for stability.
3. **Apply the scoring rubric** to determine your risk posture and set appropriate guardrails for speculative and thematic exposure.
4. **Set automatic contributions** to keep the portfolio growing without manual effort.
5. **Monitor and rebalance** (see [[concepts/rebalancing]]) semi‑annually or when any bucket drifts more than 5–10 percentage points from its target range.
