# Scoring Rubric

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A structured 0–5 rating system for key personal risk dimensions that aggregates into a total score indicating a conservative, moderate, or aggressive investment posture.

---

## Overview
The Scoring Rubric is a practical tool introduced in [[summaries/session-32_2023-06-15]] to help investors self‑assess their capacity for risk. By rating seven categories on a simple 0–5 scale, users can translate qualitative traits (such as drawdown tolerance or income stability) into a quantitative total score. That total score is then mapped to broad risk postures, which in turn guide asset‑allocation decisions and guardrails.

---

## Scoring Categories
| Category | Score 0 anchor | Score 5 anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Time horizon** | Need funds within 1–2 years | No need for funds for 10+ years |
| **Liquidity needs** | May need access soon | No foreseeable need for access |
| **Drawdown tolerance** | Panic‑sell on market drops | Calmly endure declines |
| **Concentration risk** | Portfolio dominated by few positions | Broadly spread holdings |
| **Income stability** | Irregular or uncertain income | Stable, predictable income |
| **Emergency fund** | < 1–2 months of expenses | 6+ months of expenses |
| **Behavior risk** | Frequent impulsive changes | Rare impulsive changes |

Each category is scored from 0 (least suited for high risk) to 5 (most suited). The maximum possible total is **35**.

---

## Total‑Score Bands
| Posture | Total score |
|---|---|
| **Conservative** | 0–14 |
| **Moderate** | 15–24 |
| **Aggressive** | 25–35 |

These bands are illustrative; users can adjust thresholds to match their own interpretation of risk.

---

## How It Connects to Other Concepts
- The rubric directly informs a person's **[[concepts/risk-tolerance]]** assessment by quantifying the factors that constitute tolerance.
- It supports decisions about **[[concepts/diversification]]** guardrails, helping decide how much of the portfolio can safely sit in speculative positions.
- After establishing a posture, investors may apply **[[concepts/rebalancing]]** rules (e.g., semi‑annual checks) to keep allocations within the defined ranges.
- Understanding one's posture also feeds into choosing an appropriate **[[concepts/investment-styles]]** blend of growth vs. value assets.

---

## Practical Use Steps
1. **Self‑Score** each of the seven categories using the anchors provided.
2. **Sum** the scores to obtain a total.
3. **Map** the total to a posture band (conservative, moderate, aggressive).
4. **Apply** the posture to set allocation guardrails (e.g., speculative single names 0–10%, sector/theme 10–30%, core 60–90%).
5. **Monitor** with a semi‑annual **[[concepts/rebalancing]]** calendar and a drift trigger of 5–10 %.

---

## Example
An investor who scores 4 on time horizon, 3 on liquidity, 2 on drawdown tolerance, 3 on concentration, 4 on income stability, 5 on emergency fund, and 2 on behavior risk gets a total of **23**, landing in the **moderate** band. This suggests a balanced mix of growth and value, with diversification guardrails that limit speculative exposure while maintaining a solid core.
