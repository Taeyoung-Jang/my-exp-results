# Investment Styles

Investment styles describe broad ways investors allocate capital based on their expectations for company performance and their own personal **risk profile**. The two most common styles discussed in the source material are **growth** and **value**:

- **Growth**: Targets companies expected to deliver earnings and revenue gains that outpace the market. These stocks often trade at higher price‑to‑earnings ratios and can experience rapid price movements. Growth investing aligns with investors who have a longer time horizon, can tolerate higher volatility, and seek higher upside potential.
- **Value**: Focuses on companies that appear undervalued relative to fundamentals such as earnings, book value, or cash flow. Value stocks tend to be more stable, generate steady dividends, and experience less extreme price swings. This style fits investors who prioritize capital preservation, have a lower drawdown tolerance, or need more predictable returns.

### How Styles Relate to Investor Temperament
The conversation in [[summaries/session-32_2023-06-15]] emphasizes that the “right” style depends on several personal dimensions:

1. **Time Horizon** – Longer horizons support higher‑risk growth positions; short horizons favor value or core index exposure.
2. **Liquidity Needs** – If funds may be needed soon, a conservative, liquid allocation is prudent.
3. **Drawdown Tolerance** – Past behavior during market drops signals whether an investor can stay the course with volatile growth stocks.
4. **Concentration & Income Stability** – A diversified blend reduces the impact of any single speculative holding.
5. **Behavioral Risk** – Frequent impulsive changes suggest a more conservative stance.

These dimensions map directly onto the [[concepts/scoring-rubric]] used to generate a total risk score, which then suggests a **conservative**, **moderate**, or **aggressive** posture.

### Connecting Styles with Portfolio Construction
While the style choice guides security selection, the overall portfolio still needs a **core** that provides stability. The recommended approach uses three buckets:

- **Speculative single names** (growth‑heavy, high‑volatility) – limited to a modest percentage.
- **Sector/theme funds** – provide targeted growth exposure while still being diversified.
- **Broad index/value core** – the largest slice, delivering steady market returns.

This structure embodies the principle of [[concepts/diversification]] and ensures that growth‑focused bets do not dominate overall results.

### Ongoing Management
Because market conditions and personal circumstances evolve, the portfolio should be reviewed regularly. The suggested **rebalancing** cadence is twice per year, with an additional trigger when any bucket drifts more than 5‑10 percentage points from its target range. This aligns with the [[concepts/rebalancing]] concept and helps keep the chosen investment style consistent with the investor’s evolving risk profile.
