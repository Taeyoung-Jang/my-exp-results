**Assistant:** I’d start by getting a sense of who the person is as an investor, because the “right” style really depends on their temperament and timeline. The first things I’d ask are pretty simple: how long they plan to invest before needing the money, how steady their income is, how they typically react when markets drop, and what they’re hoping this portfolio will do for them. That alone usually reveals whether they’ll feel more at home with steadier, cheaper “value” companies or faster‑growing, more volatile names.

The big trade‑off between growth and value is comfort versus potential. Growth stocks can move quickly, both up and down, and they demand patience because their prices already assume strong future results. Value stocks tend to be more grounded in current earnings, so they don’t swing as wildly, but they may not deliver the same explosive upside.

Risk tolerance ties all this together. Someone with a long timeline and the stomach for volatility can lean more toward growth, while someone who loses sleep over sharp declines is better served by a steadier mix. In practice, most people end up with a blend, adjusting the balance so they can stay invested without constantly second‑guessing themselves.

The goal is to match the portfolio to the person, not the other way around.
