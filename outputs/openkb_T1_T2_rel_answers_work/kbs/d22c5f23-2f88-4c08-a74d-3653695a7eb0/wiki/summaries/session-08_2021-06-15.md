# Summary of session-08_2021-06-15

**Main contribution:** Provides a step‑by‑step framework for newcomers to start investing with low maintenance, covering goal setting, vehicle choice, allocation, rebalancing, and emotional considerations.

## Key concepts and ideas

- **Define your investment goal and time horizon** – Clarify whether you’re saving for short‑term or long‑term needs; this filters suitable investments.
- **Prioritize diversification** – Use broad market vehicles that spread risk across many securities. Common choices are total‑market index funds/ETFs or a robo‑advisor that builds a diversified mix for you. [[concepts/diversification]]
- **Robo‑advisor vs DIY ETFs**
  - *Robo‑advisor*: Handles asset‑allocation, automatic rebalancing, and optional tax‑loss harvesting; convenient but carries a small management fee.
  - *DIY ETFs*: Same underlying assets with lower ongoing costs; you decide the stock/bond split and rebalance yourself.
- **Starter allocation templates**
  - *Moderate risk*: ~66 % stocks, 34 % bonds (e.g., total‑stock market ETF + broad bond ETF).
  - *More conservative*: 50 %‑60 % stocks and the remainder bonds (e.g., 50/50 or 60/40) for those uneasy with volatility.
  - Align the split with your **risk tolerance** and how you react to market swings. [[concepts/risk-tolerance]]
- **Rebalancing strategy** – Keep it simple: rebalance once or twice a year (e.g., every January and July) or when the portfolio drifts noticeably from the target allocation. This avoids constant monitoring while maintaining the intended risk level. [[concepts/rebalancing]]
- **Emotional/behavioral guidance**
  - Choose a more conservative mix if a 20 % market drop would cause anxiety; move toward a higher stock share as you become comfortable with normal market fluctuations.
  - Signs it’s time to increase stock exposure: reduced anxiety during dips, less frequent checking, confidence in long‑term timeline.

## Practical take‑aways
1. **Set clear goals and horizon** → determines appropriate asset class mix.
2. **Start with a diversified core** → broad index ETF or robo‑advisor.
3. **Pick an allocation** that matches your comfort level (e.g., 50/50, 60/40, or 2/3‑1/3).
4. **Automate contributions** and let the portfolio grow.
5. **Rebalance annually or when drift exceeds a set threshold**. [[concepts/rebalancing]]
6. **Gradually shift toward higher stock exposure** as you grow comfortable with market volatility.
