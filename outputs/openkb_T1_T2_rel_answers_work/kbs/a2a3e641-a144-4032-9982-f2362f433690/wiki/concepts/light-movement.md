# Light Movement

**Light Movement** refers to short, gentle physical actions performed shortly after waking to help the body transition from sleep to alertness. In the context of the morning routine outlined in [[summaries/session-02_2025-06-15]], light movement includes activities such as a slow walk around the home, simple stretches, or any minimal‑effort motions that increase circulation without feeling like a workout.

## Key Characteristics
- **Low effort:** No equipment or intense exertion is required; the goal is to stir the body gently.
- **Time‑bounded:** Typically occupies the 5‑ to 10‑minute window after the initial stretch and hydration phase.
- **Phone‑free:** Per the [[concepts/phone-free-morning]] rule, these movements are done without checking the phone, helping maintain mental calm.
- **Integrative:** Serves as a bridge between [[concepts/gentle-anchors]] (stretch & breath) and the later [[concepts/quiet-pause]] (mindful sitting).

## Practical Examples
- Marching in place for one minute.
- Shoulder rolls and neck stretches.
- A brief hallway stroll, focusing on the sensation of each step.
- Light shoulder‑blade squeezes or ankle circles.

## Benefits Highlighted in the Source Document
- **Physical awakening:** Increases blood flow and reduces grogginess.
- **Mental cue:** Signals the start of the day’s routine, reinforcing the intention‑setting practice [[concepts/intention-setting]].
- **Flexibility:** Can be shortened for a 30‑minute rushed schedule, still providing a grounding effect.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- Works alongside [[concepts/gentle-anchors]] (initial stretch & breath) to create a seamless transition.
- Precedes the [[concepts/quiet-pause]] where the mind settles.
- Complements [[concepts/night-before-preparation]] by ensuring the body is ready to enjoy a calm, phone‑free breakfast.
