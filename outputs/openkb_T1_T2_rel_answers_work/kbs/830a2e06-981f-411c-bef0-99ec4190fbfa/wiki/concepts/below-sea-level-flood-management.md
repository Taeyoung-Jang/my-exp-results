## Below‑Sea‑Level Flood Management

Below‑sea‑level flood management refers to the set of planning, engineering, and policy measures required to protect communities that sit below mean sea level from chronic and event‑driven flooding.  It combines robust **pump and levee systems**, continuous **maintenance and inspection regimes**, and long‑term **investment planning** to ensure resilience against rising water levels and storm surges.

### Core Elements
- **Continuous Pump Operation** – Reliable, regularly tested pumps with backup power sources are essential.  Maintenance schedules, recent upgrade records, and condition summaries should be publicly available.
- **Levee and Barrier Integrity** – Levees, sea walls, and flood barriers need periodic inspection (often overseen by agencies such as the [[entities/us-army-corps-of-engineers]] or [[entities/fema]]).  Reinforcement projects must be documented and funded.
- **Integrated Planning Documents** – A current [[entities/stormwater-master-plan]], [[entities/capital-improvement-plan]], and flood‑risk studies provide the roadmap for upgrades and replacements.
- **Public Transparency** – Dashboards or reports from the [[entities/city-public-works-department]] and updates from the [[entities/city-council]] give residents access to the system’s status and funding.

### Connection to Flood‑Preparedness Indicators
The approach aligns with broader [[concepts/flood-preparedness-indicators]] such as:
- Graded streets and adequate drainage
- Green spaces that act as overflow basins
- Elevated structures and raised utilities
These indicators are especially critical when the baseline elevation is already below sea level.

### Maintenance Checklist
For evaluating a below‑sea‑level area, the following items from the [[concepts/pump-levy-maintenance-checklist]] are most relevant:
1. Latest **pump station condition summary** (age, last upgrade, testing frequency).
2. **Levee inspection reports** (USACE/FEMA) and any identified deficiencies.
3. Existence of a **funded capital‑improvement plan** covering pump and levee upgrades.
4. Documentation of **backup power** capabilities and recent drills.
5. Records of system performance during recent high‑water events.

### Red Flags
- Out‑of‑date or missing inspection reports.
- Predominance of emergency repairs without a clear, funded upgrade path.
- Lack of publicly available dashboards or condition summaries.
- No documented multi‑year budgeting for pump or levee replacement.
