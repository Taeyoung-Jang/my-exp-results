# High‑Elevation Baking Adjustments

**Definition**: Practical tweaks to ingredient ratios, oven temperature, and proofing timing that compensate for lower air pressure and drier air when baking at elevations around 5,000–7,000 ft.

---

## Why Baking Changes at Altitude

- **Lower air pressure** reduces the boiling point of water and speeds the rise of leavened goods.  Baked items can expand too quickly and then collapse.
- **Dry air** draws moisture from flour and dough, making mixtures feel stiffer and causing finished products to stale faster.

These two forces require separate but complementary adjustments.

---

## Core Adjustments (Rule‑of‑Thumbs)

| Baking Item | Ingredient Tweaks | Oven/Process Tweaks |
|-------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Chocolate‑chip cookies** | • Reduce sugar by 1 Tbsp (sugar weakens structure at altitude)  
• Add +1 Tbsp all‑purpose flour (helps dough hold shape) | • Raise oven temperature 10–15 °F (so the cookie sets before it spreads) |
| **Sandwich loaf (yeast bread)** | Usually keep ingredient ratios unchanged initially. If dough over‑proofs, trim yeast by ~10 %. | • Shorten each rise period by 20‑30 % (dough ferments faster). 
• Keep oven temperature as written; focus on timing first. |
| **General cakes & quick breads** | • Slightly less leavening (½‑¼ tsp less baking powder per cup)  
• Add a bit more liquid (1‑2 Tbsp) to offset drier flour | • Increase baking temperature 5‑10 °F to set structure earlier |

### Order of Operations
1. **Ingredient ratios first** for cookies and quick breads because the dough’s ability to hold shape is the primary issue.
2. **Proofing timing first** for yeasted breads; adjust the oven temperature only if the crumb is still too gummy or the loaf collapses.
3. **Temperature adjustments** are supporting tweaks, applied after the above steps if needed.

---

## Practical Checklist
- Measure flour by weight to avoid over‑adding (dry air can make a cup seem lighter).
- Use a kitchen thermometer; aim for the **higher end** of the recipe’s temperature range.
- For doughs that feel dry, add water a teaspoon at a time.
- Chill cookie dough 15‑30 min before baking to limit spread.
- Observe rise: when the dough has roughly doubled (instead of the sea‑level target), proceed to shaping.
