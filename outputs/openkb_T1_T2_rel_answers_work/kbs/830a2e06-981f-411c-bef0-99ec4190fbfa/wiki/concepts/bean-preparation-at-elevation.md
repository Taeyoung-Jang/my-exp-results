# Bean Preparation at Elevation

Cooking dried legumes at elevations of about 5,000–7,000 ft requires compensating for the lower boiling point of water and the drier air.  The following guidelines synthesize the advice from [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]] and connect to broader altitude cooking principles.

## Core Challenges
- **Lower boiling temperature** (~95 °C) means beans cook more slowly.
- **Reduced atmospheric pressure** lessens heat transfer, extending simmer times.
- **Dry air** can cause skins to stay leathery longer.

## Recommended Workflow
1. **Overnight soak**
   - Place beans (e.g., [[entities/black-beans]] or [[entities/chickpeas]]) in a large bowl of cool water.
   - Soak 8–12 hours; this softens skins more uniformly than a quick‑heat soak.
2. **Drain & rinse**
   - Discard the soak water; use fresh, cold water for cooking.
3. **Gentle simmer with lid**
   - Add beans to a pot, cover with fresh water (about 2–3 inches above beans).
   - Bring to a low simmer; keep the lid **fully on** to retain heat.  If foam threatens to boil over, crack the lid briefly, then reseal.
4. **Extended cooking time**
   - **Black beans:** 1¼–1½ hours after soak.
   - **Chickpeas:** around 2 hours, possibly a bit longer for older stock.
5. **Optional baking‑soda aid**
   - If beans remain firm after the expected time, add a pinch of [[entities/baking-soda]]; this raises pH and softens skins.
   - Use sparingly, as excess can produce a soapy flavor.

## When a Pressure Cooker Is Available
A [[entities/pressure-cooker]] can cut cooking time dramatically (≈30 % less), but the steps above work reliably without it.

## Interaction with Other Altitude Concepts
- The need for longer boils is a direct outcome of the phenomena described in [[concepts/altitude-effects-on-cooking]].
- While primarily a cooking issue, the longer cooking times echo the broader adjustments covered in [[concepts/high-elevation-baking-adjustments]], where ingredient ratios and oven temperatures are tweaked to compensate for low pressure.

## Practical Tips
- **Check texture**: beans are done when they are tender throughout, not just soft on the outside.
- **Avoid over‑cooking**: once tender, remove from heat to prevent mushiness, especially if baking‑soda was used.
- **Season late**: add salt or acidic ingredients (tomatoes, vinegar) near the end of cooking; early addition can harden skins.
