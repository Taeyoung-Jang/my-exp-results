# Flood Preparedness Indicators

**Definition**: Observable physical and administrative signs that a community is designed or maintained to effectively manage heavy rainfall and reduce flood risk.

## Core Indicators

1. **Proper grading and slope** of streets and yards so water naturally runs off rather than pooling.  
2. **Clear, modern storm‑drain infrastructure** with visible maintenance; higher curbs or dedicated drainage cuts that direct water away.  
3. **Green spaces or low‑lying parks** intentionally positioned to serve as temporary catch‑basins during storms.  
4. **Elevated buildings and raised utility boxes** that keep critical services above potential flood levels.  
5. **Historical performance** – quick drainage after past storms and minimal resident complaints about standing water.
6. **Public documentation and planning** – availability of a storm‑water master plan, capital‑improvement plan, or similar documents showing ongoing upgrades and funding.

## How to Verify These Indicators

- Walk the neighborhood and look for sloped streets, raised curbs, and well‑kept storm drains.  
- Identify parks or open areas that sit lower than surrounding streets – these often act as designed flood basins.  
- Check for elevated home designs and raised electrical/mechanical boxes.  
- Review local flood history: fast post‑storm drainage and low flood‑related complaints are positive signals.
- Search municipal websites for the latest **[[entities/capital-improvement-plan]]**, **[[entities/stormwater-master-plan]]**, and any recent **[[entities/city-public-works-department]]** reports that detail drainage upgrades or maintenance schedules.
