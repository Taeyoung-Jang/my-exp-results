# Stormwater Master Plan

**Type:** work

## Overview
The Stormwater Master Plan is a comprehensive municipal document that maps out how a city manages rainfall, surface runoff, and flood risk. It details existing drainage infrastructure, identifies vulnerable areas, sets priorities for upgrades (including pumps and levees), and outlines funding mechanisms and timelines for future projects.

## Key Points from [[summaries/session-05_2022-06-15]]
- The plan is one of the core public documents recommended for evaluating flood‑preparedness, alongside the Capital‑Improvement Plan and annual public‑works reports.
- It provides insight into:
  - Current condition of pump stations and levee segments.
  - Scheduled upgrades and long‑term modernization road‑maps.
  - Integration with climate‑adaptation studies and resilience planning.
- For neighborhoods **below sea level**, the Stormwater Master Plan often includes specialized sections on continuous pump operation, backup power, and sea‑level rise scenarios.

## Related Entities
- [[entities/city-public-works-department]] – typically the author and maintainer of the Stormwater Master Plan.
- [[entities/city-council]] – approves funding and policy directives referenced in the plan.
- [[entities/capital-improvement-plan]] – works in concert with the Stormwater Master Plan to finance infrastructure projects.
- [[entities/fema]] and [[entities/fema-flood-zone-maps]] – provide federal flood‑risk data that feed into the plan’s risk assessments.
- [[entities/us-army-corps-of-engineers]] – may conduct levee inspections and contribute technical guidance referenced in the plan.

## Usage
Stakeholders (homebuyers, planners, engineers) use the Stormwater Master Plan to:
1. Verify that a city has an up‑to‑date, publicly accessible roadmap for flood mitigation.
2. Check that pump stations and levees are scheduled for regular inspection and replacement.
3. Identify any gaps or red flags, such as missing funding allocations or outdated project timelines.
