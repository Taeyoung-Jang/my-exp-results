# Pressure Cooker

A **pressure cooker** is a sealed pot that traps steam, raising the internal pressure and therefore the boiling point of water. This allows foods to cook faster and at higher temperatures than conventional boiling.

## Relevance in Session 33 (2024‑06‑15)
- The user noted they **do not have a pressure cooker** while trying to prepare dried beans at an elevation of 5,000–7,000 ft.
- The assistant explained that a pressure cooker would **counteract the altitude‑induced lower boiling point**, dramatically reducing the simmer time needed for beans such as [[entities/black-beans]] and [[entities/chickpeas]].
- Without a pressure cooker, the recommended method is an **overnight soak** followed by a **gentle, fully‑covered simmer**, which can take 1¼–2 hours after soaking.
