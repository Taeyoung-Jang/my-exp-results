# Sandwich Loaf

A simple, yeast‑leavened bread typically baked in a rectangular loaf pan and sliced for sandwiches.  In the context of high‑elevation cooking, the **sandwich loaf** requires specific procedural tweaks rather than major ingredient changes.

## Key facts from [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]]
- **Altitude range discussed:** 5,000–7,000 ft (≈1,500–2,100 m).
- **Primary adjustment:** Shorten proofing/rise times because dough ferments faster at lower air pressure.
- **Ingredient changes:** Generally none; if the dough rises too quickly, bakers may trim the yeast amount modestly.
- **Oven temperature:** Typically left unchanged; temperature tweaks are a secondary, supportive step.
- **Related baking concept:** See [[concepts/high-elevation-baking-adjustments]] for broader guidance on altitude‑related baking.

## Practical tips for the sandwich loaf at altitude
1. **Watch the rise:** Reduce each proofing interval by roughly 20‑30 % compared to sea‑level recipes.
2. **Check dough strength:** If the dough balloons excessively, decrease the yeast quantity slightly (e.g., 10 % less).
3. **Bake as usual:** Keep the usual oven temperature; only adjust if you notice under‑baked interiors.
