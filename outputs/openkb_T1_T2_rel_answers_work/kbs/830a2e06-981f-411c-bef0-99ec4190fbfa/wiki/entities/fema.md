## FEMA

The **Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)** is a United States government agency that coordinates the response to disasters that overwhelm local and state resources. A core function of FEMA is to produce and maintain **flood zone maps** that identify areas at risk of flooding, which are widely used by homeowners, insurers, and local governments to assess flood exposure and set insurance rates.

### Relevance to Session 05 (2022‑06‑15)
- The conversation highlights that **FEMA flood zone maps** are a practical tool for evaluating a neighborhood’s flood risk: "Local insurance rates and FEMA flood zone maps can quietly tell you a lot too." [[summaries/session-05_2022-06-15]]

- **Below‑sea‑level flood management**: areas below sea level rely heavily on accurate FEMA mapping to plan pumps and levee upgrades. [[concepts/below-sea-level-flood-management]]
- **Pump‑levy‑maintenance‑checklist**: a checklist that may reference FEMA data when evaluating the adequacy of pump stations and levee systems in flood‑prone zones. [[concepts/pump-levy-maintenance-checklist]]
- **City Public Works Department** and **City Council**: local bodies that use FEMA maps to guide capital‑improvement plans and storm‑water master plans. [[entities/city-public-works-department]] [[entities/city-council]]
- **Stormwater Master Plan**: often incorporates FEMA flood zone information to prioritize drainage projects. [[entities/stormwater-master-plan]]
- **US Army Corps of Engineers**: collaborates with FEMA on large‑scale flood control projects and levee assessments. [[entities/us-army-corps-of-engineers]]

### Key Takeaways
- FEMA provides essential flood zone mapping that underpins risk assessment and planning for flood‑resilient infrastructure.
- Residents and city officials can consult FEMA maps to gauge flood exposure, influence insurance rates, and prioritize infrastructure upgrades.
- Accurate, up‑to‑date FEMA data is a green flag indicating proactive flood management; outdated or missing FEMA information is a red flag.
