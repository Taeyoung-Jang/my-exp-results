# FEMA Flood Zone Maps

**Type:** Product

**Description:**
Official maps produced by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) that delineate flood risk zones for properties and communities. They are used by homeowners, insurers, and local governments to assess flood exposure and determine insurance requirements.

**Key Points from Session 05 (2022‑06‑15):**
- The conversation highlights FEMA Flood Zone Maps as a practical tool for evaluating a neighborhood’s flood risk.  Residents can consult these maps to see whether a property lies in a high‑risk (e.g., Zone AE) or moderate‑risk area. [[summaries/session-05_2022-06-15]]
- The maps complement other indicators of flood preparedness, such as grading, storm‑drain condition, and the presence of pumps or levees.  They help inform the checklist for assessing infrastructure reliability. [[concepts/flood-preparedness-indicators]]
- In below‑sea‑level communities, FEMA Flood Zone Maps are especially important for understanding the baseline risk that must be mitigated by pumps, levees, and long‑term planning. [[concepts/below-sea-level-flood-management]]

**Use Cases:**
1. **Homebuyer Due Diligence:** Check the map to determine if a property is in a designated flood zone before purchasing.
2. **Insurance Assessment:** Insurance companies use the maps to set rates and required coverage levels.
3. **Municipal Planning:** City planners reference the maps when developing or updating the [[entities/stormwater-master-plan]] and [[entities/capital-improvement-plan]].
4. **Infrastructure Prioritization:** Areas shown in high‑risk zones may be prioritized for pump upgrades or levee reinforcement, as outlined in the [[concepts/pump-levy-maintenance-checklist]].

**Why It Matters:**
Understanding FEMA Flood Zone Maps enables residents and officials to make informed decisions about flood mitigation, insurance, and long‑term resilience, especially in vulnerable, low‑lying neighborhoods.
