**User:** I’m doing a little life-admin cleanup and realized I’ve never really thought through who should be listed as an emergency contact in different situations (doctor’s office, workplace, travel, etc.). What makes a “good” emergency contact, and how do you decide who to ask without making it awkward?

**Assistant:** A good emergency contact is basically someone who can be reached quickly, will actually pick up the phone, and can make calm, practical decisions if something unexpected happens. You don’t need someone who lives near you, but you do want someone who knows you well enough to speak on your behalf and who won’t freeze in a stressful moment.

Most people choose a partner, close friend, or family member. If those aren’t perfect fits, someone you trust and communicate with regularly works just fine. For workplaces, it’s usually best to list someone who can handle logistics or health information; for medical offices, someone who knows your general health history; for travel, someone who can help coordinate things from afar if something goes wrong.

As for the awkwardness, a simple, direct ask usually avoids that. Something like, “Hey, I’m updating some forms—would you be okay being my emergency contact?” Most people take it as a compliment that you trust them. If they hesitate, it’s often about availability rather than feelings, and you can just thank them and choose someone else.

You don’t need one perfect person for all situations either. It’s totally normal to use different people depending on what the form is for.
