# Minimum vs. Nice‑to‑Have Emergency Contact Information

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** This concept outlines the core (minimum) data that an emergency contact needs to act effectively, and the additional (nice‑to‑have) details that can improve response but are not strictly required.

---

## Minimum Information
The absolute essentials that should be shared, typically in a short follow‑up text:
- **Allergies** (any medication or food allergies that could affect emergency treatment).
- **Current Medications** that an ER team would need to know.
- **Primary Care Provider or Preferred Hospital/Clinic** – the location where you normally receive care.

These three points are enough for a contact to convey critical health facts to a medical professional or workplace safety officer.

## Nice‑to‑Have Information
Optional details that can streamline coordination but are not required for immediate emergency response:
- **Doctor’s Phone Number** for direct clarification.
- **Secondary Emergency Contact** in case the primary contact is unreachable.
- **Location of ID/Insurance Card** or a brief note on where important documents are kept.
- **Family Contact Path** – a quick reference to who else should be notified.

Providing these items can reduce back‑and‑forth calls and speed up decision‑making, especially in complex situations like travel disruptions or multi‑party workplace incidents.

## Privacy Considerations
When sharing information via text, avoid disclosing highly sensitive data that could be misused if the phone is compromised.  Do **not** include:
- Full insurance policy numbers.
- Detailed medical history or mental‑health diagnoses.
- Any identifiers that enable identity theft (e.g., Social Security number).

Stick to the minimal set for texting; share richer details verbally or through a secure channel if needed.
