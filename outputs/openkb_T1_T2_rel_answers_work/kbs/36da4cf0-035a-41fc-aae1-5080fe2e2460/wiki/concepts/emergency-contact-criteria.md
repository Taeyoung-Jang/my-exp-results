# Criteria for a Good Emergency Contact

A good emergency contact should meet several practical and interpersonal criteria. These points are distilled from the discussion in [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-13]] and can guide you in choosing the most suitable person for different contexts (work, medical, travel).

## Core Criteria
1. **Quick Reachability** – The person can be contacted promptly, especially during odd hours. They should reliably answer calls or messages.
2. **Reliability & Availability** – They are willing and able to respond without hesitation and have a track record of being dependable.
3. **Calm Decision‑Making** – In a stressful situation, they can stay composed and make practical decisions (e.g., communicating with medical staff or coordinating logistics).
4. **Familiarity with You** – They know you well enough to convey essential personal and health information accurately.
5. **Ability to Communicate with Professionals** – They can speak clearly with doctors, employers, or travel authorities, providing concise details when needed.
6. **No Requirement for Proximity** – While not mandatory, being in a similar time zone can help, but the primary factor is availability rather than geographic closeness.

## Context‑Specific Nuances
- **Workplace** – Ideal contacts can handle logistics, understand health information, and act as a liaison for the employer.
- **Medical Settings** – Prefer someone aware of your general health history, allergies, and medication basics.
- **Travel** – Choose someone who can coordinate from afar, such as contacting family or handling airline notifications.
