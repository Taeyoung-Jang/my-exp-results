# Weekend Shelf Space

**Definition** – A mental model that treats the limited amount of weekend time and energy as a fixed “shelf” where each social activity occupies a portion of space. By visualising the weekend this way, couples can decide upfront how much they want to fill the shelf and then evaluate each invitation against the remaining capacity.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-15]])
- **Pre‑allocate a budget**: Before any invites arrive, agree on an overall energy/time budget for the weekend.
- **Two quick checks** for each invitation:
  1. *Do I actually want to do it?*
  2. *Does it fit the shared plan and remaining shelf space?*
- **Planning horizon**: Aim to schedule 1–2 weeks ahead; avoid firm commitments beyond four weeks unless the event is special.
- **Cap on plans**: Use the mini‑policy (normal, low‑energy, high‑energy caps) to set a hard limit on how many “plans” can occupy the shelf.

## Relationship Integration
The model works hand‑in‑hand with the [[concepts/partner-check-sync]] routine, where partners sync early in the week to decide the weekend vibe (low, normal, high energy). This shared decision defines the total shelf capacity for that weekend.

## Connection to Energy Management
The shelf‑space metaphor complements [[concepts/energy-budget-planning]]: the total space represents the couple’s combined energy budget, and each plan is a chunk of that budget. Adjust the number of slots based on perceived energy levels (low‑energy vs. high‑energy weekends).

## Practical Use
1. **Weekly Sync** – Discuss and set the weekend vibe; allocate joint and solo slots.
2. **Invite Evaluation** – Apply the two checks; if a plan fits, place it on the shelf; if not, use a polite decline template.
3. **Last‑Minute Options** – Treat as optional “bonus” items that only fit if the shelf still has free space.

## Benefits
- Prevents over‑booking and the associated stress.
- Makes decision‑making faster by providing a clear visual/mental cue.
- Encourages intentional, joyful social engagement rather than habit‑driven filler activities.
