# Energy Budget Planning

**Definition**

Energy Budget Planning is a decision‑making framework that helps individuals (or couples) allocate their limited personal energy and time to social activities by establishing clear caps, checking fit, and using a short‑term planning horizon.

**Core Elements**

- **Energy as a resource** – Treat your weekend time like a finite *shelf space* for activities, the same idea described in [[concepts/weekend-shelf-space]].
- **Two quick questions** – For every invitation ask:
  1. *Do I really want to do it?*
  2. *Does it fit within the energy budget we have set?*
- **Planning horizon** – Aim to schedule only 1–2 weeks ahead; avoid firm commitments beyond four weeks unless the event is special.
- **Plan definition** – A “plan” includes any activity that requires preparation, travel, or blocks a sizeable chunk of time (e.g., dinner out, day trip, party). Short drop‑ins count only if they disrupt the schedule.

**Typical Caps (from the source guidance)**

| Weekend vibe | Max number of plans | Details |
|--------------|--------------------|---------|
| Low‑energy   | 1 plan max (joint or solo) | Any extra invites receive an automatic “not this weekend.” |
| Normal‑energy| 2 plans total (1 joint + 1 solo) – hard cap 3 | Joint plan counts toward the shared slot; solo plans are optional. |
| High‑energy  | 2–3 plans, with no more than 2 on a single day | Allows extra flexibility when both partners feel upbeat. |

**Last‑minute invites**

Treat them as *bonus options*. Accept only if the current cap isn’t reached and both partners feel up to it; otherwise decline without guilt.

**Couple coordination**

When the concept is used by a couple, it dovetails with the weekly sync routine described in [[concepts/partner-check-sync]]. That routine establishes the weekend vibe and the shared energy budget before invitations are evaluated.

**Practical workflow**

1. **Weekly sync** – Decide the desired weekend energy level (low, normal, high). Set the number of joint and solo slots.
2. **Incoming invitation** – Apply the two‑question check against the budget.
3. **Decision** – Accept if it fits; otherwise use a polite decline template.
4. **Re‑evaluate** – Mid‑week, adjust the budget if circumstances change (e.g., unexpected fatigue).
