# Summary of session-06_2024-06-15

**Main Idea** – A practical framework for couples to plan weekend social activities without over‑committing, including a clear “rules of thumb” mini‑policy and a set of reusable text‑message templates.

## Core Framework
- Treat weekend time like limited **[[concepts/weekend-shelf-space]]** – decide your total energy/time budget before saying yes.
- Two quick checks for each invitation: **Do I want it?** and **Does it fit the shared plan?** – these embody **[[concepts/energy-budget-planning]]**.
- Planning horizon: **1–2 weeks** ahead; avoid committing beyond **4 weeks** unless it’s a special event.

## Adaptation for a Couple
- View the relationship as a shared resource within the shelf‑space model.
- Weekly check‑in (e.g., Sunday evening) to set the weekend vibe: *low‑energy, normal, or high‑energy*. Use the **[[concepts/partner-check-sync]]** routine to establish a baseline number of joint and solo plans.
- When an invitation comes in, pause and apply the two‑question check. That way you avoid negotiating every single invitation and keep your partner out of the “excuse” role.

## Mini‑Policy (Rules of Thumb)
| Situation | Default cap | Definition of a “plan” |
|-----------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Normal‑energy weekend** | **2 plans total** (1 joint + 1 solo) – hard cap **3** | Dinner out, party, day‑trip, anything that requires preparation/travel. Quick coffee counts only if it demands a schedule shift. |
| **Low‑energy weekend** | **1 plan max** – joint *or* solo only. | Same definition; extra invites get an automatic “not this weekend.” |
| **High‑energy weekend** | **2–3 plans** – no more than 2 in a single day. | Same definition; allows more flexibility if both partners feel upbeat. |
| **Last‑minute invites** | Treated as **bonus options**; only accepted if the current cap isn’t reached and both feel up to it. |
| **Long‑range commitments** | No firm commitments beyond **4 weeks** unless the event is special/important. |

## Decision Process (Step‑by‑Step)
1. **Weekly sync** – decide the weekend vibe and allocate joint/solo slots (**[[concepts/partner-check-sync]]**).
2. When an invitation arrives, **pause** and apply the two‑question check (**[[concepts/energy-budget-planning]]**).
3. If it fits the budget, accept; otherwise, use a polite template to decline.
4. For last‑minute options, treat them as extra slots only if the cap allows.

## Text‑Message Templates
### Partner‑Check
- “That sounds fun – let me check with **[partner’s name]** and I’ll get back to you.”
- “Sounds great! Let me sync with **[partner]** so we don’t double‑book, and I’ll confirm soon.”
### Already Booked
- “I’d love to, but we’ve already got plans that weekend. Let’s find another time.”
- “I’m already booked that day, but keep me in the loop for the next round.”
### Soft No, Keep Door Open
- “Can’t swing it this time, but I’d love to join another time. Keep me posted.”
- “ I can’t make it, but let’s pick another day that works for both of us.”
### Quick “Need More Time”
- “I think that could work, but I need a little time to see how the weekend’s shaping up. I’ll circle back shortly.”
- “Let me check what we’ve got going on and get back to you.”
### Group‑Chat Specific
- “Sounds great! Let me check with **[partner]** so I don’t double‑book us, and I’ll confirm soon.”
- “I’d be into that, but we’ve already got plans that day. Keep me posted for the next round though.”
