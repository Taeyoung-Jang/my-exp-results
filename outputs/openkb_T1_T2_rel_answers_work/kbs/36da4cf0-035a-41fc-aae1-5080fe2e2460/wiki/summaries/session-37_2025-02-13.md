## Overview
This document provides practical guidance on selecting a good **emergency contact**, how to ask someone without awkwardness, and what information to share afterward. It includes concise text templates for different contexts (work, medical, travel) and distinguishes between a **minimal** and a **more complete** follow‑up message. Privacy considerations for what not to send via text are also highlighted.

## Key Concepts
- **Good emergency contact criteria** – quick reachability, reliability, and ability to make calm decisions [[concepts/emergency-contact-criteria]].
- **Information tiers** – *minimum* (allergies, meds, primary clinic) vs. *nice‑to‑have* (doctor phone, secondary contact, ID location) [[concepts/emergency-contact-info-tiers]].
- **Privacy in emergency info** – avoid sharing insurance numbers, full medical history, or any data that could be misused if the phone is compromised [[concepts/emergency-contact-privacy]].

## Recommendations
1. **Select a contact** who is reachable at odd hours, knows you well, and can stay calm.
2. **Ask via a brief text** that clearly states the purpose and offers an easy out.
3. **Provide follow‑up info** in two possible levels:
   - **Minimal (2‑3 lines)**: allergies, medication list, primary clinic/hospital.
   - **Complete**: adds preferred hospital, doctor’s phone, and a note on who to reach for family matters.
4. **Do not include** sensitive identifiers (insurance ID, full medical history, mental‑health details) in a text; share those verbally if ever needed.

## Sample Texts
### Sister – Work Context
- **Ask:**  
  > Hey, I’m updating work paperwork and need an emergency contact. Would you be okay if I listed you?
- **Minimal follow‑up:**  
  > Thanks! I have no med allergies, I’m on [meds], and my usual clinic is [clinic].
- **Complete follow‑up:**  
  > Thanks! No med allergies, I’m on [meds], my primary doctor is at [clinic], and they’d likely take me to [hospital] if needed.

### Close Friend – Medical Context
- **Ask:**  
  > Quick favor—can I list you as my emergency contact on my doctor’s forms?
- **Minimal follow‑up:**  
  > Thanks! I have no med allergies, I take [meds], and my primary doctor is [name/clinic].
- **Complete follow‑up:**  
  > Thanks! No allergies, meds: [list], primary doctor: [name] at [clinic], preferred hospital: [hospital].

### Travel Scenario (any contact)
- **Ask:**  
  > I’m finalizing travel plans and need an emergency contact for the airline. Would you be okay being that person?
- **Minimal follow‑up:**  
  > Great, just the basics: no med allergies, meds [if any], and they’ll likely ask you for my family’s contact info.
- **Complete follow‑up:**  
  > Perfect, basics are: no allergies, meds [list], primary clinic [clinic], and if anything serious they might contact my family – you’re the point person.

## Privacy Tips
- **Avoid texting:** insurance numbers, full diagnostic history, mental‑health conditions, or any data that could enable identity theft.
- **Keep it short:** only the essentials that someone would need in an urgent call.
- **Store detailed info** securely (e.g., encrypted note app) and share verbally if the situation warrants deeper details.
