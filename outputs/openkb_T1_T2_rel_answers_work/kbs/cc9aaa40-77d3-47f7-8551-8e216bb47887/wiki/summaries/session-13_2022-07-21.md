## Summary of Session 13 (2022‑07‑21)

**Topic:** Enhancing natural light perception and overall brightness in a living‑room setting without major renovations.

### Key Recommendations

1. **Mirrors**  
   - Place a large mirror **directly across from the windows** to reflect daylight back into the room.  
   - If placement is constrained, angle the mirror to still capture window light.  
   - Prefer **oversized or medium‑sized wall mirrors**; round or simple‑frame designs feel most open, while ornate frames can add visual weight.  
   - When daylight is heavily filtered (e.g., trees outside), combine a large mirror with **layered artificial lighting** for the best effect.  
   - See [[concepts/mirror-placement]].

2. **Layered Lighting Strategy**  
   - Use a mix of **floor lamp, table lamps, and existing overhead fixture**.  
   - Aim for **2700 K–3000 K** bulb colour temperature (warm‑neutral) and **800–1100 lumens per lamp**.  
   - Consistent colour temperature across fixtures creates a calm, even glow.  
   - See [[concepts/layered-lighting]].

3. **Lighting Plan for a 15 × 20 ft Living Room**  
   - **One strong floor lamp** (back corner of seating area), **two table lamps** (either side of the sofa), plus the overhead light.  
   - Target **3,000–5,000 lumens total** across all sources.  
   - See [[concepts/room-lighting-plan]].

4. **Example Layouts**  
   - **Setup A:** Tall floor lamp in the dark TV corner + table lamps on each end of the sofa.  
   - **Setup B:** Arc floor lamp beside the sofa + single table lamp opposite the sofa + compact floor/accent lamp near the TV corner.

Strategically using mirrors and a coordinated set of warm‑white lamps can transform a dim, tree‑shaded living room into a brighter, more inviting space without structural changes.
