# Mirror Placement

**Definition**: Mirror placement refers to the strategic positioning of mirrors in interior spaces to enhance perceived brightness and depth by reflecting natural and artificial light.

## Key Principles (from [[summaries/session-13_2022-07-21]])

1. **Direct Opposition to Windows**
   - Place a large mirror **directly across from a window**. This maximizes the amount of daylight reflected back into the room, creating the illusion of a larger, brighter space.
2. **Angle When Direct Placement Is Not Feasible**
   - If a wall opposite the window is unavailable, **angle the mirror** so it still catches a portion of the window’s light. Even a slight tilt can boost reflected light.
3. **Size Matters**
   - **Oversized or medium‑sized wall mirrors** have the greatest impact because they reflect more light. A floor‑to‑ceiling mirror is optional; a sizable vertical mirror is usually sufficient.
4. **Shape and Frame**
   - Simple frames (thin metal, wood, or no frame) and **round shapes** tend to feel lighter and more open. Ornate or heavy frames can add visual weight, reducing the brightening effect.
5. **Complement with Layered Lighting**
   - Mirrors work best when combined with **[[concepts/layered-lighting]]**; a large mirror reflects daylight while lamps fill in shadows caused by external obstructions like trees.

## Practical Tips
- **Clear the Window Area**: Keep window sills and surrounding furniture free of obstacles to allow maximum light absorption before reflection.
- **Height Placement**: Position the mirror so its centre aligns with the average eye level (about 57–65 inches from the floor) for optimal visual impact.
- **Multiple Mirrors**: In rooms where a single large mirror isn’t possible, use **two or three medium mirrors** placed strategically to bounce light across different sections.
