# Layered Lighting

**Definition**: A lighting strategy that combines several types of fixtures—such as floor lamps, table lamps, and overhead lights—to create a balanced, flexible illumination that adapts to different activities and complements natural daylight.

## Core Elements

1. **Fixture Types**
   - **Floor lamps**: Provide upward and outward wash, ideal for corners or behind seating. In bright, sun‑flooded rooms a single floor lamp can serve as the primary artificial source, especially when paired with dimmable, warm bulbs.
   - **Table lamps**: Offer localized lighting for side tables or desks, filling in shadows and adding depth to the lightscape.
   - **Overhead fixtures**: Supply general ambient light, often the baseline illumination level. When daylight is abundant, the overhead layer may be reduced in favor of a minimal‑light approach.

2. **Bulb Specifications**
   - Colour temperature: **2700 K–3000 K** for a warm‑to‑neutral feel that promotes a cozy atmosphere.
   - Brightness: **800–1100 lumens per lamp** for typical layered setups, but in bright rooms a single floor lamp can work effectively at **450–600 lumens** with a dimmable LED, allowing the fixture to blend with daylight without glare.

3. **Placement Strategy**
   - Position a strong floor lamp in a back corner of the main seating area to wash light across the room. For top‑floor spaces with large south‑facing windows, place the lamp near the sofa—slightly behind or to the side—to create an ambient pool without clutter.
   - Place table lamps on either side of a sofa or on end tables for balanced side lighting and task illumination.
   - Use existing overhead lighting as the ambient base, matching the colour temperature of the lamps, or scale it back when daylight is plentiful, embracing a **minimal-lighting** approach.

## Benefits
- **Even Light Distribution**: Reduces shadows and brightens dim corners, especially when natural light is blocked by external factors (e.g., trees) or when daylight is strong and a single lamp softens evening transitions.
- **Flexibility**: Allows adjustments for different tasks—reading, watching TV, or general ambience—by simply switching individual lamps on/off or dimming them.
- **Aesthetic Warmth**: Consistent warm colour temperature across fixtures creates a cohesive, inviting atmosphere that supports a **cozy‑atmosphere**.
- **Adaptability to Bright Spaces**: In rooms flooded with sunlight, a single, shaded floor lamp with a warm, dimmable bulb can provide the necessary evening glow without adding clutter, complementing the natural light rather than competing with it.

## Example Application
In the [[summaries/session-13_2022-07-21]] discussion, a medium‑size living room (~15 × 20 ft) used one floor lamp, two table lamps, and an overhead fixture, targeting **3,000–5,000 lumens total**. This setup exemplified layered lighting, providing bright yet comfortable illumination.

A contrasting scenario appears in [[summaries/session-36_2024-07-21]], where a sun‑flooded top‑floor living room relied on a **single floor lamp** with a fabric shade, a warm 2700 K dimmable LED (450‑600 lumens), and sheer curtains to transition from bright daytime to a soft evening ambiance. This illustrates how layered lighting can be scaled down to a **minimal‑lighting** configuration while still achieving a cozy feel.
