# Room Layout

**Definition**: The strategic placement of furniture and spatial elements to establish a comfortable, functional flow in a room, serving as the foundation for lighting, textiles, and overall ambiance.

## Core Principles
- **Prioritize Circulation**: Arrange seating and major pieces to allow easy movement and clear pathways.
- **Anchor Zones**: Identify focal points (e.g., sofa, TV, reading chair) and organize around them.
- **Scale & Proportion**: Choose furniture sizes that suit the room’s dimensions to avoid overcrowding.
- **Flexibility**: Keep the layout adaptable for different activities and seasonal changes.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/room-lighting-plan]]**: Once the layout is established, lighting can be mapped to accentuate pathways and focal zones.
- **[[concepts/minimal-lighting]]**: A clear layout often requires fewer light fixtures because the space feels organized.
- **[[concepts/layered-lighting]]**: With a solid layout, layered lighting (ambient, task, accent) can be layered strategically.
- **[[concepts/mirror-placement]]**: Mirrors can be positioned based on the layout to enhance perceived space and light distribution.

## Practical Tips
1. **Sketch First**: Draw a simple floor plan or use a digital tool to experiment.
2. **Start with Large Pieces**: Place the sofa or main seating first, then add secondary items.
3. **Maintain Empty Space**: Leave breathing room around furniture to avoid a cramped feel.
4. **Test Traffic Flow**: Walk the imagined path to ensure no obstacles hinder movement.

By establishing a thoughtful **room layout**, you set the stage for a harmonious, cozy living environment that integrates lighting, textiles, and decor seamlessly.
