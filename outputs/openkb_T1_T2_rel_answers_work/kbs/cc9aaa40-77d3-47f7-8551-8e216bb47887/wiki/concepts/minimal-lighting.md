# Minimal Lighting

**Definition:** Minimal lighting is a design approach that relies on a single, thoughtfully placed light source to provide sufficient illumination while maintaining a low‑key, uncluttered visual environment.

**Key principles**
- **Single lamp focus:** Choose one lamp (often a floor lamp with a shade) rather than multiple fixtures.
- **Warm, dimmable bulbs:** Use warm‑white (≈2700 K) LEDs with adjustable brightness (≈450–600 lumens) to create a cozy glow.
- **Strategic placement:** Position the lamp near the primary seating area, slightly behind or to the side, to produce ambient, downward‑directed light.
- **Complementary treatments:** Pair with sheer curtains or texture to soften daylight transitions without adding extra lighting hardware.

**Application in [[summaries/session-36_2024-07-21]]**
- The advice recommends a floor lamp with a fabric shade, placed near the sofa, using a dimmable 2700 K LED (450–600 lumens). This exemplifies minimal lighting by limiting fixtures, controlling glare, and enhancing the [[concepts/cozy-atmosphere]].
- The approach aligns with a broader [[concepts/layered-lighting]] strategy where minimal lighting serves as the evening layer after layout decisions.

**Benefits**
- Reduces visual clutter.
- Simplifies electrical planning.
- Enhances mood with warm, adjustable light.
- Works well in spaces with abundant natural daylight, as it avoids competing with bright overhead illumination.
