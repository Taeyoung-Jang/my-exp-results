# Room Lighting Plan

A **Room Lighting Plan** is a systematic approach to arranging artificial light sources within a space to achieve functional, aesthetic, and mood objectives, taking into account room dimensions, furniture placement, natural light availability, and desired illumination levels.

## Core Elements

1. **Room Dimensions & Shape**
   - Measure length, width, and ceiling height to calculate total square footage.
   - Use this data to estimate overall lumen requirements (e.g., 200‑300 lumens per sq ft for living rooms).

2. **Furniture Layout**
   - Identify primary activity zones (seating area, media console, reading nook).
   - Position lamps (floor, table, and accent) to cover each zone without glare. For minimal‑fixture rooms, a single floor lamp placed near the sofa (slightly behind or to the side) can provide ambient illumination for the entire seating area. See also [[concepts/room-layout]].

3. **Natural Light Integration**
   - Assess window placement and daylight intensity.
   - Use sheer or lightly textured curtains to soften the transition from bright daytime sun to evening mood, reducing harsh contrast without adding fixtures.
   - Leverage mirrors and reflective surfaces to bounce natural light deeper into the room (see [[concepts/mirror-placement]]).

4. **Layered Lighting**
   - Combine ambient, task, and accent lighting for depth and flexibility (see [[concepts/layered-lighting]]).
   - In bright, sun‑flooded spaces, a **minimal‑lighting** approach may rely on one warm, dimmable floor lamp with a fabric shade that diffuses light downward, complemented by natural light and curtains.

5. **Bulb Specifications**
   - Choose colour temperature (2700‑3000 K for warm‑neutral comfort) and ensure dimmability for mood control.
   - For a single floor lamp, aim for 450‑600 lumens; for multiple lamps, 800‑1100 lumens per fixture is typical. This balances sufficient brightness with the desire to avoid glare.
   - Warm‑white LEDs provide energy efficiency and allow fine‑tuned brightness adjustments.

## Applying the Concept: Examples

### From [[summaries/session-13_2022-07-21]]
- **Room Size:** Approximately 15 × 20 ft (300 sq ft).
- **Suggested Layout:** One strong floor lamp in a back‑corner of the seating area, two table lamps on either side of the sofa, and an existing overhead light.
- **Total Lumen Goal:** 3,000‑5,000 lumens across all fixtures.
- **Mirror Placement:** A large wall‑mounted mirror opposite the windows to amplify daylight.

### From [[summaries/session-36_2024-07-21]]
- **Scenario:** South‑facing top‑floor living room flooded with sunlight most of the day.
- **Lighting Priority:** Emphasize a single, dimmable floor lamp with a fabric shade (downward diffusion) rather than multiple fixtures.
- **Lamp Placement:** Corner near the sofa, slightly behind or to the side, to create a soft ambient pool of light.
- **Bulb Specs:** Warm 2700 K LED, 450‑600 lumens, dimmable.
- **Complementary Softening:** Light, sheer curtains to ease the day‑to‑night light shift; mirrors can still be used but are not required for additional brightness.
- **Goal:** Achieve a cozy atmosphere while maintaining a minimal‑fixture aesthetic (see [[concepts/minimal-lighting]] and [[concepts/cozy-atmosphere]]).

## Benefits
- **Even illumination** reduces shadows and creates a welcoming atmosphere.
- **Energy efficiency** through targeted lighting rather than over‑illuminating the entire space.
- **Flexibility** to adjust lighting layers for different activities (reading, watching TV, entertaining).
- **Cozy minimalism**: A single well‑placed floor lamp with the right bulb and curtains can deliver comfort without visual clutter.
