# Low‑Activity Cap

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** A defined limit on duration and effort for recovery days to support 24‑hour rest.

---

The **Low‑Activity Cap** specifies how much movement is permissible on designated recovery days so that the body receives active circulation without adding measurable training stress.  In the context of the weekly plan from [[summaries/session-13_2025-05-03]], the cap is:

- **Time:** 20–40 minutes per low‑activity day.
- **Effort:** Rated 2–3 out of 10 on a perceived exertion scale (easy conversational pace).  Activities that fit this range include gentle walking, light cycling, easy mobility drills, or very low‑intensity stretching.

### Why it matters
When an athlete needs a full 24‑hour low‑activity window after any hard effort, exceeding this cap can unintentionally re‑introduce stress, prolonging fatigue and undermining recovery.  By keeping activity within the cap, the body enjoys the benefits of blood flow and joint lubrication while staying in a true recovery mode.

### Practical application
1. **Choose activity** – walking, casual bike ride, or mobility sequence.
2. **Set a timer** – stop at 40 minutes maximum.
3. **Monitor effort** – use a simple 1‑10 scale; stay at 2‑3 where you can talk comfortably and feel refreshed afterward.
4. **Assess response** – if you finish feeling better, the cap is appropriate; if you feel drained, reduce duration or intensity.

### Interaction with other concepts
- The cap is a core component of the **[[concepts/recovery-first-scheduling]]** approach, ensuring each hard day is followed by a true low‑stress buffer.
- It supports the **[[concepts/hard-day-structure]]** by providing a clear, low‑intensity contrast to the high‑intensity blocks.
- Tracking **[[concepts/recovery-markers]]** (sleep, resting heart rate, mood) helps verify that the cap is effective for the individual.
- Incremental changes to the cap can be part of a **[[concepts/weekly-progression]]** plan, such as slowly extending time or slightly increasing pace while still staying within the 2‑3 effort range.
