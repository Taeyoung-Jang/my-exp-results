# Structured Workouts

**Definition** – Structured workouts are deliberately designed, higher‑intensity training sessions (e.g., intervals, tempo runs, hill repeats) that are placed within a broader weekly plan that also contains low‑intensity, easy days. The purpose is to provide clear stimulus for physiological adaptation while preserving overall fatigue levels.

**Key Elements from [[summaries/session-36_2025-09-02]]**
- **Quality days**: Tuesdays and Thursdays feature hard intervals, and Saturdays host a longer hill‑focused session. These are the core [[concepts/structured_workouts]] in the plan.
- **Volume balance**: Total weekly volume stays roughly steady, with only modest increases week‑to‑week, ensuring the structured days do not overwhelm the athlete.
- **Recovery integration**: Easy endurance days (Monday, Friday, possibly Sunday) are kept at a conversational pace to offset the stress of the structured sessions. They are linked to [[concepts/easy_days]] and [[concepts/steady_mileage]].
- **Monitoring**: Recovery markers such as sleep quality, resting heart rate, and perceived effort are used to gauge whether the structured workouts are effective or need adjustment. See [[concepts/recovery-markers]].
- **Deload strategy**: During a deload week, both volume and intensity are reduced, but a very light version of a structured workout may be retained to maintain neuromuscular sharpness. This ties into [[concepts/deload_week]].

**How Structured Workouts Fit Within an Endurance Program**
1. **Placement** – Schedule high‑intensity sessions on non‑consecutive days (e.g., Tue/Thu) to allow at least one full day of low‑intensity work for recovery.
2. **Intensity** – Keep the sessions purposeful: interval repeats, tempo efforts, or hill repeats that target specific physiological systems.
3. **Volume Control** – Pair each structured day with an easy day that adds aerobic base without additional stress, aligning with [[concepts/training_volume]] and [[concepts/weekly-progression]].
4. **Rest Days** – A full rest day (often Wednesday or Sunday) provides a deeper reset, supporting the effectiveness of subsequent structured workouts. Refer to [[concepts/rest_day]].
5. **Flexibility** – If fatigue signs appear (e.g., elevated resting HR, lingering soreness), replace an easy day with a rest day or scale back the upcoming structured session.
