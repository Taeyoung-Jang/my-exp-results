# Deload Week

A **deload week** is a planned reduction in training load, cutting both overall volume and intensity while preserving a minimal stimulus to maintain fitness. It is used to promote recovery, prevent overtraining, and prepare the body for the next phase of progressive training.

---

## Why Deload?
- Allows physiological systems to repair and adapt after several weeks of hard work.
- Helps stabilize **recovery markers** such as resting heart rate, sleep quality, and perceived fatigue.
- Reduces risk of injury and mental burnout.

---

## Typical Adjustments
| Element | Typical Reduction |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Training volume** (total miles/kilometers, session time) | 30‑50 % lower than usual weeks [[concepts/training_volume]] |
| **Intensity** (pace, power, HR zones) | 20‑30 % easier, e.g., light intervals or very easy tempo [[concepts/interval_training]] |
| **Hard‑day structure** (intervals, hills) | Keep a very light version of the core workout, but at conversational effort [[concepts/hard-day-structure]] |
| **Easy days** | Retain easy‑day cadence, perhaps drop a day or make them ultra‑short [[concepts/easy_days]] |

---

## How to Implement
1. **Select a week** after 3‑4 weeks of consistent hard‑day blocks (e.g., after a series of interval and hill sessions). 
2. **Scale back volume**: shorten long runs/rides by roughly a third; cut the number of repeats in interval sessions. 
3. **Scale back intensity**: reduce target heart‑rate or power zones by one or two levels; keep the work feeling **easy** rather than challenging. 
4. **Maintain structure**: keep the same weekly layout (interval day, hill day, easy days) but at the reduced load, so the routine feels familiar. 
5. **Monitor recovery markers**: watch for improved sleep, stable resting HR, and a return of “freshness” after each session [[concepts/recovery-markers]].

---

## Monitoring Success
- **Performance**: slight drop in pace/power is expected, but the athlete should feel less fatigued.
- **Physiological signs**: resting HR trends down, no lingering soreness.
- **Psychological state**: motivation remains high, no dread of upcoming sessions.

If signs of excessive fatigue persist, consider extending the deload or adding an extra **rest day** [[concepts/rest_day]].

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works alongside **steady mileage** and **structured workouts** to balance long‑term progression [[concepts/steady_mileage]] [[concepts/structured_workouts]].
- Forms a key component of **weekly progression** planning, providing a built‑in recovery checkpoint [[concepts/weekly-progression]].
- Aligns with the philosophy of **recovery‑first scheduling**—prioritizing rest before adding new stress [[concepts/recovery-first-scheduling]].
