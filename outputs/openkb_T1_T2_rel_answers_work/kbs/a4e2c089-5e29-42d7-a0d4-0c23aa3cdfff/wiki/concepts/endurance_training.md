# Endurance Training

Endurance training is a systematic approach that blends **structured workouts** (e.g., intervals, hill repeats) with **steady mileage** to develop aerobic capacity while minimizing burnout. The concept emphasizes:

- **Hard‑day structure**: Designate specific days for high‑intensity sessions such as [[concepts/interval_training]] on Tuesdays and Thursdays and [[concepts/hill_training]] on Saturdays. [[concepts/hard-day-structure]]
- **Easy days**: Include low‑intensity, conversational‑effort sessions on Monday, Friday (and optionally Sunday) to add volume without compromising recovery. [[concepts/easy_days]]
- **Rest days**: Allocate at least one full [[concepts/rest_day]] (e.g., Wednesday) and remain flexible to add a second rest day if needed.
- **Training volume**: Keep weekly mileage relatively consistent, making only modest increments week to week. [[concepts/training_volume]]
- **Steady mileage**: Use longer, moderate‑pace outings to build base endurance. [[concepts/steady_mileage]]
- **Recovery markers**: Monitor sleep quality, perceived effort, resting heart rate, and overall fatigue to adjust the plan. [[concepts/recovery-markers]]
- **Deload weeks**: Periodically reduce both volume and intensity while keeping a light interval to retain sharpness. [[concepts/deload_week]]

The approach described in [[summaries/session-36_2025-09-02]] illustrates how a typical week can be organized to balance these elements, ensuring progress without excessive strain.
