## Recovery Markers

Recovery markers are the primary signals an athlete uses each morning to decide whether to proceed with a planned hard session, shorten it, or replace it with a low‑activity day. The approach is referenced in [[summaries/session-13_2025-05-03]] and reinforced by the practical guidance from [[summaries/session-36_2025-09-02]].

### Core Markers
1. **Sleep quality** – How rested you feel upon waking, depth of sleep, and any nighttime disturbances.
2. **Resting heart rate (RHR)** – Comparison to your personal baseline; a noticeable rise suggests incomplete recovery.
3. **Mood / mental energy** – Levels of irritability, motivation, and overall “readiness” during the warm‑up.
4. **Perceived effort** – Feeling challenged but not wrecked during the day; you should still be able to perform everyday tasks (e.g., errands) without excessive fatigue.
5. **Performance trends** – Gradual improvements in pace or heart‑rate at a given effort, indicating positive adaptation; a stagnation or regression can signal insufficient recovery.

### Decision Rule
The daily check consists of two simple questions:
- Did you wake up feeling refreshed (good sleep, stable RHR, neutral mood, and reasonable perceived effort)?
- Does the body feel ready during the warm‑up (no lingering heaviness, no dread, and performance metrics are stable or improving)?

If both answers are **yes**, you can follow the hard‑day structure ([[concepts/hard-day-structure]]). If either answer is **no**, you should either shorten the workout or switch to a low‑activity day limited by the low‑activity cap [[concepts/low-activity-cap]].

### Tracking and Application
Consistently logging these markers creates a trend line that informs weekly progression [[concepts/weekly-progression]] and supports a recovery‑first scheduling approach [[concepts/recovery-first-scheduling]]. Over time, athletes can see patterns such as rising RHR or declining mood that precede performance dips, allowing proactive adjustments (e.g., swapping an easy day for rest).
