# Recovery‑First Scheduling

**Definition** – A training planning approach that places a true low‑activity day after every hard workout to ensure sufficient physiological recovery before the next stressor.

## Core Principles
1. **Hard‑day followed by low‑activity day** – Each high‑intensity or high‑volume session is immediately succeeded by a day limited to gentle movement (e.g., walking, light mobility) lasting 20‑40 minutes at an effort of 2‑3 / 10. This mirrors the pattern described in [[summaries/session-13_2025-05-03]].
2. **Treat recovery as a scheduled block** – Low‑activity days are entered into the weekly calendar just like hard sessions, preventing ad‑hoc “skip‑days” and preserving training momentum.
3. **Use recovery markers** – Sleep quality, resting heart rate, and mood are checked each morning; if any marker signals insufficient recovery, the upcoming hard day is shortened or swapped for a low‑activity day (see [[concepts/recovery-markers]]).
4. **Bundle strength with hard days** – To keep recovery days truly easy, strength work is performed on the same day as the primary hard effort rather than on a separate day.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The **hard‑day structure** defines what constitutes a “hard” session (cardio, strength, or mixed) and is paired with the low‑activity cap described in [[concepts/low-activity-cap]].
- Progression over weeks follows the small‑step approach outlined in [[concepts/weekly-progression]], while the recovery‑first schedule remains unchanged.

## Practical Example (from the source)
- **Monday**: Interval cardio + short strength block → **Tuesday**: 20‑40 min easy walk (effort 2‑3 / 10).
- **Wednesday**: Strength‑focused session → **Thursday**: Low‑activity walk.
- **Saturday**: Mixed cardio/strength circuit → **Sunday**: Low‑activity walk.

This pattern provides three quality hard sessions per week while respecting the athlete’s need for a full 24‑hour low‑activity window after each effort.
