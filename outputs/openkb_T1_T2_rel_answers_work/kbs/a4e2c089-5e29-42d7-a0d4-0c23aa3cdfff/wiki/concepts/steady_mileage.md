# Steady Mileage

**Description**: Low‑intensity, consistent mileage aimed at building an aerobic base while keeping fatigue low.

**Key Principles**
- **Conversational effort**: Keep heart rate and perceived exertion low enough that the run or ride feels easy and even refreshing.
- **Volume stability**: Maintain a relatively steady weekly mileage, adjusting only modestly week‑to‑week.
- **Integration with quality sessions**: Placed on easy days (e.g., Monday, Friday, and optional Sunday) around harder workouts like [[concepts/interval_training]] and [[concepts/hill_training]] to provide aerobic stimulus without compromising recovery.
- **Recovery focus**: Serves as a buffer that supports [[concepts/recovery-markers]] such as stable resting heart rate and good sleep.

**Typical Placement in a Week (from [[summaries/session-36_2025-09-02]])**
- **Monday & Friday**: Short, easy endurance rides/runs at steady mileage.
- **Sunday** (optional): Another easy day unless a second [[concepts/rest_day]] is needed after a taxing hill workout.

**Benefits**
- Enhances aerobic efficiency and fat‑burning capacity.
- Helps maintain overall [[concepts/training_volume]] while protecting against overtraining.
- Provides a mental break from high‑intensity work, reinforcing an [[concepts/endurance_training]] mindset.

**Interaction with Other Concepts**
- Works alongside [[concepts/easy_days]] to structure low‑stress sessions.
- Complements [[concepts/structured_workouts]] by supplying the base upon which harder intervals can be performed.
- Can be tapered during a [[concepts/deload_week]] by reducing mileage further.

**Implementation Tips**
1. Choose a pace where you could hold a conversation.
2. Monitor recovery markers; if fatigue builds, consider converting a steady‑mileage day to a full [[concepts/rest_day]].
3. Keep weekly mileage increments small (e.g., <5 %) to avoid sudden spikes.
