# Rest Day

A **Rest Day** is a dedicated day without structured training, allowing the body to fully recover and adapt to prior stress. In the context of endurance training, it serves to balance hard sessions, preserve performance, and reduce injury risk.

## Role in Weekly Structure

- Typically placed on a **mid‑week** (e.g., Wednesday) or **post‑long‑run** (e.g., Sunday) to break up demanding workouts such as [[concepts/interval_training]] on Tuesday/Thursday and [[concepts/hill_training]] on Saturday.
- Provides a complete break from both **structured workouts** and **steady mileage**, contrasting with [[concepts/easy_days]] which are low‑intensity but still active.

## Indicators for Effectiveness

- Stable or improving **recovery markers** (e.g., resting heart rate, sleep quality, subjective freshness).
- Absence of lingering fatigue, muscle soreness, or performance decline in the subsequent hard session.
- Ability to maintain or improve pace/effort on easy days after the rest.

## Flexibility

- If a light spin on the rest day improves leg circulation without detracting from recovery, it can be used **conditionally**; otherwise, a full rest day is recommended.
- When a Saturday hill workout is especially taxing, the Sunday easy day may be converted to a second **Rest Day** to ensure adequate recovery before the upcoming week.

## Interaction with Deload Week

During a **deload week**, volume is reduced significantly and intensity is trimmed, but a **Rest Day** remains essential to allow the trimmed load to be fully assimilated.

## Scheduling Considerations

- Align Rest Days with the athlete’s schedule and life demands to avoid burnout.
- Use **recovery‑first scheduling** principles: prioritize recovery days before planning the next hard session.
