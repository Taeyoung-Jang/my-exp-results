# Interval Training

**Definition**: Interval training refers to short, high‑intensity work bouts separated by recovery periods, used to boost VO₂ max, lactate threshold, and overall running or cycling efficiency.

## Role in a Weekly Plan
- Positioned as the *hard‑day* workouts on **Tuesday** and **Thursday** in the routine described in [[summaries/session-36_2025-09-02]].
- Serves as a core component of [[concepts/structured_workouts]] while complementing [[concepts/steady_mileage]] on easy days.
- Provides the stimulus for performance gains without excessive volume, keeping total [[concepts/training_volume]] stable week‑to‑week.

## Practical Guidelines
1. **Intensity**: Aim for effort that feels challenging but not wrecked; use pace, power, or heart‑rate zones that push you above the aerobic threshold.
2. **Recovery**: Keep recovery periods truly easy (conversational pace) to ensure the interval quality remains high.
3. **Session Length**: Typical interval blocks last 20‑30 minutes total, fitting within the broader weekly volume.
4. **Monitoring**: Track [[concepts/recovery-markers]] such as post‑session fatigue, sleep quality, and resting heart rate to gauge effectiveness.

## Integration with Other Concepts
- **Easy Days**: On Monday, Friday (and optional Sunday) schedule [[concepts/easy_days]] at low intensity to add aerobic volume without compromising interval quality.
- **Rest Day**: Include a true [[concepts/rest_day]] (e.g., Wednesday) to allow full recovery before the next interval session.
- **Hill Training**: Saturday’s longer hill workout provides a different stimulus; intervals and hills together create a balanced [[concepts/endurance_training]] program.
- **Deload Week**: During a [[concepts/deload_week]], cut both volume and intensity, but retain a reduced‑intensity interval set to maintain neural sharpness.
- **Weekly Progression**: Apply gradual load increases only after confirming markers of recovery are positive, following a [[concepts/weekly-progression]] approach.

## Benefits
- Improves cardiovascular efficiency and speed.
- Enhances the ability to sustain higher intensities during longer events like the **spring charity ride** (mentioned in the source document).
- Allows the athlete to train consistently without burnout when paired with proper recovery.
