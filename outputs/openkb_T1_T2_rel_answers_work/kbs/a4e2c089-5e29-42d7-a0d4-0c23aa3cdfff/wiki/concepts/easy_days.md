## Easy Days

**Definition**: Easy days are deliberately low‑intensity endurance sessions performed on non‑key workout days to promote recovery while still contributing to overall [[concepts/training_volume]] and maintaining [[concepts/steady_mileage]].

**Purpose in a weekly plan** (as outlined in [[summaries/session-36_2025-09-02]]):
- Provide gentle aerobic stimulus after hard sessions such as [[concepts/interval_training]] (Tue/Thu) and [[concepts/hill_training]] (Sat).
- Keep the body moving without elevating heart rate or perceived effort to the point of fatigue.
- Serve as active recovery, helping muscles clear metabolites and preserving neuromuscular readiness for the next quality day.

**Typical characteristics**
- Duration: 30‑60 minutes for runners, 45‑75 minutes for cyclists.
- Intensity: conversational pace, heart‑rate zone 1‑2, RPE 2‑3.
- Frequency: 1‑2 easy days per week (e.g., Monday and Friday) with optional Sunday easy day if recovery permits.

**Monitoring effectiveness**
- Use [[concepts/recovery-markers]] such as feeling refreshed after the session, stable resting heart rate, and no increase in perceived fatigue.
- If the session feels taxing or mood/drowsiness rises, treat it as a [[concepts/rest_day]] instead.

**Interaction with other concepts**
- Complements [[concepts/structured_workouts]] and [[concepts/hard-day-structure]] by balancing stress.
- Supports a gradual [[concepts/weekly-progression]] without over‑loading.
- During a [[concepts/deload_week]], easy days may be shortened further or replaced by additional rest.

**Practical tips**
- Prioritize good sleep and nutrition around easy days.
- Keep external stress low; avoid adding high‑intensity cross‑training.
- Adjust volume based on how the subsequent hard day feels; flexibility is key.
