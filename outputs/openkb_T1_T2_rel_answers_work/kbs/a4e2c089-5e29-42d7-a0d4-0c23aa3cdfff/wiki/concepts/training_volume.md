# Training Volume

**Training volume** refers to the overall amount of aerobic work—running, cycling, or similar—planned over a given period, typically a week. It is a core metric for managing fitness gains while avoiding overload.

## Key Points from [[summaries/session-36_2025-09-02]]
- The plan keeps the **total volume fairly steady week‑to‑week**, with only small, incremental increases to promote gradual adaptation.
- **Easy days** (Monday, Friday, optionally Sunday) are used to add gentle aerobic volume without compromising the intensity of the hard sessions.
- **Hard‑day structure** (intervals on Tuesday/Thursday and a hill workout on Saturday) provides the quality stimulus, while the volume of these sessions is balanced by low‑intensity days.
- A **full rest day** (Wednesday or Sunday) is included to reset the system and ensure the overall volume does not become cumulative fatigue.
- During a **deload week**, both volume and intensity are reduced, preserving a light interval to maintain neural sharpness while allowing recovery.
- Monitoring **recovery markers**—such as sleep quality, resting heart rate, and perceived effort—helps decide when to adjust volume.

- **[[concepts/recovery-markers]]** guide adjustments to volume based on physiological feedback.
- **[[concepts/weekly-progression]]** (though not explicitly listed) is the principle of slightly increasing volume over time, as described in the source.

## Practical Guidelines
1. **Set a baseline weekly volume** that matches current fitness and life demands.
2. **Add small increments** (e.g., 5‑10% per week) only when recovery markers remain positive.
3. **Use easy days** to accumulate volume without taxing the nervous system.
4. **Include at least one full rest day** to allow super‑compensation.
5. **Schedule regular deload weeks** where volume is cut by 30‑50% to reset.
6. **Track recovery markers** to decide when to hold, increase, or decrease volume.

By balancing total weekly work with appropriate intensity distribution and recovery, training volume becomes a powerful lever for sustainable endurance improvements.
