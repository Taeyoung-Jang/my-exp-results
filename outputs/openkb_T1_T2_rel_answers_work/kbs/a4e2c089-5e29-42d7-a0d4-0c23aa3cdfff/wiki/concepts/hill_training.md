# Hill Training

Hill training refers to dedicated workouts that emphasize sustained climbing or repeated hill repeats. The primary goals are to develop leg strength, improve aerobic capacity, and enhance running or cycling efficiency on gradients.

## Core Elements
- **Long hill‑focused day** – Typically placed on a weekend (e.g., Saturday) to allow ample time for a moderate‑to‑long duration effort on varied inclines. [[concepts/hill_training]]
- **Integration with other key sessions** – Schedule hill work alongside hard interval days (e.g., Tuesday and Thursday) and easy endurance days to balance stress and recovery. [[concepts/interval_training]] [[concepts/easy_days]]
- **Recovery considerations** – Use a true rest day (e.g., Wednesday) or a very easy spin/run to ensure the legs are fresh for the next hard session. Monitor fatigue, sleep quality, and resting heart rate as recovery markers. [[concepts/rest_day]] [[concepts/recovery-markers]]
- **Volume and intensity management** – Keep overall weekly training volume relatively steady, but trim volume and intensity during a deload week while still including a light hill or interval set to maintain neuromuscular sharpness. [[concepts/deload_week]] [[concepts/training_volume]]

| Friday | Easy endurance | Light aerobic volume |
| Saturday | **Hill training** | Longer, hill‑focused effort |
| Sunday | Easy endurance or second rest day (if Saturday was taxing) | Light aerobic volume |

## Benefits
- **Strength gains** on climbs translate to faster flat‑ground speed.
- **Improved lactate clearance** due to sustained effort at moderate intensity.
- **Enhanced muscular endurance** for long‑duration events such as charity rides or races.

## Guidelines
1. **Duration**: Aim for 60–90 minutes of hill work, including warm‑up and cool‑down.
2. **Intensity**: Keep effort at a steady, hard‑but‑sustainable level (e.g., 70‑80% max HR).
3. **Recovery**: Prioritize easy days and at least one full rest day each week; adjust if fatigue accumulates.
4. **Deload**: Reduce hill session length by 30‑50% and lower intensity during a planned deload week.
5. **Progression**: Gradually increase hill volume or add short, sharper repeats as fitness improves, aligning with overall weekly progression plans. [[concepts/weekly-progression]]
