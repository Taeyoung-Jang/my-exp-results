**Assistant:** A good rule of thumb is that normal post‑workout tiredness feels more like heaviness or fatigue that fades within a day, while the kind of tired that means you should back off tends to linger, feels sharper or more uncomfortable, or comes with things like unusually low energy, trouble sleeping, irritability, or a drop in performance. If you’re feeling worn down before you even start a workout, that’s usually a sign your body hasn’t caught up yet.

For planning rest days, it helps to treat them like a real part of your routine rather than a pause from it. Scheduling them ahead of time keeps them from feeling like “skipped days,” and doing light, enjoyable movement—like a walk or stretching—can keep your momentum going without stressing your body. Paying attention to how you feel the day after a rest day is also useful; if you come back feeling noticeably better, that’s a good hint that your plan is on track.
