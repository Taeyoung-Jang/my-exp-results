# Conversation session 36

Date: 2025-09-02 08:00

**User:** I’ve been trying to build a consistent training routine for an upcoming endurance event, but I’m torn between doing more structured workouts vs. just piling on steady miles. If you were coaching someone who wants to get fitter without burning out, how would you structure a typical week and what would you watch for to know it’s working?

**Assistant:** I’d blend both structure and steady miles so you get the fitness benefits without digging yourself into a hole. A typical week would have a couple of clearly defined quality days, a few easy days, and one longer outing. For most people this looks like one interval or tempo‑style workout, one moderately long run or ride with a bit of steady pressure, and everything else kept genuinely easy and relaxed.

The biggest thing is making the easy days truly easy so the harder days actually feel doable. If every day feels like a grind, the balance is off. I’d also keep the total volume fairly steady week to week with only small increases.

To tell if it’s working, I’d look for signs like finishing workouts feeling challenged but not wrecked, sleeping well, and seeing your easy‑day pace or heart rate gradually improve at the same effort. If you start dreading sessions, feeling unusually sluggish, or seeing your resting heart rate creep up for several days, that’s your cue to pull back a little.

**User:** That makes sense. With my current setup (hard intervals Tuesday/Thursday and a longer hill-focused day Saturday), how would you fill in the other days—especially Monday/Friday—so I recover but still build endurance? And would you schedule a full rest day or just an easy spin/run?

**Assistant:** With hard intervals on Tuesday and Thursday and a longer hill day on Saturday, I’d make Monday and Friday genuinely easy endurance days—shorter, low‑intensity sessions meant to leave you feeling better afterward than when you started. Think conversational effort, nothing that tugs at your legs or breathing. They add gentle aerobic volume without interfering with the quality days.

I’d still include one true rest day somewhere each week. For most people, Sunday or Wednesday works well. A real day off tends to leave you fresher and able to hit your key workouts with more purpose, which does more for fitness than squeezing in another light session.

If you wake up unusually tired or your legs feel dead, swap any easy day for rest without hesitation. Keeping that flexibility is what keeps the whole plan sustainable.

**User:** If I take Wednesday fully off, would you keep Sunday as an easy endurance day or make that the second rest day after the Saturday hills? Also, how would you approach a “deload” week—cut volume, cut intensity, or both?

**Assistant:** If Wednesday is your full rest day, I’d keep Sunday as an easy endurance day unless the Saturday hill work routinely leaves you cooked. Most people handle a relaxed Sunday session well, and it helps keep the weekly rhythm without adding real stress. If Saturday starts feeling like it takes more than a day to bounce back from, then making Sunday a second rest day is totally fine—your quality days will benefit more from being well‑recovered than from squeezing in extra light volume.

For a deload week, I’d cut volume noticeably and trim intensity but not eliminate it. Think of it as keeping the flavor of your normal routine but in smaller, gentler doses. A light interval set that feels almost too easy is usually enough to maintain sharpness while letting your body absorb the training.

**User:** Funny how routines sneak up on you—since I started training for that spring charity ride, my coach has me doing hard intervals on Tuesdays and Thursdays with a long hill day on Saturday, and I’ve still been able to show up the next morning and do my usual errands without feeling wiped out. Given that, would you still push for Wednesday as a full rest day, or keep it as a very easy recovery spin and take the full rest day elsewhere?

**Assistant:** If you’re handling the current load well and everyday life still feels easy, you’ve got some freedom in how you use Wednesday.

I’d lean toward keeping Wednesday as a true rest day unless spinning easy on that day genuinely makes you feel better. A short, gentle spin can work if you wake up stiff and it helps your legs come around, but if you feel perfectly fine without it, full rest usually gives you a deeper reset and keeps the back half of the week crisp.

In the end, I’d choose based on how Thursday’s intervals feel. If you start those sessions with good pop and no heaviness, your Wednesday plan—rest or spin—is working. If Thursday ever starts feeling flat or sluggish, switch Wednesday to full rest and see if the sharpness returns.
