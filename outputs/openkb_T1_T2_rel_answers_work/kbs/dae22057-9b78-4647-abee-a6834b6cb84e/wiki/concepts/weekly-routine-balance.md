# Weekly Routine Balance

**Definition**: A weekly scheduling framework that deliberately spaces low‑intensity strength training, gentle mobility work, optional light cardio, and full rest days around a typical 9‑5 work week to manage stress, prevent over‑training, and support consistent habit formation.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-15_2024-06-15]])
- **Two strength days** – short (15‑20 min) sessions using the [[concepts/low-intensity-strength]] routines (Routine A and Routine B).  
- **Two mobility days** – 10‑15 min gentle flow based on the [[concepts/gentle-mobility-flow]] sequence.  
- **Two full rest days** – no structured exercise, only optional micro‑breathing or stretch.  
- **One optional easy‑walk day** – a light aerobic activity that can be swapped for rest when needed.  
- **Flexible “wild‑card”** – a day that can become a minimum‑effort version of any other activity or a rest day, preventing rigidity.

## Daily Checklist Structure
Each day is presented with three tiers:
1. **Full** – the planned 15‑20 min strength circuit or 10‑15 min mobility flow.  
2. **Minimum** – a 5‑minute (strength) or 2‑3 minute (mobility) version that maintains the habit without taxing the body.  
3. **Swap if tired** – an alternative (usually a mobility or rest option) chosen based on a quick self‑check of fatigue or tightness.

## Decision Framework
- **Feeling functional but busy** → use the *Minimum* tier to keep the signal alive.  
- **Feeling tight or drained** → swap to a lighter modality (mobility or rest).  
- **Feeling exhausted** → take a full rest day.

## Benefits
- **Balanced stress load** – alternates demanding (strength) and restorative (mobility, rest) activities.  
- **Habit consistency** – daily checklists with minimum options reduce the chance of complete dropout.  
- **Adaptability** – the wild‑card day and swap options let the schedule flex with work demands.
