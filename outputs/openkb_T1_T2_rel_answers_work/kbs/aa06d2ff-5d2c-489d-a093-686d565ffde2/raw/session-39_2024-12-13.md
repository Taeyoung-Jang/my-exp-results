**User:** I’m trying to figure out the least annoying way to manage my phone situation and could use some advice. Since I started the new job at the lab, they had me enroll my phone in their mobile management thing—now half my screen is just those gray ‘restricted’ placeholders, and I’ve basically stopped bothering with installs unless IT approves it first.  

Is there a good way to clean up the home screen (or hide those placeholders) without breaking any policies? And if the answer is basically “you can’t,” what do people usually do—separate work/personal profiles, second phone, web apps, something else?

**Assistant:** You can’t remove or hide those restricted placeholders in any way that circumvents policy, and you shouldn’t try—anything that alters or interferes with MDM controls will usually be treated as a violation.

What you *can* do is reorganize your home screen in ways that the MDM still allows. Most setups let you drag restricted icons into a folder, or move them to a secondary home‑screen page, so they’re out of sight but still present. Some MDM systems also let you switch to a different launcher or layout as long as it’s not blocked, which can reduce the visual clutter.

If your company supports Android Work Profile or iOS Managed Apps, enabling a separate work profile is usually the least painful setup. It keeps your personal home screen clean, and all the locked‑down items stay inside the work container.

If the organization doesn’t offer that, most people eventually pick one of two paths: keep a separate work phone so the personal one stays untouched, or move as much personal activity as possible to web apps so you’re using the managed phone less often.

The simplest next step is to check with IT whether they support a work/personal profile split. If they do, that’s usually the smoothest option.

**User:** Can you give me a more concrete “do this first, then this” checklist for both iPhone and Android?

I’m not trying to bypass anything—I just want the restricted tiles off my main page if possible. Also, how do I tell whether I’m in a Work Profile/managed container vs it being device-wide management?

**Assistant:** I can give you a simple step‑by‑step flow, written in plain sentences instead of a bullet list.

On iPhone, start by long‑pressing any of the restricted gray icons. If the device is fully supervised you won’t be able to delete or hide them, but you can still drag them. Try dragging all the restricted icons into a single folder, then move that folder to the last home‑screen page. If your phone lets you change your default home‑screen page, set your personal one as the default in Settings → Home Screen. If the icons refuse to move at all, that usually means device‑wide supervision with tight restrictions rather than a lighter “managed apps only” setup.

On Android without a Work Profile, try long‑pressing the restricted app and dragging it into a folder or onto another page. Most MDM systems still allow that. If you can’t move them, your device is probably under full device management.

On Android with a Work Profile, you’ll know because you’ll see a separate section of apps with a small briefcase badge on each icon. You can also check in Settings → Accounts; a Work Profile shows up as its own profile. If you see that, then the clutter should only be inside the Work tab or Work launcher. Your personal home screen should stay clean, and you can simply switch back to the Personal side.

On iPhone, the easiest way to tell whether you’re in a work container versus full device management is to open Settings → General → VPN & Device Management. If the profile says something like “supervised” or “this device is managed,” that’s full‑device control. If it only lists specific managed apps or settings, it’s lighter management.

If you try the above and still can’t move or hide anything, that’s normally a sign that the organization pushed down a fully supervised mode. At that point the usual solutions are asking IT whether a separate Work Profile (Android) or user enrollment (iOS) is an option, or using a second device so your personal home screen stays untouched.

**User:** On iPhone, in **Settings → General → VPN & Device Management**, it says the device is managed and supervised. I can’t delete the restricted icons, but I *can* move them around.

Can you lay out the exact steps to shove all the restricted stuff onto a single last page (or into one folder) and make my first page “personal”? Also, is there any iOS setting that stops new managed apps from auto-appearing on my home screen?

**Assistant:** Since you can move the icons, you can clean things up without violating any rules. The basic idea is to push all the managed placeholders to the very end and then set your personal page as your “main” one.

The simplest way is to press and hold any icon until the home‑screen editor appears, then drag every restricted icon into one folder. Once the folder exists, keep opening it and dragging the rest in until they’re all grouped together. After that, drag the entire folder to the far right edge of the screen until it lands on its own last page. When the pages scroll, keep pushing it rightward until you can’t go any farther. Back out of edit mode and you’ll have one tidy cluster on the final page and your personal apps on the front page.

If you don’t want a folder at all, you can instead move each restricted icon individually to the rightmost page the same way—enter edit mode, drag an icon to the right edge until the page flips, and repeat until they’re all collected on the final page.

Once everything is in place, open Settings → Home Screen and make sure the default “first” page is the one with your personal apps. On iOS this simply means putting your personal layout on the leftmost page; there isn’t a selector for a different default page the way some Android launchers allow. iOS always treats the leftmost page as the main page, so your job is just to keep personal apps there and push everything else to the right.

There isn’t an iOS toggle that prevents new managed apps from appearing on the home screen. If IT pushes a new managed app to your device, it will show up automatically. The only thing you can do afterward is move it into the folder or page where you keep the rest of the work‑managed icons.

**User:** Got it. One more thing: if IT pushes a new managed app and it shows up on my first page again, is there any faster way to “catch” it and shove it into that work folder/page without hunting for it?

Also, does using the App Library help here (like hiding whole home screen pages), or will supervised/managed apps still force themselves onto visible pages?

**Assistant:** Unfortunately there’s no automatic way to intercept new managed apps before they land on your first page. When IT pushes one, iOS drops it wherever it thinks there’s room, and you still have to move it manually. The only small “speed‑up” is that new apps almost always appear on the last page that had empty space, so you can usually swipe once or twice and spot it quickly, then drag it straight into your work folder.

Using the App Library doesn’t stop this. Even if you hide entire home‑screen pages, supervised/managed apps will still create a visible icon somewhere on an active page when they’re pushed. The App Library is helpful *after* you’ve moved everything, because you can keep most of your personal apps in the Library and keep the first page very simple, but it won’t block or absorb newly deployed managed apps.

So the routine stays the same: check for the new icon when IT pushes something, drag it to your work folder or work page, and you’re done. There isn’t a faster or more automatic iOS mechanism under supervision.
