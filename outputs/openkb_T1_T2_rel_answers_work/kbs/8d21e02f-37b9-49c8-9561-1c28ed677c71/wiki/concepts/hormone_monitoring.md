# Hormone Monitoring (E2, LH, FSH, Progesterone)

Hormone monitoring is a core component of fertility treatment cycles. By measuring **estradiol (E2)**, **luteinizing hormone (LH)**, **follicle‑stimulating hormone (FSH)**, and **progesterone**, clinics can assess ovarian response, decide when to trigger ovulation, and identify red‑flags that may delay treatment. The details below are drawn from the cheat‑sheet in [[summaries/session-32_2025-10-13]].

---

## 1. Baseline Values (Day 1‑3)
| Hormone | Typical Baseline Range | Clinical Meaning |
|---------|-----------------------|------------------|
| Estradiol (E2) | < 50 pg/mL | Indicates a quiet ovary; low estrogen is expected before follicular growth. |
| LH | Low early‑cycle range (often < 5 mIU/mL) | Quiet LH suggests no premature surge. |
| FSH | Single‑digit to low teens (≈ 3‑12 mIU/mL) | Normal follicular recruitment potential. |
| Progesterone | Very low (< 0.5 ng/mL) | Confirms pre‑ovulatory state. |

These values set the stage for the **monitoring timing** decisions that follow.

---

## 2. Developing Phase (2‑4 days after baseline)
- **Estradiol** rises in step with follicle growth. In a medicated IUI cycle, a single dominant follicle (≈ 11‑14 mm) typically drives E2 to ~ 100‑200 pg/mL. In IVF stimulation, multiple follicles cause a steeper rise, often exceeding 300‑500 pg/mL.
- **LH** remains relatively stable unless an early surge occurs; a sudden jump (> 20 mIU/mL) signals imminent ovulation and may prompt immediate action.
- **FSH** usually stays flat; dramatic spikes are uncommon after the start of stimulation.
- **Progesterone** stays low; any rise above ~ 1 ng/mL before the planned trigger is a red‑flag.

Clinicians compare these trends with the **ultrasound assessment** of follicle size and endometrial thickness to decide whether the cycle is progressing as expected.

---

## 3. Ready‑to‑Trigger / Retrieval Phase
| Cycle Type | Hormone Pattern | Follicle / Lining Targets |
|------------|----------------|---------------------------|
| Medicated IUI | Estradiol consistent with a single mature follicle (≈ 200‑300 pg/mL); LH stable; Progesterone still low. | Dominant follicle 18‑20 mm; lining ≥ 7 mm. |
| IVF Stimulation | Estradiol high (often > 1000 pg/mL) reflecting many mature follicles; LH stable; Progesterone low. | Multiple follicles 17‑20 mm; lining ≥ 7 mm. |

When these hormonal and sonographic criteria align, the clinic schedules the trigger shot (hCG or GnRH‑agonist) and plans IUI or egg retrieval.

---

## 4. Red‑Flags that Delay Progress
- **Progesterone > 1 ng/mL** before trigger – suggests premature luteinization.
- **Unexpected LH surge** (≥ 20 mIU/mL) – may cause ovulation before the planned trigger.
- **Cystic follicle** > 25 mm – can produce excess hormones and distort timing.
- **Endometrial lining not thickening** (no ≥ 0.5 mm increase over 48 h) despite rising estradiol – indicates poor estrogen response.

If any red‑flag appears, clinicians may postpone the trigger, adjust medication, or re‑evaluate the cycle.

---

## 5. Practical Patient Questions
- "What are my current estradiol, LH, FSH, and progesterone levels?"
- "How do these numbers compare to the expected range for my cycle stage?"
- "Is my hormone profile consistent with the ultrasound findings?"
- "Are there any red‑flags that could change the timing of the trigger?"

Understanding hormone monitoring empowers patients to follow the rationale behind **[[concepts/monitoring_timing]]** and **[[concepts/ultrasound_assessment]]** decisions throughout their fertility journey.
