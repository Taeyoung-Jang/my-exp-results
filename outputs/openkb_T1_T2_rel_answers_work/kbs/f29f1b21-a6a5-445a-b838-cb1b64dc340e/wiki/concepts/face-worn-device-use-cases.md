# Practical Use Cases for Face‑Worn Devices

Face‑worn devices (e.g., smart glasses) are most valuable when they **remove friction** from routine activities. The conversation in [[summaries/session-13_2023-06-15]] highlights several concrete scenarios:

## Quick‑Glance Information
- Navigation cues, notifications, or contextual data displayed in the peripheral view so users can glance briefly instead of pulling out a phone.
- Supports fast decision‑making with minimal visual attention.

## Hands‑Free Communication
- Voice‑driven messaging and calls that keep the hands free for other tasks.
- Enables discreet interaction in environments where phone use is cumbersome (e.g., cooking, cycling).

## Audio‑First Prompts
- Subtle audio cues that convey information without demanding visual focus, such as reminders, timers, or real‑time translation.
- Ideal for situations where looking at a screen is unsafe or impractical.

## Task‑Specific Support
- **Cooking:** Step‑by‑step recipe guidance displayed on the lens.
- **Cycling / Commuting:** Turn‑by‑turn directions and traffic alerts without looking down.
- **Tool Work / Assembly:** Overlay instructions or part identifiers directly in the line of sight.

These use cases align with the broader goal of making the device feel like a **convenient extension** of the user rather than a separate gadget. By delivering information **quickly**, **glanceably**, and **hands‑free**, the technology can replace frequent phone checks and streamline everyday workflows.
