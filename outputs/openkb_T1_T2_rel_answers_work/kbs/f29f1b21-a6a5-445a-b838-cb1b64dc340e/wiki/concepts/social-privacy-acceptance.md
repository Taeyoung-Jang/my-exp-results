# Social Acceptance and Privacy Concerns for Wearables

## Overview
Social acceptance and privacy concerns are the primary make‑or‑break factors for face‑worn devices. Even when comfort and battery life are adequate, users often abandon wearables if they feel self‑conscious or cause discomfort for people around them. The perception of being recorded—or the possibility of it—drives much of the hesitation.

## Core Issues
- **Privacy Visibility**: Users need clear, unmistakable signals (e.g., bright recording LEDs, physical camera shutters) to reassure by‑standers that no unwanted capture is occurring.
- **Social Etiquette**: Onboarding cues that teach when to disable the camera and how to signal privacy help establish norms and reduce awkwardness.
- **Design Language**: Glasses that resemble ordinary eyewear blend into everyday settings, lowering the "gadgety" stigma.
- **Behavioral Habits**: Simple user practices—pointing glasses downward during conversation, verbally confirming camera status, removing glasses in close quarters—significantly improve acceptance.

## Impact on Adoption
The document [[summaries/session-13_2023-06-15]] reports that the **social/privacy vibe** outweighs comfort and battery concerns. When users feel that others might be recorded, they are likely to skip using the device, regardless of its functional benefits.

## Design Recommendations
1. **Explicit Indicators** – bright LEDs and physical shutters that are always visible.
2. **Privacy‑First Onboarding** – short tutorials that explain etiquette and how to toggle recording.
3. **Everyday Aesthetic** – lens frames and styles that look like regular glasses.
4. **User‑Driven Signaling** – encourage users to communicate camera status verbally or through gestures.
