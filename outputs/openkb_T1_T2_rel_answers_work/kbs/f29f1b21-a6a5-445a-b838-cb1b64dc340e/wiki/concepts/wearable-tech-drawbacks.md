# Key Drawbacks of Wearable Technology

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-13_2023-06-15]]

## Overview
Wearable devices that are worn on the face or head bring several recurring drawbacks that limit everyday adoption. The session‑13 conversation highlights three dominant categories:

1. **Comfort / Fit** – Devices can feel intrusive or awkward when worn for long periods.
2. **Battery Life** – Limited endurance forces frequent charging, disrupting seamless use.
3. **Social & Privacy Acceptance** – Concerns that the device is recording or that it makes by‑standers uncomfortable.

These issues intersect, often amplifying each other; a bulky, short‑lived device that also looks suspicious will be quickly abandoned.

## Comfort / Fit
- Users report a persistent sensation of intrusion, especially if the hardware is heavy or poorly balanced.
- Even subtle frames can become uncomfortable over several hours, leading to removal during the day.
- The need for a snug fit to keep sensors aligned can clash with natural head movements.

## Battery Life
- Current form‑factors struggle to provide all‑day operation without a noticeable charging routine.
- Limited battery drives designers to cut features or increase device size, worsening the comfort problem.
- Users are willing to tolerate shorter battery spans only if the device delivers clear, high‑value benefits.

## Social & Privacy Acceptance
- The presence of cameras or microphones raises immediate privacy worries among by‑standers.
- Lack of clear visual indicators (e.g., recording LEDs, physical shutters) intensifies suspicion.
- Social discomfort often outweighs comfort or battery concerns; if users feel judged, they stop wearing the device.

## Implications for Design
Designers should prioritize:
- Lightweight, ergonomic form‑factors.
- Efficient power management or removable battery packs.
- Transparent privacy signals such as bright LEDs and physical shutters.
- Onboarding experiences that teach etiquette and set expectations.

Addressing these three pain points is crucial for moving wearable tech from a novelty to a truly ubiquitous utility.
