# Bankruptcy Trustee Red Flags

**Definition**: Behaviors or transactions that may appear to a bankruptcy trustee as attempts to conceal, move, or otherwise alter assets during an active bankruptcy case, potentially leading to investigations or delays.

---

## Key Red‑Flag Actions

1. **Unsanctioned Asset Sales**
   - Selling personal items (e.g., gadgets, furniture) without explicit approval from the trustee or attorney.
   - Even modest sales can raise questions if they occur after the asset inventory has been filed.

2. **Large or Unexplained Transfers**
   - Moving sizable sums between personal accounts, especially to newly opened or obscure accounts.
   - Transfers that lack a clear, documented purpose may be interpreted as asset shielding.

3. **Opening or Closing Accounts Without Reason**
   - Creating new checking/savings accounts or shutting down existing ones without a demonstrable everyday need.
   - The trustee expects a stable, traceable financial picture.

4. **Large Cash Withdrawals**
   - Withdrawing big amounts of cash in a short period can look like an attempt to hide money.
   - Regular, modest withdrawals for living expenses are acceptable.

5. **Changes to Financial Presentation**
   - Any modifications to the way finances are reported (e.g., sudden changes in budgeting methods) that are not communicated to the legal team.

## Why These Actions Matter
- **Transparency** is a core requirement of the bankruptcy process. Trustees must be able to see a clear, consistent flow of money to ensure compliance with the court’s orders.
- **Risk of Delays or Sanctions**: Suspicious behavior can trigger additional inquiries, possibly delaying case resolution or resulting in penalties.
- **Legal Exposure**: Unapproved actions might be construed as fraud, exposing the debtor to legal consequences.

## Recommended Safe Practices
- Keep **one primary checking account** for all day‑to‑day expenses.
- Maintain a **small emergency cushion** (easily documented ordinary income) for routine living costs.
- Use a **bare‑bones budget** that outlines income, essential payments, and remaining discretionary funds – see [[concepts/bare_bones_budget]].
- Document all transactions, retain receipts, and be ready to provide a simple, chronological record to the trustee.
- Before making any significant financial move—opening/closing accounts, large transfers, or selling assets—**consult your bankruptcy attorney or trustee**.
