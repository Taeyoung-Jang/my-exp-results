# Bare‑Bones Budget

**Definition**
A *bare‑bones budget* is a stripped‑down financial plan that lists only the most critical income sources and obligatory expenses. Its purpose is to give a clear, actionable snapshot of cash flow without the complexity of detailed categorisation.

**Core Elements**
1. **Income** – All regular, taxable earnings (salary, benefits, side‑gig income). 
2. **Essential Outflows** – Payments that must stay current to avoid immediate fallout:
   - Housing (rent/mortgage)
   - Utilities (electricity, water, gas)
   - Food & basic household supplies
   - Transportation (fuel, public‑transit passes)
   - Court‑approved or legally required payments (e.g., bankruptcy trustee obligations)
3. **Remaining Balance** – The amount left after covering essentials, which can serve as a tiny emergency cushion or be allocated to debt repayment.

**How to Build One**
- **Step 1:** Write down every source of regular income for the upcoming month.
- **Step 2:** List the *must‑pay* expenses above. Do not include discretionary spending like dining out, entertainment, or optional subscriptions.
- **Step 3:** Subtract the total essential outflows from total income. The result is your *available balance*.
- **Step 4:** Allocate a modest amount (e.g., $200‑$300) as a cash buffer for unexpected minor costs. Anything beyond that can stay as a modest surplus or be directed toward debt.
- **Step 5:** Keep the entire budget on a single sheet or simple digital note; update only when a major change occurs.

**Benefits**
- **Speed:** Can be drafted in under an hour.
- **Clarity:** Highlights exactly what must be protected.
- **Low Stress:** Removes the overwhelm of detailed categorisation, aligning with the advice in [[summaries/session-34_2024-06-15]].
- **Flexibility:** Easy to adjust as income or essential expenses shift.

**Relation to Bankruptcy Situations**
When an active bankruptcy case is in place, the bare‑bones budget helps ensure that *essential* obligations are met while avoiding actions that could trigger [[concepts/bankruptcy_trustee_red_flags]]. By limiting financial moves to strictly necessary items and a small buffer, the budget reduces the risk of appearing to hide assets or manipulate cash flow.

**Practical Tip**
Review the budget once a week, simply by scanning the checking‑account transaction list. If the essential bills remain paid and the buffer stays intact, you’re on track.
