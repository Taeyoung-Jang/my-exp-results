# Summary of session‑13_2023‑06‑15  

**Date:** 2023‑06‑15 14:30  

## Key Practical Uses of Face‑Worn Devices  
- Quick‑glance navigation and glanceable information displays.  
- Hands‑free messaging and audio‑first interactions.  
- Discreet audio prompts and real‑time translation.  
- Support for activities with busy hands (cooking, cycling, commuting, tool work).  

## Main Drawbacks  
- **Comfort / Fit:** Intrusive feeling when worn all day.  
- **Battery Life:** Frequent charging limits continuous use.  
- **Social Acceptance & Privacy:** Concerns about being recorded, awkwardness for by‑standers.  

These issues are covered in [[concepts/wearable-tech-drawbacks]].  

## Most Critical Make‑or‑Break Factor  
The **social/privacy vibe** outweighs comfort and battery; if users feel watched or cause discomfort, adoption drops. See [[concepts/social-privacy-acceptance]].  

## Design Priorities to Reduce Distraction  
- Minimize **cognitive load**: fast, glanceable data; non‑intrusive audio cues.  
- Ensure interactions never feel like operating a mini‑computer on the face.  
- Provide clear, instant intent signals such as:  
  - Bright recording indicator.  
  - Physical camera shutter or cover.  
  - Lens styles that resemble everyday eyewear.  
- Offer onboarding cues that teach etiquette (when to disable camera, how to signal privacy).  

## Strategies to Mitigate Social/Privacy Awkwardness  
- Use **obvious recording indicators** and **physical shutters**.  
- Adopt **everyday‑look lens designs** to blend with normal glasses.  
- Provide **onboarding prompts** about privacy etiquette.  
- Encourage user habits: point glasses down during conversation, verbally note camera‑off status, remove glasses in close‑quarters, hang them visibly on shirt or table.  

## Practical Tips for Current Users  
- Keep the glasses pointed away from people during talks.  
- Mention camera status when sitting with someone.  
- Ensure the physical shutter is closed and visibly so.  
- In cramped spaces, take the glasses off and place them visibly to signal non‑recording.
