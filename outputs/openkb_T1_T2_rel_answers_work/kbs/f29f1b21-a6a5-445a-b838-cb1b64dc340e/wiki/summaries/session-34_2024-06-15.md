# Summary of session-34_2024-06-15

**Main focus:** Practical, low‑stress steps for managing everyday money while an open bankruptcy case is active.

## 1. Immediate financial priorities
- Keep **essential expenses** (rent/mortgage, utilities, food, transportation, court‑approved payments) current first – these are the only items that can cause immediate fallout.
- Once essentials are stable, consider a **tiny emergency cushion** (e.g., a few hundred dollars) to cover small surprises without jeopardising required payments.

## 2. Building a small cash buffer
- A modest amount in a checking account for day‑to‑day living is acceptable and expected by the trustee.
- The buffer must be ordinary income used for ordinary expenses; it should not look like an attempt to hide assets.

## 3. Simple budgeting approach
- Create a **bare‑bones budget** covering:
  1. Income sources.  
  2. Must‑pay expenses (essential bills, court‑approved payments).  
  3. Remaining discretionary money.  
- The budget does **not** need to be perfect; its purpose is to provide a clear picture of cash flow.  

[[concepts/bare_bones_budget]]

## 4. Low‑overhead tracking methods
- Use a single checking account for most transactions and glance at the transaction list once or twice a week.
- Optional low‑tech tricks:
  - Photograph receipts and review later.
  - Write down a daily total instead of categorising each purchase.  
- The goal is awareness, not meticulous categorisation.

## 5. Actions that raise red flags with the trustee
- **Selling assets** on your own without explicit trustee or attorney approval.  
- **Large or unexplained transfers** between accounts, especially to new or obscure accounts.  
- **Closing or opening accounts** without a clear everyday purpose.  
- **Withdrawing big cash amounts** or moving money in ways that could appear to hide assets.  
- **Any move that changes the shape of your finances** without documentation or legal counsel.  

[[concepts/bankruptcy_trustee_red_flags]]

## 6. Transparency and communication
- Keep the financial picture simple, predictable, and easy to explain.  
- Document routine transactions and retain receipts.  
- When in doubt, consult your bankruptcy attorney or trustee before acting.

## 7. Suggested next steps for the user
1. List all **essential monthly obligations** and confirm they are covered.  
2. Set up a **minimal checking‑account cash buffer** (e.g., $200‑$300) for routine expenses.  
3. Draft a **one‑page bare‑bones budget** showing income vs. essential outflows.  
4. Choose a **simple spending‑tracking habit** (weekly transaction glance or daily total).  
5. Create a short **record of any financial move** and share with your attorney for approval when needed.
