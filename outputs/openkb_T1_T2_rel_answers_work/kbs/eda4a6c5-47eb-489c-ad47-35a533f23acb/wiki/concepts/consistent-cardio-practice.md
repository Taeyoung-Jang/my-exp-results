# Consistent Cardio Practice

**Definition**: A training approach that prioritizes doing the same cardio activity reliably 3‑4 times per week, emphasizing habit formation and modest, steady increases in volume rather than chasing the “best” modality.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-07_2025-06-14]])
- **Choose what you’ll stick with** – outdoor running, cycling, or rowing; the key is the activity you can repeat consistently.
- **Start easy** – sessions of 20‑30 minutes at a conversational effort.
- **Gradual overload** – add about 5 minutes each week and introduce a single slightly harder day after the first two weeks. This aligns with [[concepts/progressive-overload-in-endurance]].
- **Monitor for over‑reaching** – watch for fatigue, harder‑than‑expected easy pace, lingering aches, or prolonged elevated heart rate. See [[concepts/overreaching-warning-signs]] for details.
- **Supportive routine** – simple warm‑up (walk + light jog) and cool‑down, plus twice‑weekly strength work targeting core, glutes, and lower‑leg stability.

## Why Consistency Beats Modality
The conversation stresses that endurance gains come from **frequency** and **habit**, not from the specific cardio mode. By keeping the effort low and the schedule predictable, the body adapts without excessive stress, reducing injury risk and improving long‑term adherence.

## Practical Take‑aways
1. **Pick a day pattern** (e.g., Mon‑Wed‑Fri‑Sat) and stick to it.
2. **Maintain easy sessions** for the first two weeks, then add 5 minutes each week.
3. **Introduce one harder session** after week 2, keeping intensity still conversational.
4. **Check warning signs** regularly; if two appear, cut the next session’s duration in half or replace it with a very easy activity.
5. **Add short strength sessions** on non‑cardio days to reinforce joint stability and prevent injuries.

By following these steps, athletes can build a reliable cardio habit that gradually improves endurance while minimizing burnout.
