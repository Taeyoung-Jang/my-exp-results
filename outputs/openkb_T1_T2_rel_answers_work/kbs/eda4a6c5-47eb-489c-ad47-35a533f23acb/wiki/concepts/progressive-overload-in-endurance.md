# Progressive Overload in Endurance Training

Progressive overload is the cornerstone of safely improving cardiovascular endurance. It means **systematically increasing the training stimulus**—usually by adding time, distance, or modest intensity—so the body adapts and becomes more efficient.

## Core Principles
- **Start Easy**: Begin with a workload you can comfortably maintain for the full session (e.g., conversational pace). 
- **Small, Incremental Steps**: Add a modest amount each week (5 minutes of duration or a slight increase in effort). This matches the approach described in [[summaries/session-07_2025-06-14]].
- **Consistency**: Regular sessions (3‑4 times per week) provide the repetition needed for adaptation; this ties into the idea of [[concepts/consistent-cardio-practice]].
- **One Harder Session**: After a few weeks of easy work, insert a single session with a slightly higher intensity to stimulate further gains while still protecting recovery.

## Application to Cardio Modalities
- **Running**: Extend run length by 5 min each week, then introduce a longer continuous run or a gentle progression run.
- **Cycling/Rowing**: Increase easy‑day duration by 5 min and later raise the steady‑effort segment on the “hard” day.
- The plans in the source document follow this pattern, illustrating how progressive overload can be tailored to different activities.

## Balancing Load and Recovery
Even modest increases can become excessive if recovery is insufficient. Watch for the signals in [[concepts/overreaching-warning-signs]]:
- Unusual fatigue before workouts
- Easy pace feeling unusually hard
- Prolonged elevated heart rate after sessions
- Persistent minor aches

If two or more appear, reduce the next session’s volume or intensity.

## Practical Tips
1. **Track Duration/Intensity**: Keep a simple log of minutes and perceived effort.
2. **Increase by ≤10 % per week**: This rule of thumb keeps overload gradual.
3. **Maintain a “hard” day**: Limit it to one per week and keep it only slightly tougher than easy days.
4. **Include Strength Work**: Short, twice‑weekly sessions support the primary cardio stimulus and help prevent injury.

By following these steps, athletes can steadily improve endurance while staying within safe limits, as demonstrated in the 4‑week plans of [[summaries/session-07_2025-06-14]].
