# Mobility Aid Safety

**Definition**: Mobility Aid Safety refers to the set of practices and environmental adjustments that help individuals use devices such as crutches, walkers, or walking boots safely, especially when navigating stairs.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-09-11]])
- **Primary support**: Use a sturdy handrail as the main point of balance; avoid relying solely on the aid itself for support.
- **Foot placement**: Keep the injured or booted foot completely off the step and move it only when safely on level ground.
- **Pacing**: Move at a comfortable, unhurried speed. If you feel wobbly, pause, reset, and continue.
- **Hands free**: Carry items in a backpack or cross‑body bag so both hands remain available for the rail.
- **Environment**: Ensure stairways are well‑lit, free of clutter, and have clear, non‑slippery steps. Adding a chair at the top or bottom can provide an extra balance point.
- **Professional guidance**: Detailed step‑by‑step technique should be demonstrated by a physical therapist.

## Supporting Strategies
- **One‑Level Living**: By consolidating essential items on a single floor, you reduce the frequency of stair use, complementing safe‑aid practices. See [[concepts/one-level-living]].
- **Staging Essentials**: Creating duplicate kits of high‑use items on each level minimizes the need for quick trips that increase risk. See [[concepts/staging-essentials]].

## Practical Tips
1. **Check handrail stability** before each use.
2. **Use a sturdy chair** at stair ends for short rests if needed.
3. **Carry a small tote** for trips between floors to keep hands free.
4. **Increase lighting** in hallways and stairwells to reduce hesitation.
5. **Clear pathways** of obstacles and keep items at waist height.
6. **Consult a PT** for personalized crutch or boot technique.
