# One‑Level Living

**Definition**: A short‑term strategy that groups all high‑use items and living spaces on one floor, creating a temporary “base camp” to minimise the need for stair navigation during periods of limited mobility.

## Core Elements (derived from [[summaries/session-37_2025-09-11]])

1. **Identify the primary floor** – Choose the level where you will spend the longest uninterrupted stretch (e.g., the ground floor at home or the main work area).
2. **Move essential sleep and hygiene gear** – Bring a sofa, guest bed, or fold‑out mattress, plus toiletries, change of clothes, medication, chargers, and personal comfort items to that floor.
3. **Create a staging kit** – Assemble a small tote, backpack, or laundry basket containing duplicates of frequently needed items (lip balm, phone charger, water bottle, snacks). This aligns with [[concepts/staging-essentials]].
4. **Portable organizer** – Use a cross‑body bag or backpack so hands remain free for handrails and crutches, supporting safe movement as described in [[concepts/mobility-aid-safety]].
5. **Lighting and clutter control** – Ensure bright lighting in hallways and keep pathways clear; keep items at waist or counter height to avoid awkward reaching.
6. **Workplace adaptation** – Keep a personal stash at your desk (pain relievers, snacks, water, small documents) and bundle restroom trips (before work, lunch, before leaving) to limit stair use.
7. **Temporary support aids** – Add a sturdy chair at the top/bottom of stairs for extra balance points if needed.

## Benefits
- **Reduced stair trips**: Fewer ascents/descents lower the risk of falls and lessen strain on a healing foot or boot.
- **Increased independence**: By having everything needed on one level, you can manage daily tasks without constant assistance.
- **Energy conservation**: Less physical effort translates to more energy for rehabilitation activities.

## Practical Tips
- Pack all items for a planned floor change into a single bag to avoid multiple trips.
- Duplicate small high‑use objects on each level rather than shuttling them back and forth.
- Communicate with supervisors about flexible break timing to align with your staged setup.
