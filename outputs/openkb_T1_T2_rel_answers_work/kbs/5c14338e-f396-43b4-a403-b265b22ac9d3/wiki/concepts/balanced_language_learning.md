## Balanced Language Learning

Balanced Language Learning is an approach that interleaves short, focused study of grammar or vocabulary with immediate, authentic language production. The goal is to keep learning steady and motivating by treating study material as fuel rather than the main activity.

### Core principles
- **Brief study blocks** – 15‑20 minutes of a single grammar point or a handful of new words.
- **Immediate output** – write a short paragraph, message, or summary right after the study session, using the new material.
- **Regular rhythm** – a weekly schedule that alternates study days with input‑focused days, providing consistent exposure without overload.
- **Self‑correction** – a quick checklist verifies that the target structure was used, agreements are correct, language sounds natural, and at least one learned item is reused. See [[concepts/self_correction_checklist]].
- **Writing‑first focus** – the approach emphasizes written production over speaking, making it suitable for learners who prefer text‑based practice. See [[concepts/writing_first_approach]].

### Example from a source
The weekly plan described in [[summaries/session-09_2024-06-15]] applies Balanced Language Learning to French study. It schedules three study days (Mon, Wed, Fri) with short grammar/vocab sessions followed by writing tasks, and four input days (Tue, Thu, Sat, Sun) that involve reading or listening and then writing short reflections. The plan also includes a simple self‑correction checklist and a feedback loop where the learner sends raw French text for correction.

### Benefits
- **Retention** – immediate use reinforces memory of new forms.
- **Motivation** – frequent production creates a sense of progress.
- **Flexibility** – short blocks fit into busy schedules.
- **Confidence** – learners see tangible results without the pressure of spoken interaction.

### Applicability
While illustrated with French learning, the method can be adapted to any language or skill that benefits from the cycle of input → study → output. See [[entities/french]] for the language context.
