# Speaking as Show‑Up

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** A mindset that frames language‑speaking practice as simply being present and willing to communicate, rather than delivering a flawless performance.

## Core Idea
The concept encourages learners to shift their focus from *evaluation* to *participation*. By treating each interaction as a chance to "show up"—to be there, to try, and to be heard—speakers reduce anxiety, accept mistakes as natural, and build sustainable confidence.

## Key Details from [[summaries/session-39_2025-06-15]]
- **Low‑pressure micro‑interactions** (e.g., ordering coffee, brief phone calls) are used as warm‑ups that reinforce the "show‑up" attitude.
- **Daily missions** are framed as short, concrete tasks rather than exhaustive study sessions, reinforcing the idea that simply being present is valuable.
- **Progress tracking** focuses on time spent speaking in French and recovery speed after a freeze, not on perfect grammar.
- **Panic phrases** provide safety nets, allowing speakers to stay in the conversation without feeling judged.

## Practical Application
1. **Set a daily "show‑up" goal** – e.g., 10 minutes of any spoken French, regardless of content.
2. **Choose a micro‑interaction** – a quick call, a voice note, or a brief chat, treating it as a warm‑up.
3. **Use panic phrases** when you feel stuck, keeping the flow alive.
4. **Reflect** with a self‑correction checklist: note moments of hesitation, how long you stayed in French, and any recovery strategies.
5. **Iterate** – gradually increase interaction length while maintaining the "show‑up" mindset.

## Benefits
- Reduces performance anxiety.
- Encourages consistent, low‑stakes practice.
- Builds confidence through repeated presence.
- Facilitates natural language acquisition by treating speaking as a lived experience.
