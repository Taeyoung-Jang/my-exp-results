# Writing‑First Approach

The **Writing‑First Approach** is a language‑learning methodology that places written production at the core of study, using brief grammar and vocabulary sessions as fuel for immediate writing tasks. It is designed for learners who feel more comfortable expressing themselves in writing than speaking, and it structures practice so that written output consistently follows each input segment.

## Core Principles
- **Write before you speak**: Small, focused study periods (≈20 min) are followed by a short writing activity, ensuring the new material is applied right away.
- **Incremental input**: On non‑study days, learners engage in light reading or listening, then produce a few sentences that recycle discovered language.
- **Feedback loop**: Learners submit raw French text to a tutor or AI for correction. The corrected version, along with explanations, reinforces learning.
- **Self‑correction**: A quick checklist helps learners evaluate their own work before sending it for external feedback.

## Weekly Structure (example from [[summaries/session-09_2024-06-15]])
| Day | Activity | Time | Output | Submission |
|-----|----------|------|--------|------------|
| **Mon** | Grammar/vocab + short paragraph | 20 min + 10‑15 min | Paragraph using new material | Send paragraph for correction |
| **Tue** | Light reading + short sentences | 15 min | 3‑4 sentences inspired by reading | Send sentences |
| **Wed** | Grammar/vocab + new paragraph | 20 min + 10‑15 min | Paragraph on a new topic | Send paragraph |
| **Thu** | Reading/listening + summary | 15 min | Brief summary (4‑5 sentences) | Send summary |
| **Fri** | Light study + casual text message | 20 min + 10‑15 min | Text‑message style paragraph | Send message |
| **Sat** | Longer diary‑style entry | 15‑20 min | Diary entry about the week | Send entry |
| **Sun** | Optional input + reflective sentences | 15 min | 2‑3 reflective sentences | Optional send |

## Self‑Correction Checklist
The approach incorporates the [[concepts/self_correction_checklist]] to help learners quickly verify:
1. Use of the targeted grammar point.
2. Correct verb endings and agreement.
3. Naturalness when read aloud.
4. Reuse of at least one word or phrase from the input.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
The Writing‑First Approach supports a broader [[concepts/balanced_language_learning]] framework by ensuring that study time is balanced with productive output, while the self‑correction checklist provides a systematic way to maintain quality in writing.
