# Micro‑Interactions

**Definition**
Micro‑Interactions are short, low‑stakes conversational moments with real people—such as a quick phone call to a café, a brief voice message, or a 2‑minute shop inquiry. They provide repeated exposure to authentic language while keeping anxiety low, allowing learners to "show up" rather than perform.

**Why they matter**
- They align with the *show‑up* mindset of [[concepts/speaking-as-show-up]], emphasizing presence over perfection.
- By spreading multiple tiny contacts throughout a week, they create a balanced practice schedule, supporting the principles of [[concepts/balanced_language_learning]].
- When a freeze occurs, learners can smoothly fall back on [[concepts/panic-phrases]] to stay in the target language.

**Key elements from [[summaries/session-39_2025-06-15]]**
1. **Phone call to a café (Day 2)** – Ask about opening hours and a specific menu item.
2. **Shop inquiry (Day 6)** – Verify product availability and request a recommendation.
3. **Voice message (Day 4)** – Unscripted 30‑second narration of a daily event.
4. **Meetup participation (Day 5)** – Brief self‑introduction and contribution to group chat.

Each of these examples follows the micro‑interaction pattern: a clear, limited goal, a short duration, and a concrete prompt that can be rehearsed in advance.

**Practical checklist for designing a micro‑interaction**
- **Select a real‑world context** (café, store, online chat, language‑exchange app).
- **Craft 1‑2 concise prompts** (e.g., “Vous êtes ouverts jusqu’à quelle heure aujourd’hui ?”).
- **Set a time limit** (1‑5 minutes) to keep pressure low.
- **Record a quick self‑assessment** using a 1‑5 confidence/comprehension/fluency score.
- **Apply a panic phrase** if needed, then continue.

**Benefits**
- Builds *automaticity* through repetition of functional language.
- Encourages *active listening* and on‑the‑spot thinking.
- Provides measurable data for progress tracking (seconds spoken before switching language, recovery speed).

**Linkage**
Micro‑Interactions serve as the connective tissue between daily practice missions and broader language‑learning frameworks, making them a cornerstone of effective, immersive yet manageable language development.
