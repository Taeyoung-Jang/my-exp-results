# Style Sheet Usage

A **style sheet** is a lightweight reference document that tracks recurring textual decisions so you can apply them consistently throughout a manuscript, especially during the copyedit stage.

## Why Use a Style Sheet?
- Prevents accidental inconsistencies in names, places, and terminology.
- Saves time by providing a single source of truth for repeated formatting rules.
- Acts as a memory aid, allowing you to focus on higher‑level edits without re‑checking details.
- Supports the workflow described in [[concepts/revision_passes]] and the prioritisation outlined in [[concepts/copyedit_priorities]].

## Typical Sections (as exampled in [[summaries/session-33_2025-03-12]])
- **Names** – Full name at first mention, preferred spelling, titles (e.g., *Elias Maren*, *Dr. Fallow*).
- **Places** – Canonical spelling and capitalization (e.g., *Northbridge*, *The Lower Docks*).
- **Timeline Facts** – Key temporal anchors (e.g., "Prologue set in spring", "Main story begins six years after prologue").
- **Italics & Capitals** – Rules for ship names, internal thoughts, magic orders, spells.
- **Numbers** – When to spell out versus use numerals, time notation.
- **Hyphenation & Compounds** – Specific hyphenated forms (e.g., *long‑forgotten*, *ice‑blue*).

## How to Maintain It
1. **Add entries on the fly** whenever you encounter a new name, place, or rule.
2. **Review the sheet at the start of each copy‑edit pass** (see the buffer‑day schedule in [[concepts/buffer_days_in_writing_schedules]]).
3. **Use it to inform decisions in the flowchart** described in [[concepts/decision_flowchart_for_editing]] – if an edit conflicts with an entry, you know whether to accept, query, or override.

## Integration with the 10‑Day Game Plan
During the first several days you populate the sheet with core elements (names, places, timeline facts). Later passes (language polish, tone check) reference the sheet to ensure you don’t unintentionally deviate from established conventions.

## Benefits
- **Consistency** across the whole manuscript, reducing reader distraction.
- **Efficiency** – fewer second‑guess moments, especially on tight deadlines.
- **Clarity for collaborators** – editors can see your stylistic intent at a glance.
