# Copyedit Priorities

**Definition**: The set of high‑impact editing targets that writers should address first when copy‑editing a manuscript under a strict deadline.

---

## Core Priorities (as outlined in [[summaries/session-33_2025-03-12]])
1. **Continuity & Factual Consistency**
   - Verify character ages, timelines, and any world‑building details (names of places, spellings, dates). These errors are the most noticeable to readers and can break immersion.
2. **Clarity‑Related Language Issues**
   - Fix glaring awkward phrasing, misused words, or register mismatches that cause momentary confusion. Aim for *touch‑up* fixes, not full rewrites.
3. **Mechanical Polish**
   - Scan for repeated words, redundant sentences, and minor punctuation errors. This is a low‑pressure pass that smooths the text without altering its voice.
4. **Flag Major Structural Concerns**
   - If an edit would require a substantial rewrite (e.g., altering a plot point or major scene), note it for the editor instead of resolving it yourself.

## How It Connects to Other Concepts
- The prioritization fits within the broader **[[concepts/revision_passes]]** framework, where the first pass handles clarity, the second handles voice/rhythm, and the third addresses mechanical issues.
- Maintaining a concise **[[concepts/style_sheet_usage]]** helps track continuity details (names, places, timeline facts) so they can be addressed efficiently.
- The decision‑making process aligns with the **[[concepts/decision_flowchart_for_editing]]**, offering a quick accept/query/override routine for each change.
- Buffer periods, as described in **[[concepts/buffer_days_in_writing_schedules]]**, give space to revisit flagged items from the copyedit priorities without rushing the main passes.

---

## Practical Checklist
- ☐ Verify every proper noun against the style sheet (spelling, capitalization).
- ☐ Cross‑check chronological references (e.g., "six years after the prologue").
- ☐ Accept any edit that *improves clarity* or *fixes a clear error*.
- ☐ Override edits that *intentionally alter tone* or *conflict with established facts*.
- ☐ Query any change that feels ambiguous or would require a larger rewrite.

---

## Benefits
- **Speed**: Focuses effort on edits that most affect reader perception.
- **Consistency**: Reduces factual slip‑ups that are hard to spot when tired.
- **Voice Preservation**: By limiting deep rewrites, the author's unique tone remains intact.
