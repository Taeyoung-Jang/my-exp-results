# Green and Red Flags in Career Evaluation

**Scope**: This concept outlines how to interpret *green* (positive) and *red* (negative) signals when assessing potential fields of study or career paths. It draws on the advice and examples from [[summaries/session-12_2021-10-12]].

## Connection to Decision Making

Evaluating a field involves multiple **decision‑criteria** such as personal interest, job‑market outlook, skill transferability, and flexibility. Green‑ and red‑flags act as qualitative filters that help weight these criteria during the selection process. See [[concepts/decision-criteria]] for the broader framework.

## Typical Green Flags (Positive Indicators)
- **Concrete, engaging day‑to‑day description** – the professional can detail varied tasks that sound interesting.
- **Enjoyable core tasks** – the parts of the job they look forward to align with your own interests.
- **Positive surprises** – unexpected aspects like creativity, autonomy, or supportive culture.
- **Relevant everyday skills** – daily work relies on abilities you enjoy or wish to develop.
- **Career flexibility** – ease of moving between roles, industries, or upskilling.
- **Growth‑oriented industry trends** – the field is expanding, adapting, or showing steady demand.
- **Personality fit** – the description of the ideal person sounds like you.
- **Helpful hindsight advice** – concrete tips that would have benefited you as an undergraduate.

## Typical Red Flags (Warning Signs)
- **Vague or “it depends” answers** about daily work, suggesting burnout or lack of clarity.
- **Dominance of disliked tasks** – most of the day is spent on activities you dread.
- **Negative surprises** – high stress, monotony, or limited autonomy.
- **Reliance on unwanted skills** – the job mainly requires abilities you dislike or lack.
- **Narrow, hard‑to‑pivot career path** – limited options for transition or advancement.
- **Shrinking or volatile market** – industry contraction or instability.
- **Misaligned personality profile** – success requires traits vastly different from yours.
- **Regret‑laden advice** – expressions of wishing they had chosen a different path for concerning reasons.

## Using Flags in an Informational Interview
When conducting an [[concepts/informational-interview]], structure your questions to elicit both green and red signals (see the question list in the source summary). After each response, note your emotional reaction:
- **Lean‑in / excitement** → potential green flag.
- **Hesitation / disengagement** → potential red flag.

Synthesizing these signals across multiple contacts helps you prioritize majors or careers that consistently generate green flags and avoid those flagged red.
