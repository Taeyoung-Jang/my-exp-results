# Decision Criteria for Field Selection

**Definition**: The set of primary considerations that help individuals evaluate and choose an academic discipline or major that aligns with personal goals and future career prospects.

## Core Criteria (as discussed in [[summaries/session-12_2021-10-12]])

1. **Genuine Interest** – Sustained curiosity and enjoyment in the subject matter; the motivation to engage with the material over the long term.
2. **Job‑Market Health** – Current and projected demand for professionals in the field, including salary prospects and employment stability.
3. **Transferable Skills** – Abilities that are applicable across multiple roles or industries (e.g., analytical thinking, communication, coding), increasing career flexibility.
4. **Flexibility & Adaptability** – The degree to which the field allows movement between sectors, adaptation to new technologies, or pivoting into related disciplines.

## Connecting Concepts
- The evaluation of these criteria often informs the framing of questions in an [[concepts/informational-interview]] to gather real‑world insights.
- Interpreting answers using the [[concepts/green-red-flags]] framework helps identify whether the criteria are met (green flags) or present concerns (red flags).

## Practical Use
When exploring potential majors, use the criteria as a checklist and seek evidence through low‑stakes experiences (online courses, books, talks) and conversations with professionals. Positive signals across these dimensions suggest a strong alignment between the field and your long‑term goals.
