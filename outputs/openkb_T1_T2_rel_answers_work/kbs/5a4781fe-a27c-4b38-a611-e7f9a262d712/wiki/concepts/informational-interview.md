# Informational Interview Strategies

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-12_2021-10-12]]

Informational interviews are low‑stakes conversations with professionals or senior students aimed at uncovering the real‑world nature of a field or role. They complement personal exploration methods and help apply the broader **decision criteria** for selecting a study path.

## Core Question Set
1. **Typical day description** – Look for concrete, varied tasks (green flag) versus vague or “it depends” answers (red flag).
2. **Enjoyable vs. disliked tasks** – Align the enjoyable parts with your interests; dominance of disliked tasks signals a warning.
3. **Surprises after joining the field** – Positive surprises (creativity, autonomy) are green flags; negative ones (stress, monotony) are red flags.
4. **Everyday vs. school‑only skills** – Identify skills you’ll use daily; relevance to your strengths is a green flag.
5. **Career flexibility** – Ability to move between roles/industries indicates a flexible path (green); a narrow, hard‑to‑pivot field warns otherwise.
6. **Industry evolution** – Growth or adaptability signals health; shrinking or volatile markets raise concerns.
7. **Ideal personality fit** – When the described successful traits match you, it’s a green flag.
8. **Undergraduate hindsight advice** – Concrete, helpful advice is encouraging; regret‑laden or discouraging reflections suggest red flags.

## How to Use the Answers
- **Emotional gauge:** Notice whether each response makes you lean in (interest) or pull back (disinterest). This gut reaction often outweighs the factual content.
- **Flag assessment:** Map each answer to the **green‑red‑flags** framework to quickly spot promising versus risky aspects of a major.

## Connection to Decision‑Making
These strategies inform the **decision‑criteria** you apply when choosing a field of study: they help assess interest, market relevance, transferable skills, and flexibility through real‑world insights.
