## Summary of Session 12 (2021-10-12)

**Main focus:** How to evaluate potential fields of study and gather actionable information from professionals to inform major selection.

### Core ideas
- **Decision criteria** for selecting a study field: genuine interest, job‑market health, transferable skills, and flexibility [[concepts/decision-criteria]].
- **Low‑stakes exploration** methods: short courses, beginner books, talks, following practitioners, and imagined‑week exercises (plain text, not linked).
- **Informational interview** strategy: ask about daily tasks, enjoyable vs. dreaded aspects, unexpected realities, essential vs. peripheral skills, career flexibility, industry evolution, personality fit, and hindsight advice [[concepts/informational-interview]].
- **Green‑flag vs. red‑flag** signals: concrete engaging day‑to‑day descriptions, alignment of enjoyable tasks with personal interests, positive surprises, relevance of everyday skills, ability to move across roles, growth‑oriented industry trends, personality compatibility, and helpful undergraduate advice [[concepts/green-red-flags]].

### Recommended question list (8‑10)
1. *What does a typical day look like for you?* – Green: varied, engaging; Red: vague, “it depends”.
2. *Which tasks do you look forward to and which do you avoid?* – Green: enjoyable tasks match the asker’s interests; Red: disliked tasks dominate.
3. *What surprised you about the field after you started?* – Green: creative/autonomous aspects; Red: stress, monotony.
4. *Which skills do you use daily versus those only needed in school?* – Green: alignment with personal strengths; Red: reliance on disliked skills.
5. *How flexible is your career path?* – Green: easy to move between roles/industries; Red: narrow, hard to pivot.
6. *How has the field changed in recent years?* – Green: growing, adaptable opportunities; Red: shrinking or volatile market.
7. *What type of person thrives in this major/career?* – Green: description matches the asker; Red: requires a fundamentally different profile.
8. *What do you wish you’d known as an undergraduate?* – Green: concrete, helpful advice; Red: regret‑laden, discouraging reflections.

### Practical takeaway
Use the above questions in informational interviews, then gauge your emotional reaction to each answer. Positive, “lean‑in” reactions function as personal green flags, while hesitation or disengagement signals potential red flags.
