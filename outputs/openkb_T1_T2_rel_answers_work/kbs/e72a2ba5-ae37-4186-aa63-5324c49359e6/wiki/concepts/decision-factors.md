# Decision Factors

**Definition**

Decision factors are the internal cues and external conditions that help a person choose between maintaining a current state ("settle‑in") and initiating a significant change ("shake things up").

---

## Core Components (as outlined in [[summaries/session-08_2024-06-15]])

1. **Energy perception** – How the body feels toward each option. A heavier feeling often signals a pull toward staying, while a lighter feeling signals openness to change.
2. **Current needs vs. growth potential** – Whether the present situation still offers room for development or feels like a stopgap.
3. **Sense of direction** – Whether the idea of change feels like genuine progress or merely an escape from discomfort.
4. **Emotional tone** – Tension indicates resistance; curiosity or relief indicates alignment.

These elements combine to create a quick, embodied read‑out that can guide decisions without extensive analytical over‑thinking.

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/habit-formation]]** – Regularly checking in on these factors can become a habit, making the decision process more automatic and less stressful.
- **[[concepts/runway-mindset]]** – When adopting a long‑term "settle‑in" plan, treating the period as a runway helps align decision factors with strategic, compound growth rather than short‑term comfort.

---

## Practical Application

1. **Pause and sense** – When faced with a choice, pause for a few breaths and note the bodily sensation toward each path.
2. **Label the cues** – Identify which of the core components are most prominent (e.g., “I feel tension staying” or “I feel relief exploring”).
3. **Align with goals** – Match the dominant cue to your broader objectives (health, career, relationships, etc.).
4. **Iterate** – Re‑evaluate periodically, especially during monthly reviews, to ensure the factors remain accurate.
