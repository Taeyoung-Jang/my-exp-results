# Address Change Management

**Definition**: A coordinated set of actions to ensure all relevant parties are notified of a new address, preventing service interruptions, missed mail, and financial complications.

## Why It Matters
During a relocation, failure to update addresses promptly can lead to:
- Missed billing statements and payments.
- Gaps in utility services.
- Loss of important mail, especially from banks and government agencies.
- Inconveniences with subscriptions and loyalty programs.

## Core Steps (Derived from [[summaries/session-34_2025-06-15]])
1. **Create a Master List** – Compile every account, subscription, and service that requires an address update. Use a single note or spreadsheet to track status.
2. **Prioritize Critical Entities** – Focus first on:
   - Employer HR/payroll
   - Banks and credit cards
   - Insurance providers (health, auto, renters)
   - Government IDs (driver’s license, state ID) and vehicle registration
   - Medical providers (doctors, dentist, pharmacy)
   - [[entities/usps]] mail forwarding (submit at least one week before move‑in)
3. **Schedule Updates** – Allocate specific days for each category (e.g., banking on Day 15, medical on Day 14, subscriptions on Day 13) to avoid missed deadlines.
4. **Leverage Online Portals** – Most banks, utilities, and subscription services allow address changes via their websites or mobile apps, saving time.
5. **Confirm Confirmation** – After each update, capture confirmation emails or screenshots as proof.
6. **Review & Follow‑Up** – One week before moving day, verify that all critical changes are processed; follow up on any pending items.

## Timing Considerations
- **USPS Forwarding**: Initiate at least 7 days before the move to allow processing time.
- **Utility Transitions**: Align shut‑off/start dates with move‑in day to avoid service gaps; see [[concepts/utility-transition-planning]] for detailed coordination.
- **Subscription Pauses/Cancellations**: Adjust or pause gym memberships, subscription boxes, and streaming services at least two weeks out to avoid unwanted charges.

## Integration with Moving Checklists
Address change tasks fit naturally into a moving‑checklist framework. In a 3‑week plan, they are typically scheduled in the third week, after initial packing and before final move‑out cleaning. See [[concepts/moving-checklist-design]] for templates that embed address‑change milestones.

## Decision Factors
When planning address updates, consider:
- **Impact on Financial Flow** – banks and credit cards are high‑priority due to billing cycles.
- **Legal Requirements** – driver’s license and vehicle registration changes are often mandated by law.
- **Service Dependency** – utilities and internet service need precise timing to avoid outages; tie these into [[concepts/utility-transition-planning]].
