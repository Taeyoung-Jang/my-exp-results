# Budgeting

Budgeting is the process of planning how to allocate income across essential costs, discretionary spending, savings, debt repayment, and automated investments to achieve financial stability and meet personal goals.

## Core Steps Demonstrated in Session 14
1. **Inventory Current Income and Expenses**
   - Net monthly take‑home: $3,600.
   - Fixed essentials (rent, utilities, phone, car loan, insurance, gas, basic maintenance) total about $3,317.
2. **Identify Discretionary Categories**
   - Subscriptions (Netflix, Spotify, gym), eating out, and higher‑range grocery spending.
3. **Set Target Allocations**
   - Keep essential expenses stable.
   - Trim groceries to the lower end of the range and eating‑out to ~$160.
   - Pause non‑essential subscriptions (gym) to free up $45.
4. **Allocate Surplus**
   - Direct $150‑$200 each month to a starter emergency fund (~$3,000).
   - Use remaining discretionary surplus as a buffer for irregular costs.
5. **Iterate and Adjust**
   - Reassess after the emergency fund goal is met; shift more money toward extra car‑loan payments while maintaining a small ongoing savings contribution.

## Additional Steps Demonstrated in Session 38
1. **Automate by Category**
   - **Fixed essentials** (rent, utilities, insurance, phone, debt) are auto‑debited immediately after each payday.
   - **Future‑you money** – automatic transfers to a savings account or brokerage; can be split across the 1st and 15th paychecks or done once a month.
   - **Flexible spending** – a separate checking account receives the remainder for day‑to‑day purchases.
2. **Payday Timing**
   - Align transfers to occur within a day of the 1st and 15th paychecks (see [[concepts/payday-timing]]). This ensures money is out of sight before it can be spent.
3. **Buffer Management**
   - Maintain a modest cash cushion in the bills/checking account (≈ $200‑$300 when rent is present; $150‑$200 when rent is absent) to absorb utility swings and subscription creep (see [[concepts/buffer]]).
4. **Investment Automation**
   - Either split the monthly brokerage contribution between the two paychecks for smoother cash flow or consolidate into a single transfer after the first paycheck, depending on comfort level (see [[concepts/investment-automation]]).
5. **Pitfalls to Watch**
   - Forgetting to update automations after life changes.
   - Assuming autopay eliminates the need for periodic statement reviews.
   - Over‑tight scheduling that breaks when an irregular bill appears.
   - Ignoring subscription creep – small recurring charges that erode the budget.

## Linking Budgeting to Other Concepts
- The **cost‑cutting** approach identifies low‑pain adjustments (e.g., reducing eating out, pausing the gym) that create budgetary breathing room.
- Building an **emergency‑fund** is a primary budgeting priority before accelerating debt repayment.
- Defining **essential‑expenses** helps separate non‑negotiable costs from discretionary spending.
- Using simple **expense‑tracking‑methods** such as a dedicated “eating‑out wallet” supports the budgeting process without complex spreadsheets.
- Aligning budget allocations with **financial‑goals** (e.g., debt reduction vs. savings growth) ensures the plan serves long‑term objectives.
- A **buffer** provides safety for variable bills and subscription changes.
- Proper **payday‑timing** and **investment‑automation** streamline cash flow and reduce manual oversight.
