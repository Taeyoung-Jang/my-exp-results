## Emergency Fund

An **emergency fund** is a liquid cash reserve set aside to cover unexpected expenses or income interruptions. It provides financial stability, allowing you to avoid high‑interest debt when emergencies arise.

### Core Principles
1. **Starter Goal**: Aim for roughly one month of your *essential expenses* as an initial target. In the context of the budget discussed in [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-15]], this equates to about **$3,000**.
2. **Funding Rate**: Contribute a consistent amount each month (e.g., **$150‑$200**) until the starter goal is reached. At $200 per month, the $3,000 target is met in about six months.
3. **Post‑Goal Allocation**: After reaching the starter fund, keep a smaller contribution (e.g., **$50/month**) to continue growing the reserve, while directing the remaining discretionary cash (e.g., **$150/month**) toward accelerated debt repayment, such as the car loan.
4. **Scaling the Goal**: Once debt pressure eases, increase the target to cover **2‑3 months of essential expenses** for greater security.

### What Counts as Essential Expenses?
Essential expenses are costs you cannot pause during an emergency. Based on the source budget, they include:
- Rent
- Utilities (electric, gas, water)
- Internet
- Phone bill
- Groceries
- Car loan principal & interest
- Car insurance
- Gas for transportation
- Basic car maintenance

Non‑essential items like **subscriptions** (e.g., Netflix, Spotify), dining out, and optional services are excluded from the essential total.

- **[[concepts/financial-goals]]**: Prioritizing an emergency fund is often the first step before tackling accelerated debt repayment.

### Practical Tips
- **Automate Contributions**: Set up an automatic transfer to a separate high‑yield savings account right after payday.
- **Use Separate Accounts**: Keep the emergency fund in an account you don’t use for everyday spending to reduce temptation.
- **Review Periodically**: As your essential expenses change (e.g., moving or renegotiating rent), adjust the fund target accordingly.

By following these guidelines, you create a safety net that protects your financial health while still allowing progress on other goals like paying off the car loan.
