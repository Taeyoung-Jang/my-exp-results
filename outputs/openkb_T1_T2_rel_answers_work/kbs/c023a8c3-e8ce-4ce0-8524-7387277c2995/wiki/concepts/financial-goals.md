# Financial Goals

**Definition**: A set of prioritized actions that balance building a safety net, reducing debt, and maintaining an acceptable lifestyle while leveraging automation for consistency.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-15]] and [[summaries/session-38_2025-05-16]])
1. **Establish a starter emergency fund**
   - Target: ≈ $3,000, roughly one month of *essential expenses*.
   - Contribution: $150‑$200 each month until the goal is reached (about 6 months at $200/month).
2. **Prioritize emergency savings over extra debt payments**
   - While the fund is below the target, extra cash should flow to the emergency fund rather than accelerating the $410 car loan.
3. **Introduce a small, permanent buffer**
   - Keep $150‑$300 in a checking/bills account to absorb utility swings, subscription creep, and irregular costs. This aligns with the [[concepts/buffer]] practice.
4. **Leverage payday‑timed automation**
   - With bi‑monthly pay (1st & 15th), schedule automatic transfers for essentials, savings, and investments immediately after each paycheck. See [[concepts/payday-timing]].
5. **Automate investment contributions**
   - Split the monthly brokerage transfer across the two paydays (or use a single monthly pull) to smooth cash flow and maintain a “set‑it‑and‑forget‑it” rhythm. Refer to [[concepts/investment-automation]].
6. **Shift allocation after the starter fund is met**
   - Suggested split: **$50** per month continues to grow the emergency fund, **$150** (or the remaining freed‑up amount) goes toward an extra car‑loan payment.
   - Re‑evaluate the emergency‑fund goal once the car loan balance drops or cash flow improves; aim for 2‑3 months of *essential expenses*.
7. **Maintain flexibility**
   - Keep a modest cushion (see point 3) and review all automations monthly to adjust for life changes or new recurring charges.

## Practical Steps
1. **Calculate essential monthly expenses** using the figures from the session (rent $1,450, utilities $160, internet $65, phone $55, groceries ~$400, car loan $410, insurance $155, gas $130, maintenance $60 ≈ $3,000).
2. **Set up automatic transfers** after each payday:
   - Move $150‑$200 to a high‑yield savings account for the emergency fund.
   - Transfer half of the intended monthly brokerage amount on the 1st and the other half on the 15th (or a single transfer after the 1st).
   - Refill a designated “bills” checking account with enough to cover all fixed and variable expenses, plus the agreed‑upon buffer ($150‑$300).
3. **Maintain the buffer** in the bills account to cover utility fluctuations and occasional subscription surprises.
4. **Monitor progress** monthly:
   - Review the emergency‑fund balance.
   - Verify that automations executed on schedule (payday‑timed transfers).
   - Adjust the buffer size if you notice consistent overruns.
5. **Re‑assess annually**:
   - If the car loan is significantly reduced, increase emergency‑fund contributions to reach a 2‑3‑month cushion.
   - Consider reallocating any surplus toward debt repayment or additional investing, following the split strategy outlined above.
