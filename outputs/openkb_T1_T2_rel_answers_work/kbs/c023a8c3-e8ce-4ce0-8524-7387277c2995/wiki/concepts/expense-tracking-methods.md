# Expense Tracking Methods

Effective expense tracking helps you stay within a budget, identify low‑friction cuts, and build an emergency fund. Below are common approaches, illustrated with examples from [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-15]].

## 1. Dedicated “wallet” or envelope system
- Allocate a fixed amount of cash, a prepaid card, or a separate debit card for discretionary categories such as **eating out**. In the session‑14 conversation the user was advised to load **$160** into a specific “eating‑out wallet” and stop spending once it’s depleted. This tactile limit reduces the need for daily spreadsheet updates.
- Works well for other variable costs like **groceries** or **miscellaneous purchases**.

## 2. Simple digital tools
- Use budgeting apps that let you set category limits and send a notification when you approach the cap. The tool should sync with your bank to avoid manual entry but keep the interface minimal to prevent tracking fatigue.
- Pair the app with the wallet method for categories you prefer to handle in cash.

## 3. Periodic “snapshot” reviews
- Instead of recording every transaction, take a weekly glance at your bank statements focusing on the major categories: rent, utilities, subscriptions, and the discretionary buckets you’re monitoring.
- Adjust the next week’s allocation based on the snapshot, e.g., reduce the eating‑out envelope if you overspent.

## 4. Automated categorization
- Set up automatic transfers to separate accounts for **savings**, **emergency fund**, and **extra debt payments**. For example, after covering essential expenses, an automatic $150 transfer to the emergency fund and $50 to the car loan can enforce discipline without daily effort.
