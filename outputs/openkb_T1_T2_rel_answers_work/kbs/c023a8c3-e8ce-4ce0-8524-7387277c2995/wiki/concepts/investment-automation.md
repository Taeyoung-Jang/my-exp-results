## Investment Automation

**Definition**: Investment automation is the practice of scheduling regular, hands‑off transfers of money from checking or a bills account into a brokerage or investment vehicle, timed to match income events and financial objectives.

### Why Automate?
- **Consistency** – Guarantees contributions each month, supporting long‑term growth.
- **Simplicity** – Removes the need to remember dates or manually initiate transfers.
- **Cash‑flow harmony** – Aligns investments with pay periods, preventing overdrafts.

### Core Elements (as described in [[summaries/session-38_2025-05-16]])
1. **Payday timing** – Use the bi‑monthly pay schedule (1st & 15th) as anchors. Transfer a portion of the intended monthly investment amount immediately after each paycheck arrives. This follows the guidance in [[concepts/payday-timing]].
2. **Split vs. single transfer** –
   - *Split*: Half the contribution on the 1st, half on the 15th. Keeps each paycheck balanced and smooths cash flow.
   - *Single*: One full transfer after the first paycheck if cash flow permits and rent is no longer a fixed expense.
3. **Buffer** – Maintain a modest safety cushion in the bills/checking account (e.g., $150‑$300) to absorb variable expenses before the investment transfer occurs. See [[concepts/buffer]] for more on why a buffer matters.
4. **Link to broader budgeting** – Investment automation is a sub‑component of an overall [[concepts/budgeting]] system that also covers essential expenses, savings, and discretionary spending.

### Practical Example from the Source Session
- **1st payday**: Transfer $X to the bills account, then move 50% of the monthly investment target to the brokerage.
- **15th payday**: Transfer the remaining 50% of the investment amount and top‑up the bills account for later‑month subscriptions.
- **Buffer**: Keep $200‑$300 in the bills account as a safeguard against utility fluctuations.

### Pitfalls to Watch
- **Failing to adjust amounts** when income or expense patterns change.
- **Over‑tight scheduling** that leaves no room for irregular bills, causing overdrafts.
- **Ignoring subscription creep** – Small recurring charges can erode the amount available for investing.
