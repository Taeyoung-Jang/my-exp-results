# Essential Expenses

**Definition**: Essential expenses are the recurring costs that a household must cover to maintain basic living standards and cannot realistically be stopped or delayed in an emergency.

## What Counts as Essential (based on [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-15]])
- **Housing**: Rent ($1,450)
- **Utilities**: Electricity, gas, water ($160) and Internet ($65)
- **Communication**: Phone bill ($55)
- **Transportation**: Car loan ($410), insurance ($155), fuel/gas ($130), routine maintenance ($60)
- **Food**: Groceries (target $400 per month)

These items align with the "fixed essentials" category described in [[summaries/session-38_2025-05-16]], which emphasizes that rent/mortgage, utilities, insurance, phone, and any debt payments are the core essential expenses that should be automated immediately after each payday.

## What Is Usually Excluded
- **Subscriptions**: Netflix, Spotify, gym membership, etc.
- **Discretionary Spending**: Eating out, take‑out, entertainment, miscellaneous household items.
- **Optional Services**: Cable, premium streaming, non‑essential memberships.

## Automation and Buffer Considerations (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-05-16]])
- **Payday Timing**: Essential expenses should be funded right after each paycheck (e.g., on the 1st and 15th) using the [[concepts/payday-timing]] approach. This ensures that rent, utilities, and other fixed essentials are covered before any discretionary spending occurs.
- **Buffer**: A small cash cushion in the checking or bills account helps absorb fluctuations in variable essential costs such as utilities. The recommended starting buffer is roughly $200–$300 when rent is present, or $150–$200 when rent is absent, linking directly to the [[concepts/buffer]] concept.
- **Investment Automation**: While not an essential expense, the routine transfer to a brokerage account can be split across paychecks or done once a month, following [[concepts/investment-automation]] guidelines, without jeopardizing the coverage of essential expenses.

## Relevance to Other Concepts
- Understanding **[[concepts/budgeting]]** requires first identifying essential expenses to calculate the baseline cash‑outflow.
- **[[concepts/cost-cutting]]** strategies often focus on reducing non‑essential items while preserving the essential core.
- The size of an **[[concepts/emergency-fund]]** is commonly defined as a multiple of essential expenses (e.g., one to three months of the costs listed above).
- Prioritizing **[[concepts/financial-goals]]**—such as building an emergency cushion before accelerating debt repayment—relies on a clear essential‑expenses baseline.
- Simple **[[concepts/expense-tracking-methods]]** like a dedicated “essential‑expenses” envelope or a budgeting app can help ensure these costs are always covered.
