# Cost Cutting

**Definition**: A set of low‑friction, high‑impact actions that reduce discretionary expenses while preserving overall quality of life.

## Core Elements

1. **Identify high‑impact discretionary categories** – In the budget from [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-15]] the biggest flexible costs were groceries, eating out, and optional subscriptions.
2. **Set modest target reductions** – e.g., bring groceries toward the low end of the observed range, cap eating‑out at $160/month, and pause non‑essential memberships (gym, streaming services).
3. **Implement simple tracking mechanisms** – Use a dedicated “eating‑out wallet” or prepaid card to enforce the cap without daily spreadsheets.
4. **Prioritize cuts that feel least restrictive** – The goal is to free $150‑$180 per month with minimal lifestyle disruption.

## How It Fits Within a Financial Plan

- **Budgeting** – Cost‑cutting is a tactical layer that refines the numbers produced in [[concepts/budgeting]].
- **Emergency Fund Building** – Freed cash is first directed to a starter emergency fund, as described in [[concepts/emergency-fund]].
- **Financial Goals** – By preserving a buffer, cost‑cutting supports broader objectives like debt repayment and savings growth ([[concepts/financial-goals]]).
- **Essential vs. Non‑essential** – The approach distinguishes *essential expenses* (rent, utilities, groceries, car costs) from *non‑essential* items such as streaming services (e.g., [[entities/netflix]], [[entities/spotify]]) and discretionary dining.

## Practical Steps (Illustrated by Session 14)

| Step | Action | Expected Savings |
|------|--------|------------------|
| 1 | Trim groceries to the low end ($380→$400) | $20‑$70 |
| 2 | Cap eating‑out at $160 (down from $220) | $60 |
| 3 | Pause gym membership ($45) | $45 |
| **Total** |  | **$125‑$175** |

These modest adjustments generate enough surplus to allocate $150‑$200 each month toward an emergency fund, achieving a $3,000 starter cushion in about six months.
