## Visa Logistics

**Definition**: The systematic planning and execution of obtaining, maintaining, and leveraging a work‑related visa for an international relocation.

### Core Elements
1. **Identify the correct visa category** – The specific work permit determines eligibility, employer sponsorship requirements, and the rights (e.g., spouse work permission, job‑switch flexibility).  
2. **Employer responsibilities** – Filing the application, providing supporting documents, and communicating filing dates and approval notifications.
3. **Timeline management** – Track filing date, expected processing time, approval date, and the earliest lawful entry date.  Delays cascade to travel plans, registration deadlines, and payroll setup.
4. **Post‑approval implications** – How the visa ties you to an employer, renewal windows, and required documentation for downstream steps such as IRP registration, PPS number, and bank account opening.

### Key Failure Points (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-14]])
- Assuming the employer’s immigration team will automatically stay on top of filings.
- Not verifying the exact **earliest lawful entry date**; entering too early or too late can trigger border issues.
- Overlooking the need for documented proof of address and other paperwork for [[concepts/irp_registration_process]].
- Ignoring the timing of renewal windows, which can require proof of ongoing employment well before the visa expires.

### Practical Checklist Elements (derived from the source)
| Step | Owner | Proof | Slip Consequence |
|------|-------|-------|------------------|
| Work authorisation application filed | Employer | Confirmation email | All downstream dates shift |
| Work authorisation approved | Employer | Approval letter | Travel may need to be postponed |
| Earliest lawful entry date | Me | Approval letter | Border complications |
| IRP registration appointment | Me (or employer‑assisted) | Appointment confirmation | Risk of status gap |
