# Break‑Even Cost Analysis for Housing

**Definition**

Break‑Even Cost Analysis (BECA) is a simple financial framework for deciding whether to rent or buy a home. It estimates the point in time when the cumulative costs of owning (including upfront expenses and ongoing monthly outlays) are matched—or surpassed—by the cumulative costs of renting.

---

## Core Steps
1. **Gather Inputs**
   - Monthly rent
   - Home price
   - Down‑payment percentage
   - Mortgage interest rate (term usually 30 years)
   - Property‑tax rate (percent of home value per year)
   - Homeowner’s insurance (annual premium)
   - Maintenance estimate (typically 1 % of home value per year)
   - Closing‑cost estimate (percent of purchase price)
   - PMI rate if down‑payment < 20 %
2. **Calculate Monthly Owning Cost**
   - Principal + Interest (PI) from a mortgage calculator
   - Taxes (PITI component)
   - Insurance
   - Maintenance
   - PMI (if applicable)
   - **All‑in monthly cost** = PI + taxes + insurance + maintenance + PMI
3. **Compute Up‑Front Costs**
   - Down‑payment amount
   - Closing costs
4. **Determine Break‑Even Time**
   - If owning is cheaper per month than renting, break‑even ≈ `up‑front costs ÷ (rent − all‑in owning cost)` months.
   - If owning is more expensive per month, the break‑even relies on equity build‑up and appreciation; the simple formula gives a rough horizon, but the true break‑even may be many years or never.

---

## Example from [[summaries/session-08_2024-05-15]]
The session provides a concrete illustration:
- **Rent:** $2,400 /mo
- **Home price:** $520 k, 10 % down → loan $468 k
- **Mortgage (6.75 % 30 yr):** ≈ $3,030 /mo
- **Taxes (1.2 %/yr):** ≈ $520 /mo
- **Insurance ($1,600/yr):** ≈ $130 /mo
- **Maintenance (1 %/yr):** ≈ $430 /mo
- **All‑in owning cost:** ≈ $4,120 /mo
- **Up‑front cash:** down‑payment $52 k + closing costs $15.6 k ≈ $67.6 k

Since owning costs about $1,720 more per month, the simple break‑even calculation (`$67.6k ÷ $1,720 ≈ 39 months`) does **not** apply; instead, the user would need equity gains or price appreciation to offset the higher monthly outflow, which typically pushes the break‑even horizon well beyond a few years.

---

## Practical Takeaways
- **Use BECA early** to gauge affordability before committing to a purchase.
- **Adjust levers** (price, down‑payment, interest rate) to move the break‑even horizon into a desired window.
- **Combine with [[concepts/emergency_fund]] planning** to ensure cash reserves cover both up‑front costs and a safety net.
- **Integrate with [[concepts/rent_vs_buy]] decision‑making**: BECA quantifies the monetary side, while rent‑vs‑buy also weighs lifestyle and stability factors.

---

## When BECA Is Less Precise
Because BECA treats future home appreciation and tax‑deduction benefits simplistically, it is best used as a **back‑of‑the‑envelope** tool. For high‑stakes decisions, supplement with a detailed cash‑flow model or consult a financial advisor.
