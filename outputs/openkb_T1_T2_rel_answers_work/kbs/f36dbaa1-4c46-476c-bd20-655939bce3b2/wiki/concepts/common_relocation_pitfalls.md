# Common Relocation Pitfalls

**Definition** – Repeated errors, missed steps, or unrealistic assumptions that cause delays, extra costs, or legal complications when moving abroad for employment.

---

## Core Pitfalls Highlighted in [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-14]]

1. **Assuming the employer’s immigration team will handle everything**
   - Many candidates trust that the company will file, track, and follow‑up on visa paperwork without confirming who is responsible.  This often leads to silent delays.
2. **Under‑estimating processing timelines**
   - Government processing times can be weeks or months.  Not building a buffer can force travel postponements or even gaps in legal status.
3. **Missing or incomplete documentation for IRP registration**
   - The Irish immigration office ([[entities/gnib]] / [[entities/burgh_quay_registration_office]]) requires a solid proof of address, the approved work permit, employment contract, and health insurance.  Forgetting any of these triggers rescheduling or status issues.
4. **Not aligning visa dates with contract start and entry dates**
   - If the earliest lawful entry date, employment start date, or IRP appointment window do not line up, you risk being out of status or losing pay.
5. **Overlooking ancillary tasks**
   - PPS number, Irish SIM, bank account, health insurance/GP registration, and Revenue/myAccount setup are often delayed, causing payroll problems and limited access to services.
6. **Ignoring the cost and time of document authentication**
   - Apostilles, translations, and notarizations can be expensive and slow; failing to start them early adds unexpected expense.
7. **Choosing a destination solely for lifestyle without assessing bureaucracy**
   - Even attractive cities like [[entities/dublin]] can become painful if the local immigration and administrative processes are not well‑understood.

---

## How the Pitfalls Interact with Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/visa_logistics]]** – The visa category determines employer obligations, renewal windows, and the possibility of switching jobs.  Misreading these rules is a common root cause of the pitfalls above.
- **[[concepts/irp_registration_process]]** – Successful registration hinges on having the correct documents at the right time; missing any proof of address or contract detail directly triggers Pitfall 3.
- **[[concepts/emergency_fund]]** – While not a pitfall itself, maintaining an emergency fund can mitigate financial stress when unexpected fees or timeline extensions arise.

## Practical Mitigation Checklist (derived from the session)

| Step | Owner | Proof to Save | Risk if Missed |
|------|-------|---------------|----------------|
| Work authorisation filed | Employer | Submission email | Timeline shift |
| Work authorisation approved | Employer | Approval letter | Travel delay |
| Earliest lawful entry date | Me | Approval letter | Border issues |
| Book Burgh Quay IRP appointment | Me | Confirmation email | Status gap |
| Gather proof of address (Airbnb booking, lease, utility bill) | Me | Printed confirmation | Appointment reschedule |
| PPS number application | Me | PPS letter | Payroll delay |
| Open Irish bank account | Me | Welcome letter | Salary hold |
| Set up Revenue/myAccount | Me | Confirmation screen | Tax filing problems |
| Health insurance & GP registration | Me | Policy & GP letter | IRP complications |
| IRP card receipt | Me | Card & receipt | Travel restrictions |

## Summary

The most common relocation pitfalls revolve around **communication gaps**, **timeline mismanagement**, and **incomplete documentation**.  By explicitly assigning ownership, tracking key dates, and preparing backup documents (e.g., multiple proof‑of‑address options), candidates can avoid the majority of delays and legal hiccups documented in the Dublin relocation scenario.
