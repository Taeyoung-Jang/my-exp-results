# Emergency Fund Planning for Home Purchase

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** Guidelines for sizing an emergency fund when planning to buy a home.

## Why a dedicated emergency fund matters
When you are preparing to purchase a home, you face both recurring monthly costs (mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance) and large one‑time expenses (down payment, closing costs, moving costs). An inadequate cash cushion can force you to dip into the down‑payment savings or take on high‑interest debt, jeopardising the purchase and your financial stability.

## Core recommendation from [[summaries/session-08_2024-05-15]]
The conversation identifies a baseline expense figure of **$4,300 per month** (essential expenses $1,900 + current rent $2,400). The suggested emergency fund range is **3‑to‑6 months** of these total living expenses, which translates to:

- **Low end:** $4,300 × 3 ≈ **$13,000**
- **High end:** $4,300 × 6 ≈ **$26,000**

Because the user plans to buy within the next 2‑3 years, the advice leans toward the upper end of the range to avoid feeling financially stretched.

## How to calculate your own target
1. **Identify total monthly baseline expenses** – include rent (or projected mortgage), utilities, food, transportation, insurance, and any other recurring obligations.
2. **Multiply by 3–6** depending on your risk tolerance and the length of your home‑buy timeline. Longer timelines and higher‑cost markets generally merit the 6‑month figure.
3. **Keep this fund in liquid, low‑risk accounts** (high‑yield savings, money‑market) separate from your down‑payment savings.

## Interaction with other concepts
- This concept is tightly linked to [[concepts/rent_vs_buy]] because the size of the emergency fund influences whether renting or buying is a safer short‑term option.
- It also feeds into [[concepts/break_even_analysis]]; a larger cash reserve can allow a larger down payment, which reduces monthly mortgage costs and shortens the break‑even horizon.

## Practical steps for home‑buyers
- **Start early:** Begin allocating a fixed portion of each paycheck to the emergency fund as soon as you decide to house‑hunt.
- **Separate accounts:** Use a dedicated account to avoid accidental spending.
- **Re‑evaluate annually:** Adjust the target if your income, expenses, or home‑price expectations change.
