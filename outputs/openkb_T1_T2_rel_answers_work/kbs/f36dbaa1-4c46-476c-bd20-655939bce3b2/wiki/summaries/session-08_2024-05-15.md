# Summary of Session 08 (2024-05-15)

## Overview
This conversation walks through how to evaluate the trade‑offs between renting and buying, performs a back‑of‑the‑envelope break‑even analysis, and derives concrete home‑price and down‑payment targets to keep the total monthly housing cost around $3,300.

## Renting vs Buying Decision [[concepts/rent_vs_buy]]
* Identify timeline: stay ≥ a few years → buying becomes more viable; likely relocation → rent for flexibility.  
* Compare **all‑in monthly costs** (mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance, PMI) against rent.  
* Consider what “stability” means—predictable cash flow vs control over the property.

## Simple Break‑Even Calculation [[concepts/break_even_analysis]]
1. Gather inputs: rent, home price, down‑payment %, interest rate, property tax rate, insurance, maintenance %, closing‑cost %.  
2. Compute monthly mortgage (principal + interest) and add taxes, insurance, maintenance, PMI.  
3. Break‑even time ≈ (up‑front costs) ÷ (monthly cost difference). If owning costs more per month, the break‑even relies on equity build‑up and appreciation and usually takes many years.

## Emergency Fund Recommendation [[concepts/emergency_fund]]
* Baseline expenses = essential costs ($1,900) + current rent ($2,400) ≈ $4,300/mo.  
* Recommended cash reserve: 3‑6 × baseline → **$13k – $26k**; aim for the upper end when planning to buy within 2–3 years.

## Adjusting Assumptions to Fit a $3,300 Monthly Budget
* Levers: lower home price, increase down payment, wait for lower rates, boost cash reserves.  
* PMI (0.6 %/yr) applies when down‑payment < 20 %.  
* Target all‑in cost = PITI + maintenance + PMI ≤ $3,300.

## Scenario Breakdowns
### 1. $390,000 home with 10 % down (≈ $39k)  
| Component | Monthly Cost |
|---|---|
| Principal & Interest (30 yr @ 6.75 %) | $2,270 |
| Property Tax (1.2 %/yr) | $390 |
| Homeowner’s Insurance ($1,600/yr) | $130 |
| Maintenance (1 %/yr) | $325 |
| PMI (0.6 %/yr) | $175 |
| **Total** | **$3,290** |

### 2. $450,000 home with 20 % down (≈ $90k) – no PMI  
| Component | Monthly Cost |
|---|---|
| Principal & Interest (30 yr @ 6.75 %) | $2,330 |
| Property Tax (1.2 %/yr) | $450 |
| Homeowner’s Insurance ($1,600/yr) | $130 |
| Maintenance (1 %/yr) | $375 |
| **Total** | **$3,285** |

Both scenarios stay just under the $3,300 target.

## Required Down Payment for a $520,000 Home to Hit $3,300/mo
* Non‑mortgage costs (taxes, insurance, maintenance) ≈ **$1,060**.  
* Desired mortgage‑related payment ≈ **$2,240**.  
* That corresponds to a loan ≈ $320k, meaning a down payment of roughly **$200k** (≈ 38 % of price).

## Key Takeaways
* Use a structured input list and simple monthly‑cost formula to compare rent vs buy.  
* An emergency fund of 3‑6 months of total living expenses provides a safety net.  
* Achieving a $3,300 all‑in cost is realistic by either lowering the price point to the high‑$300k range with modest down payment or by saving for a 20 % down payment on a mid‑$400k home.  
* For a $520k target, a substantially larger down payment (~ 38 %) is needed to meet the cost ceiling.
