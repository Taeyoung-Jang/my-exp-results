# Irish SIM Card

A **Irish SIM card** is a prepaid or contract mobile subscription purchased in Ireland, typically used by expatriates and short‑term arrivals to obtain a local phone number and mobile data.

## Key facts from [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-14]]
- **When to get it:** The relocation checklist recommends buying an Irish SIM soon after arrival in [[entities/dublin]] (or elsewhere in [[entities/ireland]]) to receive verification codes from banks, employers, and government services.
- **Why it matters:** Without a local number, tasks such as setting up a PPS number, opening a bank account, or confirming payroll can be delayed, which is a common pitfall in international moves [[concepts/common_relocation_pitfalls]].
- **Proof to keep:** Keep the purchase receipt or email confirmation; this can serve as proof of address for certain registration steps and is useful if the SIM is needed for identity verification later.
- **Potential slip:** If obtaining the SIM slips, you may miss time‑sensitive communications (e.g., bank two‑factor codes) that can stall payroll or tax registration.

## Recommended actions
1. **Purchase** a SIM from a major Irish provider (Vodafone, Three, Eir) either before travel (online order) or on arrival.
2. **Save the receipt** digitally and print a copy for your relocation folder.
3. **Register the SIM** (if required) with your name and address to align with other proof‑of‑address documents for [[concepts/irp_registration_process]].
4. **Update contact details** with your employer, bank, and any service that requires a local number.
