# Package Theft Risk

**Definition**

Package theft risk refers to the likelihood that a parcel left unattended—at a front door, lobby, or other unsecured location—will be taken by an unauthorized person. This risk is heightened in multi‑unit dwellings where delivery drivers may have limited access and where package visibility from the street is high.

**Key Factors Identified in [[summaries/session-35_2024-12-13]]**

- **Locked front doors with buzzer directories** that may not list the resident’s name, causing drivers to hesitate or leave packages in less secure spots.
- **Short, clear delivery instructions** that reassure drivers they can buzz the correct unit, reducing "no access" attempts that often result in missed deliveries or abandoned packages.
- **Use of free carrier preference tools** (USPS Informed Delivery, UPS My Choice, FedEx Delivery Manager) to authorize unattended delivery; while convenient, these settings can unintentionally increase exposure to theft if the drop‑off area isn’t secure.
- **Alternative drop‑off locations** such as [[entities/amazon-locker]] or other free pickup points, which remove the package from the building’s common areas.

**Mitigation Strategies**

1. **Leverage [[concepts/carrier-preference-tools]] wisely**
   - Enable "driver release" only for packages that do not require a signature.
   - Pair the setting with concise [[concepts/delivery-instructions]] that specify where it is safe to leave the item (e.g., "buzz 3B; I’m home").

2. **Craft effective [[concepts/message-drafting]] for delivery notes**
   - Keep instructions under 120 characters, using confident phrasing (e.g., "Buzz 3B – name may not appear").
   - Avoid lengthy explanations; drivers often skim short notes.
   - Consider the [[concepts/communication-style]]: direct, routine language reduces uncertainty.

3. **Utilize [[concepts/contact-preferences]] for alerts**
   - Turn on all text or email notifications from [[entities/usps]], [[entities/ups]], and [[entities/fedex]] so you can react quickly if a driver reports "no access."
   - Prompt response can allow you to buzz the driver in or arrange an alternative delivery.

4. **Choose secure alternative locations**
   - Free pickup points like [[entities/amazon-locker]] or local UPS Access Points keep packages out of the building’s lobby.

5. **Physical measures**
   - Ensure your name and unit number are clearly displayed on the buzzer panel.
   - If possible, place a small, official‑looking label near the door indicating the unit number.

**Balancing Convenience and Security**

Granting drivers blanket permission to leave packages at the door improves delivery success but also raises the **[[concepts/package-theft-risk]]**. Evaluate the security of your entryway before enabling such settings, and consider using alternative lockers for high‑value items.
