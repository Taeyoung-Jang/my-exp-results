## Indoor Humidity Management

Managing indoor humidity is essential for seasonal comfort, preventing static electricity, and safeguarding wood furniture. The goal is to keep relative humidity in the **35 %–45 %** range during colder, drier months, which balances dryness (reducing static shocks) with the need to avoid condensation and mold growth.

### Key Practices
1. **Monitoring** – Use a basic hygrometer to track humidity levels. The device provides real‑time data, allowing adjustments before conditions become uncomfortable or unsafe. [[entities/hygrometer]]
2. **Humidification** – Operate a small [[entities/humidifier]] on a moderate, steady setting rather than intermittent bursts. This steady output helps maintain target humidity without oversaturation.
3. **Passive Moisture Sources** –
   - Air‑dry a small load of laundry indoors.
   - Keep the bathroom door open after a warm shower.
   These methods add moisture gently and complement the humidifier.
4. **Placement** – Position the humidifier a few feet away from wood surfaces and elevate it on a nightstand or shelf so mist disperses upward. This aligns with best practices outlined in [[concepts/humidifier_placement]].
5. **Airflow & Ventilation** – Ensure proper circulation by keeping furniture a short distance from vents or radiators and occasionally opening windows to exchange stale air.

### Benefits
- **Reduced static electricity** on wooden tables and other surfaces.
- **Improved comfort** through balanced moisture, especially in heated indoor environments.
- **Protection of wood furniture** from warping or finish damage due to excess moisture.
