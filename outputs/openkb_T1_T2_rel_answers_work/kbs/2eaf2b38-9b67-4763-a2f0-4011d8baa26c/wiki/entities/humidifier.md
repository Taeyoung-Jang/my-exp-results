# Humidifier

A **humidifier** is a household device that emits fine water mist to raise indoor relative humidity. In the context of [[summaries/session-32_2025-03-11]], it is recommended as the primary tool for achieving the target **35 %–45 %** humidity range during cold, dry seasons.

## Key facts from session‑32
- **Purpose**: Prevent static shocks, dry skin, and wood furniture issues by maintaining balanced moisture levels.
- **Placement**:
  - Position a few feet (≈1‑2 m) away from wood surfaces such as tables to avoid direct mist contact.
  - Elevate the unit on a nightstand, shelf, or other stable surface so mist disperses upward.
  - Aim the mist toward the center of the room rather than directly at furniture, following guidance from [[concepts/humidifier_placement]].
- **Operation**:
  - Run on a moderate, steady setting rather than a high burst to keep humidity rising evenly.
  - Monitor humidity with a basic **hygrometer** (see [[entities/hygrometer]]) to stay within the safe 35‑45 % window and avoid condensation or mold.
- **Seasonal relevance**: Acts as a core component of [[concepts/seasonal_comfort]] strategies for colder, drier months, complementing other adjustments like thicker curtains and rugs.
- **Related concepts**: Managing indoor moisture ties into [[concepts/indoor_humidity]] and overall comfort planning.

## Practical tips
1. Place the humidifier in an open corner where air circulates freely.
2. Adjust distance or mist angle if you notice damp patches on nearby wood.
3. Pair with passive humidity sources (e.g., indoor‑drying laundry) for a balanced approach.
