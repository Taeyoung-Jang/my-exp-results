# No. 4½ Smoothing Plane

The **No. 4½ smoothing plane** is a heavyweight, bevel‑down hand plane traditionally used for achieving glass‑y, fine‑grained finishes. It is commonly recommended in hand‑tool woodworking for its stability and the ease with which it can be tuned for optimal performance.

## Key Characteristics
- **Weight & Size:** Slightly larger and heavier than a standard No. 4, providing a solid platform that resists chatter.
- **Cutting Angle:** Typically a standard bevel‑down angle (~45°), which works well with a well‑set chipbreaker.
- **Versatility:** Suitable for both general smoothing and handling figured grain when properly adjusted.

## Setup Guidance from Session 14
- **Sharp Iron Preparation:** A dead‑sharp, lightly cambered iron with a polished back is the most important factor for a glassy surface. See [[concepts/sharp-iron-preparation]].
- **Chipbreaker Setup:** Start with the chipbreaker about 0.1 mm (≈0.004 in) from the iron edge; adjust closer for fine shavings or tricky grain. Refer to [[concepts/chipbreaker-setup]].
- **Frog & Mouth:** Ensure the frog gives solid support; tighten the mouth only when necessary for difficult grain, as described in the discussion of tear‑out diagnosis.
- **Tear‑out Diagnosis:** Use the plane’s standard angle and chipbreaker first; if tear‑out persists on highly figured wood, consider a higher cutting angle or a back bevel. See [[concepts/tear-out-diagnosis]].
