# Quick‑Release Vise (product)

A **quick‑release vise** is an accessory that replaces the traditional long‑hand crank on a bench vise with a lever that opens the jaws in a single motion.  It is especially useful for operators who need to keep the wrist neutral and avoid repetitive twisting, making it a core component of a **[[concepts/light-touch-workflow]]**.

## Key facts from [[summaries/session-33_2023-12-13]]
- Recommended as part of the ergonomic re‑layout for a small workshop corner to reduce wrist strain.
- Works well with the suggested **arm’s‑reach zone** layout, allowing the vise to be opened with a hip‑level push rather than a hand‑twist.  
- Pairs nicely with other low‑effort hardware such as **[[concepts/toggle-clamp-advantage]]** toggle clamps and a **[[entities/large-diameter-bit-height-wheel]]** for easy depth adjustments.
- Enables quick changes when moving parts between the bench, drill press, and router table without fiddling with small knobs.

## Why it matters
- **Reduces wrist motion**: The lever action removes the need for a long, torque‑heavy crank, which can aggravate nerve‑damage‑related injuries.
- **Speeds up workflow**: Parts can be clamped and released with a single, palm‑press motion, keeping the workflow within the **[[concepts/arm-reach-zone]]**.
- **Integrates with precision setup**: When combined with **[[concepts/shim-based-depth-adjustment]]**, the operator can lock the vise in place and make fine depth changes using shims rather than fiddling with the vise jaws.

## Typical use cases
1. **Setup of jigs and carriers** – Quickly lock a workpiece onto a custom sled before feeding it through a router table.
2. **Tool changes** – Release the workpiece to swap bits on a drill press without having to reach down to a crank.
3. **Final passes** – Keep the workpiece steady while the machine performs whisper‑thin cuts, allowing the operator to stay in a neutral position.
