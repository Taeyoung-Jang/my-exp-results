# Bevel‑Up Smoothing Plane

A **bevel‑up smoothing plane** is a hand‑tool used for fine finishing work in which the cutting iron is installed with its bevel side facing upward.  This configuration allows the user to vary the cutting angle by adjusting a micro‑bevel on the blade, giving extra control on difficult grain while still producing a very flat, glassy surface.

## Key Characteristics Mentioned in [[summaries/session-14_2022-06-15]]
- **Cutting angle flexibility** – The plane can employ a higher cutting angle (via a back bevel or a steeper micro‑bevel) when standard angle and chipbreaker settings fail to stop tear‑out on highly figured wood.
- **Reliance on a perfect edge** – Because a bevel‑up plane typically lacks a chipbreaker, the iron must be extremely sharp and well‑prepared, matching guidance from [[concepts/sharp-iron-preparation]].
- **Comparison to other tools** – It is presented as an alternative to traditional bevel‑down smoothing planes such as the [[entities/no-4-smoothing-plane]] or the heavier [[entities/no-4-5-smoothing-plane]], which rely more on chipbreaker geometry.

## Practical Use
When a woodworker has a single well‑tuned plane (as in the scenario of the user in the session) and needs to address stubborn tear‑out, the bevel‑up plane offers a **non‑destructive tweak**: increasing the micro‑bevel angle rather than altering the chipbreaker or mouth.  This adjustment is reversible and can be evaluated by observing cleaner shavings and a smoother surface.
