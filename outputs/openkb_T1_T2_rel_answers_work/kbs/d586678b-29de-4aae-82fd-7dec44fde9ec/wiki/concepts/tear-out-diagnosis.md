# Tear‑Out Diagnosis and Mitigation

**Definition**: The process of recognizing why a hand plane is producing tear‑out and applying targeted adjustments to eliminate it.

---

## Typical Symptoms
- Streaks or patches where fibers are ripped rather than cleanly cut.
- Inconsistent shavings: long, ragged strips versus fine, uniform curls.
- Increased effort required to push the plane, often felt as a "starved" motion.

## Primary Causes
| Cause | How it manifests | Typical fix |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| **Dull or improperly cambered iron** | Fuzzy surface, higher thrust needed. | See [[concepts/sharp-iron-preparation]] for sharpening and camber guidelines. |
| **Excessive chipbreaker gap** | Long, continuous tear‑out even in good grain. | Reduce gap per [[concepts/chipbreaker-setup]] (≈0.1 mm or 0.004 in). |
| **Mouth too tight** | Shavings bunch, plane feels starved. | Open mouth slightly; keep moderate opening. |
| **Grain direction reversal** | Streaks appear where grain changes. | Adjust plane angle or skew; consider a higher cutting angle. |
| **Improper frog/bed alignment** | Unstable iron support, chatter. | Ensure solid frog alignment; move frog forward only when tighter mouth is needed. |

## Diagnostic Steps (from [[summaries/session-14_2022-06-15]])
1. **Check iron sharpness** – Run a fingernail test; if it feels dull, sharpen.
2. **Inspect chipbreaker** – Measure gap; start at ~0.1 mm and adjust based on wood and shaving thickness.
3. **Test mouth opening** – Loosen slightly if shavings are crinkly; tighten only for very fine shavings on tricky grain.
4. **Observe grain direction** – Flip or skew the board; if tear‑out disappears, it’s a grain issue.
5. **Feel plane resistance** – A "smooth" feel indicates proper setup; a "starved" feel points to mouth or chipbreaker problems.

## Mitigation Strategies
- **Primary tweak**: Adjust the chipbreaker closer to the iron (see [[concepts/chipbreaker-setup]]). This is reversible and often resolves most tear‑out.
- **Secondary tweaks**:
  - Sharpen the iron and apply a light camber (refer to [[concepts/sharp-iron-preparation]]).
  - Slightly open the mouth if the plane feels starved.
  - Use a higher cutting angle (e.g., a [[entities/york-pitch-frog]] or back bevel) for highly figured wood.
  - Switch to a plane with a more suitable angle, such as a [[entities/no-4-smoothing-plane]] or [[entities/no-4-5-smoothing-plane]].
  - For extreme cases, a [[entities/bevel-up-smoother]] with a higher micro‑bevel can provide additional control.

---

**Quick test**: After each adjustment, take a few passes on the workpiece. Clean, uniform shavings and a consistently smooth surface indicate the tear‑out source has been mitigated.
