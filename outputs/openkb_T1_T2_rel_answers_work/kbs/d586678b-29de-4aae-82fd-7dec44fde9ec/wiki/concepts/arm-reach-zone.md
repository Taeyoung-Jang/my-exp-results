# Arm’s‑Reach Zone

**Definition**: An ergonomic layout principle for a small workshop corner that places the tools and accessories you adjust most often within a shallow arc directly in front of you, allowing the elbow to stay close to the body and the wrist to remain neutral.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Reach Arc** – Visualize a half‑circle extending a foot forward from your seated or standing position. Everything inside this arc should be reachable without extending the arm or rotating the wrist.
2. **Tool Types** – The zone contains items that require frequent handling or fine adjustment: 
   - Small squares, depth gauges, and setup blocks
   - Frequently used drill bits, router bits, and hex keys for fence or bit changes
   - Pencils, markers, and a light mallet
   - Quick‑release mechanisms (e.g., [[entities/quick-release-visor]])
3. **Storage Format** – A shallow pull‑out tray or low‑friction drawer positioned just under the bench surface. The tray can be opened with a hip or forearm push so you never need to twist a lid or reach up.
4. **Placement Logic** – Heavy or infrequently used items (large squares, clamps, jigs) belong on a wall rail or higher shelf, keeping the reach zone uncluttered.

---

## Benefits

- **Wrist protection** – Eliminates the need for repetitive pinching, twisting caps, or applying a death‑grip, which is crucial for users with wrist or nerve injuries.
- **Speed** – Reduces motion waste; you can grab or return a tool with a single, fluid motion.
- **Consistency** – Keeps the hand in a neutral posture, improving repeatability for precision cuts.

---

## Application in Session‑33

The concept is directly derived from the workflow advice in [[summaries/session-33_2023-12-13]]. The assistant recommends:
- Storing measuring tools, pencils, and small hex keys in a shallow drawer directly beneath the bench.
- Using a wall rail for larger clamps and jigs.
- Positioning the vise, drill press, and router table so parts move within the reach arc, minimizing torso twists.

- [ ] Install a low drawer or slide‑out tray at that radius.
- [ ] Populate the tray with the core tool list above.
- [ ] Mount a wall rail above eye level for larger, grab‑and‑go items.
