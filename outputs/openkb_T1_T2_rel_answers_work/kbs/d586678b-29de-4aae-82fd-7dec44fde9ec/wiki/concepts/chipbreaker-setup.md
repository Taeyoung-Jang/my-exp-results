# Chipbreaker Setup and Distance

The chipbreaker (or scraper) is a critical component in hand‑plane tuning. Its distance from the cutting edge determines how the wood fibers are split and how the shavings are released, directly affecting surface quality and tear‑out.

## Recommended Starting Gap
- Begin with a gap of **≈0.1 mm (about 0.004 in)** between the chipbreaker edge and the iron bevel. This is roughly the thickness of a fine shaving.
- This setting works well for most woods when taking thin, finishing cuts.

## Adjusting for Wood Grain and Shaving Thickness
- **Softer or less figured grain:** You can back the chipbreaker out slightly (e.g., 0.15 mm) for smoother chip flow.
- **Highly figured, tricky grain:** Creep the chipbreaker closer, but never so close that the plane chokes or the shavings crinkle.
- **Heavier shavings:** Increase the gap modestly to allow the larger chips to pass without jamming.

## Interaction with Other Settings
- A **dead‑sharp iron** (see [[concepts/sharp-iron-preparation]]) is the primary factor; the chipbreaker fine‑tunes the result.
- The **mouth opening** should be moderate; a tighter mouth helps only when the grain is especially problematic.
- The **frog/bed alignment** must provide a stable platform so the chipbreaker maintains a consistent gap.

## Diagnosing Problems
- **Excessive tear‑out:** May indicate the chipbreaker is set too far, allowing fibers to lift before the cutter severs them.
- **Clogging or crinkled shavings:** Usually a sign the breaker is too close or the mouth is overly tight.
- Use the diagnostic cues from [[concepts/tear-out-diagnosis]] to differentiate between grain direction issues and setup errors.

## Practical Tuning Procedure
1. Ensure the iron is honed to a keen edge.
2. Install the [[entities/chipbreaker]] and set the gap to ~0.1 mm using feeler gauges or a piece of paper.
3. Take a few test passes on a scrap piece of the work wood.
4. Observe shavings and surface texture:
   - Clean, thin shavings and a uniformly smooth surface indicate an optimal setting.
   - Long, ragged shavings or surface streaks suggest widening the gap.
5. Iterate in small increments (≈0.02 mm) until the desired result is achieved.

## Typical Applications
- **Smoothing planes** such as the [[entities/no-4-smoothing-plane]] or the broader [[entities/no-4-5-smoothing-plane]] benefit most from precise chipbreaker adjustment for a glassy finish.
- When using a **bevel‑up smoother**, the chipbreaker is absent; instead, a higher cutting angle may be employed, but the principle of controlling fiber split remains.

## Summary
Proper chipbreaker setup—starting at about 0.1 mm gap and fine‑tuning based on wood grain and shaving thickness—offers the most reversible and effective method to reduce tear‑out while preserving the plane’s natural feel.
