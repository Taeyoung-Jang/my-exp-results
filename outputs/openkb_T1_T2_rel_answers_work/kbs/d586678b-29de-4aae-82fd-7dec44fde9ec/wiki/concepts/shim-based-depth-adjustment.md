# Shim‑Based Depth Adjustment

**Definition**: A method of achieving precise material removal by using thin shims, feeler gauges, setup blocks, and stop collars instead of repeatedly turning small adjustment knobs.

## Why it matters
- Minimizes repetitive micro‑adjustments that strain the wrist.
- Provides repeatable, tactile increments that can be swapped quickly.
- Fits naturally into a [[concepts/light-touch-workflow]] by shifting precision from the hand to fixed references.

## Core techniques (as described in [[summaries/session-33_2023-12-13]])
1. **Shim insertion** – Slip a known‑thickness shim (brass, veneer, playing card) between a fence and its stop to move the cut a precise amount. No twisting of knobs is required.
2. **Feeler gauge depth setting** – Place a feeler gauge against the workpiece or a dedicated stop block; the gauge’s thickness defines the exact depth.
3. **Setup block reference** – Use a calibrated block on the work surface; the router or drill bit is set to just touch the block, locking the depth.
4. **Stop collar on the spindle** – Once the desired depth is found, tighten a collar on the bit shaft. Future cuts return to the same depth without further adjustment.
5. **Spacer swapping (drill press)** – Stack or remove thin spacers under the workpiece or table to fine‑tune vertical position, avoiding wheel turns.

## Benefits
- **Ergonomic**: Reduces wrist flexion and twisting motions, crucial for users with wrist injuries.
- **Speed**: Quick “add or remove a shim” changes are faster than rotating a depth wheel.
- **Consistency**: Shims and gauges offer repeatable increments, improving part-to-part uniformity.
- **Flexibility**: Works for both horizontal (router table) and vertical (drill press) machining setups.

## Related concepts
- [[concepts/light-touch-workflow]] – the broader strategy of off‑loading effort to fixtures.
- [[concepts/toggle-clamp-advantage]] – low‑effort clamping that complements shim‑based adjustments.
- [[concepts/arm-reach-zone]] – positioning tools within easy reach to enable rapid shim changes.

## Practical tips
- Keep a small tray of common shim thicknesses (e.g., 0.025", 0.050", 0.100") in the arm‑reach zone for fast access.
- Label each shim or gauge so you can select the right increment without searching.
- When using a stop collar, choose a model with a **palm‑style knob** (or large‑diameter bit‑height wheel) to avoid pinching.
- Combine shim adjustments with a **toggle clamp** on the workpiece to lock it securely while you change references.
