## Sharp Iron Preparation

A well‑prepared iron is the single most critical factor for achieving a glassy surface on hand‑plane cuts. The preparation steps focus on edge geometry, sharpness, and surface finish.

### Key Elements
1. **Dead‑Sharp Edge** – Use a high‑quality sharpening system (diamond stones, oil stones, or a jigsaw with a fine abrasive) to achieve a keen, consistent edge. The goal is a cutting edge that slices fibers cleanly without pulling.
2. **Light Camber** – Apply a very slight camber (often just a few microns) to a smoothing plane iron. This reduces the likelihood of track marks on the workpiece while still allowing a thin shaving.
3. **Polished Back** – Polish the back of the iron to a mirror‑like finish. This reduces friction and helps the iron sit evenly on the frog, contributing to a smoother cut.
4. **Even Bevel** – Ensure the bevel is uniform across the entire width of the iron; an uneven bevel can cause uneven shaving thickness and surface chatter.
5. **Consistent Angle** – Maintain the factory‑specified bevel angle (typically 25–30° for bevel‑down planes) unless a specific micro‑bevel is being employed for higher angles.

### Why It Matters
- A sharp iron removes fibers cleanly, which minimizes tear‑out regardless of grain direction.
- The combination of a light camber and polished back reduces the need for overly tight mouth settings, allowing smoother material flow.
- Proper preparation lessens the reliance on other adjustments such as chipbreaker gap or mouth opening.

### Practical Tips
- Test the sharpness by shaving a piece of soft pine; the shavings should be thin, continuous, and lift away cleanly.
- After sharpening, wipe the iron with a light oil to prevent rust and to keep the polished surface free of particles.
- Re‑check the edge after each use; even minor nicks can degrade the finish.
