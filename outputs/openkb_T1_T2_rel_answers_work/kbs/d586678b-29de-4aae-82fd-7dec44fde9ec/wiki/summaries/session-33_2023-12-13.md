### Overview
The user seeks an ergonomic re‑layout for a small workshop corner to protect a wrist injury. The assistant proposes a **[[concepts/light-touch-workflow]]** focused on shifting effort from the hand to fixtures and machine setup.

### Arm’s‑reach layout
- Keep frequently‑adjusted tools (squares, depth gauges, hex keys, pencils, light mallet) in a shallow tray directly under the bench – the **[[concepts/arm-reach-zone]]**.  
- Store heavier, grab‑and‑go items (large squares, clamps, jigs) on a wall rail.  
- Position the vise, drill press, and router table so parts move by a small pivot rather than a full reach.

### Recommended jigs & carriers (priority order)
1. **General clamping board** – 12×18 in, ¾‑in plywood, two light‑pressure toggle clamps, grip‑tape surface.  
2. **Router‑table carrier sled** – 8×10 in, rear fence, dome‑shaped palm‑heel handle, one toggle clamp, UHMW sliding base.  
3. **Drill‑press carrier** – 6×8 in base, angled dowel handle, wedge‑pair clamps.  
4. **Pull‑out tool tray** for setup tools.  
5. **Wall rail** for large squares, clamps, and pre‑built jigs.  
6. Optional **[[entities/quick-release-visor]]** and **[[entities/large-diameter-bit-height-wheel]]** for faster adjustments.

### Clamp & hardware preferences
- **Toggle clamps** – low‑effort flip, consistent pressure. See [[concepts/toggle-clamp-advantage]].  
- Cam clamps – usable but require more wrist rotation.  
- Wedge locks – very light but slower to engage.  
- Palm‑style knobs, quick‑release levers, and T‑handle chucks replace tiny twist knobs.

### Dial‑in methods for whisper‑thin final passes
- Use **thin shims** (brass, veneer) between fence and stop to incrementally move the cut without turning knobs.  
- Employ **feeler gauges / setup blocks** as depth references; swap spacers instead of tweaking depth wheels.  
- On the router table, place a shim behind the fence and use a stop collar for repeatable depth.  
- On the drill press, stack spacers under the workpiece or table and use a palm‑friendly depth‑stop collar.  

These techniques rely on **[[concepts/shim-based-depth-adjustment]]**, allowing refinements without demanding wrist precision.
