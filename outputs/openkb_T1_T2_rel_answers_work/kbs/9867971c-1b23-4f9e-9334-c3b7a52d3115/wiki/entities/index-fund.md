# Index Fund

An **Index Fund** is a pooled investment product that seeks to replicate the performance of a specific market index (e.g., S&P 500, total‑stock market) by holding the same securities in roughly the same proportions.  It offers investors broad market exposure with minimal trading and typically very low expense ratios.

---

## Key Characteristics
- **Passive tracking** of a chosen benchmark index.
- **Low fees** compared to actively managed funds, which helps maximize long‑term compounding.
- **Broad diversification** across many stocks, reducing idiosyncratic risk.
- **Ease of automation** – contributions can be auto‑invested, supporting a consistent, hands‑off strategy.

## Typical Usage
Investors often allocate the majority of their equity exposure to an index fund, using it as the **foundation** of a long‑term, “set‑and‑forget” strategy.  Supplemental assets—such as [[entities/investment-grade-corporate-bond]]s or [[entities/total-bond-fund]]s—are added to address specific goals like income, stability, or diversification.
