# Investment‑Grade Corporate Bond

**Type:** Product

Investment‑grade corporate bonds are debt securities issued by financially stable companies and rated BBB‑ or higher by major rating agencies. In the context of *session‑02_2020‑06‑15*, they are discussed as a modest‑yield, lower‑risk component of a bond allocation that can complement Treasury ballast.

## Role in a Portfolio
- Serve primarily as a **stability** and **income** source, fitting the [[concepts/bond-allocation-role]] of diversification without taking on excessive credit risk.
- Provide slightly higher yields than [[entities/us-treasury-securities]] while maintaining a defensive character when kept to an **investment‑grade** quality.
- Recommended to occupy **10–20 % of the bond bucket** when paired with intermediate‑term Treasuries, ensuring the bulk of the ballast remains in low‑risk government debt.

## Key Characteristics Highlighted
- **Credit Quality:** High credit ratings limit price volatility during equity market stress.
- **Duration Considerations:** Short‑ to intermediate‑duration exposure (often 3–7 years) aligns with the need for moderate rate‑sensitivity, complementing the [[concepts/intermediate-term-treasury-strategy]].
- **Correlation:** Lower correlation with stocks compared to high‑yield bonds, preserving the portfolio’s defensive profile.

## Comparison with Other Fixed‑Income Options
| Feature | Investment‑Grade Corporate Bond | [[entities/high-yield-bond]] | [[entities/us-treasury-securities]] |
|---------|--------------------------------|------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Credit Risk | Low (BBB+ or higher) | High | Very Low (government) |
| Typical Yield | Moderate | High | Low |
| Volatility | Moderate (depends on duration) | High (equity‑like) | Low |
| Use Case | Defensive income, modest diversification | Aggressive income, equity‑like risk | Pure ballast, minimal credit risk |

## Practical Guidance from the Session
1. **Assess Role:** Confirm the bond slice is meant for stability/income before allocating.
2. **Maintain Quality:** Stick to investment‑grade issuers to keep the slice defensive.
3. **Size Appropriately:** Keep the corporate portion at roughly **10‑20 %** of the overall bond allocation.
4. **Monitor Correlation:** Periodically review how the corporate slice behaves during stock drawdowns to ensure it isn’t becoming too “stock‑like.”
