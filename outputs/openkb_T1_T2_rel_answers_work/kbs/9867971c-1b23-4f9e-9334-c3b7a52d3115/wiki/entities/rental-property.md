## Rental Property

**Type:** Product (real‑estate asset)

**Definition**: A rental property is a residential or commercial real‑estate unit that is owned by an investor and leased to tenants for regular rental income. In personal finance contexts, rental properties are considered an illiquid, long‑term growth bucket.

**Key facts from [[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]]**
- **Quantity**: 5 properties.
- **Monthly carrying costs (PITI + HOA)**: $2,900, $3,250, $2,400, $3,800, $2,650 (total ≈ $15,000 per month).
- **Annual repair/turnover budget**: $18,000 (≈ $1,500 per month).
- **Role in liquidity planning**: Part of the **[[concepts/liquidity-framework]]** and **[[concepts/reserve-buckets]]**. The framework recommends a rental reserve of 1‑1.5 × monthly carrying costs plus average repair costs to avoid forced liquidation.
- **Interaction with other assets**: Combined with a **[[entities/high-yield-savings-account]]** for short‑term liquidity and **[[entities/private-equity-commitment]]** for alternative, higher‑risk capital.

**Typical considerations**
- **Liquidity**: Rentals are illiquid; cash needed for vacancies, repairs, or tenant turnover must be reserved.
- **Risk**: Concentration risk (geography, tenant type, property type) should be monitored.
- **Income**: Provides steady cash flow that can supplement personal expenses after the emergency fund is funded.

**Related concepts and entities**
- [[concepts/liquidity-framework]] – outlines how rental properties fit into the three‑bucket liquidity model.
- [[concepts/reserve-buckets]] – describes the specific reserve calculations for rentals.
- [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]] – the cash bucket used for emergency and reserve funding.
- [[entities/private-equity-commitment]] – another long‑term, illiquid investment bucket.

**Practical framework snippet**
```
Total Monthly Carrying Cost = $15,000
Rental Reserve = (1.5 × $15,000) + ($18,000 / 12) = $24,000
```
This reserve, together with the personal emergency fund and private‑equity reserve, defines the liquidity floor for the investor.
