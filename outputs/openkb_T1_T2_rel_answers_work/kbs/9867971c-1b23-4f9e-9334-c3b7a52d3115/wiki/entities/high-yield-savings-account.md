# High‑Yield Savings Account

A **High‑Yield Savings Account (HYSA)** is a deposit account that offers a higher interest rate than traditional checking or savings accounts while maintaining easy access to funds. It is typically used as the primary vehicle for short‑term cash reserves because it combines liquidity with modest returns.

## Role in Liquidity Management

In the context of the **[[concepts/liquidity-framework]]** discussed in [[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]], the HYSA serves as the *cash bucket* that funds:

1. The **personal emergency fund** (months of personal expenses).
2. The **rental reserve** for vacancies, repairs, and turnover on [[entities/rental-property]] assets.
3. The **private‑equity reserve** for uncalled [[entities/private-equity-commitment]] capital.

These three *[[concepts/reserve-buckets]]* together define the **liquidity floor**—the minimum cash balance needed to avoid forced asset sales.

## Practical Considerations

- **Liquidity vs. Return:** HYSA balances provide near‑instant access, making them suitable for the cash bucket, but the trade‑off is lower yield compared to longer‑term investments such as [[entities/high-yield-bond]] or [[entities/us-treasury-securities]].
- **Safety:** Funds are typically FDIC‑insured up to applicable limits, adding a layer of protection for the emergency fund.
- **Integration with Other Strategies:** While the HYSA focuses on cash, it can coexist with **[[concepts/low-cost-index-investing]]** and **[[concepts/bond-allocation-role]]** strategies that allocate longer‑term capital to index funds and bonds.
