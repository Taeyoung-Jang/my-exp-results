# High‑Yield Bond

**Type:** Product  
**Description:** A corporate bond with below‑investment‑grade credit that pays a higher coupon to compensate for increased risk.

## Overview
High‑Yield Bonds are debt securities issued by companies with lower credit ratings. Because of the greater default risk, they provide higher coupon payments than [[entities/investment-grade-corporate-bond]].

## Role in Portfolio (as discussed in [[summaries/session-02_2020-06-15]])
- May be considered when an investor explicitly wants a risk‑ier income component.
- In the referenced session, the assistant advised that a tiny high‑yield allocation is only suitable if the investor wants equity‑like behavior and accepts the associated volatility.
- Generally not recommended for the primary *stabilizing* purpose of a bond slice; instead, focus on [[concepts/bond-allocation-role]] of stability, income, or diversification.

## Risks and Benefits
- **Higher Yield:** Compensation for lower credit quality.
- **Increased Volatility:** Correlates more with equities, especially during market stress.
- **Credit Risk:** Potential for issuer default.

## Usage Guidelines (from the session)
- Keep high‑yield exposure small (often < 5 % of the total bond allocation) if included at all.
- Prefer investment‑grade corporate bonds for defensive ballast; use high‑yield only when seeking additional yield and accepting equity‑like drawdowns.
