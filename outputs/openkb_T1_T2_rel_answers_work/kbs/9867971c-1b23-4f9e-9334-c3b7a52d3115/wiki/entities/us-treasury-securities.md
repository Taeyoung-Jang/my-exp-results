# U.S. Treasury Securities (product)

## Overview
U.S. Treasury securities are debt instruments issued by the United States Department of the Treasury to fund federal operations. Because they are backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government, they are considered the benchmark for credit‑risk‑free assets.

## Role in a Portfolio
In the context of [[summaries/session-02_2020-06-15]], Treasury securities are recommended as the primary **stabilizing** component of a bond allocation. They provide a clean ballast that tends to hold value when equities decline, fulfilling the [[concepts/bond-allocation-role]] of **stability** rather than income.

## Duration Strategy
The discussion recommends an **intermediate‑term** approach, typically a duration of about five to seven years. This aligns with the [[concepts/intermediate-term-treasury-strategy]] concept: enough duration to cushion equity drawdowns while limiting sensitivity to interest‑rate moves.

## Comparison with Other Fixed‑Income Products
- Compared with [[entities/investment-grade-corporate-bond]], Treasuries carry no credit risk but generally offer lower yields.
- A [[entities/total-bond-fund]] may blend Treasuries with corporates, diluting the pure stabilizing effect that plain Treasuries provide.

## Practical Use
Investors can hold Treasuries directly or via an index fund that tracks the Treasury market, keeping costs low in line with [[concepts/low-cost-index-investing]]. Automated contributions to such a fund can maintain the desired duration exposure without active management.
