# Summary of session-02_2020-06-15

## Core Advice for a Simple, Low‑Maintenance Investment Plan
- Begin with a clear assessment of **risk tolerance, time horizon, and financial goals** to define the portfolio’s purpose.  
- Use a **broad, low‑cost index fund core** such as a [[entities/broad-market-index-fund]] or other [[entities/index-fund]] to capture market returns with minimal tinkering. See [[concepts/low-cost-index-investing]].  
- Emphasize **consistency, automation, and low fees** over market timing or stock‑picking.  
- Warn against common traps: chasing trends, reacting to short‑term moves, taking excessive emotional risk, and neglecting cash for near‑term needs.

## Evaluating the Bond Slice
- Clarify the **role of bonds** (stability, income, or diversification) – see [[concepts/bond-allocation-role]].  
- **Key signals** to assess whether bonds are “stock‑like”:  
  1. **Credit quality mix** – lower‑rated corporates increase volatility.  
  2. **Duration** – long duration amplifies interest‑rate risk.  
  3. **Correlation with equity drawdowns** – high correlation reduces the ballast effect.  
- Common mistakes: chasing high yield without recognizing added equity‑like risk, using overly long durations, and forgetting to rebalance the bond allocation as needs change.

## Choosing Between Treasuries and Total Bond Funds
- **U.S. Treasuries** provide clean, credit‑risk‑free ballast and tend to hold value during equity sell‑offs. See [[entities/us-treasury-securities]].  
- **Total bond funds** mix corporates and Treasuries, offering convenience but diluting the stabilizing effect. See [[entities/total-bond-fund]].  
- Preference depends on whether stabilization outweighs modest income needs.

## Selecting Treasury Duration
- **Short‑term Treasuries**: very low volatility, limited protective effect.  
- **Long‑term Treasuries**: strong equity offset but high rate‑sensitivity.  
- **Intermediate‑term Treasuries** (≈5–7 years duration) strike a balance—enough cushioning with manageable rate swings. See [[concepts/intermediate-term-treasury-strategy]].

## Allocation Guidelines
- If keeping a corporate slice, allocate **10–20 % of the bond bucket** to high‑quality corporates; the rest stays in intermediate Treasuries.  
- Use [[entities/investment-grade-corporate-bond]] for a defensive stance.  
- A tiny allocation to [[entities/high-yield-bond]] is only justified if the investor explicitly wants a riskier income component; otherwise, stick to investment‑grade bonds.  

**Actionable checklist**  
1. Define investment goal, horizon, and risk comfort.  
2. Build a core of low‑cost broad index funds.  
3. Decide the bond role and evaluate credit mix, duration, and equity correlation.  
4. Choose intermediate‑term Treasuries (5–7 yr) for ballast.  
5. If adding corporates, limit to 10–20 % of the bond allocation and stay investment‑grade.  
6. Automate contributions; review allocation periodically.
