# Liquidity Framework

The **Liquidity Framework** is a practical, three‑bucket approach for individuals who hold a mix of cash, real‑estate assets, and private‑equity commitments. It translates time‑horizon and risk tolerance into concrete cash‑reserve targets, enabling a clear assessment of whether a high‑yield savings account (HYSA) balance is sufficient, borderline, or excessive.

## Core Buckets

1. **Personal Emergency Fund** – Cash set aside for day‑to‑day living expenses.  
   Formula: `Monthly Personal Spending × Desired Months` (commonly 3‑12 months).  
   *Illustrated in* [[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]] *as a 6‑month target.*
2. **Rental Reserve** – Buffer to cover vacancies, repairs, turnover, and other property‑specific cash drains.  
   Formula: `(Total Monthly Carrying Cost × Reserve Factor) + Avg. Monthly Repair Cost`.  
   The reserve factor typically ranges from 1‑2 months.  
   *Linked to* [[entities/rental-property]] *and the broader* [[concepts/reserve-buckets]] *concept.*
3. **Private‑Equity Reserve** – The full amount of uncalled capital commitments that must be treated as unavailable cash.  
   Formula: `Uncalled PE Commitments`.  
   Directly related to [[entities/private-equity-commitment]].

## Determining HYSA Thresholds

- **Liquidity Floor** = Emergency Fund + Rental Reserve + Private‑Equity Reserve.  
- **Green Zone**: HYSA ≥ Liquidity Floor (fully funded).  
- **Yellow Zone**: HYSA slightly below the floor (typically 5‑10 % under) – acceptable but close.  
- **Red Zone**: HYSA significantly below the floor – indicates liquidity risk.

In the example from [[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]], the calculations yield:
- Emergency Fund: $69,000
- Rental Reserve: $24,000
- Private‑Equity Reserve: $360,000
- **Liquidity Floor**: $453,000

With a current HYSA of $275,000, the user falls in the **Red Zone**, suggesting a need to increase cash reserves or rebalance assets.

## Why It Matters

Applying the Liquidity Framework helps investors:
- Avoid forced sales of illiquid assets during emergencies.
- Keep a disciplined view of how much cash is truly "free" versus committed.
- Make transparent, quantitative decisions about cash allocation across diverse holdings.
