# Bond Allocation Role

## Overview
The **bond allocation role** describes the specific function that the fixed‑income portion of a portfolio is meant to fulfill.  In the context of a simple, low‑maintenance investment plan, three primary objectives are considered:

1. **Stability (ballast)** – dampening the volatility of equity holdings during market downturns.
2. **Income** – providing a regular cash flow that may supplement other sources of earnings.
3. **Diversification** – introducing return streams that behave differently from stocks, reducing overall portfolio risk.

Understanding which of these goals is most important to you guides the choice of bond types, duration, and credit quality, as highlighted in [[summaries/session-02_2020-06-15]].

## Key Signals to Evaluate the Role
When assessing whether a bond slice is meeting its intended purpose, look at:

- **Credit quality mix** – a higher proportion of lower‑rated corporate bonds (e.g., [[entities/high-yield-bond]]) tends to increase volatility, making the slice behave more like equities.
- **Duration** – long‑duration bonds are sensitive to interest‑rate changes; short‑duration bonds are steadier but provide less cushioning during equity sell‑offs.
- **Correlation with equity drawdowns** – if the bond slice moves down with stocks, its stabilizing effect is weakened.

These signals help you decide if the allocation is truly serving a **stability** role or if it is drifting toward **income** or **diversification**.

## Common Mistakes
- **Chasing yield** by adding high‑yield corporate exposure without recognizing the added equity‑like risk.
- **Over‑extending duration**, which turns a defensive bond position into a rate‑sensitive one.
- **Neglecting periodic reviews**, leading to a bond slice that no longer matches the investor’s risk tolerance or time horizon.

## Practical Guidance
- If **stability** is the priority, concentrate on high‑quality, intermediate‑term government debt such as [[entities/us-treasury-securities]] (see [[concepts/intermediate-term-treasury-strategy]] for duration guidance).
- For an **income‑focused** approach, a modest allocation to [[entities/investment-grade-corporate-bond]] can boost yield while retaining a defensive posture.
- When seeking **diversification**, consider a broad [[entities/total-bond-fund]] that blends government and investment‑grade corporate bonds.

A typical rule of thumb—derived from the session discussion—is to keep the **corporate portion** at roughly **10 %–20 %** of the overall bond bucket, preserving the majority of the ballast in Treasuries.  This maintains a clear **stability** role while allowing a small income boost.

## Relations to Other Concepts
- The bond allocation role often complements a core built on [[concepts/low-cost-index-investing]] and a broad market index exposure like [[entities/index-fund]] or [[entities/broad-market-index-fund]].
- Choosing the right Treasury duration aligns with [[concepts/intermediate-term-treasury-strategy]], which balances protection during equity downturns with manageable rate‑sensitivity.
