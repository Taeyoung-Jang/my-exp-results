# Intermediate‑Term Treasury Strategy

**Definition**: An investment approach that allocates a portfolio’s bond portion to U.S. Treasury securities with an average duration of roughly five to seven years, aiming to provide meaningful downside protection for equities while limiting exposure to interest‑rate volatility.

---

## Why Choose Intermediate‑Term Treasuries?

- **Stabilization**: They tend to hold value better than stocks during market sell‑offs, offering a defensive ballast.
- **Rate Sensitivity**: Compared with long‑term Treasuries, the price swing from interest‑rate changes is moderate, avoiding the “roller‑coaster” effect of very long durations.
- **Yield Trade‑off**: Short‑term Treasuries are ultra‑stable but deliver minimal cushioning; intermediate‑term securities strike a balance between yield and volatility.

These traits make the strategy a core component of a broader [[concepts/bond-allocation-role]] that prioritizes **stability** over income.

---

## Key Parameters

| Parameter | Typical Range | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Duration** | 5 – 7 years | Sufficient to offset equity drawdowns while keeping rate risk manageable. |
| **Security Type** | Plain [[entities/us-treasury-securities]] (government‑issued, credit‑risk free) | Guarantees the defensive nature of the bond slice. |
| **Portfolio Share** | Often the majority of the bond allocation; e.g., 80‑90 % of the bond bucket in a simple plan. |

---

## Implementation Options

1. **Direct Treasury Funds** – Passively managed funds that track intermediate‑term Treasury indexes.
2. **ETF Exposure** – Exchange‑traded funds focused on the 5‑7 year Treasury segment.
3. **Managed Mutual Funds** – Actively managed funds with a mandate to stay within the target duration range.

All three options provide the same underlying exposure; the choice depends on cost, liquidity, and personal preference.

---

## Interaction with Other Portfolio Elements

- When paired with a **low‑cost index fund** core (see [[concepts/low-cost-index-investing]] and [[entities/index-fund]]), the intermediate‑term Treasury slice delivers a simple, “set‑and‑forget” structure.
- If a small **investment‑grade corporate bond** slice is retained for modest yield, it typically occupies 10‑20 % of the bond allocation, leaving the remaining 80‑90 % to the intermediate‑term Treasuries.
- Adding **high‑yield bonds** would shift the risk profile toward equity‑like behavior and is generally avoided in a pure stabilization strategy.

---

## Practical Steps

1. **Define the bond role** – Confirm the primary objective is stability (see [[concepts/bond-allocation-role]]).
2. **Select a fund/ETF** that targets a 5‑7 year duration of [[entities/us-treasury-securities]].
3. **Allocate the appropriate share** of the bond bucket, adjusting for any corporate exposure.
4. **Automate contributions** and set periodic rebalancing to maintain the target duration.
