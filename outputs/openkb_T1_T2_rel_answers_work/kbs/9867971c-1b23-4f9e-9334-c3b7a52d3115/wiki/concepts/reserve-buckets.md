# Reserve Buckets

**Definition**: Reserve Buckets is a structured liquidity approach that splits cash needs into distinct categories—personal emergency fund, rental‑property reserve, and private‑equity capital‑call reserve—to ensure sufficient liquidity while avoiding excess idle cash.

## Core Components

1. **Personal Emergency Fund**
   - Covers day‑to‑day living expenses for a chosen horizon (e.g., 3‑12 months).
   - Formula: `Monthly Personal Spending × Desired Months`.
   - In the example from [[summaries/session-31_2023-06-15]], a 6‑month horizon yields a $69,000 bucket.

2. **Rental Reserve**
   - Buffers against vacancy, repairs, and turnover for each [[entities/rental-property]].
   - Calculated as `Total Monthly Carrying Cost × Reserve Factor` plus average monthly repair costs.
   - The session‑31 worksheet used a 1.5× factor and added $1,500 of monthly repair allowance, resulting in a $24,000 bucket.

3. **Private‑Equity Reserve**
   - Treats the full amount of uncalled [[entities/private-equity-commitment]] as unavailable cash.
   - No prorating; the entire $360,000 uncalled commitment is reserved.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- The Reserve Buckets model is a concrete implementation of the broader [[concepts/liquidity-framework]], which emphasizes matching assets to time horizon and risk tolerance.
- It complements [[concepts/reserve-buckets]] (the same concept) and can be combined with [[concepts/low-cost-index-investing]] or [[concepts/bond-allocation-role]] when planning the allocation of the remaining investable assets.

## Practical Use

1. **Calculate each bucket** using the formulas above.
2. **Sum the buckets** to obtain the *Liquidity Floor* (e.g., $453,000 in the session‑31 example).
3. **Compare the floor to the balance in a [[entities/high-yield-savings-account]]** to assess liquidity health:
   - **Green**: Balance ≥ Liquidity Floor.
   - **Yellow**: Slightly below the floor (e.g., $400k‑$450k).
   - **Red**: Significantly below the floor, indicating a need to increase cash reserves.

## Benefits
- Provides a clear, numeric target for cash holdings.
- Reduces the risk of forced asset sales during market or property downturns.
- Helps investors balance liquidity with long‑term growth objectives.
