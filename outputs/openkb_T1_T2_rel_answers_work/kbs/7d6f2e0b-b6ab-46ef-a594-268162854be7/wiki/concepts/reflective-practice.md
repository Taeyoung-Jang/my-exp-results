## Reflective Practice

**Definition**

Reflective practice is a brief, intentional pause that encourages self‑awareness, gratitude, and meaning‑making. It often involves quiet attention to breath, a short prayer, or a simple mental note of gratitude or intention.

**Key Elements from [[summaries/session-08_2023-06-13]]**
- **Pre‑service rituals** such as naming a gratitude and a hope, focusing on breathing, or repeating a short mantra.
- **Post‑service rituals** like holding a piece of the sermon in mind, mentally naming a person or situation to pray for, or briefly noting a resonant line.
- Options that require **no writing** and a **notebook variation** for those who want a tangible record.

**Why It Works**
- The practice is **short (5–10 min)**, fitting easily into existing routines.
- It leverages **habit anchoring** by attaching the pause to the already‑established Sunday service, creating a natural cue for the reflective moment.
- It can be **flexible**, allowing both quiet solitude and a communal flavor when shared with a friend over coffee.

**Connections to Other Concepts**
- Supports the broader idea of [[concepts/communal-practice]] when paired with a brief shared activity after the service.
- Relies on [[concepts/habit-anchoring]] by using the church service as the anchor point for the new micro‑habit.

**Practical Guidelines**
1. **Choose a cue** – arrive a few minutes early to the service.
2. **Select a micro‑practice** – breathing pause, gratitude statement, or short prayer.
3. **Keep it brief** – aim for 5 minutes before and after the service.
4. **Optional notebook** – jot a single sentence or line if you want a tangible reminder.
