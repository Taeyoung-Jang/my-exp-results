# Habit Anchoring

**Definition**: Habit Anchoring is the practice of tying a new, desired habit to an already‑established routine or cue, leveraging the existing habit’s automaticity to make the new behavior more likely to stick.

## Core Elements
- **Anchor Cue**: An existing, reliable event (e.g., attending an Anglican service) that reliably occurs each week.
- **Micro‑Habit**: A small, low‑effort action (5–10 minutes) that can be performed before or after the anchor cue.
- **Stacking**: Placing the new micro‑habit directly before or after the anchor cue creates a habit stack, reducing friction and decision‑making.

## Application in [[summaries/session-08_2023-06-13]]
In the referenced session, the user already has a weekly Anglican service. The assistant suggested:
- **Pre‑service micro‑habits** such as a brief gratitude note, breathing pause, or short mantra.
- **Post‑service micro‑habits** like a reflective walk, a prayerful naming, or a one‑line notebook entry.
These suggestions exemplify habit anchoring by using the church service as the anchor cue and layering brief reflective or communal actions around it.

## Benefits
- **Consistency**: By attaching to a fixed weekly event, the new habit inherits the reliability of the anchor.
- **Low Cognitive Load**: The micro‑habit requires minimal effort, making it easier to maintain even during busy weeks.
- **Flexibility**: Options that require no writing or a quick notebook entry allow the practitioner to adapt to mood and context.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- [[concepts/reflective-practice]]: The pre‑ and post‑service habits are designed to foster brief moments of reflection.
- [[concepts/communal-practice]]: Incorporating a short coffee chat or walk with a fellow congregant adds a communal dimension to the anchored routine.

## Practical Tips for Implementing Habit Anchoring
1. **Identify a strong anchor** – a routine you never miss (e.g., Sunday service).
2. **Choose a tiny action** – something that takes 5 minutes or less.
3. **Tie the timing** – perform the new action immediately before or after the anchor.
4. **Use a cue reminder** – a mental note or a physical reminder (e.g., a sticky note on your church bulletin).
5. **Iterate** – after a couple of weeks, adjust the micro‑habit for better fit or expand it gradually.
