# Flexible Schedule

**Definition**: A flexible schedule is a planning approach that designates certain activities as *fixed* (non‑negotiable) while allowing other tasks to shift as needed, creating a resilient weekly rhythm that can absorb unexpected events.

## Core Elements
- **Fixed anchors** – Core obligations that stay constant each week (e.g., [[entities/sunday-mass]], [[entities/weekday-mass]], a Thursday [[entities/holy-hour]]). These provide structure and a sense of continuity.
- **Movable blocks** – Activities that can be relocated without breaking the overall flow, such as workouts, personal prayer, social evenings, or free/rest nights. The schedule treats these as interchangeable slots.
- **Swap zones** – Designated days (often Monday, Friday, Saturday) where movable blocks are most easily shifted to accommodate conflicts.
- **Rest protection** – At least one weeknight is deliberately kept unscheduled to preserve recovery and mental space.

## How It Appears in Session 34
In the *busy workweek* plan from [[summaries/session-34_2024-06-13]], the user establishes:
- Fixed items: Sunday Mass, the Thursday 7 pm holy hour, a Tuesday rest night, and a Wednesday morning weekday Mass.
- Movable items: daily prayer, three workout sessions, and a social evening.
- A simple rule for swapping: when a parish event occurs, protect the fixed anchors and shift activities within the swap zones.

## Benefits
- **Adaptability** – Allows seamless integration of spontaneous parish events or changes in work demands.
- **Reduced overwhelm** – By limiting the number of immutable commitments, the schedule feels lighter and more manageable.
- **Sustained habit formation** – Fixed anchors anchor the routine, while movable blocks keep the overall pattern fluid, supporting both [[concepts/habit-anchoring]] and [[concepts/reflective-practice]].
