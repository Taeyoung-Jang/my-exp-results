# Summary of Session 14 (2023‑06‑15)

This document captures a conversation about keeping large, active online forums organized and navigable. The key ideas revolve around **hierarchical categories**, **flexible tagging**, **search support**, and a **lightweight triage workflow** for deciding when to create new categories.

## Core Concepts
- **[[concepts/forum_structure]]** – A mix of stable high‑level categories and dynamic tags provides both clarity and adaptability.
- **Categories vs. tags** – Categories describe enduring, broad areas; tags capture specific, overlapping, or transient attributes.
- **Search tools** – Effective search (including suggested similar threads) reduces duplication and helps users locate older discussions.
- **Moderation culture** – Community norms, such as descriptive titles and on‑the‑fly redirection, scale better than rigid rules.
- **[[concepts/category_proliferation]]** – Treat categories as long‑term infrastructure; periodically review and collapse inactive ones.
- **Workflow for new categories** – A simple decision tree lets moderators quickly assess whether a thread fits an existing category or signals a new one.
- **[[concepts/triage_checklist]]** – A 5–7 question checklist (fit, duplicate, tag‑sufficiency, hesitation pattern, stability of grouping, navigation benefit) guides filing decisions.
- **Category creation threshold** – Create a new category only after observing a steady pattern (e.g., a few related threads per week over several weeks) rather than isolated spikes.

## Practical Recommendations
1. **Maintain a small, stable set of categories** and rely on tags for nuance.
2. **Use search and thread‑suggestion features** to prevent repeat posts.
3. **Encourage cultural norms** (good titles, focused topics, gentle redirection) among moderators and veteran members.
4. **Apply the triage checklist** when filing new threads to decide between:
   - (a) existing category + tags
   - (b) flag for review as a possible new category
   - (c) merge/redirect to an existing discussion.
5. **Monitor flag volume**: if two‑three similar flags appear within a short span and persist over weeks, consider a new category.

By combining structural hierarchy, supportive tools, and community‑driven practices, forums can stay navigable even as posting volume grows.
