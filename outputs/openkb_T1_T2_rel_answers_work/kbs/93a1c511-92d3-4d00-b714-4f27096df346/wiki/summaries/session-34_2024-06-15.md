## Summary

This conversation provides a practical framework for establishing healthier digital habits without a full “digital detox.” The core ideas are:

- **Gentle defaults** – treat boundaries as default settings rather than strict bans, reducing reliance on willpower. [[concepts/gentle_defaults]]
- **Friction points** – introduce small obstacles (e.g., moving apps off the home screen, logging out after use, app timers) that prompt a moment of reflection before opening an app. [[concepts/fractional_friction]]
- **Offline anchors** – pair online time with phone‑free periods such as meals, mornings, or the hour before bed to create natural breaks. [[concepts/offline_anchors]]
- **Notification control** – turn off non‑essential notifications to eliminate constant pull.

### iPhone “Set‑and‑Forget” Implementation

1. **Screen Time – App Limits**
   - Open *Settings → Screen Time → App Limits*.
   - Add a limit for chosen social apps and set the allowed time to a minimal value (e.g., 1 minute).
   - Use *Customize Days* to disable the limit on Saturday and Sunday, keeping weekdays restricted.
2. **Screen Time – Downtime**
   - In *Settings → Screen Time → Downtime*, schedule a weekday evening block.
   - Ensure the social apps are **not** listed under *Always Allowed*.
3. **Passcode protection**
   - Set a Screen Time passcode that you cannot easily guess in the moment (optionally have a trusted friend set it).
4. **Home‑screen management**
   - Move social apps into a hidden folder or off the home screen to remove visual cues.
5. **Notification settings**
   - Disable all notifications for the restricted apps.

### Making Boundaries Hard to Override

- Combine **App Limits** with **Downtime** for layered protection.
- Use a passcode to prevent quick overrides.
- Leverage the timer screen as a friction point that reminds you “not today.”
