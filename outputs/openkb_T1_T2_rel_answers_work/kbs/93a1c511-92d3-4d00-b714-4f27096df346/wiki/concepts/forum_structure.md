## Forum Structure: Hierarchy + Tagging

**Definition**: A forum architecture that uses a limited set of high‑level categories for broad, stable subjects while employing tags for specific, overlapping, or transient attributes.

### Why Combine Both?
- **Clarity**: Categories give newcomers a clear "map" of the community, reducing the cognitive load of browsing.
- **Flexibility**: Tags let users describe nuances without proliferating categories, keeping the hierarchy shallow.
- **Scalability**: As the forum grows, the stable category layer remains manageable, while tags adapt to emerging topics.

### Practical Guidelines (from [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-15]])
1. **Define Core Categories** – Identify a small set of enduring, high‑level areas that everyone intuitively recognizes (e.g., *General Discussion*, *Support*, *Announcements*).
2. **Use Tags for Specifics** – Apply descriptive tags for technologies, sub‑topics, moods, or any detail that changes frequently or overlaps multiple categories.
3. **Avoid Category Explosion** – Treat categories as long‑term infrastructure; before adding a new one, verify whether a tag under an existing category suffices. Periodically audit and merge inactive categories. See [[concepts/category_proliferation]] for more on controlling growth.
4. **Support with Search** – Pair the hierarchy with robust search and thread‑suggestion tools so users can locate older discussions without needing a new category.
5. **Cultural Reinforcement** – Encourage moderators and veteran members to model good practices: clear titles, appropriate category choice, and thoughtful tagging.

### Interaction with Other Concepts

- Effective **[[concepts/forum_structure]]** reduces the need for frequent structural changes, supporting a healthy balance between stability and adaptability.
