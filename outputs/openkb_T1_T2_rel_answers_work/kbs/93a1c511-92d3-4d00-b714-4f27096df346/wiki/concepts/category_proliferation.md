# Managing Category Proliferation

**Definition**: Managing Category Proliferation is the practice of keeping the number of high‑level forum categories small, stable, and purposeful while still allowing flexibility through tags and other organizational tools.

**Why it matters**
- Excessive categories overwhelm newcomers and make navigation harder.
- A bloated hierarchy dilutes moderation focus and reduces the usefulness of search.
- Stable categories act as long‑term infrastructure, whereas tags handle nuance and change.

**Key practices (from [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-15]])**
1. **Treat categories as permanent infrastructure** – before creating a new one, verify whether the topic can fit under an existing category with appropriate tags.
2. **Periodic review** – regularly audit categories for activity; collapse or merge those that are inactive.
3. **Use a lightweight decision process** – the [[concepts/triage_checklist]] helps moderators decide when a thread signals a genuine need for a new category.
4. **Set a creation threshold** – only introduce a new category after observing a steady pattern (e.g., a few related threads per week over several weeks), not after isolated spikes.
5. **Leverage tags** – tags capture specific, overlapping, or transient attributes, reducing pressure to create new categories for every nuance.

**Related concepts**
- [[concepts/forum_structure]] – the overall combination of categories, tags, search tools, and community norms that keep a forum navigable.
- [[concepts/triage_checklist]] – a practical checklist that includes questions about category fit and hesitation, which directly supports managing proliferation.
- [[concepts/category_proliferation]] – the broader concept that encompasses the strategies described here.

**Implementation tip**
Create a simple internal guideline document that references the above practices and the triage checklist. Train moderators and power users to apply the guideline consistently, and schedule quarterly reviews of the category list.
