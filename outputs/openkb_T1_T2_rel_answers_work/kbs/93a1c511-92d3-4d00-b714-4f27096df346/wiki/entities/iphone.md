# iPhone

The iPhone is Apple's line of smartphones that serves as the primary device for implementing the digital‑habit strategies discussed in [[summaries/session-34_2024-06-15]]. The conversation highlights several iPhone‑specific features and settings used to create healthier online boundaries.

## Key Points from the Session

- **Screen Time**: The iPhone’s built‑in *Screen Time* tool is employed to set *App Limits* and *Downtime* for social media apps, providing a "set‑and‑forget" approach to weekday restrictions.
- **Gentle Defaults**: By configuring defaults (e.g., minimal allowed time, weekend exemptions) the iPhone helps users rely less on willpower and more on system‑enforced limits. [[concepts/gentle_defaults]]
- **Fractional Friction**: Small obstacles such as moving apps off the home screen, logging out after use, and requiring a Screen Time passcode introduce modest friction that prompts intentional decisions. [[concepts/fractional_friction]]
- **Offline Anchors**: The advice recommends pairing the iPhone‑free periods with activities like meals or the hour before bed, creating natural breaks from the device. [[concepts/offline_anchors]]
- **Notification Management**: Turning off non‑essential notifications on the iPhone reduces constant digital pull.

## Practical Steps Mentioned

1. Open **Settings → Screen Time → App Limits** to set minute‑level limits for chosen social apps, customizing days to keep weekends unrestricted.
2. Use **Settings → Screen Time → Downtime** to schedule weekday evening blocks, ensuring the apps are not listed under *Always Allowed*.
3. Set a Screen Time passcode to make overrides difficult.
4. Relocate social apps to a hidden folder or off the home screen to remove visual cues.
5. Disable all notifications for the restricted apps.

These iPhone‑specific configurations illustrate how the device can support the broader concepts of digital wellbeing discussed in the session.
