# Boundary Setting

**Definition**: Boundary Setting is the practice of establishing clear personal limits to protect one’s wellbeing and maintain healthy relationships.

## Core Elements
- **Framing the Need**: Present quiet time as self‑care that improves how you contribute to the household, not as a withdrawal.
- **Physical Cues**: Use headphones, a partially closed door, or a designated "quiet corner" to signal the start of a boundary.
- **Consistent Routine**: Choose a regular time slot (e.g., morning or after work) and communicate it in advance so family members know when to expect it.
- **Gentle Scripts**: Use warm, respectful language when requesting time, responding to interruptions, and establishing routines.
- **Respectful Reassurance**: Emphasize that the need for space is about personal energy, not criticism of anyone, especially elders.
- **Phrase Avoidance**: Steer clear of wording that sounds blaming or overwhelming (e.g., "I need space from everyone").

## Application in a Multigenerational Home
The session‑12 conversation highlights how Boundary Setting can be adapted when parents and grandparents share the same space:
- **Adjust Scripts** to acknowledge constant presence while still asserting personal limits.
- **Leverage Shared Areas** by claiming a temporary spot (e.g., a chair with headphones) rather than demanding a private room.
- **Provide Reassurance** that you remain reachable for urgent matters.
