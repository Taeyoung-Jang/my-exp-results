## Noise Scoring Rubric

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A repeatable rubric for objectively scoring apartment noise during viewings.

---

### Overview
The Noise Scoring Rubric provides a structured way to evaluate the acoustic environment of a rental unit using clear, numeric anchors. It was created in the context of an apartment‑hunting checklist (see [[summaries/session-34_2025-02-13]]) and is designed to be quick enough for on‑the‑spot use while still offering enough granularity to compare multiple properties reliably.

---

### Scoring Structure
The rubric breaks noise assessment into three sub‑categories, each scored on a **0‑2** scale. The maximum raw total is **6**, which can then be converted to the weighted score used in the overall scoring template (see [[concepts/weighted-scoring-template]]).

| Sub‑category | Anchor 0 | Anchor 1 | Anchor 2 |
|--------------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Outside noise** (windows open/closed) | Loud even with windows shut; traffic or constant hum clearly audible | Manageable with windows shut; noticeable with windows open | Quiet with windows shut; low and occasional with windows open |
| **Inside / building noise** (hallway, neighbours, pipes) | Regular footsteps, voices, plumbing or lift noise clearly heard | Light or occasional sounds; nothing loud but still present | Barely any sound from neighbours, hallways or pipes |
| **One‑minute silent test** | Multiple interrupts (voices, traffic bursts, banging) | One or two mild noises; nothing sustained | No noticeable noise during the minute |

**Calculating the weighted noise score**
1. Add the three sub‑category scores (0‑6 total).
2. Divide the raw total by **2** to map it onto the 0‑3 scale used in the overall template.
3. Record the result as `Weighted noise score (out of 3): ___ / 3`.

---

### How to Use During a Viewing
1. **Outside noise** – Open and close the windows, listen with them both open and shut.
2. **Inside noise** – Walk the hallway, listen for neighbour activity, run a tap or flush the toilet to hear plumbing.
3. **One‑minute test** – Stand still for a full minute in the main living area, noting any interruptions.
4. Record each sub‑score next to the description, sum them, and apply the conversion.

---

### Relationship to Other Concepts
- The rubric feeds directly into the **[[concepts/weighted-scoring-template]]**, where noise carries the highest weight (3 points).
- It is a core component of the **[[concepts/apartment-viewing-checklist]]**, providing the objective measurement that balances subjective impressions.
- It helps identify **[[concepts/red-flags-apartment-hunting]]** such as persistent external traffic noise or poor internal sound insulation.

---

### Benefits
- **Consistency:** Same anchors across all viewings reduce bias.
- **Speed:** Simple 0‑2 choices can be made in seconds.
- **Comparability:** Raw totals can be aggregated across multiple properties for easy ranking.

---

### Limitations & Tips
- The rubric captures a snapshot; repeat visits at different times of day can reveal temporal variations.
- Use the rubric alongside other checklist items (commute, costs, building condition) for a holistic evaluation.
