# Shelf Placement Rules

Shelf Placement Rules are a simple hierarchy for deciding where household items belong on shelves. The rules prioritize three attributes of each item:

1. **Fragility** – Delicate or easily misshapen items belong on the **top shelf** where they are less likely to be crushed.
2. **Frequency of use** – Items accessed regularly should be placed at eye‑level or waist‑level, i.e., the **middle shelf**, for convenient reach.
3. **Weight** – Heavy or bulky items go on the **bottom shelf** to prevent them from falling and to provide a stable base.

These three criteria form a quick decision‑making flow that can be used without over‑thinking. When an item meets multiple criteria, the most restrictive (usually weight or fragility) determines placement.

## Supporting Practices

- **Buffer zone**: Leave open space or use small bins and soft liners around fragile items to create a protective cushion [[concepts/buffer_zone]].
- **Quick checklist**: A printable set of yes/no questions mirrors the three attributes and speeds up placement decisions [[concepts/quick_checklist]].
