# Quick Checklist for Shelf Placement

**Definition**: A short, printable list of yes/no questions that guides the immediate decision of whether an item belongs on the top, middle, or bottom shelf.

## Origin
This checklist was introduced in [[summaries/session-01_2023-06-15]] as a practical tool for organizing household storage without over‑thinking each item.

## The Checklist
1. **Is it fragile or easily misshapen?** → place on the **top shelf**.
2. **Do I use it often enough to want it at eye or waist level?** → place on the **middle shelf**.
3. **Is it heavy enough that I’d rather not lift it above my shoulders?** → place on the **bottom shelf**.
4. **Would it damage something else if it shifted or fell?** → place on the **bottom shelf**.
5. **Is it light but only used occasionally or seasonally?** → place on the **top shelf**.
6. **Does it need to stay upright or unstacked to avoid bending?** → place on the **top** (or middle if frequently used).

## How to Use
- Print the list and attach it to a closet door or shelf edge.
- Scan the questions in order; the first "yes" determines the appropriate shelf.
- Combine with the broader [[concepts/shelf_placement_rules]] to reinforce the three‑factor approach (fragility, usage frequency, weight).

## Benefits
- **Speed**: Reduces decision time to a few seconds per item.
- **Consistency**: Ensures uniform placement across the household.
- **Memory Aid**: Acts as a visual reminder of the underlying [[concepts/buffer_zone]] principle—leaving space around delicate items.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/shelf_placement_rules]] – the overarching framework of fragility, frequency, and weight.
- [[concepts/buffer_zone]] – the practice of leaving open space or using containers to protect fragile items.
