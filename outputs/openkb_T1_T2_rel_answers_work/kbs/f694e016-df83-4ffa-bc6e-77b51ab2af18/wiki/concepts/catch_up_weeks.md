# Catch‑Up Weeks

**Definition**: Dedicated buffer periods inserted into a downsizing timetable to allow the mover to recover from delays, re‑align tasks, and maintain momentum without stress.

## Why They Matter
- **Time budgeting**: When working full‑time, weekly effort is limited to 3–5 hours. Unexpected interruptions can push tasks beyond their allotted slot. Catch‑up weeks provide a safety net.
- **Prevent burnout**: Rather than extending the entire plan, a short, focused buffer helps keep the overall timeline realistic.
- **Maintain flow**: By designating specific weeks (e.g., Week 9 and Week 15 in the [[summaries/session-32_2024-12-16]] plan), the schedule preserves its logical room‑by‑room progression while giving flexibility.

## Placement in the Schedule
- **Integrated into the [[concepts/downsizing_schedule]]**: The schedule alternates active decluttering weeks with admin weeks. After a series of action weeks, a catch‑up week is inserted before moving to the next room or category.
- **Buffer zone**: Acts as a temporal buffer similar to the idea in [[concepts/buffer_zone]], but it is explicitly scheduled rather than ad‑hoc.

## Typical Activities During a Catch‑Up Week
| Activity | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Review the [[concepts/tracking_template]] | Identify items that lag behind their deadlines. |
| Complete unfinished decluttering tasks | Finish any leftover items from previous rooms. |
| Extra donation runs | Ensure items slated for donation are dropped off. |
| Quick admin chores | Respond to buyer messages, adjust prices, or schedule pickups if needed. |

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works alongside [[concepts/admin_weeks]]: while admin weeks focus on logistics, catch‑up weeks address overdue work.
- Supports the [[concepts/review_routine]]: the double‑weekly review helps decide when to trigger a catch‑up week.
- Complements [[concepts/time_budgeting]]: by allocating explicit buffer time, the overall weekly hour budget remains realistic.

## Implementation Tips
1. **Mark the week in your calendar** as a *Catch‑Up* slot and treat it like any other work block.
2. **Limit new tasks** – focus only on outstanding items; avoid adding fresh categories.
3. **Use the tracking sheet** to prioritize actions (e.g., items with approaching sell‑by dates).
4. **Celebrate small wins** after each catch‑up week to keep motivation high.
