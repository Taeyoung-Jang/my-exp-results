# Sentimental Decluttering

**Definition**: A focused approach to sorting and deciding the fate of keepsake items during a downsizing effort, aimed at minimizing emotional overload while ensuring meaningful memories are preserved.

## Overview
Sentimental decluttering is the dedicated week (Week 13) in the [[summaries/session-32_2024-12-16]] downsizing schedule where the primary goal is to process mementos, photos, and other memory‑laden objects.  The process balances emotional well‑being with the practical need to reduce possessions before an international move.

## Core Steps (as used in the 16‑week plan)
1. **Set a Physical Limit** – Prepare a small container (e.g., a tote) and declare it the “keep limit.”  Once the container is full, stop adding items.
2. **Quick Review** – Pick up each sentimental piece once, ask two questions:
   - *Will it serve a purpose in my life in Tokyo?*
   - *Would a photo capture its memory just as well?*
3. **Document** – Take a photo of any item you release.  Store the photos in a digital folder for later reminiscence.
4. **Assign Status** – Mark each item in the tracking template as **keep**, **donate**, or **trash** and record the next action (e.g., "pack" or "donate drop‑off").
5. **Pack Early** – Items marked **keep** should be placed in the suitcase list immediately so they are not re‑examined later.

## Integration with the Wider Plan
- The sentimental week follows the review routine and precedes the final whole‑home review (Week 14), ensuring that emotional decisions are settled before the final clearance.
- It respects the overall time budgeting principle of 3–5 hours per week, typically requiring 2–4 focused evening sessions.
- Although it is a distinct week, it may overlap with admin weeks (e.g., photographing items) and can be supported by a quick checklist for rapid decision‑making.

## Tips for Success
- **Buffer Zone**: If you find the week stretching, use a catch up weeks buffer to avoid feeling rushed.
- **Room‑Based Context**: While the sentimental week is not tied to a single room, you can still sweep through rooms in the order used by the room based planning schedule, ensuring nothing is overlooked.
- **Review Frequency**: Update the tracking spreadsheet twice a week (start and end) as part of the review routine to keep deadlines and next actions current.

## Benefits
- Reduces the risk of lingering emotional items that can stall the move.
- Provides a systematic way to preserve memories digitally while freeing physical space.
- Aligns with a structured downsizing schedule that balances emotional and logistical tasks.
