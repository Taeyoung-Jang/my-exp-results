# Scheduling Boundaries

**Definition**: A set of personal or team‑level rules that define when an individual is available for meetings and when work‑related communications should stop, typically to protect work‑life balance.

## Key Elements (derived from [[summaries/session-15_2024-09-14]])
- **Clear time windows** – e.g., “available until about 5 pm” or “no evening meetings.”
- **Team‑norm framing** – position the boundary as a shared expectation rather than a personal request.
- **Emergency clause** – a brief statement that true emergencies will still be addressed outside normal hours.
- **Alternative options** – suggest earlier meeting slots, async updates, or flexible daytime windows.

## Practical Applications
1. **Slack messaging** – concise, friendly notes that reinforce the boundary while encouraging the team to avoid after‑hours meetings.  
2. **Email communication** – subject lines and formal drafts that present the boundary as a team‑wide working‑hours norm.
3. **Pushback responses** – short, firm replies when others claim a specific time is the only option.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/communication_templates]] – reusable message formats that incorporate scheduling boundaries.
- [[concepts/team_norms]] – broader cultural practices that support consistent work‑hour expectations.

## Example from the Source Document
> “Quick scheduling note for everyone: I’d love for us to make earlier‑in‑the‑day meetings our default … I’m not available for evening calls anymore, but I’ll always respond if something is a genuine emergency.”

This exemplifies a **scheduling boundary** that balances personal need (child’s nightly care) with team collaboration, using a tone that promotes a shared norm.
