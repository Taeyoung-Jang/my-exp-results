# Weekly Practice Plan

**Definition**: A repeatable, 7‑day schedule that allocates 10–60 minutes each day to focused ear‑training drills for the core [[concepts/mixing_fundamentals]], progressing from gain staging to EQ, compression and reverb, and ending with integrated mix practice.

---

## Core Components
- **Gain Staging** – establishing clean levels before any tonal processing.
- **EQ** – identifying and shaping frequency balance.
- **Compression** – controlling dynamics and learning the feel of transparent vs. pumped sound.
- **Reverb** – perceiving depth and space without over‑loading the mix.
- **Ear‑training** – blind listening exercises to internalise the effect of each tool.

These components map directly onto the learning order presented in the source [[summaries/session-04_2025-06-18]].

---

## Daily Schedule (Mon–Sun)
| Day | Focus | Time Blocks | Self‑Test |
|-----|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Monday** | Gain staging | 10 min pull‑down, 10 min rebuild | Predict the level needed to re‑introduce a muted track. |
| **Tuesday** | EQ | 15 min sweep, 10 min precise cuts | Guess the frequency of a harsh or muddy spot before viewing the EQ display. |
| **Wednesday** | Compression | 10 min exaggerate, 10 min dial‑back | Detect when the compressor is bypassed purely by feel. |
| **Thursday** | Reverb | 10 min add single reverb, 10 min switch types | Name the reverb type (room, plate, hall) in a short song excerpt. |
| **Friday** | Integrated mix | 30 min apply all fundamentals | After a 5‑minute break, identify one immediate fix; faster instincts signal improvement. |
| **Saturday** | Pure ear training | 20 min pink‑noise EQ sweep (using [[entities/pink_noise]]) | Call out the frequency range before checking the EQ curve. |
| **Sunday** | Critical listening | 15 min analyse instrument placement in favourite tracks | Describe left‑right, front‑back and bright‑dark placement more precisely than a month ago. |

---

## Self‑Test Design
Each day ends with a short, meter‑free test that forces the learner to rely on auditory perception:
1. **Prediction** – estimate a parameter (level, frequency, reverb type) before visual confirmation.
2. **Recognition** – identify a processing state (e.g., compressor active vs. bypassed) by feel.
3. **Description** – verbally or in writing summarise the perceived change.

Consistent success on these tests indicates growing internal models of sound.

---

## Measuring Progress
- **Versioned Mixes**: Save a brief mix every week; after a short break, compare instincts vs. actual adjustments.
- **Speed of Decision**: Track how quickly the self‑test can be answered correctly.
- **Depth of Description**: Over time, the ability to articulate spatial and tonal characteristics improves.
