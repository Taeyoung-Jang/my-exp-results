# Ear Training for Audio Production

**Definition**: A set of deliberate listening drills aimed at sharpening the ability to hear, diagnose, and manipulate the core [[concepts/mixing_fundamentals]]—gain staging, EQ, compression, and reverb—within a music mix.

## Why ear training matters
- Professional mixes rely on *intentional* use of the fundamentals, not just visual metering.
- Consistent, short‑duration drills condition the brain to recognize frequency, dynamic, and spatial cues quickly, leading to faster, more confident decisions in a DAW.

## Core components (as outlined in [[summaries/session-04_2025-06-18]])
1. **Gain staging drills** – Pull all faders down, raise them back up slowly, then predict the level needed to re‑balance a muted track.
2. **EQ identification** – Sweep a narrow boost across the spectrum on a single source, then replace the boost with a subtle cut; guess the problematic frequency before viewing the EQ display.
3. **Compression perception** – Over‑compress a loop to hear obvious pumping, then back it off; close your eyes and detect when the compressor is bypassed solely by feel.
4. **Reverb discrimination** – Toggle a dry sound to wet with a single reverb, switch between room, plate, and hall types, and name the space you hear in a reference track.
5. **Pure frequency‑calling** – Play pink noise (or any broadband source) and sweep an EQ boost, calling out the frequency range before checking the meter.

## Weekly practice integration
The concept is embedded in a [[concepts/weekly_practice_plan]] that spreads these drills across a seven‑day schedule, each day lasting 10‑30 minutes.  Each session ends with a quick *self‑test* to measure improvement without relying on visual meters.

## Measuring progress
- **Version comparison**: Save mix snapshots before and after weekly cycles; note faster instinctive adjustments.
- **Self‑test accuracy**: Track how often frequency guesses, level predictions, or reverb type identifications are correct.
- **Listening depth**: Over time, describe instrument placement (left‑right, front‑back, bright‑dark) with increasing detail.
