# Digital Audio Workstation (DAW)

A **Digital Audio Workstation (DAW)** is a software application used for recording, editing, arranging, and mixing audio. It provides the virtual environment where musicians and audio engineers apply the [[concepts/mixing_fundamentals]]—gain staging, EQ, compression, and reverb—to create a polished sound.

## Key facts from the session‑04_2025-06-18 document
- The weekly practice plan suggests loading tracks into a **DAW** to practice gain staging, EQ sweeps, compression experiments, and reverb auditioning.
- Daily drills rely on the DAW’s ability to mute, solo, and adjust fader levels quickly, enabling the self‑tests described in the plan.
- The session emphasizes using the DAW for **ear training** exercises, such as sweeping an EQ while blind‑folded or listening to pink‑noise sweeps.
- The plan’s “integrated mix” day has the learner apply all fundamentals inside the DAW, then compare versioned mixes to gauge improvement.
