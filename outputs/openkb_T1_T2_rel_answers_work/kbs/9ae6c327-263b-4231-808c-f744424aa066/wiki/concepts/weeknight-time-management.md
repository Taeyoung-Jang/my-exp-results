# Weeknight Time Management

**Definition**: A set of practices that help people allocate limited evening hours—especially after a demanding work shift—to prepare and enjoy nutritious meals with minimal active cooking time.

## Core Elements
1. **Advance Batch Prep** – Dedicate a short, focused session (often on a day off) to cook staple components such as starches, roasted vegetables, and protein. In *session-39_2025-06-09* a 45‑minute Sunday prep covered rice, roasted veg, and shredded [[entities/rotisserie-chicken]].
2. **Assembly‑First Meals** – Structure dinner around quickly combining pre‑made ingredients. The plan uses [[concepts/quick-dinner-assemblies]] like warm‑and‑mix bowls, a microwave‑baked potato, and canned beans.
3. **Minimal Active Cooking** – Target a 15‑minute active window on weeknights. Techniques include microwaving [[entities/microwave-rice]], reheating roasted veg, and using ready‑to‑heat proteins.
4. **Mild, Flexible Flavors** – Keep sauces simple (e.g., lemon‑yogurt drizzle using [[entities/lemon]] and [[entities/plain-yogurt]]) to avoid extra prep.
5. **Time‑Blocking** – Allocate specific slots (e.g., 0:00‑0:45 for Sunday prep, 6:45‑7:00 for weekday assembly) to maintain consistency.

## Benefits
- Guarantees a nutritious dinner before the typical bedtime of 9 PM for early‑shift workers.
- Reduces decision fatigue by having a set menu and ingredient inventory.
- Limits evening screen time and stress, supporting better sleep.
