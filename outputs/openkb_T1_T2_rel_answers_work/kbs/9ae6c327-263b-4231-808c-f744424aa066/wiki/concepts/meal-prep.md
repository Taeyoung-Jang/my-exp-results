# Meal Prep

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Preparing staple foods in advance so that weekdays meals can be assembled quickly with minimal cooking time.

---

## Core Principles
- **Batch cooking:** Make a neutral base (e.g., rice, quinoa, roasted potatoes) and a set of vegetables in one session.
- **Protein shortcuts:** Rely on pre‑cooked or ready‑to‑heat proteins such as rotisserie chicken, canned beans, or pre‑cooked lentils.
- **Assembly focus:** Combine the pre‑made components with a simple sauce or dressing at meal time; each dinner should take ≤ 15 minutes.
- **Timeboxing:** Limit the prep day to about 45 minutes, allocating specific timestamps for cooking rice, roasting veg, and shredding chicken.

## Benefits Highlighted in [[summaries/session-39_2025-06-09]]
- Enables a **early‑shift bakery worker** to finish dinner by 9 PM.
- Reduces weekday chopping and active cooking time.
- Provides variety through interchangeable bases, proteins, and mild sauces.
- Supports dietary preferences (low‑spice, mild flavors) while keeping meals feeling like "real food."

## Typical Sunday Prep Workflow (from the source)
1. **Rice:** Cook a pot of rice (≈20 min) **or** use 3‑4 packets of [[entities/microwave-rice]].
2. **Roasted vegetables:** Sheet‑pan roast baby carrots, broccoli florets, and cherry tomatoes (≈25 min).
3. **Protein:** Shred a [[entities/rotisserie-chicken]].
4. **Cool & store** components in separate containers for the week.

## Example Weeknight Assemblies
- Warm rice, veg, and chicken; finish with lemon, [[entities/olive-oil]], and a pinch of salt.
- Microwave‑ready baked potato topped with veg, chicken, and [[entities/plain-yogurt]].
- Bean‑and‑rice bowl with cheese and a lemon‑yogurt drizzle.
