# Quick Dinner Assemblies

**Definition**: A meal‑planning approach that relies on a handful of pre‑cooked bases, proteins, and vegetables, allowing a complete dinner to be assembled in 15 minutes or less.

## Core Principles
1. **Batch preparation** – Cook a neutral starch (e.g., [[entities/microwave-rice]] or dry rice) and roast or steam vegetables once a week.
2. **Convenient proteins** – Use store‑bought shortcuts such as [[entities/rotisserie-chicken]], canned beans, or pre‑cooked lentils.
3. **Simple assembly** – Combine the base, protein, and veg, then finish with a quick sauce or drizzle.
4. **Mild flavor focus** – Enhance with easy sauces like a lemon‑yogurt mix (using [[entities/lemon]] and [[entities/plain-yogurt]]) or a splash of [[entities/olive-oil]].

## Example from [[summaries/session-39_2025-06-09]]
- **Sunday prep**: Cook rice, roast baby carrots, broccoli, and cherry tomatoes, and shred a rotisserie chicken.
- **Monday**: Warm rice and veg, add chicken, finish with lemon and olive oil.
- **Tuesday**: Microwave‑ready baked potato ( [[entities/microwave-baked-potato]] ), split, top with veg, chicken, and yogurt or cheese.
- **Wednesday‑Friday**: Rotate canned beans, lentils, or soup with the same base components, using the same quick sauces.

## Benefits
- **Time efficiency** – ≤ 15 minutes cooking on a work night.
- **Reduced effort** – Minimal chopping or stovetop work.
- **Flexibility** – Mix‑and‑match components for variety.
- **Consistent nutrition** – Balanced meals with carbs, protein, and veg.
