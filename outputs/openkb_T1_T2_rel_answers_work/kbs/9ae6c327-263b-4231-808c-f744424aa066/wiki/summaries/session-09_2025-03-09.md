# Summary of session-09_2025-03-09

**Main contribution**: A step‑by‑step, flexible wind‑down schedule (9:30 PM – 10:30 PM) with optional cues, low‑stimulation activities, and a fallback plan for when sleepiness doesn’t arrive.

---

## Core wind down routine
- **Signal the transition** (9:30‑9:40): dim lights, tidy briefly, choose one cue – either a cup of herbal tea **or** a warm shower.
- **Low‑effort activity** (9:40‑10:00): sip tea while reading or listening to calm audio; after a shower, stretch, journal, or simply sit.
- **Gentle winding** (10:00‑10:20): continue reading, do slow stretches, or lie with a dim lamp to let thoughts settle.
- **Sleep transition** (10:20‑10:30): turn off bright sources, get into bed, and let the body drift.

## What to **avoid** screen avoidance
- Bright screens (phones, tablets, computers).
- Work‑related tasks or intense conversations.
- Stimulating activities that raise arousal (e.g., vigorous exercise, emotionally charged media).
- Anything that feels like a chore; keep the hour pleasant and predictable.

## Consistent consistent bedtime
- Target bedtime: **10:30 PM**.
- Start the routine exactly one hour before, maintaining the same cue pattern each night to strengthen habit.

## Handling "not sleepy at 10:30" drowsiness cues
1. **Leave the bed** for **10‑20 minutes** (or until natural drowsiness appears).
2. Engage in **low‑stimulation activities**:
   - Reading mild material in dim light.
   - Very light stretching.
   - Slow, deep‑breathing while seated.
3. **Avoid** bright light, emotionally engaging content, or cognitively demanding tasks.
4. When a subtle wave of sleepiness arises, **return to bed**.

## Key takeaways
- Keep the routine **short, pleasant, and repeatable** to increase adherence.
- Use a **single environmental cue** to signal winding down.
- Stick to **screen‑free, low‑arousal activities** throughout the hour.
- When sleep doesn’t come, a brief, calm out‑of‑bed period prevents bed‑associated frustration and supports the next attempt.
