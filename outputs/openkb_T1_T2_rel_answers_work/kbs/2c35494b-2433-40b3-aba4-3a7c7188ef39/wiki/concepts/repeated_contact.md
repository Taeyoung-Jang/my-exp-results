# Repeated Contact

**Definition**: Repeated Contact refers to the habit of meeting the same person(s) on a consistent, recurring basis so that interactions evolve from feeling *scheduled* to feeling *natural* and effortless.

---

### Why it matters
- In adult life, **busy schedules** often push socialising into occasional, planned events, which can keep relationships at a surface level.  By embedding **regular contact** into a shared activity (e.g., a class, club, hobby group, or volunteer project), the friction of planning is reduced and the friendship gains a rhythm.
- The repetition provides the *shared ease* needed for small talk to deepen into more authentic conversation.

---

### Core strategies (from [[summaries/session-39_2024-06-15]])
1. **Join structured groups** that meet regularly – fitness classes, language clubs, community volunteering, etc.
2. **Leverage built‑in schedules** so you don’t have to constantly initiate new plans.
3. **Use the regular meet‑ups as a platform** for incremental vulnerability and low‑stakes 1:1 moments (see [[concepts/incremental_vulnerability]] and [[concepts/low_stakes_one_on_one]]).

---

### Relationship to other concepts
- **[[concepts/incremental_vulnerability]]** – Once repeated contact establishes a comfortable baseline, sharing modest personal details becomes easier and feels less risky.
- **[[concepts/low_stakes_one_on_one]]** – Regular group interaction creates trust, making low‑stakes one‑on‑one invitations feel natural rather than forced.
- **[[concepts/shared_ease]]** – Repeated encounters build a sense of ease, reducing the need for explicit coordination and allowing conversations to flow.
- **[[concepts/life_busy_phase]]** – Even during hectic periods, embedding interaction into pre‑existing routines helps maintain connection without adding extra planning load.

---

### Practical example
> *“I joined a weekly community gardening group. After a few meetings, I started inviting a fellow participant for coffee after the session. Because we already saw each other consistently, the invitation felt low‑pressure, and we could gradually share more personal thoughts.”*

---

### Take‑away
Creating **repeated contact** is a cornerstone for turning casual acquaintances into *real friends* by providing the structural cadence that lets authenticity and trust develop organically.
