# Summary of session-10_2021-07-24: Block Party Planning

This document provides a practical, printable one‑page sheet for organizing a neighborhood block party. It covers all essential phases—from securing permits to day‑of execution—via a concise [[concepts/event_planning_checklist]], a [[concepts/timeline_planning]] timeline, and a [[concepts/community_budgeting]] table with low, medium, and high budget examples.

## Core Elements

- **Permit & Insurance**: Identify city requirements and submit at least six weeks ahead.
- **Budget Levels**: Sample cost ranges for low ($75‑$150), medium ($200‑$350), and high ($500‑$900) budgets, broken down by permits, food/drinks, rentals, kids’ supplies, and misc.
- **Food Strategy**: Potluck vs. grill vs. food truck, with a potluck sign‑up grid for main dishes, sides, snacks, desserts, drinks, and ice.
- **Music & Kids’ Activities**: Simple Bluetooth playlist or neighbor DJ; low‑maintenance activities like chalk, bubbles, and yard games.
- **Volunteer Roles**: Setup, cleanup, music coordinator, kids’ activity helper, and street/safety point‑person – a volunteer roles matrix.
- **House Rules**: Quiet hours, trash disposal, pet policy, and responsible alcohol consumption.
- **Contact Section**: Blank fields for name, phone, and email, with a note that the planner is the final day‑of decision maker.

## Timeline Snapshot

1. **6 weeks out** – Confirm date, check permit rules, begin application.
2. **4 weeks out** – Announce event, settle budget approach.
3. **2 weeks out** – Finalize food plan, collect potluck sign‑ups, assign volunteers.
4. **Few days before** – Purchase supplies, send reminders.
5. **Day of** – Gradual setup, apply house rules, coordinate volunteers.

The sheet is ready to copy into an email or flyer, making community event organization clear, inclusive, and low‑stress.
