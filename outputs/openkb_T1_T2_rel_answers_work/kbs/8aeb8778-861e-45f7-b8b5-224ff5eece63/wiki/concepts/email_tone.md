# Email Tone

**Definition**: Email tone refers to the level of formality, directness, and overall attitude conveyed through word choice, structure, and phrasing in written correspondence.

## Key Elements
- **Formality** – Determines how professional or casual the language is (e.g., "Dear" vs. "Hi").
- **Directness** – The degree to which the request or information is stated plainly without excessive politeness buffers.
- **Warmth** – Inclusion of friendly sign‑offs or personal touches to soften the message.

## Application in Session 31
The document [[summaries/session-31_2023-07-24]] illustrates three practical tone variations for a condo‑board inquiry:
1. **Formal** – Uses polite conventions and a structured layout, suitable when addressing senior officials.
2. **Direct** – Gets straight to the point while remaining courteous, useful for efficiency‑focused communication.
3. **Friendly** – Adds a warm, steady tone to maintain a collaborative atmosphere.

These examples show how adjusting tone can balance firmness with politeness, especially when requesting clarification on a [[concepts/verification_process]].

## Usage Tips
- **Match the audience**: Higher formality for board presidents, more relaxed for property managers.
- **Align with purpose**: Direct tone for urgent clarification; friendly tone for relationship‑building.
- **Maintain consistency**: Keep tone uniform across initial email, follow‑up, and escalation to avoid mixed signals.
