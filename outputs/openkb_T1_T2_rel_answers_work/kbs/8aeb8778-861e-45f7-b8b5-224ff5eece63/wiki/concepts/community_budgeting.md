# Community Budgeting for Block Parties

Community budgeting is the process of estimating and allocating funds for the essential elements of a block party, allowing organizers to choose a realistic financial plan that matches the neighborhood’s expectations and resources. The concept is illustrated in [[summaries/session-10_2021-07-24]] and ties closely to the broader [[concepts/event_planning_checklist]] and [[concepts/timeline_planning]] frameworks.

## Why Budgeting Matters
- **Transparency:** Neighbors see where money is spent, fostering trust.
- **Scalability:** A tiered approach (low, medium, high) lets the same event be scaled up or down.
- **Resource Allocation:** Ensures essential items—permits, food, rentals, kids’ supplies, and miscellaneous costs—are covered before optional extras are considered.

## Budget Tiers (sample ranges)
| Tier | Permit/Insurance | Food & Drinks | Rentals | Kids’ Supplies | Miscellaneous |
|------|------------------|---------------|---------|----------------|----------------|
| **Low** | $0–$25 | $20–$60 (mostly potluck) | $0 | $10–$20 | $10–$25 |
| **Medium** | $0–$40 | $60–$120 | $50–$100 (tables/chairs) | $20–$40 | $20–$50 |
| **High** | $0–$75 | $250–$500 (food truck or catering) | $100–$200 (tables, shade tents, sound) | $40–$80 | $40–$60 |

### How to Use the Tiers
1. **Assess Community Appetite** – Survey neighbors for preferred level of spending.
2. **Match to Timeline** – Align budgeting milestones with the planning timeline (e.g., secure permits 6 weeks out, finalize food costs 2 weeks out).
3. **Track Expenses** – Use a simple spreadsheet or checklist to record actual spend against each category.
4. **Adjust Flexibly** – If a high‑budget item is unavailable, shift to the next lower tier without compromising core event quality.

## Integration with Planning Tools
- **Event Planning Checklist** – Include budget decisions as checklist items (e.g., "Set budget level").
- **Timeline Planning** – Schedule budget‑related tasks at appropriate weeks (permit application, food cost confirmation, rental bookings).

## Practical Tips
- **Leverage Sponsorships:** Local businesses may cover rentals or drinks, effectively lowering the budget tier.
- **Encourage Potluck Contributions:** Reduces food costs and engages neighbors.
- **Reuse Materials:** Reusing tables, chairs, or decorations from previous events cuts rental expenses.

By following this structured budgeting approach, block party organizers can create a clear financial roadmap that supports a smooth, enjoyable celebration for the whole neighborhood.
