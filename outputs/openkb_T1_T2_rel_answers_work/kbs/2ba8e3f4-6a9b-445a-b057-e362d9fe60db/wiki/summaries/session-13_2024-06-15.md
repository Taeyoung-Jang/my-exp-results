# Summary of session-13_2024-06-15

**Main focus:** Strategies to enhance *good‑quality sleep* by redesigning evening habits and troubleshooting nighttime awakenings.

## Key Concepts
- **Sleep quality** – defined by ease of falling asleep, depth of sleep, and feeling rested, not just total hours. [[concepts/sleep_quality]]
- **Evening routine** – a predictable wind‑down period with dim lighting, low‑stimulus activities, and consistent timing. [[concepts/evening_routine]]
- **Environmental factors** – room temperature, light exposure, noise, bedding comfort. [[concepts/environmental_sleep_factors]]
- **Behavioral factors** – timing of caffeine, alcohol consumption, meal size, stimulating activities. [[concepts/behavioral_sleep_factors]]
- **Medical considerations** – sleep apnea, reflux, chronic pain, anxiety, overactive bladder as possible causes of fragmented sleep. [[concepts/medical_sleep_issues]]

## Findings & Recommendations
1. **Temperature experiment**
   - Set thermostat ~2–5 °F lower (mid‑60 °F range) and use breathable bedding or a gentle fan. [[entities/thermostat]] [[entities/fan]]
   - Keep all other habits unchanged.
   - *Win criteria*: fewer awakenings, shorter wake‑up duration, faster return to sleep, or higher morning restfulness. This constitutes a simple temperature‑focused trial.
2. **Caffeine cutoff**
   - Move last caffeine intake to no later than late morning (≈10 am). [[entities/caffeine]]
   - Observe for reduction in night‑time wake‑ups.
3. **Alcohol adjustment**
   - Eliminate or shift evening wine to earlier, smaller amount. [[entities/wine]]
   - Monitor for decreased fragmentation in the second half of night.

## Experiment Order & Methodology
- Change one variable at a time, each for **~1 week** to allow sleep architecture to stabilize.
- Recommended sequence: **temperature → caffeine → alcohol** for quickest observable impact. This ordering helps isolate each factor’s effect.

## Practical Takeaway
Focus first on **cooling the bedroom**, then adjust **caffeine timing**, and finally **modify alcohol consumption**, while using the simple tracking sheet to assess changes in wake‑up frequency, latency back to sleep, and morning alertness.
