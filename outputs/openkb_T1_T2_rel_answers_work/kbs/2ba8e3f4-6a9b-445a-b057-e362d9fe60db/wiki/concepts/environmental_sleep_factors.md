# Environmental Sleep Factors

**Definition**: The physical conditions of the sleep environment that affect how easily a person falls asleep, stays asleep, and feels rested in the morning. These include temperature, light, noise, air flow, and bedding comfort.

## Key Elements

### Temperature
- Cooler rooms (mid‑60 °F / ~18 °C) promote deeper sleep.
- Adjusting the [[entities/thermostat]] a few degrees lower and using breathable bedding can reduce nighttime awakenings. 
- In **[[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]]**, a temperature experiment was recommended as the first step to improve sleep quality.

### Light Exposure
- Dim, warm lighting in the hour before bed signals the brain to wind down.
- Reducing bright overhead lights and screens minimizes melatonin suppression.

### Noise & Airflow
- Quiet or steady‑sound backgrounds help maintain sleep continuity.
- A gentle [[entities/fan]] can improve air circulation without creating disruptive drafts.

### Bedding & Comfort
- Mattress firmness, pillow support, and breathable sheets affect sleep comfort and can prevent micro‑arousals.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/sleep_quality]]**: Environmental factors are a primary driver of perceived sleep quality.
- **[[concepts/behavioral_sleep_factors]]**: Behaviors such as evening screen use interact with environmental conditions.
- **[[concepts/medical_sleep_issues]]**: Persistent environmental disturbances may mask or exacerbate medical conditions like [[entities/sleep_apnea]].

## Practical Recommendations (Based on Session 13)
1. **Cool the room**: Set the [[entities/thermostat]] 2‑5 °F lower than usual.
2. **Improve airflow**: Use a quiet [[entities/fan]] or open a window for gentle ventilation.
3. **Optimize lighting**: Switch to low‑intensity, warm lights at least 30 minutes before bedtime.
4. **Minimize noise**: Employ earplugs, white‑noise machines, or soft fans to create a consistent soundscape.

These steps target the most common environmental disruptors cited in **[[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]]** and can be tested individually to gauge impact.
