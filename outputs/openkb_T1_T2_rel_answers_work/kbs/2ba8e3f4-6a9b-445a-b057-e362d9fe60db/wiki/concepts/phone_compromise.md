# Phone Compromise Rule

**Definition**: A set of concrete limits on mobile device usage in the early part of a morning routine, allowing only essential utility checks while prohibiting distracting apps.

## Core Principles
1. **Allowed actions (first 20 minutes)**
   - Dismiss alarm and turn off snooze.
   - Hydrate (drink water) and any brief health‑related checks.
   - Quick glance at the calendar or notes to identify the day’s single priority.
2. **Forbidden actions (first 20 minutes)**
   - Email inbox, news feeds, social media, group chats, or any app that generates notifications.
   - Browsing, streaming, or any activity that pulls attention into reactive mode.
3. **Post‑20‑minute window**
   - Functional apps needed for work (task manager, notes, timers) are permitted.
   - Still avoid prolonged scrolling until the routine is complete.

## Rationale
- **Morning momentum**: By eliminating early‑day digital noise, the brain can stay in the refreshed state achieved after a good night’s sleep, reinforcing [[concepts/morning_momentum]].
- **Backbone alignment**: The rule fits neatly into the [[concepts/morning_backbone]] of wake‑up → movement → focus anchor → hygiene → transition, ensuring the phone does not disrupt any pillar.
- **Transition cue**: Allowing a brief, purposeful phone check (e.g., calendar) provides a mental hand‑off without derailing focus.

## Interaction with Other Practices
- **Coffee timing**: Placing coffee preparation after the 20‑minute phone‑free window leverages caffeine as a reward and transition cue, linking to [[concepts/coffee_as_transition]].
- **Work‑from‑home flow**: The rule supports the [[concepts/wfh_morning_flow]] by keeping the pre‑work period distraction‑free, helping you start focused work immediately.
- **Minimum‑viable mornings**: Even in a compressed 10‑minute routine, the phone compromise can be applied by reserving the first two minutes for essential checks only, preserving the essence of the rule ([[concepts/minimum_viable_morning]]).

## Implementation Tips
- **Set a timer** for 20 minutes to enforce the rule automatically.
- **Configure Do‑Not‑Disturb** to silence all non‑essential notifications during this window.
- **Create a shortcut** that opens calendar/notes with a single tap, reducing friction for the allowed checks.
