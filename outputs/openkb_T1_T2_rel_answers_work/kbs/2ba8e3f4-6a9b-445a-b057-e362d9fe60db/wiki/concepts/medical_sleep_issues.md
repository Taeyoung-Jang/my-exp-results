# Medical Sleep Issues

**Definition**: Health‑related conditions that cause fragmented or non‑restorative sleep, often requiring medical evaluation beyond environmental or behavioral adjustments.

## Common Medical Contributors
- **Sleep apnea** – breathing pauses that lead to frequent awakenings, dry mouth, and gasping. [[entities/sleep_apnea]]
- **Acid reflux / GERD** – nighttime heartburn can wake the sleeper.
- **Chronic pain** – discomfort from injuries or conditions (e.g., arthritis) interrupts sleep cycles.
- **Anxiety or stress‑related arousal** – mental “on” state that can trigger wake‑ups.
- **Overactive bladder** – frequent nighttime urination disrupts continuity.
- **Dry mouth** – may signal mouth breathing or mild apnea.

## When to Consider Medical Causes
1. **Persistent awakenings** despite fixing [[concepts/environmental_sleep_factors]] (temperature, light, noise) and [[concepts/behavioral_sleep_factors]] (caffeine timing, alcohol use, evening routine). [[concepts/evening_routine]]
2. **Red‑flag symptoms** such as loud snoring, gasping, choking, noticeable pauses in breathing, or waking with a dry mouth.
3. **Lack of improvement** after a 1‑week trial of the recommended environmental and behavioral experiments (cooling the room, adjusting caffeine, reducing wine).

## Interaction with Sleep Quality
Medical sleep issues directly lower overall [[concepts/sleep_quality]] by reducing deep sleep stages and increasing sleep fragmentation. Addressing them often requires a healthcare professional’s assessment, possible sleep studies, or targeted treatments (e.g., CPAP for apnea, medication for reflux, therapy for anxiety).
