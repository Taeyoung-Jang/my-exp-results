# Sleep Quality

**Definition**: Sleep Quality refers to how well a sleep period fulfills the body’s restorative needs, encompassing how quickly one falls asleep, how deeply and continuously sleep is maintained, and how refreshed a person feels upon waking.

---

## Core Components
1. **Sleep Onset Latency** – time needed to transition from wakefulness to sleep.
2. **Sleep Continuity** – minimal awakenings and short wake‑after‑sleep‑onset periods.
3. **Sleep Depth** – proportion of deep (slow‑wave) and REM sleep stages.
4. **Morning Restorative Feeling** – subjective rating of how rested one feels.

---

## Influencing Factors
### Behavioral
- Timing of **[[entities/caffeine]]** intake, late‑day alcohol (**[[entities/wine]]**), and stimulating activities affect both onset latency and continuity.  
- Consistent **[[concepts/evening_routine]]** with dim lighting and low‑stimulus tasks promotes smoother transitions to sleep.

### Environmental
- Room temperature, darkness, and noise levels are critical.  Adjusting the **[[entities/thermostat]]**, using breathable bedding, or adding a gentle **[[entities/fan]]** can improve continuity and reduce mid‑night awakenings.

### Medical
- Conditions such as **[[entities/sleep_apnea]]**, reflux, chronic pain, or anxiety can fragment sleep despite optimal behavior and environment.

## Insights from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]]
The session highlighted a practical approach to enhancing Sleep Quality:
- **Temperature experiment**: lowering the thermostat to the mid‑60 °F range and using lighter bedding to reduce warm‑induced awakenings.
- **Caffeine cutoff**: moving the last caffeine dose to early morning to minimize its residual impact on sleep continuity.
- **Alcohol adjustment**: reducing or shifting evening wine consumption to prevent second‑half‑night fragmentation.
- Tracking a simple nightly log (bedtime, wake‑ups, duration, restfulness) to quantify improvements.

---

## Practical Takeaways
1. Prioritize a cool, dark, quiet bedroom environment (**environmental_sleep_factors**).
2. Establish a predictable wind‑down period (**evening_routine**) and limit late‑day stimulants (**behavioral_sleep_factors**).
3. Monitor for medical signs (dry mouth, snoring) and consider evaluation for **[[entities/sleep_apnea]]** if awakenings persist.
