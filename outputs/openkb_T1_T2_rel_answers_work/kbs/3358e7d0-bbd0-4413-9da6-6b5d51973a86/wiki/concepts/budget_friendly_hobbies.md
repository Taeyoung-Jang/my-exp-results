# Budget‑Friendly Hobbies

**Definition**: Activities that require little to no upfront investment in equipment, materials, or subscriptions, making them accessible for anyone on a modest budget.

## Core Characteristics
- **Minimal equipment** – Use items most people already have (paper, pencils, water‑based paints, a phone).  
- **Low ongoing cost** – Supplies are inexpensive or reusable; many options are free (e.g., writing, sketching, journaling).  
- **Flexibility** – Can be practiced in short blocks of time, fitting easily into a weekly routine like the one outlined in [[summaries/session-11_2024-06-15]].

## Examples from Session 11
| Hobby | Required Items | Why it fits a budget‑friendly approach |
|-------|----------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Watercolor painting** | Small palette, brushes, paper | Basic set costs under $20 and can be reused many times. |
| **Sketching** | Pencil, eraser, paper | Often already owned; no consumables beyond paper. |
| **Journaling / creative writing** | Notebook or digital device | Paper or free online tools; no material cost. |
| **Phone photography** | Smartphone | Uses existing device; free editing apps available. |
| **Reading** | Library books or e‑books | Free borrowing from [[entities/local-library]] or online resources. |

## Integration with Weekly Planning
These hobbies pair well with the [[concepts/weekly_routine_planning]] framework from the session summary.  They can fill the 10:30‑12:00 or 1:00‑3:00 slots on non‑volunteer days, providing a structured yet relaxing activity without financial strain.

## Connection to Volunteering
When a volunteer day feels too physically demanding, a budget‑friendly hobby can serve as a gentle alternative.  For instance, swapping a shift at an [[entities/animal-shelter]] with a sketching session maintains routine consistency while reducing exertion.
