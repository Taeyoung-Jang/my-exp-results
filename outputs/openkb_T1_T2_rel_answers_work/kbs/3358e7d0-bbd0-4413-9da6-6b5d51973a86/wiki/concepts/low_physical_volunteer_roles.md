# Low‑Physical Volunteer Roles

**Definition**: Volunteer positions that require little physical strain, making them suitable for seniors, those with limited mobility, or anyone seeking a low‑impact way to give back.

**Typical low‑physical duties**:
- Front‑desk greeter or visitor guide – welcoming guests and providing information.
- Phone support – answering calls, scheduling appointments, or providing basic information.
- Administrative assistance – light paperwork, data entry, filing, or managing donation records.
- Outreach liaison – coordinating community events, handling email newsletters, or managing social‑media posts.
- Light animal‑care tasks – feeding small animals, grooming supplies, or helping with adoption paperwork (as opposed to heavy kennel cleaning or dog walking).

**Where to look**:
- Contact local shelters such as [[entities/animal-shelter]] and ask about front‑desk or administrative shifts.
- Reach out to a [[entities/food-pantry]] for volunteer roles that involve sorting donations or greeting visitors.
- Check online platforms like [[entities/volunteermatch]] or the bulletin board at your [[entities/community-center]].
- Inquire at the [[entities/local-library]] for desk‑assistant or program‑support positions.

**Integrating into a weekly routine**:
Low‑physical roles fit naturally into a structured but flexible schedule, as described in [[concepts/weekly_routine_planning]].  A 2‑hour shift (e.g., 10:00‑12:00 or 13:00‑15:00) can be paired with budget‑friendly hobbies discussed in [[concepts/budget_friendly_hobbies]].

**Tips for contacting a volunteer site**:
1. Ask about typical shift hours.
2. Inquire whether training is required and its length.
3. Clarify the physical demands of the role.
4. Determine how far in advance you need to schedule.
5. Request a trial shift before committing.
