## Layering for Cold Weather

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A three‑layer clothing system that manages moisture, insulation, and wind protection to keep the body warm in cold conditions.

### Core Idea
The principle behind *layering* is to combine garments that each serve a distinct function:
1. **Base layer** – moisture‑wicking fabric that moves sweat away from the skin, keeping you dry.
2. **Mid layer** – an insulating material (fleece, down, or synthetic) that traps warm air.
3. **Outer layer** – a wind‑ and water‑resistant shell that shields the inner layers from the elements.

When these layers are used together, they work synergistically: the base layer prevents chill from dampness, the mid layer retains body heat, and the outer layer blocks wind and precipitation.

### Practical Tips from [[summaries/session-39_2025-06-15]]
- Choose a thin, breathable base (e.g., merino wool or synthetic polyester) that doesn’t trap sweat.
- For the mid layer, opt for lightweight fleece or a thin down jacket; adjust thickness based on expected temperature.
- The outer layer should be wind‑proof and, if possible, water‑resistant (e.g., a hard‑shell jacket).
- Cover extremities—gloves, a warm hat, and a neck gaiter—because significant heat is lost there.
- Pre‑warm your core with light activity or an indoor heat source before heading outside; this makes the layering system more effective.

### Benefits
- **Flexibility:** Add or remove layers as temperature changes.
- **Comfort:** Moisture management prevents the chilling effect of wet clothing.
- **Protection:** Wind‑blocking outer layer reduces convective heat loss.
