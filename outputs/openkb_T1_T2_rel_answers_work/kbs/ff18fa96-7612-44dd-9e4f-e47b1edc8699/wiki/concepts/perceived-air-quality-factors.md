# Perceived Air Quality Factors

**Definition**: The combination of environmental cues—humidity, temperature, wind, pressure, sunlight, and cloud characteristics—that together determine how the air feels to a person, independent of what a thermometer or hygrometer may show.

## Core Components

| Factor | Typical Sensation | What It Tells You |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Humidity** | Heavy, sticky, or muggy when high; light and crisp when low. | Indicates moisture content of the air and its capacity to retain heat. |
| **Temperature** | Small shifts can make the same air feel sharper or heavier. | Direct thermal comfort; interacts with humidity to modify perceived heat. |
| **Wind** | Immediate cooling, sense of movement; direction hints at air‑mass source. | A quick cue for stability vs. change; see [[concepts/air-mass-origin-and-wind-sensation]] for how direction reflects origin. |
| **Air Pressure** | Subtle: higher pressure feels clear and steady; lower pressure feels "loose" or heavy. | Often linked to upcoming weather systems; felt through bodily sensations (headaches, joint stiffness). |
| **Sunlight / Cloud Cover** | Direct sun warms skin, making air feel hotter; clouds mute this effect. | Signals high‑pressure clear skies vs. moisture‑laden low‑pressure conditions. |
| **Cloud Form** | Tall, fast‑growing clouds → instability; thin, high clouds → distant system. | Visual cue for atmospheric stability.

## How These Factors Interact
The perception of air quality is a **holistic** experience. For instance, a day with moderate temperature but high wind can feel cooler than a still day with the same temperature. Conversely, strong sunlight can amplify a modest temperature rise, making the air feel warmer even if humidity stays constant.

## Practical Use
By training oneself to notice these cues, a person can develop a rapid, instrument‑free sense of the current weather pattern. The **30‑second checklist** described in [[concepts/quick-atmosphere-checklist]] is a practical application of these factors.
