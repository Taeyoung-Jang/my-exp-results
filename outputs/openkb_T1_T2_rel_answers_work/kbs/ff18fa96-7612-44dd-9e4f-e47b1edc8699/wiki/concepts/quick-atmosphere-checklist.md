# Quick Atmosphere Checklist

**Concept type:** Concept

## Definition
A practical, 30‑second outdoor routine for evaluating the current feel of the atmosphere using only the senses. It condenses the most salient cues—wind, sunlight, clouds, air weight, temperature, and visibility—into a quick checklist that helps anticipate short‑term weather changes without instruments.

## Checklist Items (in order)
1. **Feel the wind** – Note speed and any temperature of the breeze. A steady, dry wind often signals stable weather; gusty or warm wind can hint at an approaching system.
2. **Observe wind direction** – Air coming from the sea or large water bodies feels softer and more humid; inland or high‑terrain winds feel drier and clearer.
3. **Assess sunlight** – Direct, sharp sun suggests high pressure and settled conditions; diffused or weak sun indicates moisture or a shift.
4. **Examine clouds** – Tall, fast‑growing clouds imply instability; thin, high, streaky clouds point to a distant system.
5. **Sense the "weight" of the air** – Still, crisp air feels light (high pressure); dull or heavy air feels denser (falling pressure).
6. **Check skin temperature** – A sudden warm or cool sensation indicates a new air mass has arrived.
7. **Gauge visibility** – Sharp clarity aligns with stable, high‑pressure weather; haziness can mean low pressure, moisture, or pollutants.
