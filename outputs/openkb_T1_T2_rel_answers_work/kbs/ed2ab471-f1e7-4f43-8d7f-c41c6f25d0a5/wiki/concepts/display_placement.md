# Display Placement and Spatial Breathing Room

**Definition**

Display placement refers to the strategic positioning of a focal object—such as a locked case for vintage ceramics—within a room, while spatial breathing room denotes the intentional empty space surrounding that object to prevent visual clutter and promote a welcoming atmosphere.

**Key Guidelines from [[summaries/session-01_2024-06-15]]**

- **Maintain clearance**: Keep a modest gap between the display case and surrounding furniture. This ensures the case reads as a distinct focal point rather than being lost among other items.
- **Eye‑level positioning**: Place the case at a height where it can be comfortably viewed without the need to lean or stretch, supporting a relaxed viewing experience.
- **Avoid crowding**: Do not position large pieces of furniture directly adjacent to the case; allow at least a few feet of open floor space when possible.
- **Complementary lighting**: Pair breathing room with soft, directed light (see [[concepts/lighting_design]]) to draw the eye toward the case without harsh glare.
- **Integrated signage**: Position a subtle plaque in the lower corner of the case (see [[concepts/signage_etiquette]]) so it is visible but does not compete with the displayed items.

**Why It Matters**

Creating breathing room around a display enhances perceived spaciousness, reduces visual noise, and signals that the object is important enough to merit undivided attention. This spatial strategy also subtly communicates that the items are to be admired, not handled, reinforcing the etiquette conveyed by signage.
