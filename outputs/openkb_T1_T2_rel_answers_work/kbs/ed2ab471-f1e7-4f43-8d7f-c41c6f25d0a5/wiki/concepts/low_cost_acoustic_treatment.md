# Low‑Cost Acoustic Treatment

Low‑cost acoustic treatment refers to simple, inexpensive strategies that improve a room’s sound quality by adding soft, absorptive surfaces using items you likely already own. These techniques are especially useful in new or empty spaces where echo makes the environment feel larger and less comfortable.

## Core Principles
- **Floor absorption**: Place a rug, blanket, or several folded blankets on hard flooring to break up reflections. This provides the greatest echo reduction for the least effort.
- **Wall and window softening**: Hang a curtain, sheet, or blanket on windows or large wall sections. Loose draping increases sound absorption.
- **Additional vertical absorbers**: Use cushions, laundry baskets filled with clothes, or an open bookshelf with items to further dampen sound.
- **Strategic placement**: Cover one larger area rather than scattering many small patches; focus on the floor and the wall opposite the primary reflective surface (often the window).

## Practical Steps (from [[summaries/session-35_2025-02-13]])
1. **Floor** – Lay a single large rug or blanket across the main traffic area.
2. **Window** – Hang a sheet or curtain that isn’t pulled tight; a draped fabric works best.
3. **Opposite wall** – Add a blanket or cloth on the wall opposite the window to interrupt sound bounce.
4. **Optional extras** – Place cushions, a filled laundry basket, or a bookshelf with books and decorative items to round out the treatment.

- **Cost‑effective**: Uses items you already have; no need for specialized acoustic panels.
- **Rapid results**: Noticeable reduction in echo after the first implementation step.
- **Dual purpose**: Enhances both acoustic comfort and aesthetic warmth.
