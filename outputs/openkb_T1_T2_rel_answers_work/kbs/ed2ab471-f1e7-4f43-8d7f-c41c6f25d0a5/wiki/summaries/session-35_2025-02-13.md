**Summary of session-35_2025-02-13**

**Main focus:** Helping a user settle into a new living space quickly, create a warm atmosphere, and reduce echo using items they already own.

### Key ideas & practical tips
- **Unpack personal items first** – books, blankets, plants, photos to add identity and comfort.  
- **Warm lighting** – use lamps with warm‑tone bulbs; dim overhead lights for a cozier feel. [[concepts/lighting_design]]
- **Soft fabrics** – throw blankets, scarves, extra pillows, or any fabric you have to introduce texture.  
- **Create memory‑making moments** – cook, play music, host friends to turn the space into “yours”.

### Echo‑reduction strategy (low‑cost, existing items)
1. **Floor softening** – place a rug, blanket, or several folded blankets on the floor; this gives the biggest echo reduction per effort.  
2. **Wall/window softening** – hang curtains, sheets, or a blanket over windows or large wall sections to absorb sound.  
3. **Additional soft surfaces** – use cushions, laundry baskets filled with clothes, open bookshelves with items, or move furniture slightly off walls.  
4. **Opposite‑wall treatment** – adding a blanket or fabric on the wall opposite the window further breaks up sound reflections.

### Priority order for a mostly empty living room
1. Cover the floor with a large soft area (rug/blanket).  
2. Hang a soft covering on the window or wall (curtain, sheet, blanket).  
3. Add a soft vertical element (bookshelf with items, cushioned chair, wall blanket) if needed.

### Implementation notes
- **Cover a single larger area** rather than scattering small patches for maximum acoustic impact.  
- **Loose drape** on windows/curtains works best; a tight pull reduces absorption.  
- Even a **partial wall covering** opposite the window can significantly cut echo.
