# Meeting Clustering

**Definition**: Meeting clustering is the practice of scheduling multiple meetings back‑to‑back within a dedicated time window, creating a distinct *meeting zone* that isolates interruptions from deep work periods.

## Why It Matters
- **Preserves focus**: By concentrating meetings in one part of the day, you keep the rest of the schedule free for uninterrupted tasks.
- **Reduces cognitive load**: Fewer switches between meeting mode and solo work mode lower mental fatigue.
- **Improves predictability**: Colleagues know when you are available for meetings, and you know when you can safely enter deep work.

## Core Elements
1. **Designated meeting block** – a contiguous period (e.g., early afternoon) where all scheduled meetings are placed.
2. **Buffer before/after** – short gaps to transition into and out of the meeting zone, preventing spill‑over into focus time.
3. **Limited interruptions** – during the meeting block, other communication (email, messages) is handled in brief, scheduled check‑ins.

## Practical Tips
- **Plan ahead**: Use a calendar to block out the meeting zone and invite participants accordingly.
- **Set boundaries**: Decline ad‑hoc meeting requests that fall outside the designated block.
- **Include transition time**: Allocate 5‑10 minutes before and after the block for mental reset.
