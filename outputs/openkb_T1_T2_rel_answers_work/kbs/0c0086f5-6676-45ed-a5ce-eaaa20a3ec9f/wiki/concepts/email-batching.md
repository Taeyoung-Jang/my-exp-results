# Email Batching

**Definition**: Email batching is the practice of consolidating email and instant‑message handling into a few short, pre‑planned windows rather than checking continuously throughout the day.

## Why It Matters
- Reduces the number of context switches, preserving mental energy for deep work.
- Creates predictable times for communication, making you more reliably reachable.
- Allows urgent messages to be handled separately (e.g., a quick hourly glance for true emergencies).

## How It Is Applied in the Session‑14 Guidance
- Two dedicated check‑ins are recommended:
  1. **Mid‑day** – right after the 8‑11 am deep‑focus zone (≈10‑15 min) to clear overnight messages.
  2. **Late afternoon** – around 4:30 pm (≈10‑15 min) to wrap up any pending correspondence before the day ends.
- Each window is used to **batch** reading, replying, and triaging messages, leaving the rest of the day free for focused solo work or meetings.
- During the protected focus block (8‑11 am), only a **quick emergency glance** (≤30 seconds) is allowed for truly urgent pings.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader [[concepts/focus-zones]] strategy that divides the day into distinct functional zones.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/meeting-clustering]] by grouping all communication‑heavy activities (emails, messages, meetings) into compact periods.
