# Availability Messaging

**Availability Messaging** is the practice of crafting short, clear communications that inform colleagues about when you are most reachable, how to contact you, and any meeting‑booking preferences. The goal is to set expectations while maintaining a friendly tone, helping teams coordinate without unnecessary interruptions.

## Core Elements

1. **Availability Window** – Specify the hours you are in deep‑work mode (e.g., *2 – 5 pm*), noting when you are most responsive (often mornings or lunch).
2. **Contact Preferences** – Indicate the preferred channel for non‑urgent queries (e.g., Slack) and the method for truly urgent matters (text or call).  
   - Example from [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-07]]: *“Slack me and I’ll reply after 5; text/call only if it’s truly urgent.”*  
   - This aligns with [[concepts/communication-preferences]] and highlights the use of [[entities/slack]].
3. **Meeting Guidance** – Gently request that meetings be avoided during focused periods, phrasing the preference as a friendly request rather than a rule.
4. **Tone** – Balance professionalism with approachability; provide both a formal and a casual version if needed.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/communication-preferences]]** – Availability Messaging is a concrete application of broader communication‑preference strategies, defining when and how to be contacted.
- **[[concepts/focus-zones]]** – By announcing focus zones (e.g., afternoon deep‑work), you protect high‑productivity periods.
- **[[concepts/meeting-clustering]]** – Encouraging teams to cluster meetings outside the focus zone supports efficient scheduling and reduces interruptions.

## Practical Example (Derived from Session 39)

> *Hey all, just a heads‑up that I’m easiest to reach in the morning or around lunch. I’m usually in deep‑work mode from 2–5, so I’d appreciate keeping meetings out of that window when possible. If you ping me on Slack I’ll get back to you after 5, and text or call only for anything truly urgent.*

This template demonstrates a concise, three‑line availability message that:
- Highlights the most responsive times.
- Sets a clear focus zone.
- Provides contact options based on urgency.
- Softly communicates meeting preferences.

## Benefits

- **Reduced interruptions** during high‑focus periods.
- **Clear expectations** for teammates, improving response times.
- **Improved work‑life balance** by aligning communication with personal productivity rhythms.
