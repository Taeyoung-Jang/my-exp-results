# Focus Zones

**Definition**: A time‑management approach that partitions a day into distinct zones—deep‑focus, communication, meeting‑clustering, and buffer periods—to minimise context switching and protect productivity.

**Origin**: Described in [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-09]] which recommends protecting an 8‑11 am deep‑focus zone, scheduling email‑batching windows, clustering meetings, and ending with a buffer. The concept was further illustrated in [[summaries/session-39_2024-12-07]], where a user defined an afternoon deep‑focus window (2‑5 pm) and set clear communication preferences.

**Key Components**
- **Deep‑focus zone** – uninterrupted work on high‑cognitive tasks (e.g., 8‑11 am or 2‑5 pm). 
- **Communication windows** – short, scheduled periods for email, messages, or Slack, aligned with [[concepts/communication-preferences]] and sometimes linked to [[concepts/email-batching]].
- **Meeting clustering** – grouping meetings together in a contiguous block, related to [[concepts/meeting-clustering]].
- **Buffer time** – a brief period at day’s end for wrap‑up, planning, and mental cooldown.

**Benefits**
- Reduces cognitive load by limiting frequent task‑switching.
- Improves response predictability for collaborators.
- Provides clear structure for both “maker” and “meeting‑heavy” days.
- Supports clear availability messaging (see [[concepts/availability-messaging]]) and respectful use of [[entities/slack]] for non‑urgent communication.

**Application**
1. Define a fixed deep‑focus window (morning or afternoon) based on personal productivity peaks.
2. Insert communication windows (e.g., brief Slack checks, email‑batching periods) respecting preferred contact methods.
3. Cluster required meetings into a single block, avoiding the deep‑focus zone.
4. Reserve end‑of‑day buffer time for wrap‑up and planning.
5. Communicate the schedule to teammates, noting that meetings should be avoided during the deep‑focus zone unless absolutely necessary.
