# Reflection Tracking

**Definition**: A lightweight, structured approach for logging personal reactions and observations after each low‑commitment experiment or informational interview, enabling evidence‑based career‑decision making.

## Purpose
- Turns subjective "gut feelings" into observable data.
- Helps identify consistent patterns of enjoyment, curiosity, and fit across multiple fields.
- Supports the broader [[concepts/career-decision-making]] process by providing concrete feedback.

## Core Components (as described in [[summaries/session-12_2020-04-14]])
1. **Qualitative notes** – brief free‑text answers to prompts such as:
   - What parts felt good?
   - What parts dragged?
   - Would I do this again tomorrow?
   - How curious am I to explore further?
2. **Numeric ratings** – two simple 1‑to‑5 scales:
   - *Enjoyment* (how pleasant the activity was)
   - *Continued interest* (likelihood of pursuing more of it)
3. **Aggregation** – over the 2‑week experiment window, average the scores per field to reveal the most promising option.

## Practical Implementation for Busy Undergrads
- **Timing**: Record notes immediately after each interview or mini‑project (5‑10 minutes).
- **Tooling**: A spreadsheet, note‑taking app, or a dedicated journal with columns for the prompts and rating scales.
- **Frequency**: Aim for one entry per activity; with ~3‑4 activities per field, the dataset remains manageable.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Acts as the **feedback loop** for [[concepts/low-commitment-experiments]], turning short experiments into measurable insights.
- Complements the **decision‑making framework** by providing the data needed to compare fields objectively.

## Example Scorecard Template
| Activity | Enjoyment (1‑5) | Continued Interest (1‑5) | Notes |
|----------|----------------|--------------------------|-------|
| CS interview | 4 | 3 | Felt energized by problem‑solving, but unsure about teamwork. |
| Business mini‑project | 3 | 2 | Interesting data work, but routine felt dull. |
| Psychology survey | 5 | 4 | Loved reading responses, enjoyed interpreting patterns. |

By consistently applying this **Reflection Tracking** method, students can move beyond fleeting impressions and make a more informed choice about which academic path aligns with their long‑term motivations and strengths.
