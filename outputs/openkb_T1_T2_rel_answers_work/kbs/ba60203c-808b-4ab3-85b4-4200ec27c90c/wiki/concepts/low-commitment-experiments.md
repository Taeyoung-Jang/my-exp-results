# Low‑Commitment Experiments

**Definition**: A series of short, inexpensive activities—such as informational interviews, introductory tutorials, and mini‑projects—that let a person experience the day‑to‑day aspects of a potential career or field of study without a large time or resource investment.

---

## Why they matter
Low‑commitment experiments help bridge the gap between abstract interest and concrete experience. By trying out a field briefly, students can evaluate fit based on real‑world tasks and personal reactions, reducing the risk of choosing a major or career path that feels attractive on paper but unsatisfying in practice.

---

## Core Components (as described in [[summaries/session-12_2020-04-14]])

1. **Informational interviews** – 15‑minute chats with a current student and a professional in the target area. Useful venues include the **[[entities/career-center]]** and **[[entities/linkedin]]**.
2. **Introductory hands‑on tasks** – short tutorials or exercises (e.g., a coding mini‑lesson, a business case analysis, a psychology survey) lasting 30–60 minutes.
3. **Mini‑project deep‑dives** – slightly longer (45–90 minutes) projects in the two most promising fields to gauge sustained engagement.

---

## How to structure a low‑commitment experiment

| Phase | Activity | Typical Duration | Goal |
|-------|----------|------------------|------|
| **Week 1** | Informational interviews | 15 min each | Capture realistic day‑to‑day description and uncover hidden skills. |
| **Week 2** | Intro tasks | 30–60 min each | Sense curiosity and initial enjoyment. |
| **Weeks 3‑4** | Mini‑project | 45–90 min each | Observe sustained motivation and skill fit. |

---

## Tracking & Reflection

To turn subjective impressions into actionable data, pair each experiment with a brief reflection log (see [[concepts/reflection-tracking]]). A simple scorecard asks for:
- A 1‑5 enjoyment rating.
- A 1‑5 "would continue" rating.
- Short free‑text notes on what felt energizing vs. draining.

Aggregating these scores across weeks highlights the field that consistently scores highest, guiding the final decision.

---

## Practical tips for busy undergraduates
- Keep total weekly time under 2 hours.
- Leverage existing resources (career‑center staff, professors, LinkedIn contacts) to find interview subjects quickly.
- Choose mini‑projects that require only a single deliverable (a script, a one‑page memo, or a short survey).
