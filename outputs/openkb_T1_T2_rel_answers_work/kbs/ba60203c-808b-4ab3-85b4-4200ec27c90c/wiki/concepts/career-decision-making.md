# Career Decision Making

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A systematic approach to choosing a field of study or career by weighing personal interest, job‑market realities, earning potential, and experiential fit.

## Core Elements
1. **Personal Interest** – The primary driver; sustained motivation is crucial.
2. **Job Market Outlook** – Assess growth, stability, and demand for the field.
3. **Earning Potential** – Align expected income with lifestyle goals.
4. **Fit Testing** – Use short, low‑risk activities to gauge day‑to‑day satisfaction.

## Process Highlights (from [[summaries/session-12_2020-04-14]])
- **Informational Interviews** – 15‑minute chats with a student and a professional per field, facilitated via the university [[entities/career-center]] or [[entities/linkedin]].
- **Low‑Commitment Experiments** – Hands‑on mini‑projects such as a coding tutorial, a business case memo, or a brief psychology survey, described in detail under [[concepts/low-commitment-experiments]].
- **Reflection Tracking** – After each interview or experiment, log qualitative notes and numeric ratings (enjoyment, willingness to continue) using a simple scorecard, as outlined in [[concepts/reflection-tracking]].

## Practical Steps for Undergraduates
1. **Define Priorities** – Rank interest, market, and earnings according to personal values.
2. **Gather Data** – Conduct the informational interviews and note market signals.
3. **Run Experiments** – Complete 30‑60 minute tasks in each candidate field.
4. **Record Reactions** – Use the reflection‑tracking scorecard to capture enjoyment (1‑5) and curiosity.
5. **Compare Scores** – Aggregate ratings across weeks; the field with consistently higher scores signals the best fit.
