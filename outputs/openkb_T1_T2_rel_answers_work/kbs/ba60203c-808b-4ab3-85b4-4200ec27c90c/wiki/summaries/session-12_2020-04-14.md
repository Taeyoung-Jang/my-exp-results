# Summary of session-12_2020-04-14

**Main contribution** – A practical framework for undergraduates to evaluate potential majors/careers (computer science, business, psychology) through short‑term experiments, structured informational interviews, and a simple reflection scorecard.

## Key Concepts
- [[concepts/career-decision-making]]: weighing interest, job market, earning potential, and personal fit.
- [[concepts/low-commitment-experiments]]: brief, low‑risk activities (interviews, mini‑projects, tutorials) to sample a field.
- [[concepts/reflection-tracking]]: post‑activity log with qualitative notes and numeric ratings.

## 4‑Week Action Plan
| Week | Activity | Duration | Goal |
|------|----------|----------|------|
| 1 | Set up 1‑2 informational interviews per field (student + professional) | 15 min each | Capture realistic day‑to‑day description |
| 2 | Complete an introductory task per field (coding tutorial, business case, psychology mini‑lecture/survey) | 30‑60 min each | Sense curiosity and engagement |
| 3‑4 | Deep‑dive mini‑project in the two most promising fields | 45‑90 min each | Observe sustained motivation and skill fit |

## Core Question Sets
### General (usable for any field)
1. What does a typical day look like for you?
2. Which parts of the work feel energizing versus routine?
3. What skills do you use most that you didn’t expect?
4. How much time is spent on independent work vs collaboration?
5. What would you tell your past self before choosing this path?
6. How did you realize this field was (or wasn’t) the right fit?

### Field‑Specific
- **Computer Science**: How much time is spent coding vs design/bug‑fixing vs team discussions?
- **Business**: How data‑driven vs relationship‑driven is the work, and what deadline pressure looks like?
- **Psychology**: Is the role more research‑heavy, client‑focused, or paperwork‑intensive? How emotionally taxing is it?

## Tracking & Scorecard
After each interview or mini‑project, log:
1. *Enjoyment*: 1‑5 rating
2. *Will‑continue interest*: 1‑5 rating
3. Short free‑text notes answering:
   - What parts felt good?
   - What parts dragged?
   - Would I do this again tomorrow?
   - How curious am I to explore further?

Over the two‑week period, aggregate the ratings; the field with consistently higher scores signals the best fit.

## Practical Tips for Busy Undergrads
- Limit total time to ~2 hours per week.
- Use existing school resources (career-center, professors) to find interview contacts; you can also reach out via LinkedIn.
- Keep mini‑projects low‑stakes: a single script, a one‑page memo, or a short survey.
- Store the log in a simple spreadsheet or note‑taking app for quick review.
