# Summary of Session 35 (2023-04-14)

## Overview
This conversation provides a compact set of **systems‑based** strategies for newcomers to hospital rotations who face long, unpredictable hours. The assistant breaks advice into three main areas: daily routines & meal prep, an overnight‑call “starter kit” organized by location (coat pocket, locker, car), and rapid‑search tactics for **cheap but safe** housing near a hospital.

## Routine & Meal‑Prep Strategies
- **Batch‑cook & default meals** – prepare two‑three simple meals each week (e.g., grain bowls, protein wraps) that can be eaten cold, eliminating the need for a microwave. 
- **Snack stash** – nuts, trail mix, fruit, jerky, granola bars; keep them in the bag for quick energy.
- **Sleep hygiene** – a consistent 20‑minute wind‑down routine, dim lighting, phone‑free buffer, blackout curtains, and white‑noise apps to aid sleep regardless of shift timing.
- **Financial hacks** – pack food, use a refillable water bottle, and schedule “anchor days” for laundry, meal prep, or rest.

*Key concepts:* batch cooking, sleep hygiene, financial habits for shift workers

## Overnight‑Call Starter Kit (Bag/Locker/Car)
- **Coat pockets (instant needs):** pen, small notebook, phone charger cable, lip balm, 1‑2 protein bars, pain‑med packet.
- **Locker (comfort & hygiene):** deodorant, dry shampoo, toothbrush & toothpaste, hand lotion, clean socks, hoodie, earplugs, sleep mask.
- **Car (backup supplies):** spare scrubs, blanket, extra water bottle, ready‑to‑eat food.
- **General items:** sturdy water bottle, electrolyte packets, basic meds (ibuprofen, acetaminophen, antacids), band‑aids, sanitizer, charger, spare socks.

*Key concepts:* overnight call kit, gear organization by location

## Finding Affordable Housing Fast
1. **Short‑term flexibility first** – month‑to‑month sublets, room‑shares, or student/medical‑community Facebook groups.
2. **Leverage internal networks** – ask program administrators or senior residents for unofficial housing lists.
3. **Expand radius modestly** – a short bus or bike ride can dramatically lower rent while keeping call‑day commutes feasible.
4. **Prioritize safety & livability** – look for older but well‑maintained buildings, active street life, nearby transit, and late‑hour shops.

### Neighborhood Traits
- Proximity to public transit
- Older, well‑kept structures
- Presence of other shift workers/students
- Visible activity in evenings (lights, dog walkers, cafés)

### Red‑Flag Checklist for Room‑Shares/Sublets
- Avoids showing full space or is vague about who lives there
- Unclear rent/deposit terms
- Broken locks, windows that don’t close, flimsy doors
- Neglected common areas (dirty kitchen, bathroom)
- Host or roommates seem evasive or the vibe feels off

*Key concepts:* affordable housing near hospital, neighborhood safety red flags, room share screening, shift worker housing strategies

*All recommendations emphasize small, repeatable systems over sweeping lifestyle overhauls, allowing new clinicians to stay fed, rested, and financially stable while navigating the chaotic demands of hospital rotations.*
