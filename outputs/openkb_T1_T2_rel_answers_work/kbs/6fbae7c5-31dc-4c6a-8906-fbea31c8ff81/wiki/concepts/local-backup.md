## Local Backup

Local backup refers to the practice of copying a phone's data—photos, notes, messages, and other files—to a personal computer, external hard drive, or other on‑premises storage media.

### Why Use Local Backup?
- **Full control**: You own the storage, so there are no subscription fees and you decide how long data is kept.
- **Fast restores**: Restoring from a local drive is typically quicker than downloading large amounts of data from the cloud.
- **Privacy**: Data never leaves your trusted hardware, reducing exposure to third‑party services.

### Drawbacks
- **Manual effort**: You must remember to connect the phone and run the backup routine.
- **Single point of failure**: If the computer or external drive fails, the backup is lost unless another copy exists elsewhere.
- **No automatic syncing**: Unlike [[concepts/cloud-backup]], changes are not pushed in real time.

### Role in a Hybrid [[concepts/backup-strategy]]
The source document "session-07_2024-06-15" recommends using cloud backup as the primary safety net and supplementing it with local backups on a computer. This hybrid approach combines the convenience of [[concepts/cloud-backup]] with the security and speed of local copies.

### Practical Tips from the Source
- Schedule a monthly or quarterly backup to your computer.
- Verify that the backup includes **photos**, **notes**, and **messages** before the process finishes.
- Keep the computer’s own backup routine (e.g., external drive or another cloud service) to avoid losing the local copy.

### Related Entities
- For iPhone users, local backups are often performed via tools provided by **[[entities/apple]]** (e.g., Finder or iTunes).
- Android users may use **[[entities/google]]**‑endorsed utilities such as **[[entities/google-photos]]**, **[[entities/google-keep]]**, or **[[entities/google-one]]** to export data to a PC.
