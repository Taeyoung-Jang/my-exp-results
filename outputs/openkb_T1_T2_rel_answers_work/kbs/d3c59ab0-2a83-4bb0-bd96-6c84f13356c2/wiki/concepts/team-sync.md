# Team Sync

**Definition**
A *team sync* is a short (typically 10–15 minute) meeting that aligns a group on preferred communication styles, coordination habits, workflow priorities, and how to handle periods of controlled ambiguity. It helps maintain focus on core work while teams navigate changes such as reorganizations or onboarding transitions.

**Key Characteristics**
- **Length:** 10–15 minutes, keeping it focused and low‑friction.
- **Frequency:** Can be a one‑off check‑in or a standing session (e.g., every other week) to sustain alignment.
- **Format:** Casual or neutral tone, matching team culture; can be held in a group chat, video call, or in‑person.
- **Agenda Items:** 
  - Quick poll of preferred communication channels ([[concepts/communication-style]]) such as chat, email, or brief calls.
  - Review of current onboarding or training blocks ([[concepts/onboarding-as-work]]) and how they affect workload.
  - Discussion of time‑split expectations ([[concepts/time-split-framing]]) to balance ramp‑up activities with deliverables.
  - Confirmation of core work priorities ([[concepts/core-work-anchor]]) and any adjustments needed during the reorg.
  - Option for individuals to respond asynchronously if they cannot attend.

**Use Cases**
- New or transitioning team members needing to “get in the loop” after a role change.
- Teams experiencing friction due to mismatched expectations about communication or workload during a reorganization.
- Situations where a manager wants to formalise a recurring touch‑point to discuss onboarding schedules, time‑split planning, and maintaining focus on core deliverables without heavy overhead.

**Implementation Guidance**
1. **Propose a concise invitation** that states purpose, duration, and optional asynchronous response. See example drafts in [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]] and [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]].
2. **Offer a recurring line** (e.g., “If it’s helpful, we can make this a standing 15‑minute check‑in every other week…”) to establish a predictable rhythm.
3. **Capture feedback** on communication preferences, onboarding load, and time‑split allocation; revisit them periodically to keep the sync relevant.
4. **Use the sync to surface controlled ambiguity** by encouraging participants to voice unclear priorities and agree on next‑step questions ([[concepts/controlled-ambiguity]]).

**Benefits**
- Clarifies how the team shares information, strengthening collaboration.
- Reduces misunderstandings caused by differing [[concepts/communication-style]] expectations.
- Provides a low‑effort forum for continuous improvement of processes, especially when onboarding and reorgs shift responsibilities.
- Helps the team stay anchored to core work while transparently managing ramp‑up time and workload distribution.
