# Controlled Ambiguity

**Definition**

Controlled Ambiguity is the practice of treating periods of organizational change—such as a company reorg—as a managed state of limited clarity. Rather than seeking perfect information immediately, you maintain a steady, purposeful approach, anchoring your work to what is already known and valuable.

**Why It Matters**

During a reorg, reporting lines, roles, and priorities can shift rapidly. Trying to resolve every unknown can lead to analysis paralysis. By embracing Controlled Ambiguity, you:

- Preserve productivity while the new structure settles.
- Reduce stress by focusing on concrete, high‑impact tasks.
- Communicate more clearly, because you know exactly what you can commit to.

**Key Practices (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]])**

1. **Anchor to Core Work** – Keep delivering the tasks that directly support your team or customers. This aligns with the [[concepts/core-work-anchor]] principle.
2. **Simple, Frequent Communication** – Use short, direct questions (e.g., “Is this still the most important thing for me to focus on this week?”) to confirm priorities, reflecting [[concepts/communication-style]].
3. **Steady Mindset** – Accept that full clarity will arrive later; treat the ambiguity as a temporary, controllable condition.
4. **Transparent Bandwidth Framing** – Phrase capacity limits as planning statements rather than complaints, linking to [[concepts/time-split-framing]] when discussing onboarding vs. deliverables.

**Relation to Other Concepts**

- **[[concepts/communication-style]]**: Clear, concise updates help navigate uncertainty.
- **[[concepts/core-work-anchor]]**: Focusing on core deliverables provides a stable foundation.
- **[[concepts/onboarding-as-work]]**: Viewing onboarding as real work fits within a controlled‑ambiguity mindset, allowing you to allocate time without feeling overwhelmed.
- **[[concepts/time-split-framing]]**: Communicating a time split (e.g., 60 % onboarding, 40 % deliverables) is a practical expression of managing ambiguity.
