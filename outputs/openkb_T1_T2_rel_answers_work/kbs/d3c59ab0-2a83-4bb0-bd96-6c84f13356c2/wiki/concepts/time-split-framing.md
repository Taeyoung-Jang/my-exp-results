# Time‑Split Framing

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A communication strategy that explicitly allocates a percentage of work time to onboarding/training versus productive deliverables, helping set clear expectations during a role transition.

---

## Core Idea

During a company reorganization or role change, people often need to balance mandatory onboarding activities with ongoing work responsibilities. **Time‑Split Framing** proposes stating the expected split—e.g., "60 % onboarding, 40 % deliverables"—as part of the conversation with managers. This framing:

1. **Signals commitment** to learning the new role while still contributing value.
2. **Provides a concrete reference** for managers to assess capacity and priority.
3. **Reduces ambiguity** about workload, preventing assumptions that the employee is either over‑committed or under‑performing.

## Practical Usage from Session 37

In the conversation [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]], the assistant recommended a default 60/40 split and offered two alternative phrasings:

- **Conservative split:** "around seventy percent of my time on onboarding and training while I get grounded."
- **Aggressive split:** "roughly half my time available for early deliverables alongside the onboarding sessions."

These examples show how to adapt the framing based on the manager’s response or the perceived urgency of deliverables.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify your onboarding schedule** – List the training sessions and their estimated hours.
2. **Calculate a realistic split** – For a typical two‑week ramp‑up, a 60/40 split is often a safe baseline.
3. **Communicate clearly** – Include the split in your bandwidth message to both old and new managers (see [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]] for template messages).
4. **Invite feedback** – Ask the manager to confirm or adjust the split based on team priorities.

## Benefits

| Benefit | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| **Clarity** | Managers instantly see how much capacity is devoted to learning vs. output. |
| **Expectation management** | Reduces risk of misaligned expectations or perceived slacking. |
| **Flexibility** | Allows easy adjustment (e.g., shifting to 70/30) as onboarding progresses. |
| **Professionalism** | Demonstrates proactive planning rather than passive coping. |

---

## Caveats

- Avoid overly precise percentages if your schedule is fluid; use rounded figures (50 %, 60 %, 70 %).
- Pair the split with **specific deliverable proposals** to show you’re still ready to contribute.
- Re‑evaluate after the first week to see if the split needs tweaking based on actual workload.
