# Onboarding as Work

**Definition**: Viewing mandatory onboarding sessions, training modules, and role‑specific ramp‑up activities as integral, billable work that occupies a defined portion of an employee's capacity.

## Why It Matters
During a company reorg, employees often face a sudden influx of onboarding requirements. Treating these activities as "extra" can lead to unrealistic expectations, hidden overtime, and fragmented focus. Recognizing onboarding as work:
- legitimizes the time spent learning the new role,
- allows clear communication of bandwidth to managers, and
- supports a smoother transition without sacrificing core deliverables.

## Key Elements
1. **Scheduled Time Blocks** – Allocate explicit calendar slots for training, just as you would for project work.
2. **Capacity Framing** – Discuss the split between onboarding and deliverables (e.g., a 60/40 split) to set expectations.
3. **Visibility** – Include onboarding activities on status reports and team syncs so stakeholders see the effort involved.
4. **Outcome‑Oriented** – Treat completed training modules as milestones comparable to finished tasks.
