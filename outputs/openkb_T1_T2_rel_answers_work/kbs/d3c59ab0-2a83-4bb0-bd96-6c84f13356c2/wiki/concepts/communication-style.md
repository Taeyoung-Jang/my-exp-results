# Communication Style

**Definition**: The preferred methods and norms a person or group uses to share information, provide updates, and coordinate tasks.

## Overview
Communication style encompasses the channels (chat, email, calls), tone (formal vs. casual), frequency, and level of detail people expect when interacting at work. Understanding these preferences helps reduce friction, improves clarity, and strengthens workplace relationships. During periods of **controlled ambiguity** (see [[concepts/controlled-ambiguity]]), keeping communication *simple and frequent* becomes especially important to maintain alignment.

## Key Elements
- **Channel Preference**: Some team members favor instant messaging for quick questions, while others rely on email for documentation and traceability.
- **Tone and Formality**: The same message can be written in a casual, upbeat voice or a straightforward, neutral voice, affecting how it is received.
- **Timing and Frequency**: Determining whether regular short syncs (e.g., a 10‑15‑minute [[concepts/team-sync]]) or ad‑hoc check‑ins are more effective.
- **Feedback Loop**: Asking direct questions like "What communication method works best for you?" surfaces preferences and signals respect for colleagues' workflows.
- **Simplicity & Directness**: Use concise, question‑oriented language (e.g., "Is this still the most important thing for me to focus on this week?") to cut through uncertainty and keep conversations clear.

## Practical Tips
1. **Ask Directly**: Include a short survey or ask during a brief sync what channels and response times colleagues prefer.
2. **Adapt Your Tone**: Choose upbeat/casual or straightforward/neutral language based on the audience and context.
3. **Document Preferences**: Keep a shared note or quick reference so new members can onboard with the established style.
4. **Iterate**: Revisit preferences periodically, especially after introducing a new recurring sync.
5. **Keep It Simple**: Use concise, question‑focused language when clarity is needed, particularly during ambiguous periods.
6. **Frame Bandwidth with Time‑Split**: When discussing workload during transitions, reference a clear split (e.g., 60 % onboarding, 40 % deliverables) to set realistic expectations [[concepts/time-split-framing]].
