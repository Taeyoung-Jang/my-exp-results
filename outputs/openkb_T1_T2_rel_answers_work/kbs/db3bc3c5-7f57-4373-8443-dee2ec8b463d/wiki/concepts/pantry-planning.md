# Pantry Planning

**Definition**: Pantry planning is the practice of curating a small, versatile set of shelf‑stable and ready‑to‑use ingredients that enable quick, balanced meals with minimal preparation.

## Core Principles
1. **Versatility** – Choose items that pair well with a variety of protein‑based deli mains (e.g., meatloaf, roasted chicken, salmon, pasta bakes).  
2. **Low‑effort** – Prioritize zero‑chop or microwave‑ready foods such as pre‑washed greens, frozen veggies, and instant grains.  
3. **Flavor boosters** – Keep a handful of sauces, dressings, and finishing touches (pesto, vinaigrette, lemon, parmesan, chili crisp) to elevate bland dishes without extra cooking.
4. **Shelf‑life awareness** – Mix long‑lasting pantry goods (canned beans, lentils) with items that stay fresh for weeks (frozen vegetables, pre‑washed salads).

## Example Staple List (from [[summaries/session-39_2025-02-10]])
- Microwave rice or quinoa packets
- Frozen broccoli and peas (steam‑in‑bag)
- Pre‑washed baby spinach or arugula
- Cherry tomatoes (no chopping needed)
- Baby carrots (ready‑to‑eat)
- Canned beans (black beans, chickpeas)
- Pre‑cooked lentils (vacuum‑sealed)
- Jarred pesto
- Vinaigrette dressing
- Hummus
- Lemon (squeeze‑only)
- Olive oil
- Parmesan or chili crisp (finisher)

## How It Connects to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/zero-chop-cooking]]** – Pantry planning supplies the zero‑chop ingredients that make a kitchen ready for instant meals.
- **[[concepts/batch-cooking]]** – While batch cooking focuses on preparing large portions ahead of time, pantry planning ensures you have the complementary components on hand to finish those batches quickly.

## Benefits
- Reduces decision fatigue by limiting the number of possible side‑dish combinations.
- Cuts down on prep time, allowing dinner to be ready in under five minutes.
- Supports nutritional balance (vegetables, whole grains, protein) without additional cooking steps.
- Provides flexibility to adapt to any ready‑to‑heat deli entrée.
