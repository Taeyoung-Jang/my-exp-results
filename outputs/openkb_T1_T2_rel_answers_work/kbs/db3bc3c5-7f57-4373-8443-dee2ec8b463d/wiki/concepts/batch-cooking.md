# Batch Cooking

**Definition:** Preparing larger quantities of food at once to create multiple meals or components for future use.

**Core ideas**
- Cook once, eat many times.  
- Focus on versatile bases (grains, proteins, vegetables) that can be recombined.
- Saves time, reduces decision fatigue, and supports consistent nutrition.

**Key practices from [[summaries/session-39_2025-02-10]]**
- Use microwave‑ready rice or quinoa packets as a quick carbohydrate base.
- Prepare pre‑cooked lentils or canned beans in bulk for protein.
- Freeze individual portions of vegetables (e.g., broccoli, peas) for easy reheating.
- Pair batch‑cooked staples with ready‑to‑heat deli mains (meatloaf, roasted chicken, salmon, pasta bake) to create balanced meals in minutes.

**Relationship to other concepts**
- Overlaps with [[concepts/zero-chop-cooking]] by emphasizing minimal prep (no chopping).  
- Informs [[concepts/pantry-planning]]: maintaining a stocked set of batch‑ready staples is a cornerstone of an effective pantry.

**Benefits**
- Reduces daily cooking effort.
- Lowers food waste through planned portions.
- Enables quick, nutritious meals even on busy evenings.

**Typical starter kit**
- Microwave rice/quinoa packets, frozen vegetables, pre‑washed greens, canned beans, pre‑cooked lentils, and flavor boosters (pesto, vinaigrette, lemon, olive oil).
