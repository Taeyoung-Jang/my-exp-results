**User:** I’m trying to figure out how people manage weeknight dinners when life gets busy—what are some realistic strategies for eating decently without turning every evening into a big cooking project?

**Assistant:** A lot of people get through busy weeks by lowering the bar a bit and building simple habits that still feel like real meals. Things like cooking a larger batch of one or two easy dishes on the weekend and stretching them into different meals during the week can make a big difference. Having a few “default” dinners—things you can make almost on autopilot, like a quick pasta, stir‑fry, or sheet‑pan meal—keeps you from decision fatigue. Prepping just one or two ingredients ahead of time, like chopped veggies or cooked rice, can also make a fast dinner feel much easier. And it helps to keep your kitchen stocked with reliable shortcuts, whether that’s rotisserie chicken, frozen vegetables, or pre‑made sauces. The idea isn’t to cook something impressive every night, just to make the path to a decent meal as short and stress‑free as possible.

**User:** That makes sense. I’ve been relying a lot on ready-to-heat deli meals lately—what are a few good “upgrade” sides or add-ons I can keep around (fast veggies, salads, grains, sauces) so they feel more balanced without adding much work?

**Assistant:** You can make deli meals feel much more like a real dinner just by pairing them with one quick fresh thing or one quick warm thing. A bag of pre‑washed greens is an easy win—you can toss a handful on the plate with olive oil, lemon, or whatever dressing you like. Frozen vegetables are another great fallback; things like broccoli, peas, or mixed veggies steam in a few minutes and instantly balance heavier deli dishes. Cooked grains that come in microwave pouches—rice, farro, quinoa—are also handy and turn something like rotisserie chicken or deli entrées into a more complete meal. And keeping a couple of flavor boosters around, like a good vinaigrette, pesto, or chili crisp, makes even basic add‑ons feel a little more intentional without adding any real effort.

**User:** Could you give me a short “keep on hand” list of specific items to buy (like 10–15 staples) for those quick upgrades, and a few mix-and-match combos that work with common deli mains (meatloaf, roasted chicken, salmon, pasta bakes, etc.)?

**Assistant:** Here’s a compact set of staples that stay useful without much effort:  
Microwave rice or quinoa, frozen broccoli, frozen peas, baby spinach, arugula, cherry tomatoes, baby carrots, pre‑cooked lentils, canned beans, jarred pesto, vinaigrette, hummus, lemon, olive oil, and a “finisher” like chili crisp or parmesan.

A few quick pairings:  
Meatloaf works well with microwave mashed potatoes or rice plus steamed broccoli and a drizzle of olive oil and lemon.  
Roasted chicken feels more complete with arugula tossed in vinaigrette and a side of warm lentils or quinoa.  
Salmon is great with cherry tomatoes and spinach wilted quickly in a pan or even just microwaved peas with lemon.  
Pasta bakes pair easily with a simple salad of greens and tomatoes, or with peas mixed in and a spoon of pesto for extra flavor.
