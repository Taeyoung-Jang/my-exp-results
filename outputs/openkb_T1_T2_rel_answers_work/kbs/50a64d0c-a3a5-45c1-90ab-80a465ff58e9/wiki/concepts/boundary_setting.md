# Boundary Setting

**Definition**: A set of strategies for communicating and maintaining clear limits on social engagements, work tasks, or other obligations to preserve personal well‑being and energy.

## Core Elements
1. **Pre‑set End Times** – Decide on a concrete finish time before the interaction begins and share it with others.
2. **Energy‑Based Language** – Explain boundaries in terms of personal energy needs (e.g., *"I need to wind down before my night shift"*).
3. **Consistent Wind‑Down Routine** – Follow a short, repeatable ritual after social time to signal transition back to personal mode.
4. **Polite Decline Scripts** – Ready phrases to use when friends or colleagues suggest extending an activity (e.g., *"I’m keeping my pre‑shift routine intact, let’s plan another night"*).

## Why It Matters
- Protects **sleep hygiene** by ensuring enough rest before work or other obligations.
- Supports **[[concepts/energy_management]]** by aligning social time with personal energy peaks.
- Enables a sustainable **[[concepts/social_rhythm]]** rather than a rigid schedule.

## Application in Night‑Shift Lifestyle
In the *night‑shift* context described in [[summaries/session-36_2024-12-13]], boundary setting is crucial because:
- Social evenings occur before a demanding work period, so extending them can impair performance.
- Clear, communicated cut‑offs help friends understand the need for early nights.
- Simple scripts reduce awkwardness and keep relationships healthy.

## Sample Scripts from Session 36
- *"I’m keeping my pre‑shift routine intact, so I need to head home by 8 p.m."*
- *"I’d love to join later, but I have a recharge night before work tomorrow."*
- *"Thanks for the invite, I need to wind down now to stay functional for my shift."*
