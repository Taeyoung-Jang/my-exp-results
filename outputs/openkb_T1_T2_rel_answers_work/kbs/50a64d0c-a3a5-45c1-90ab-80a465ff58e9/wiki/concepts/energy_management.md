# Energy Management

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Strategies for recognizing, allocating, and preserving personal energy to support balanced social life, work responsibilities, and rest.

---

## Core Idea
Energy Management is the practice of monitoring how you feel across the day and intentionally scheduling activities—especially social ones—when you have sufficient mental and physical reserves. It complements concepts like [[concepts/social_rhythm]] and [[concepts/flexible-scheduling]] by adding a personal‑energy layer to time‑blocking.

---

## Key Elements from [[summaries/session-36_2024-12-13]]
1. **Identify peak energy windows** – For night‑shift workers, the “evening” may actually be late afternoon. The template suggests waking around 3:30‑4 p.m. and using the first few hours after waking for social outings because energy is highest then.
2. **Match activity type to energy level** – Active outings (walks) are placed earlier, while more passive gatherings (movie night) are scheduled later, when a gentle wind‑down is needed.
3. **Create a wind‑down routine** – After each social slot, a brief routine (shower, stretching, low‑light) signals the body to shift from social energy back to a rested state, protecting the upcoming work shift.
4. **Use energy‑based boundaries** – When friends push to extend the night, explicitly cite energy limits (“I need to stay functional for my shift”)—a tactic linked to [[concepts/boundary_setting]].
5. **Low‑effort connection** – On non‑social days, micro‑interactions (voice notes, quick texts) keep relationships alive without draining energy, illustrating the overlap with [[concepts/low_effort_connection]].

---

## Practical Steps
| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|------|--------|----------------|
| 1. Track energy | Note mood/alertness in a simple log for a week. | Reveals natural peaks and troughs. |
| 2. Anchor social slots | Schedule friend time during identified peaks (e.g., 5‑7 p.m. for night‑shifters). | Maximizes enjoyment and minimizes fatigue. |
| 3. Set clear end times | Define a finish point (e.g., 7:30 p.m.) and communicate it. | Supports [[concepts/boundary_setting]] and prevents overrun. |
| 4. Wind‑down ritual | 10‑15 min of calming activity (shower, stretch, dim lights). | Transitions energy from social to restorative mode. |
| 5. Low‑effort touchpoints | Send a voice note or quick photo on off‑days. | Keeps connections alive with minimal energy cost. |

## When to Apply
- Night‑shift workers with limited evening availability.
- Anyone experiencing burnout from over‑committing socially.
- Situations where a single weekly social slot must be both enjoyable and non‑draining.
