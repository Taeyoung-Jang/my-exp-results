# Name Recall Strategies

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Practical approaches to remembering or politely asking for names in workplace interactions, tailored for low‑stress, shop‑floor environments.

---

## Why name recall matters
- Builds rapport and psychological safety among teammates.
- Signals respect and attentiveness, especially during orientation or safety drills.
- Reduces anxiety for newcomers who may feel like the "new kid".

## Core strategies (derived from [[summaries/session-36_2025-02-15]])
1. **Honest, friendly acknowledgment** – Admit the lapse directly with a casual tone.
   - Example: "Man, first‑day brain—what was your name again?"
2. **Contextual cueing** – Reference the recent activity to make the request feel natural.
   - Example: "We were just working together and it slipped—remind me of your name?"
3. **Badge‑save line** – Use the laminated badge as a light‑hearted prop.
   - Example: "They gave me a fancy badge but not the memory to go with it—what’s your name again?"
4. **Pairing with a compliment or observation** – Shows genuine interest beyond the name.
   - Example: "I liked how you handled that drill—what’s your name again?"
5. **Note‑taking** – Keep a quick notebook or phone note of names and roles right after introductions.

## Implementation checklist for apprentices
- **Smile** and maintain eye contact when you ask.
- Keep the phrasing **concise**; avoid long explanations.
- Follow up by **repeating the name** immediately after they respond to reinforce memory.
- Log the name in a personal reference sheet for later use.
