# Workplace Community

**Definition**
A workplace community is a sense of belonging and mutual support among team members that goes beyond formal roles and tasks.

**Core Elements**
- **Psychological safety** – team members feel safe to be themselves, share ideas, and admit mistakes. [[concepts/psychological_safety]]
- **Authentic daily interactions** – small, genuine touchpoints embedded in regular work rather than forced activities.
- **Low‑effort touchpoints** – brief, optional moments such as shared chat threads, coffee‑pairing bots, quick voice notes, or Friday photo shares that keep people visible to one another. [[concepts/low_effort_touchpoints]]

**Why it Matters**
When a workplace community exists, collaboration improves, engagement rises, and turnover drops because people feel valued and connected.

**Insights from [[summaries/session-11_2024-06-15]]**
- Trust and safety are the foundation of community.
- Embedding connection in work (e.g., pairing on projects) is more effective than separate “team‑building” events.
- Maintaining visibility through low‑effort, voluntary rituals helps sustain the community as workloads shift or teams become more distributed.

**Practical Practices**
1. Create an informal channel for quick wins, jokes, or personal updates.
2. Use a rotating coffee‑pairing system to prompt brief one‑on‑ones.
3. Encourage short weekly round‑ups where members share a win or focus.
4. Allow optional photo or personal‑update posts to keep the social tone alive.

Cultivating a workplace community is an ongoing process that relies on consistent, authentic, and low‑friction interactions.
