# Orientation Day

**Definition**: An orientation day is the initial, low‑pressure introduction for a new apprentice that combines company overview, workspace tour, team introductions, and essential safety briefings.

---

## Core Components
- **Welcome & Company Overview** – Brief talk about the organization’s mission, values, and basic policies.
- **Workspace Tour** – Walkthrough of the shop floor, key equipment locations, and common work areas.
- **Team Introductions** – Meeting supervisors, mentors, and peers; often includes name‑sharing activities.
- **Safety Briefing** – Introduction to first‑aid kit locations, emergency exits, basic protective‑gear usage, and initial hazard‑reporting procedures.

## Psychological Safety & Low‑Effort Touchpoints
- Creating a welcoming environment helps apprentices feel safe to ask questions and admit gaps, supporting [[concepts/psychological_safety]].
- Simple, informal interactions such as casual name‑recall phrases or sharing a badge anecdote act as [[concepts/low_effort_touchpoints]] that strengthen early rapport.

## Name Recall Strategies
- Apprentices may forget names during initial drills. Using easy, shop‑floor language (e.g., "Man, first‑day brain—what's your name again?") aligns with [[concepts/name_recall_strategies]] and reduces perceived carelessness.

## Safety Question Bank
- Effective day‑one inquiries include:
  1. Where are the first‑aid kits and incident‑report forms?
  2. Who handles lockout/tagout (LOTO) procedures?
  3. Who should be notified if a power tool seems off?
  4. Where can I get replacement PPE if it doesn’t fit?
  5. Where are the emergency‑stop controls for my area?
- These questions exemplify the [[concepts/safety_question_bank]] approach—practical, non‑show‑off queries that demonstrate a genuine safety mindset.

## Building Workplace Community
- Early interactions, shared drills, and open safety dialogue foster a sense of belonging and collaborative culture, linking to [[concepts/workplace_community]].
