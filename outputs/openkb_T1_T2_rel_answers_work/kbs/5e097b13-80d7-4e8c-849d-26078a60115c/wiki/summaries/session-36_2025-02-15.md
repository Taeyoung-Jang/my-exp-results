# Summary of session-36_2025-02-15

**Date:** 2025-02-15  

**Topic:** Guidance for a new apprentice on orientation day, focusing on (1) low‑key strategies for forgetting names and (2) practical safety questions for a hands‑on trade environment.

---

## Orientation overview
- Orientation is a low‑pressure introduction: welcome chat, company overview, workspace tour, meeting teammates, and basic safety brief.  
- First‑day impression tips: punctuality, attentive body language, occasional practical questions, and genuine curiosity.  
- These practices relate to [[concepts/orientation_day]] and support [[concepts/psychological_safety]] by creating a welcoming atmosphere.

---

## Handling forgotten names on the shop floor
- **Core principle:** Acknowledge honestly, keep tone friendly, and pair the request with context.  
- **Suggested casual phrases** (shop‑floor tone):
  1. “Man, first‑day brain—what was your name again?”  
  2. “We were just working together and it slipped—remind me of your name?”  
  3. “Sorry, I blanked for a second—what's your name again?”  
  4. **Badge‑save line:** “They gave me a fancy badge but not the memory to go with it—what’s your name again?”  
- Use a smile and brief eye contact; it signals honesty, not carelessness.  
- These phrases align with [[concepts/name_recall_strategies]] and can be seen as [[concepts/low_effort_touchpoints]] for building rapport.

## Day‑one safety questions for hands‑on trade apprentices
| # | Question (shop‑floor phrasing) | Why it matters |
|---|--------------------------------|----------------|
| 1 | “Who should I tell if a power tool sounds off or isn’t working right?” | Shows proactive hazard reporting. |
| 2 | “Where are the first‑aid kits and incident‑report forms kept?” | Immediate access in emergencies. |
| 3 | “What’s the lockout/tagout (LOTO) procedure for machines we’ll be using?” | Critical for preventing accidental energization. |
| 4 | “If my PPE (gloves, goggles, etc.) doesn’t fit or is damaged, who do I talk to for a replacement?” | Ensures proper protection. |
| 5 | “Where are the emergency‑stop buttons or pull‑cords for my work area?” | Quick shutdown in urgent situations. |

These queries are practical, non‑show‑off, and demonstrate a genuine focus on safe work practices. They belong in the [[concepts/safety_question_bank]].

## Actionable takeaways for apprentices
1. Keep a small notebook or digital note of names and roles as you meet people.  
2. Use one of the casual name‑recall lines if you slip; pair it with a light‑hearted comment about the badge if appropriate.  
3. Prioritize safety by asking the five practical questions above during your first shift.  
4. Follow up by noting the answers in a personal safety quick‑reference sheet.  
5. Observe how seasoned workers communicate; adapt your tone to fit the shop‑floor culture and contribute to the [[concepts/workplace_community]].
