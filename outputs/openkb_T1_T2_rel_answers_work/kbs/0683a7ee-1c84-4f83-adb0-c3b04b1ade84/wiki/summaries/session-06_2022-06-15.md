# Summary of session‑06 (2022‑06‑15)

**Main topic:** How drivers decide whether to keep a car long‑term or replace it, focusing on cost/reliability trade‑offs, typical high‑cost failures, and a practical early‑warning maintenance checklist.

## Decision factors
- **Cost vs. reliability** – the primary drivers; when annual repair costs approach the yearly payment for a new car, replacement becomes sensible.
- **Safety & technology** – newer safety features or infotainment upgrades can nudge owners toward a swap.
- **Emotional/novelty factor** – boredom or desire for change can override purely practical considerations.

## Tipping‑point analysis
- **Predictability**: When a vehicle feels increasingly unpredictable, even low‑cost fixes feel expensive.
- **Big‑ticket repairs** that often trigger replacement:
  - [[entities/transmission]]
  - Major [[entities/engine]] work
  - [[entities/hybrid-battery]] or [[entities/ev-battery]] replacement
  - Major [[entities/suspension]] overhaul, air‑conditioning‑system failure, chronic electrical issues
- **Typical timing**: These failures become more likely after **≈10 years** or **150‑200 k miles**, though brand, maintenance history, and luck vary.
- **Early warning**: A cluster of medium‑cost repairs signals an approaching expensive phase.

## Early‑warning signs for relatively new cars
- Changes in sound, smell, or feel (e.g., rough idle, hesitant shifts, higher temperature gauge)
- New fluid leaks or spots under the vehicle
- Flickering or intermittent warning lights
- Deteriorating transmission smoothness, engine idling, or suspension comfort

## Practical maintenance checklist
| Item | Frequency | What to watch for |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Engine oil, coolant, brake fluid | **Monthly** | Low level, discoloration, contamination |
| Visual under‑car inspection | **Weekly** | New drips, puddles, corrosion |
| Driving feel & sounds | Ongoing (pay attention when driving) | Harsh engine noise, transmission hesitation, rough ride |
| Dashboard warning lights | Ongoing | Any flicker or repeat activation, even if brief |
| Tire condition (pressure, wear, sidewall cracks) | **Every 2 weeks** | Uneven wear, low pressure, visible damage |

Following this lightweight routine helps catch issues early without becoming obsessive.
