## Overview
This document provides practical advice for reducing stress while driving in dense urban environments and offers a step‑by‑step parallel‑parking routine, with special tweaks for small hatchbacks and low‑visibility conditions.

## City‑Driving Stress‑Reduction Tips
- **Smooth movements**: decelerate early, take wider angles, pause briefly to align before maneuvers.  
- **Intentional parking spot selection**: choose spots that allow forward exit or provide extra side clearance.  
- **Key vehicle features**: backup camera, parking sensors, 360‑degree camera, light steering, short overhangs, automatic parking assist. See [[concepts/vehicle-assist-features]] for a broader view.

## Mirror and Sensor Adjustments
- Set mirrors slightly farther outward to reduce blind spots and improve curb visibility. This falls under [[concepts/mirror-and-sensor-adjustments]].  
- Increase parking‑sensor sensitivity via the vehicle’s settings menu.  
- Add a convex stick‑on mirror to the passenger‑side mirror for a better curb view. Use [[entities/convex-stick-on-mirror]] and [[entities/passenger-side-mirror]].

## Parallel‑Parking Routine (General)
1. **Positioning** – Align rear bumpers with the car ahead, leaving roughly an arm’s length of lateral space.  
2. **Start reverse** – When the rear door handle (or midpoint for hatchbacks) lines up with the adjacent car’s taillight, turn the wheel toward the curb. Refer to [[entities/rear-door-handle]] for the reference point.  
3. **Rear‑wheel‑to‑curb cue** – Watch the passenger‑side mirror; when the rear wheel appears close to the curb, straighten the wheel.  
4. **Front‑clearance cue** – When the front car’s rear bumper is fully visible in the side mirror, turn the wheel away from the curb to tuck the nose in.  
5. **Final adjustments** – Move forward/backward slowly to center the vehicle and sit parallel.

See [[concepts/parallel-parking-routine]] for a consolidated view of this process.

## Adjustments for Tight Spots
- Exaggerate the initial turn (more decisive wheel angle).  
- Anticipate a short back‑and‑forth at the end; keep speed low for precise corrections.

## Hatchback‑Specific Checkpoints
- Use the **midpoint of the rear door** (instead of the handle) as the alignment reference because of the shorter rear overhang.  
- Hatchbacks pivot quickly; a smooth, moderate wheel turn is sufficient.

## Low‑Visibility / Curb‑Visibility Strategies
- Rely on mirror tilt cues: notice the car’s lower edge tilting toward the curb in the passenger mirror.  
- Slight head‑tilt adjustments can reveal reflections or shadows of the curb at night or in rain.  
- Slow the maneuver further to let subtle cues (tire noise, street‑light reflections) become noticeable.

## Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them
- **Turning too early** – leads to a shallow angle and excessive distance from the curb. Correct by waiting for the rear‑door/taillight alignment cue.  
- **Reversing too fast** – reduces ability to make incremental corrections. Maintain a slow, controlled speed throughout.  
- **Missing curb cue** – if the curb disappears, pause, pull forward slightly, and retry the final alignment slowly.

By consistently applying these visual checkpoints, mirror/sensor tweaks, and speed controls, drivers—especially those in compact hatchbacks—can make parallel parking predictable, repeatable, and far less stressful.
