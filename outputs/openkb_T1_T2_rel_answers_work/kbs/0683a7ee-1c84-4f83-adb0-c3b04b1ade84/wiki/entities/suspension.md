# Suspension (product)

The **suspension** is the set of components—including springs, shock absorbers, struts, and linkages—that connect a vehicle’s wheels to its frame, controlling ride quality, handling, and road‑holding ability. It is a critical *product* in automotive engineering.

## Role in ownership decisions
- A failing suspension can become a [[concepts/big-ticket-repairs]] item because replacement or major overhaul often costs thousands and adds little resale value.
- Early signs of suspension wear (clunking noises, uneven ride, or poor handling) are part of [[concepts/early-warning-maintenance]], helping owners decide whether to retain or replace a vehicle.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-06_2022-06-15]]
- The conversation identifies **suspension overhauls** as one of the “quiet” tipping‑point repairs that may push a driver toward a new car.
- It typically becomes a concern after a vehicle reaches **≈10 years** or **150‑200 k miles**, though exact timing varies by make, maintenance history, and driving conditions.
- Monitoring for early signals (noises over bumps, uneven tire wear, degraded ride comfort) aligns with the practical checklist recommended for early detection.

## Maintenance and warning checklist
- **Visual inspection**: Look for worn or leaking shocks/struts during the weekly under‑car glance.
- **Feel & sound**: Note any clunking or increased harshness when driving over uneven surfaces (see the monthly fluid check and sound monitoring in the checklist).
- **Tire wear**: Uneven wear can indicate suspension misalignment; check tires every two weeks.
- **Professional inspection**: If multiple medium‑cost repairs appear, consider a full suspension diagnostic.
