# Big‑Ticket Repairs

**Definition**: Vehicle components or systems whose failure incurs a repair cost that approaches or exceeds the remaining value of the car, typically prompting owners to consider replacement rather than repair.

## Core components
- **[[entities/transmission]]** – major transmission failures can run into the thousands and rarely add resale value.
- **[[entities/engine]]** – extensive engine work (e.g., rebuilt heads, crankshaft replacement) is similarly costly.
- **[[entities/hybrid-battery]]** and **[[entities/ev-battery]]** – replacement often costs several thousand dollars and offers limited return on investment for older cars.
- **[[entities/suspension]]** – major suspension overhauls are expensive and can signal systemic wear.
- **Air‑conditioning system** – while not always a replacement trigger, costly AC failures are a notable "quiet" tipping point.

## Why they matter
These repairs are highlighted in [[summaries/session-06_2022-06-15]] as the primary financial thresholds that shift an owner's decision from **[[concepts/ownership-decision-factors]]** to replacement. When the projected yearly repair spend approaches the cost of a new‑car loan payment, owners typically opt to retire the vehicle.

## Typical timing
- Age: **≈10 years** or older
- Mileage: **150–200 k miles**
- Variation: brand‑specific reliability, maintenance history, and random failure risk can advance or delay these thresholds.

## Interaction with other concepts
- **[[concepts/early-warning-maintenance]]**: Regular monitoring can identify early symptoms (e.g., fluid leaks, noises) that may avert a full‑scale failure of a big‑ticket component.
- **[[concepts/ownership-decision-factors]]**: Cost, reliability, safety tech, and emotional factors converge around the presence or risk of big‑ticket repairs.

## Practical takeaways
1. Track repair costs annually; compare them to a realistic estimate of a new‑car payment.
2. Watch for early warning signs (odd noises, leaks, warning lights) that often precede big‑ticket failures.
3. When multiple medium‑cost repairs start stacking, treat it as a signal that a big‑ticket repair may be imminent.
