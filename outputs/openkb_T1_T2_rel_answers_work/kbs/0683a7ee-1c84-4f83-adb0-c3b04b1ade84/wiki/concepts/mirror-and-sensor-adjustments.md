# Mirror and Sensor Adjustments

Improving visual feedback and proximity alerts is a core part of stress‑free city driving. The following adjustments, drawn from [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]], help drivers of small hatchbacks (and other vehicles) gain clearer sight lines and earlier warnings when parallel parking or negotiating tight streets.

## Mirror Positioning
- **Outward offset**: Set the passenger‑side mirror slightly farther outward than default. This reduces blind spots and makes the curb appear earlier in the mirror view. [[entities/passenger-side-mirror]]
- **Convex add‑on**: Attach a small convex stick‑on mirror to the corner of the passenger‑side mirror for an expanded field of view without clutter. [[entities/convex-stick-on-mirror]]
- **Head tilt technique**: Slightly tilt your head while checking the mirror to catch reflections of low curbs or street‑light glints in low‑visibility conditions.

## Sensor Calibration
- **Sensitivity boost**: Increase parking‑sensor sensitivity in the vehicle’s settings menu so alerts trigger earlier, giving you more time to react. [[entities/parking-sensors]]
- **Rear‑door reference**: Align the midpoint of the rear door (or the rear‑door handle) with the adjacent car’s taillight before starting the reverse; this visual cue works well with calibrated sensors.

## Camera Utilization
- **Backup camera**: Use the backup camera as a primary reference for lateral distance, especially when the curb is hard to see. [[entities/backup-camera]]
- **360‑degree view**: If equipped, enable the 360° camera mode for a bird’s‑eye perspective that simplifies parallel‑parking geometry. [[entities/360-degree-camera]]

## Integration with Parking Routine
These mirror and sensor tweaks complement the standard [[concepts/parallel-parking-routine]] and the broader set of [[concepts/vehicle-assist-features]]. By combining adjusted mirrors, calibrated sensors, and camera assistance, drivers can achieve:
- Consistent curb‑distance perception.
- Earlier warning of obstacles.
- Smoother, slower maneuvers that reduce the need for abrupt corrections.

## When Visibility Is Low
- Rely on the **tilt cue** in the passenger‑side mirror: notice the car’s lower edge beginning to angle toward the curb.
- Slow the maneuver further to let subtle cues (tire noise, street‑light reflections) become noticeable.
- If the curb remains invisible, pause, pull forward slightly, and re‑attempt the final alignment using the enhanced mirror view.

By routinely applying these adjustments, drivers can make parallel parking predictable, reduce stress, and maintain better situational awareness in dense urban environments.
