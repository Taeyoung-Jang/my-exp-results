# Parallel Parking Routine

**Definition**: A structured, repeatable set of visual cues and vehicle actions that lets drivers consistently and safely parallel‑park, even in cramped city streets.

## Core Steps (from [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]])
1. **Initial alignment** – Pull up alongside the car ahead so your rear bumpers are roughly even and leave about an arm’s length of lateral space.
2. **Reference point** – When the rear door handle (or the midpoint of the rear door on a hatchback) lines up with the adjacent car’s taillight, begin reversing and turn the wheel toward the curb.
3. **Rear‑wheel‑to‑curb cue** – Watch the passenger‑side mirror; as soon as the rear wheel appears close to the curb, straighten the wheel to let the car drift deeper.
4. **Front‑clearance cue** – When the rear bumper of the car in front becomes fully visible in the side mirror, turn the wheel away from the curb to tuck the nose in.
5. **Final centering** – Move forward or backward slowly to center the vehicle and sit parallel.

## Adjustments for Tight Spots
- Exaggerate the initial turn angle.
- Anticipate a short back‑and‑forth at the end, keeping speed low for precise corrections.

## Hatchback‑Specific Tweaks
- Use the **midpoint of the rear door** as the alignment reference instead of the handle, due to the shorter rear overhang.
- Because hatchbacks pivot quickly, a smooth, moderate wheel turn is sufficient.

## Low‑Visibility Strategies
- Rely on **mirror tilt cues**: notice the car’s lower edge tilting toward the curb in the passenger‑side mirror.
- Slight head‑tilt adjustments can reveal reflections or shadows of the curb at night or in rain.
- Slow the maneuver further to let subtle cues (tire noise, street‑light reflections) become noticeable.

## Common Mistakes & Fixes
- **Turning too early** – leads to a shallow angle and excessive curb distance. Wait for the rear‑door/taillight alignment cue.
- **Reversing too fast** – reduces ability to make incremental corrections. Maintain a controlled, slow speed.
- **Missing curb cue** – if the curb disappears, pause, pull forward slightly, and retry the final alignment slowly.
