**User:** Could you walk me through a simple, repeatable parallel-parking “routine” I can practice (like the exact alignment points and when to turn the wheel), and how it changes if the spot is tighter than usual?

**Assistant:** You can make parallel parking feel almost automatic if you use the same visual cues every time. Start by pulling up beside the car in front of the space so your rear bumpers are roughly even, leaving about an arm’s length of space between you. From there, begin rolling back slowly and turn the wheel toward the curb as soon as the back of that car appears just behind your shoulder. That usually tucks your rear end into the space cleanly.

As soon as your rear wheel is close to the curb—something you can spot in your passenger mirror—start unwinding the wheel and let the car drift in while staying slow and steady. When your front end clears the car ahead, turn the wheel the opposite direction to bring the nose in. At that point it’s mostly tiny adjustments forward or backward to center yourself and sit parallel.

If the spot is tight, the motions are the same; you just have to exaggerate them a bit. Turn the wheel more decisively at the start so the back of the car swings in sooner, and be ready to straighten and counter‑turn a little earlier so you don’t clip the car in front. Tight spaces usually mean a short back‑and‑forth at the end, which is normal—just keep the movements slow and let the mirrors and sensors guide you.
