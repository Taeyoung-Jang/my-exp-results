# Summary of session-13_2024-06-15

**Main contribution**: Practical advice for planning a visit to an unfamiliar building with a focus on finding accessible entrances and elevators before arrival.

## Key Concepts
- [[concepts/building-layout-planning]] – Use online resources (website floor plans, PDFs, interactive maps, virtual tours, street‑view) to get a high‑level view of entrances, stairs, and vertical routes.
- [[concepts/accessibility-information]] – Look for dedicated accessibility or visitor‑information pages that list step‑free entrances and public elevators.
- online floor plans and virtual tours – Often provided by commercial, institutional, or government buildings; useful for visualizing floor levels.
- street‑view tools – Give a sense of exterior doors and signage.
- [[entities/front-desk]] communication – Calling or emailing reception, security, or an [[entities/accessibility-office]] is the most reliable way to confirm current elevator status, badge restrictions, and any maintenance.
- elevator accessibility – Verify that the elevator is public, operational, and not behind badge access.
- step‑free routes – Ensure the entire path from the chosen entrance to the destination contains no stairs.
- [[concepts/pre-visit-planning]] – Combine online research with direct staff contact; arrive early to allow on‑site clarification.
- accessibility script – A concise, word‑for‑word phone or email script to request entrance, elevator, and backup information.

## Findings & Recommendations
1. **Start with online research**: Check the building’s website for floor plans, maps, or virtual tours. Use street‑view to locate main doors.
2. **Contact building staff**: Call or email the [[entities/front-desk]], security, or an [[entities/accessibility-office]]. Ask specific questions about:
   - Closest entrance to a public elevator.
   - Presence of any stairs on the route.
   - Current elevator service status and badge restrictions.
3. **Prepare a script**: Use a short, polite script (provided in the original conversation) to ensure all details are covered and to request a backup step‑free entrance/elevator if needed.
4. **Backup plan**: If the preferred elevator is restricted or under maintenance, ask for an alternative entrance and elevator that are visitor‑accessible, and note any temporary arrangements or staff contacts (e.g., [[entities/reception]]).
5. **On‑site verification**: Arrive a few minutes early to confirm directions with reception and to handle any unexpected changes.

## Example Scripts
- **Phone script**: “Hi, I’m visiting for the first time and need a fully step‑free route because I can’t use stairs. Could you tell me which entrance is closest to a public elevator that visitors can use without a badge? I also want to confirm that the route from that entrance has no stairs and that the elevator is currently working. And if that elevator is unavailable or badge‑only when I arrive, what’s the best alternative entrance and elevator I should use instead?”
- **Email version**: “Hello, I’ll be visiting your building and need a completely step‑free route. Could you please let me know which entrance is closest to a public elevator that doesn’t require a badge, whether there are any stairs on that route, and whether the elevator is currently in service? If that elevator happens to be restricted or out of service when I arrive, could you also tell me the best alternative entrance and elevator for visitors? Thank you.”

These steps help ensure a smooth, accessible visit without unexpected obstacles.
