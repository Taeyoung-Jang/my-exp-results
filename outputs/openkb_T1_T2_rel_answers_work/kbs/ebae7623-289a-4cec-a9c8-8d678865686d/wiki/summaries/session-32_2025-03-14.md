## Summary of session-32_2025-03-14

**Context** – The user wants to rebuild a consistent morning [[entities/5k-run]] routine after physical‑therapy clearance and is looking for concrete guidance on setting an “easy pace” that avoids over‑pacing.

**Key recommendations**
1. **Prioritize pacing before structure** – Start by training the body to run at a conversational pace. Once this feels natural, weekly schedules and warm‑ups become easier to add.
2. **Talk test as primary cue** – Use the ability to hold a conversation as the main indicator of an easy run. It adapts to weather, fatigue, and stress without needing equipment. ([[concepts/talk-test]])
3. **Heart‑rate guardrail (optional)** –
   - Estimate aerobic cap with simple formulas: `180 – age` (≈146 bpm for a 34‑year‑old) or ~70 % of estimated max HR (≈130‑150 bpm). 
   - Set a practical ceiling around **145 bpm**. 
   - Treat it as a soft reminder, not a strict rule; brief spikes on hills are acceptable. ([[concepts/heart-rate-guardrail]])
4. **Pace range** – The user’s typical 5K time of 26‑27 min translates to roughly **5:20‑5:30 min/km**. Running a little slower (≈5:45‑6:00 min/km) can reinforce the easy‑run feel.
5. **Identify “too high for too long”** – Staying above the mid‑140 bpm cap for several continuous minutes early‑mid run, or needing to breathe hard to maintain pace, signals a need to ease back. ([[concepts/aerobic-zone]])

**Practical steps**
- Perform a quick warm‑up (5‑10 min walk or dynamic stretch).
- Begin the 5K at a deliberately slower pace than usual; check the talk test.
- Glance at HR (if available) to ensure it stays under ~145 bpm.
- Adjust speed on the fly; if conversation becomes strained or HR spikes, reduce pace for a minute.
- After 1‑2 weeks, gradually tighten the pace while staying within the talk‑test zone.
