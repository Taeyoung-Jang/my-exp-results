# Heart‑Rate Guardrail

A **heart‑rate guardrail** is a loosely‑defined upper limit for heart‑rate during easy‑pace runs. It serves as a soft reminder rather than a strict rule, helping runners stay within their aerobic zone without over‑exertion.

## Why use a guardrail?
- Provides a numeric cue when you have a heart‑rate monitor, complementing the more intuitive [[concepts/talk-test]].
- Helps maintain an easy effort across varying conditions (hills, temperature, fatigue).
- Reduces the risk of unintentionally drifting into a too‑intense pace, which can lead to burnout or injury.

## How to estimate it
The source document [[summaries/session-32_2025-03-14]] suggests two quick, equipment‑free methods:
1. **180 minus age** – For a 34‑year‑old, this yields roughly **146 bpm**.
2. **~70 % of estimated max HR** – Using the common max HR formula (220 – age) gives a max of ~186 bpm; 70 % is about **130‑150 bpm**.

Both approaches converge on a practical guardrail around **145 bpm** for the example runner.

## Applying the guardrail
- **Treat it as a guide:** Brief spikes above the cap (e.g., on a hill) are acceptable.
- **Identify “too high for too long”:** Sustained readings above the mid‑140 bpm range early or midway through a 5K, coupled with labored breathing, signal the need to ease back.
- **Combine with the talk‑test:** If you can still hold a conversation, you’re likely within the easy zone even if the HR briefly exceeds the guardrail.

## Relation to other concepts
- The guardrail works hand‑in‑hand with the [[concepts/aerobic-zone]] concept, which defines the broader intensity range for endurance training.
- It acts as a quantitative backup to the [[concepts/talk-test]], offering a concrete metric when the latter feels subjective.

## Practical tip
Set your wearable’s alert to vibrate when you cross the guardrail (≈145 bpm). Use the alert as a cue to briefly reduce speed, then resume once you’re back below the threshold.
