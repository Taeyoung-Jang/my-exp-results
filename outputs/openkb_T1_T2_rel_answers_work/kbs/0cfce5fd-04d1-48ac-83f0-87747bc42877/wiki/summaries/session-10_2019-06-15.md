## Session 10 – 2019-06-15

**Main contribution**: a concrete, printable worksheet for side‑by‑side comparison of up to three local election candidates, focused on community‑oriented performance rather than corporate influence.

### Key concepts
- [[concepts/candidate-evaluation-checklist]] – 12 targeted questions that probe concrete actions, community input, and measurable outcomes.  
- [[concepts/scoring-rubric]] – Simple 0‑2 point system allowing quick quantitative comparison.  
- [[concepts/red-flags]] – Short list of verbal cues (slogans, dodging, lack of examples) indicating vague promises.  
- side‑by‑side comparison – Layout that fits three candidates on a single page for efficient printing.  
- community priorities – Emphasis on local services, neighborhood groups, and small‑scale organisations.  
- donor influence safeguards – Questions that surface protections against single‑donor dominance.  
- concrete outcome measurement – Prompt to name a specific result to be judged after one year.

### Structure of the worksheet
1. Header with candidate names.  
2. Twelve rows, each containing the question, a score field (0‑2), and a notes column.  
3. Total score tallies per candidate.  
4. “Overall Impression” and “Follow‑Up Questions” boxes for qualitative assessment.

### Intended use
- Print and bring to town‑hall meetings or candidate forums.  
- Fill in scores and notes in real time to capture specificity of answers.  
- Use red‑flag reminders to flag evasive or buzz‑word‑heavy responses.  
- Compare total scores and qualitative impressions to decide which candidates align with community‑first values.
