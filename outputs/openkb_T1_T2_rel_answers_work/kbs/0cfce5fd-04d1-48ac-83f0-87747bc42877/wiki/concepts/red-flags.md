# Red‑Flag Indicators

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Observable cues that suggest a candidate is giving vague, evasive, or buzz‑word‑heavy answers, indicating a lack of concrete planning.

## Definition
Red‑Flag Indicators are specific verbal or behavioral signals that help voters quickly spot when a candidate is not providing substantive detail. They are useful in fast‑paced town‑hall settings where time is limited.

## Core Indicators (from [[summaries/session-10_2019-06-15]])
1. **Repeated slogans** – The candidate recycles phrases like “strong community” without offering steps, timelines, or examples.
2. **Avoidance of specifics** – Answers gloss over details, steering the conversation to unrelated topics.
3. **Missing personal involvement** – Fails to name their own role or collaborators on past projects.
4. **Lack of measurable outcomes** – No reference to metrics, milestones, or how success will be measured.
5. **Dodging follow‑up** – When pressed, the candidate pivots or refuses to elaborate further.

## How to Use Red‑Flag Indicators
- **During the event:** Keep a short checklist (see [[concepts/red-flags]]) and tick any indicator that appears.
- **After the event:** Review the flagged items alongside the scores from the [[concepts/scoring-rubric]] to adjust your overall impression.
- **Research phase:** Prioritize follow‑up questions for any flagged area, using the “Follow‑Up Questions” box in the candidate comparison sheet.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The indicators complement the **[[concepts/candidate-evaluation-checklist]]**, providing a qualitative overlay to the quantitative scores.
- They inform the **[[concepts/scoring-rubric]]** by highlighting where lower scores (0‑1) may be justified.
