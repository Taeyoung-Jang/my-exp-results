# Scoring Rubric

**Definition**: A lightweight quantitative framework that assigns a score of 0, 1, or 2 to each question in the [[concepts/candidate-evaluation-checklist]], allowing rapid side‑by‑side comparison of multiple candidates.

## Core Structure
- **Score range**: 0 = unclear or vague response, 1 = partially specific, 2 = clear, concrete, and grounded.
- **Application**: Each of the 12 checklist questions receives an individual score; scores are recorded in the worksheet template from [[summaries/session-10_2019-06-15]].
- **Total calculation**: Sum of the 12 scores yields a maximum of 24 points per candidate.

## How to Use
1. **Listen or research** each candidate’s answer to a checklist question.
2. **Assign** the appropriate score based on the clarity and specificity of the response.
3. **Record** brief notes alongside the score for context.
4. **Add** the scores to obtain a total that can be compared across candidates.
5. **Complement** the numeric result with qualitative impressions and any [[concepts/red-flags]] observed.

## Rationale
- **Speed**: The 0‑2 scale is quick to apply during live events (town halls, debates) without over‑analyzing.
- **Objectivity**: By anchoring scores to concrete criteria (clarity, specificity, evidence), the rubric reduces personal bias.
- **Transparency**: Scores and notes are visible on the printed worksheet, making the evaluation process auditable.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The rubric is the scoring backbone for the [[concepts/candidate-evaluation-checklist]].
- It works in tandem with the [[concepts/red-flags]] list, which highlights verbal cues that may justify a lower score.
