# High‑Value Regulations

**Definition** – Regulations that provide clear, evidence‑based protection for public safety, health, or community quality of life while imposing low administrative cost and predictable compliance for small businesses.

## Characteristics
- Directly address a documented risk (e.g., fire, foodborne illness, structural failure).
- Produce measurable benefits such as reduced injuries, illness outbreaks, or nuisance complaints.
- Have straightforward, consistent enforcement procedures and short, transparent approval timelines.
- Avoid duplication with other rules and do not require frequent renewals for trivial changes.

## Examples from [[summaries/session-31_2022-06-15]]
- **Fire‑suppression requirements** for restaurants and other occupancies.
- **Building and electrical codes** that ensure structural integrity and safe wiring.
- **Food‑handling and health inspection standards** to prevent contamination.
- **Noise and odor limits** that protect residential neighbors from late‑night or industrial disturbances.
- Basic **safety‑equipment inspections** for older buildings.

## Relationship to other concepts
- Serves as the positive complement to [[concepts/low-value-red-tape]] – the rules that add cost without measurable benefit.
- Reducing [[concepts/regulatory-friction]] focuses on keeping high‑value rules while eliminating unnecessary steps.
- Evaluation of high‑value regulations can follow the [[concepts/scoring-rubric]] or the [[concepts/candidate-evaluation-checklist]] to ensure they meet the criteria of risk mitigation, benefit magnitude, low burden, and lack of overlap.

## Why they matter
Maintaining a core set of high‑value regulations encourages entrepreneurship by giving businesses confidence that the rules they must follow are necessary, stable, and not a hidden barrier. It also protects residents and the environment, creating a healthier, more attractive community for both commerce and living.
