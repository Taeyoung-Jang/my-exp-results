# Candidate Evaluation Checklist

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A standardized list of twelve questions designed to evaluate how well local election candidates prioritize community needs, concrete actions, and safeguards against corporate or single‑donor influence.

**Origin:** Derived from the worksheet created in [[summaries/session-10_2019-06-15]].

## Core Questions
1. What community input shaped your priorities?
2. Describe a recent local project you worked on and your role.
3. What concrete steps would you take in your first few months?
4. How will you measure whether your proposals are working?
5. What obstacles do you expect, and how would you handle tradeoffs?
6. Give an example of changing course after hearing from residents.
7. What local partners or groups do you work with when shaping programs?
8. How do you balance neighborhood groups, small orgs, and larger stakeholders?
9. What safeguards ensure no single donor or interest group has too much influence?
10. Which core local services most need attention, and how would you support them?
11. Which parts of your plan can start immediately with existing resources?
12. Name one concrete outcome you want to be judged on after a year.

## Application
- **Side‑by‑side comparison**: The checklist can be printed in a three‑candidate format to allow direct scoring.
- **Scoring**: Typically paired with a simple 0‑2 [[concepts/scoring-rubric]] per question, yielding a total out of 24.
- **Red‑flag detection**: Used alongside [[concepts/red-flags]] to note evasive or vague responses.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/scoring-rubric]] – the 0‑2 point system used to quantify answers.
- [[concepts/red-flags]] – indicators of non‑specific or buzzword‑heavy replies.
