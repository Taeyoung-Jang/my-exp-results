# Low‑Value Red Tape

**Definition** – Low‑value red tape refers to administrative requirements, approvals, or renewals that create unnecessary time, cost, or complexity for businesses while providing little or no demonstrable benefit to public safety, health, or community quality.

---

## Key Characteristics (from [[summaries/session-31_2022-06-15]])
- **Unnecessary duplication** – multiple departments reviewing the same information (e.g., multi‑step sign approvals, separate approvals for minor renovations).
- **Frequent trivial renewals** – permits that must be renewed quarterly or annually for equipment that rarely changes.
- **Paper‑only processes** – in‑person filings for routine updates that could be handled electronically.
- **Lack of measurable outcome** – rules that do not address a documented risk or improve a clear community metric.

---

## Typical Examples
| Example | Why it’s low‑value | Impact on businesses |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Multi‑step sign approval (design checked twice) | Redundant review, no safety gain | Delays opening, adds design costs |
| Separate departmental permits for a small interior remodel | Overlap, duplicated paperwork | Increases staff time, creates confusion |
| Quarterly renewal of a minor equipment permit | Rarely changes, administrative overhead | Ongoing fees and paperwork for negligible benefit |
| Mandatory in‑person filing for trivial updates | Inefficient, digital alternatives exist | Travel cost, lost productivity |

- **[[concepts/high-value-regulations]]** – Contrasts with rules that protect health, safety, and nuisance, which are retained.

- **[[concepts/candidate-evaluation-checklist]]** – Provides a checklist to evaluate whether a rule belongs in the low‑value category.
- **[[concepts/red-flags]]** – Low‑value red tape often triggers red‑flags in regulatory audits (e.g., high processing time, low benefit).

---

## Mitigation Strategies
1. **Regular Review Cycle** – Implement a neutral, biennial review involving staff, business owners, and residents (as suggested in the session summary).
2. **Objective Criteria** – Use the [[concepts/scoring-rubric]] to assess each rule’s necessity, overlap, and cost.
3. **Automatic Sunset Dates** – Set expiration dates for administrative rules; require evidence of continued need for renewal.
4. **Streamline Approvals** – Consolidate departmental reviews into a single touchpoint and allow electronic submissions.
5. **Simplify Renewal Processes** – Extend renewal periods for low‑risk permits and eliminate unnecessary paperwork.

---

## Benefits of Eliminating Low‑Value Red Tape
- Faster business start‑up times and lower entry barriers.
- Reduced operating costs for existing small businesses.
- Increased attractiveness of the town for new entrepreneurs.
- Ability to reallocate municipal resources toward high‑value regulations and community services.
