# Regulatory Friction

**Definition**: Regulatory friction refers to the cumulative burden that unclear, unpredictable, or duplicated regulations place on small businesses, slowing their start‑up, growth, and day‑to‑day operations.

## Core Elements

1. **Uncertainty & Complexity**
   - Vague permit requirements, shifting timelines, and surprise fees increase perceived risk for entrepreneurs.
2. **Redundant Approvals**
   - Multiple departmental sign‑off steps for simple tasks (e.g., minor renovations) create unnecessary delays.
3. **Administrative Overhead**
   - Frequent renewals of trivial permits or mandatory paper filings add time and cost without public benefit.

## Why It Matters

High regulatory friction discourages new entrants, raises operating costs, and can push existing businesses to relocate or close. Reducing friction typically leads to more startups, higher hiring rates, and more stable local prices.

## Strategies Highlighted in [[summaries/session-31_2022-06-15]]

- **Predictable Permit Process**: Clear, consistent requirements and realistic timelines.
- **Scale‑Sensitive Rules**: Simpler processes for modest expansions (e.g., a café patio) compared to large projects.
- **Regular Review Cycle**: A neutral, mixed‑stakeholder review using objective criteria to retire outdated rules.
