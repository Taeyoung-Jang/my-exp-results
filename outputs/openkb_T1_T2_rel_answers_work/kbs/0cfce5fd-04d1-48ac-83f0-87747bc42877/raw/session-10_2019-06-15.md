**User:** I’m trying to get my thoughts together before my local election and could use some help making a simple checklist for comparing candidates. I've always leaned towards candidates who focus on community support rather than corporate interests, it's just how I've viewed things for years. Can you suggest a few practical questions I can ask at a town hall (or look up on their sites) to figure out who actually matches that, and how to spot vague promises vs real plans?

**Assistant:** You can absolutely prep for a town hall without getting into anything partisan or candidate‑specific. Since you’re interested in understanding how grounded a candidate’s plans are, you might focus on questions that reveal how they think, how detailed their proposals are, and what evidence they can point to.

You could ask them to explain how they decided on their priorities and what community input shaped those ideas. Asking them to walk through a recent project or policy they’ve supported and describe exactly what they did, who they worked with, and what the outcome was can also show whether they operate in a hands‑on, community‑focused way or just speak in broad themes.

Another useful angle is to press for specifics: how they would measure success, what steps they’d take in their first months, and what challenges they expect. People with real plans can usually talk about obstacles and tradeoffs; people making vague promises tend to stay at the slogan level.

When looking at their websites, pay attention to whether they describe concrete actions, timelines, or past results, or whether they mostly rely on general statements like “supporting families” or “building a stronger community” without explaining how. Specifics, even small ones, usually signal that they’ve thought things through.

Keeping it conversational and asking for examples is often the quickest way to separate aspirational talk from actual planning.

Also, could you include a couple questions that specifically tease out whether they prioritize local services and small/community groups over big corporate donors (without sounding accusatory)?

**Assistant:** Here’s a printable, one‑page style checklist written in simple, conversational lines you can scan down and leave space beside for notes. It stays neutral but helps you get the clarity you’re looking for.

Questions to ask or look up:
Ask what specific community needs shaped their priorities and how they gathered that input.  
Ask them to describe a recent local project they worked on and what their personal role was.  
Ask what concrete steps they would take in their first few months if elected.  
Ask how they plan to measure whether their proposals are actually working.  
Ask what obstacles they expect and how they would handle tradeoffs.  
Ask for an example of a past decision where they changed course after hearing from residents.  
Ask what local partnerships they rely on when designing programs or services.  
Ask how they balance the needs of small organizations, neighborhood groups, and larger stakeholders.  
Ask what safeguards they use to prevent any single donor or interest group from having too much influence.  
Ask how they would protect or improve core local services, and which ones they think need the most attention.  
Ask what parts of their plan can start immediately with existing resources and what would require new funding.  
Ask them to name one concrete outcome they want to be judged on in a year.

Quick red‑flag signs:
If they repeat slogans without describing any steps, examples, or tradeoffs.  
If they avoid naming who they’ve worked with or what they personally did on past projects.  
If they answer questions about priorities by pivoting to unrelated talking points.  
If they describe support for “the community” or “local businesses” but can’t name actual groups, programs, or needs.

You can print this as a simple list with space on the right for jotting notes during the event.
