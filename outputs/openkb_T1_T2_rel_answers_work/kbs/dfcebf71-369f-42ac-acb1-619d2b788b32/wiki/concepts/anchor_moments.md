# Anchor Moments

**Definition**: Anchor moments are recurring, low‑commitment time blocks intentionally set aside each week to maintain and deepen existing friendships.

## Core Characteristics
- **Regularity** – Occur on a predictable day or time (e.g., every Tuesday evening).  Consistency builds trust and signals reliability, a key factor in [[concepts/friendship_stickiness]].
- **Low pressure** – Activities are simple and flexible (cooking together, a quick dinner, a shared TV show).  The goal is presence, not performance.
- **Minimal planning** – Because the slot is predefined, it avoids the decision‑fatigue of “when should we meet?” and reduces the risk of over‑commitment.
- **Integrates with routine** – Anchors fit naturally into an existing weekly rhythm, making them easy to sustain alongside work and other obligations.

## Why Anchor Moments Work
The session‑09_2024-06-12 discussion highlights that friendships “stick” when there is **consistent, small‑scale contact** rather than sporadic, intense events.  By carving out a dedicated yet lightweight window, friends can stay visible to each other without the emotional gymnastics that larger, less frequent gatherings often demand.

## Practical Implementation (from [[summaries/session-09_2024-06-12]])
1. **Pick a day** – e.g., Tuesday evenings.
2. **Choose a simple activity** – cooking, a brief dinner, or a shared media experience.
3. **Keep it flexible** – rotate which friends attend each week to avoid exclusivity and to fit varying schedules.
4. **Fallback options** – if the anchor is missed, a micro‑interaction (quick text, funny photo) keeps the connection alive.

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/friendship_stickiness]]** – Anchor moments provide the repeated, low‑intensity contact that underpins long‑term friendship durability.
- **[[concepts/low_pressure_meeting]]** – While anchor moments focus on existing friends, the same low‑pressure mindset can be applied when meeting new people, creating a harmonious social rhythm.

## Benefits
- Reduces social fatigue by limiting formal commitments to one predictable slot.
- Enhances emotional safety: friends know there is a reliable time to reconnect.
- Supports a balanced social life, leaving room for spontaneous or new‑person interactions.
