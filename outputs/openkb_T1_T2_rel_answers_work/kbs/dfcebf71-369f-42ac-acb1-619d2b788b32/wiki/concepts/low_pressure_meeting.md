# Low‑Pressure Meeting

**Definition**: A low‑pressure meeting is an informal, interest‑based interaction that lets new connections form naturally, avoiding the sense of obligation or forced socializing.

## Core Principles
- **Shared activity**: Meet people while doing something you already enjoy (e.g., a hobby class, coffee shop, board‑game night). The activity supplies conversation material so you don’t have to manufacture small talk.
- **Minimal commitment**: Choose events or settings that require little time and no long‑term RSVP, making it easy to attend or skip without guilt.
- **Open signaling**: A brief, friendly gesture (a smile, brief hello, or short chat) signals receptivity and invites others to respond.
- **Routine integration**: Embed the encounter in places you already frequent, turning regular spots into informal networking hubs.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- It supports **[[concepts/friendship_stickiness]]** by providing low‑stakes opportunities to build the small, repeated contacts that sustain friendships.
- It can be scheduled as one of the **[[concepts/anchor_moments]]** in a weekly rhythm, serving as the “new‑people window” that balances existing ties with fresh connections.

## Practical Tips from Session 09 (2024‑06‑12)
- Choose a single weekday (e.g., Thursday) for a light social window: attend a casual class, visit a board‑game café, or work from a community coffee shop.
- If energy is low, shift to an even lighter version: a brief conversation with a coworker, a neighbor, or a quick message to someone you met previously.
- Use micro‑interactions as fallbacks—sending a funny photo or a quick check‑in keeps the momentum without demanding a full meet‑up.

## Example Weekly Rhythm
The document suggests a two‑window model: one anchored slot for close friends and one low‑pressure slot for meeting new people. The low‑pressure slot can be any activity that feels natural and requires minimal planning, such as a casual meetup or a coffee‑shop visit.
