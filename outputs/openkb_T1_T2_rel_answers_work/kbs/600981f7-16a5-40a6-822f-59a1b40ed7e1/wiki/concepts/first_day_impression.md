# First‑Day Impression

**Type:** Concept

## definition
A First‑Day Impression is the set of low‑key actions a new employee takes to demonstrate competence, reliability, and professionalism during the initial days on the job, while deliberately avoiding over‑exposure or perceived arrogance.

## key components (derived from [[summaries/session-31_2025-06-08]])
- **Quiet foundation building** – Get the laptop, email, and essential tools working before anyone notices. This includes setting up a password manager, enabling MFA, and applying system updates.
- **Security hygiene without theatrics** – Lock the screen, use encryption, and follow basic security practices, but do not broadcast every step.
- **Organised note‑taking** – Keep a simple notebook or digital note file for names, team norms, and process tips; refer to it privately rather than asking repetitive questions.
- **Targeted curiosity** – Ask one or two thoughtful questions in conversations rather than trying to absorb everything at once.
- **Minimal but visible presence** – Sign into communication platforms such as Microsoft Teams and Slack so teammates can see you are reachable, but refrain from customizing shared settings or creating complex email rules until you receive guidance.

## practical checklist (excerpt)
1. Install/configure a password manager and add work credentials. 
2. Enable MFA on all available services.
3. Apply required OS updates and set a reasonable screen‑lock timeout.
4. Verify email send/receive and set a simple signature.
5. Log into Teams and Slack for visibility.
6. Keep a private notes document for team names and processes.

## when to defer actions
- Complex email rules, VPN or network configuration changes, additional software installations, and any modifications that could affect shared resources should be postponed until after a conversation with the manager or IT.
