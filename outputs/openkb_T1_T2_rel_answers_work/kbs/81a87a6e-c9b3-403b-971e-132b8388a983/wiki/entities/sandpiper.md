# Sandpiper

Sandpipers are a diverse group of small to medium‑sized shorebirds that frequent marshes, mudflats, and other wetland habitats. In the context of the *session‑15_2024‑05‑08* discussion, sandpipers are used as a benchmark species for teaching reliable field marks and behavioral cues.

## Key Identification Points
- **Size**: Similar to a sparrow, often described as “sandpiper‑sized” in field notes.
- **Bill**: Thin, straight, and relatively short compared to herons; ideal for probing shallow water.
- **Legs**: Usually medium‑length; leg color can vary but often pale.
- **Behavior**: Rapid, repetitive pecks or a constant tail‑bob; tends to stay horizontal and move quickly across the substrate.
- **Field Marks**: Occasionally a pale eyebrow stripe; color patterns are subtle, making behavior and bill shape primary cues.

These traits are highlighted in the [[summaries/session-15_2024-05-08]] as part of the broader advice on [[concepts/bird_identification]] and the importance of recognizing [[concepts/field_marks_and_behavioral_cues]].

## Comparison with Similar Wetland Birds
- **Herons (e.g., [[entities/green_heron]] and [[entities/bittern]])**: Longer, dagger‑like bills; vertical posture; slower, deliberate stalking.
- **Other Shorebirds**: May share size but differ in bill length, leg proportion, or movement rhythm.

## Tools for Identification
- The app **Merlin Bird ID** (referenced as [[entities/merlin_bird_id]]) can filter sightings by habitat and include sandpipers among likely matches for wetland observations.

## Relevance to Wetland Birdwatching
Sandpipers exemplify the [[concepts/wetland_birdwatching]] practice of focusing on a few reliable cues—size, bill shape, leg length, and movement—to make quick, confident IDs, especially when birds are only briefly visible.
