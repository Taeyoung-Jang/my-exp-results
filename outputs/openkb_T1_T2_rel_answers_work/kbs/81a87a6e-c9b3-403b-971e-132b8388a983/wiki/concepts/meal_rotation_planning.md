# Meal Rotation Planning

**Definition** – A systematic approach to scheduling low‑effort, single‑pan meals over a recurring cycle, aimed at minimizing daily decision‑making, waste, and cooking complexity.

## Core Components (as outlined in [[summaries/session-34_2025-01-05]])

1. **Master Pantry & Freezer List**
   - Focuses on items that support many dishes, e.g., tortillas, canned beans, frozen mixed vegetables, and pre‑cooked proteins.  This aligns with the broader idea of [[concepts/low_effort_pantry_staples]].

2. **Two‑Week Rotation Schedule**
   - Provides a clear calendar of meals, limiting cooking to five dinners per week and reserving Saturday for a simple “heat‑and‑eat” option.  The schedule emphasizes single‑pan recipes, reinforcing [[concepts/single_pan_cooking]].

3. **Flavor Boosters**
   - Small additions (lemon, salsa, soy sauce, herbs) that keep the rotation from feeling repetitive without adding extra dishes.

4. **Waste‑Reduction Strategies**
   - Use whole cans as the main protein, buy mini‑cans or pouch‑sized beans, and freeze leftovers in portion‑sized bags to avoid half‑opened cans.

5. **Fridge‑Friendly Checklist**
   - A printable, stick‑on list that combines weekly shopping needs with the cooking plan, making the rotation easy to follow.

## Benefits
- **Decision Fatigue Reduction** – By pre‑defining meals, the user spends less mental energy choosing what to cook each night.
- **Time Efficiency** – All meals are designed to be ready in 10–15 minutes with minimal cleanup.
- **Cost Control** – Reusing pantry staples across multiple dishes keeps grocery spend low.
- **Variety without Extra Work** – Flavor boosters and rotating vegetable mixes provide perceived variety while keeping the process simple.

## Implementation Tips
- Review the master list monthly and restock any depleted items.
- Adjust the two‑week calendar to match personal taste preferences, swapping sauces or vegetable mixes as needed.
- Keep the checklist visible on the fridge to reinforce the routine.
