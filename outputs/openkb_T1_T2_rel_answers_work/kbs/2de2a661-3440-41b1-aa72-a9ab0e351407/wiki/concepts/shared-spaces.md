# Shared Spaces

**Shared Spaces** are household areas that multiple members use regularly—such as the kitchen, living‑room, and other common zones. Effective management of these spaces supports **[[concepts/household-norms]]**, reduces friction, and nurtures a sense of community across generations.

## Key Elements (from the Family Home Agreement)
- **Kitchen**: A "reset" routine where each person wipes counters, clears dishes, and returns items after use. The agreement includes a placeholder for grandparents’ preferred kitchen time, illustrating how shared‑space rules can be tailored to generational needs.
- **Living‑Room / TV**: First‑come, first‑serve access with an emphasis on early‑evening group viewing and low‑volume or headphone use later at night. This balances collective entertainment with individual privacy.
- **Noise Management**: Quiet‑hours windows (e.g., 9:30 p.m.–7:00 a.m. or 10:00 p.m.–8:00 a.m.) apply to all shared zones, ensuring early sleepers and night‑owls can coexist peacefully.
- **Guest Coordination**: Simple heads‑up etiquette for visitors in shared spaces helps everyone anticipate extra traffic and maintain order.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/conflict-resolution]]**: Clear shared‑space expectations provide a foundation for resolving disputes quickly and calmly.
- **[[concepts/household-norms]]**: Shared‑space guidelines are a core subset of overall household norms, defining how communal areas are used and respected.
