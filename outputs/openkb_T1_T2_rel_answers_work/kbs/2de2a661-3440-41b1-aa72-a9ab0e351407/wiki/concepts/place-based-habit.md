# Place‑Based Habit

**Definition**: A place‑based habit is a small, repeatable action performed in the same physical spot each day, creating a mental link between the location and the behavior.

---

## Core Idea

When you consistently do a task in a particular area—such as reading by a window, journaling at a favorite table, or making a morning drink in a cozy corner—your brain starts to associate that space with the habit. This spatial cue reinforces the routine, making the new environment feel more personal and home‑like.

---

## Key Details from [[summaries/session-34_2024-06-15]]

- **Morning anchor**: Make a warm drink and sit in a chosen spot for five minutes, breathing and noticing the view. The spot becomes a cue for starting the day.
- **Evening anchor**: Dim the lights, tidy a small surface, and breathe before bed, all performed in the same area (e.g., bedside table). This creates a consistent wind‑down cue.
- **Variations**: Low‑energy versions keep the location constant but simplify the actions, ensuring the habit persists even on low‑energy days.

---

## Benefits

- **Spatial memory**: Repeating actions in the same place strengthens memory pathways, making the habit easier to start.
- **Sense of ownership**: The space feels “yours” as it becomes linked to personal routines.
- **Ease of transition**: When moving to a new home, establishing place‑based habits accelerates the feeling of belonging.

## Practical Tips

1. **Pick a spot** you enjoy (a window seat, a favorite chair, a small table).
2. **Choose a simple action** (drink, breathe, tidy) that takes 5‑10 minutes.
3. **Do it daily** at the same time to reinforce the cue.
4. **Adjust gradually** – as the habit feels natural, you can expand or shift the location.
