# Household Norms

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A set of mutually‑agreed practices that guide how members of a household interact with shared spaces, schedule privacy, and resolve conflicts.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Shared‑Space Guidelines**
   - Define how common areas like the kitchen and living‑room are used, kept tidy, and accessed.  
   - Example from [[summaries/session-10_2022-06-15]]: a kitchen "reset" rule and first‑come‑first‑serve TV use.

2. **Privacy & Independence**
   - Establish quiet‑hour windows, personal‑zone signals (e.g., closed doors), and designated retreat spots for different age groups.

3. **Routine Check‑Ins**
   - Short, regular meetings to review what works, surface concerns, and tweak the norms.  
   - The family agreement includes a five‑minute weekly check‑in.

4. **Conflict‑Resolution Process**
   - A calm, step‑by‑step approach (pause, express feelings, listen, agree on a tweak).  
   - Linked to the broader [[concepts/conflict-resolution]] framework.

## Why Household Norms Matter
- **Balance**: They help balance the needs of early sleepers, night owls, grandparents, teens, and other members.
- **Predictability**: Clear expectations reduce misunderstandings and friction in multigenerational homes.
- **Flexibility**: Norms are intentionally lightweight, allowing families to adjust times and priorities without formal rule‑making.

## Practical Example
The "Family Home Agreement" created in [[summaries/session-10_2022-06-15]] operationalizes household norms through:
- Bullet‑point kitchen reset expectations.
- Living‑room TV sharing etiquette.
- Flexible quiet‑hour options (e.g., 9:30 pm–7:00 am or 10:00 pm–8:00 am).
- Simple heads‑up guest policy.
- A brief conflict‑handling checklist.

These elements illustrate how a concrete, printable document can capture the abstract idea of household norms and make it actionable for a specific family.
