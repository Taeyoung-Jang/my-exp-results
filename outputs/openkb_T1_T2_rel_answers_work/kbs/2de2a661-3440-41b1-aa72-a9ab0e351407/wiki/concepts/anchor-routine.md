## Anchor Routine

**Definition**: An Anchor Routine is a short, consistent set of actions performed at the beginning or end of the day to create mental framing, structure, and a sense of home, especially after a major living‑situation change.

### Core Characteristics
- **Duration**: Typically 5–10 minutes, keeping the habit low‑effort and sustainable.
- **Timing**: One routine anchors the **morning**, another the **evening**; they act as bookends for the day.
- **Repetition**: Performed daily to build automatic cue‑response loops.
- **Location‑Based**: Often tied to a specific spot in the home (e.g., a window seat or a bedside table) to reinforce a sense of place.
- **Flexibility**: Includes low‑energy variations that can be applied on days with reduced motivation.

### Example from [[summaries/session-34_2024-06-15]]
- **Morning Anchor** (5–10 min)
  1. Make a warm drink.  
  2. Sit in a chosen spot.  
  3. Take five slow breaths.  
  4. Notice the room or view for a minute.  
  5. Identify one task for the day.
- **Evening Anchor** (5–10 min)
  1. Dim the lights.  
  2. Play a soft song or switch to quiet mode.  
  3. Tidy one small surface for a minute.  
  4. Sit or lie down and take five slow breaths.  
  5. Put the phone away.
- **Low‑energy variations** (both morning and evening) strip the routine to the minimum essential actions, ensuring the habit remains doable.

### Benefits
- Provides **structure** in a new environment, reducing uncertainty.
- Enhances **psychological safety** by creating predictable cues.
- Encourages **personalization** of the space through repeated, meaningful interaction.
- Supports **habit formation** by keeping the routine short and consistent.
