# Anti‑Spiral Scripts

**What they are**

Anti‑Spiral Scripts are short, pre‑written statements people can quietly say to themselves when a triggering situation threatens to launch a spiral of negative thoughts or panic. The scripts act as a mental reset button, shifting attention from the distressing thought to a deliberate, calming cue.

**Why they matter**

- Provide an immediate, low‑effort way to break rumination.
- Can be used in high‑stress micro‑moments (e.g., at a security keypad or when entering a quiet, empty apartment).
- Complement other transition supports such as [[concepts/anchor-routines]] and the [[concepts/low-risk-practice-plan]] for code memory.

**Scripts from the source document**

1. **Keypad fallback** – when the security code pattern blanks:
   > "I pause, I reset, and the pattern comes back. One breath, then try again."
2. **Apartment entry fallback** – when the loneliness of a new place feels overwhelming:
   > "I’m here, I’m safe, and it’s okay that this feels strange. I’m allowed to take this one moment at a time."

Both scripts are deliberately short (one sentence), use present‑tense affirmative language, and include a breathing cue to re‑ground the nervous system.

**How to use them**

1. **Memorize** the exact phrasing during a calm period (e.g., while reviewing the [[concepts/code-pattern-memory]] rhythm).
2. **Practice silently** in low‑stakes moments (like while waiting for a coffee) to embed the phrasing.
3. **Deploy** the moment you notice a spike in anxiety – say the script quietly, inhale, and then proceed with the intended action.

**Connection to other concepts**

- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/anchor-routines]] to create a layered safety net for transitions.
- Supports the confidence‑building loop described in [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-16]].
- Can be integrated into a broader [[concepts/transition-support]] framework for people undergoing major life changes.

**Practical tip**

If a script feels stale after a few weeks, rewrite it in your own words while preserving the structure: a brief pause cue, a calming statement, and a breath reminder. This keeps the script feeling fresh and personal.
