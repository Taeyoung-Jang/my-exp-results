# Transition Support

**Definition**: A set of low‑effort practices that help people stabilize their mental state and build reliable habits during major life changes such as moving to a new home, starting a new job, or adjusting to new security procedures.

## Core Elements (as described in [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-16]])

1. **Anchor Routines** – Tiny, repeatable actions that create mental stability. The source document outlines three concrete routines that take under five minutes:
   - **Morning** – Open blinds or a light, sip water, take a slow breath.
   - **Walk‑in‑the‑Door (60‑second reset)** – Close the door, breathe, place keys + badge in a fixed spot, say a short mental cue, turn on a light or soft sound.
   - **Bedtime** – Dim lights, put the phone face‑down, perform a brief body check‑in.
   - Each routine includes a “rough‑day fallback” for when energy is low.
   
   These routines are an instance of [[concepts/anchor-routines]].

2. **Code‑Pattern Memory** – Techniques for remembering security codes without writing them down. The method focuses on rhythm, finger patterns, or mnemonic phrases that capture the *feel* of the code rather than the digits themselves. This aligns with [[concepts/code-pattern-memory]].

3. **Low‑Risk Practice Plan** – A 1–2 week schedule for rehearsing the code pattern discreetly (e.g., tapping on a thigh). The plan specifies frequency, duration, and what to avoid (no verbalizing digits, no typing the code outside the keypad). This is detailed under [[concepts/low-risk-practice-plan]].

4. **Anti‑Spiral Scripts** – Short self‑talk phrases to calm panic at the keypad or when the new apartment feels empty. Examples include:
   - At the keypad: “I pause, I reset, and the pattern comes back. One breath, then try again.”
   - Entering the apartment: “I’m here, I’m safe, and it’s okay that this feels strange. I’m allowed to take this one moment at a time.”
   
   These scripts are captured in [[concepts/anti-spiral-scripts]].

## How the Elements Work Together

- **Anchor routines** provide immediate grounding moments that reduce the likelihood of mental spiraling.
- **Anti‑spiral scripts** act as verbal anchors when emotional spikes occur, reinforcing the calm created by the routines.
- **Code‑pattern memory** and its **low‑risk practice plan** give a concrete, secure skill that further builds confidence in the new environment.
- Together, they form a holistic approach to transition support, enabling individuals to create structure, maintain security, and manage anxiety without adding cognitive load.

## Benefits

- Minimal time investment (each routine < 5 minutes).
- No reliance on external reminders (e.g., sticky notes), preserving security.
- Scalable: can be adapted to other life changes beyond moving.
- Supports mental health by providing clear, actionable steps during periods of uncertainty.
