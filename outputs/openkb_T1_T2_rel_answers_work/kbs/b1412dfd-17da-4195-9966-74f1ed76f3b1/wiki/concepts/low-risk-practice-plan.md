# Low‑Risk Practice Plan

**Overview**
A Low‑Risk Practice Plan is a short, structured rehearsal schedule that helps a person internalise a security‑code *pattern* (rhythm, finger motion, or mnemonic) while avoiding any exposure of the actual numbers. The plan is deliberately low‑effort, discreet, and designed to minimise security risk.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Frequency & Reps**
   - **Days 1‑4**: 5 quick rhythm reps each morning and afternoon (≈30 seconds total).  
   - **Days 5‑9**: 2 reps in the morning, 1 in the afternoon.
   - **Days 10‑14**: One brief rehearsal per day.
   - **After week 2**: Optional every‑other‑day reminder.

2. **Method of Rehearsal**
   - Tap the pattern on your thigh, leg, or the side of a chair – it looks like normal fidgeting.
   - Do **not** say the digits aloud, write them down, or type them anywhere except the real keypad.

3. **What to Avoid**
   - Repeating the actual numeric code verbally or in writing.
   - Creating a pattern that directly maps to the numbers (e.g., “1‑2‑3‑4” as “one‑two‑three‑four”).
   - Practising in a way that could be observed by others.

## Practical Example (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-16]])

> **Day 1‑4:** 5 taps of the 4‑digit rhythm “strong‑soft‑soft‑strong” on the thigh each morning and afternoon.  
> **Day 5‑9:** Reduce to 2 morning taps, 1 afternoon tap.  
> **Day 10‑14:** One tap session per day, then taper to every other day.

---

## Benefits

- **Security‑first**: No written or spoken digits, reducing leakage risk.
- **Discreet**: Looks like ordinary fidgeting, suitable for a desk or public space.
- **Low cognitive load**: Takes under a minute per session, fitting easily into daily flow.
- **Builds muscle memory**: Repetition of the *pattern* rather than the numbers makes recall under pressure more reliable.

---

## Implementation Checklist

- [ ] Choose a quiet, private spot (e.g., under the desk).
- [ ] Decide on the rhythm/finger pattern for your code.
- [ ] Set a reminder for the first 4 days (morning & afternoon).
- [ ] Track progress in a simple log (date, reps performed).
- [ ] After week 2, evaluate if the pattern feels automatic; if so, taper.
