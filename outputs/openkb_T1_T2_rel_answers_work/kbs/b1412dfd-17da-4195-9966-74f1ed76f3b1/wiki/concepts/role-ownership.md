# Role Ownership

**Definition**: A lightweight, visible mapping of who is responsible for which parts of a workflow, enabling the team to know immediately who owns a task at any stage.

## Core Elements
- **Strength‑based mapping** – Quickly identify each team member’s natural strengths and assign primary responsibilities accordingly. This can be a simple list or a column on an existing board (e.g., Trello).
- **Shared view** – Keep the ownership map in a place everyone checks daily, such as the team’s Trello board or a dedicated Slack channel pinned message, so it remains top‑of‑mind without extra meetings.
- **Dynamic fallback** – The map acts as a “backup language” when the usual tacit flow breaks (e.g., a member is out or a new person joins).

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/definition-of-done]] by tying each owner to a clear “done” checklist for their stage.
- Complements the [[concepts/handoff-signal]]; once the owner marks their step as done, the agreed signal (card move, tag, or Slack note) cues the next owner.

## Practical Steps (from [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-16]])
1. **Rapid team chat** – Spend a few minutes listing each person’s strengths and noting them on the board.
2. **Create a visible role map** – Add a “Owner” label to each Trello column or a pinned message in [[entities/slack]] that lists who handles each stage.
3. **Pair with DoD** – Attach the one‑sentence definition of done to each card, so the current owner knows exactly when to hand off.
4. **Standardized signal** – Agree that moving the card and tagging the next owner (or posting the template Slack message) is the universal cue.

## Benefits
- Reduces ambiguity when work spikes or personnel change.
- Eliminates the need for additional meetings; the ownership information lives where the work already is.
- Supports onboarding and temporary coverage by providing an at‑a‑glance reference.
