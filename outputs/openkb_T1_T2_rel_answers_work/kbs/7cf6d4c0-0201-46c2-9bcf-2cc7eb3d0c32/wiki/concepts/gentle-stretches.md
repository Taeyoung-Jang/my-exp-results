# Gentle Stretches

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Gentle seated movements that maintain spinal neutrality and reduce tension.

---

## Overview
Gentle stretches are low‑impact, mostly seated motions designed to relieve stiffness and improve circulation without stressing the lower back. They are especially useful for people who experience chronic lumbar discomfort, cannot lift heavy objects, or find prolonged standing painful.

## Key Characteristics
- **Seated or minimal‑standing:** Performed while remaining at the workstation, reducing the need to leave the desk.
- **Spinal neutrality:** Movements keep the natural lumbar curve intact; no deep forward folds or aggressive twists.
- **Short duration:** Typically 30–60 seconds, fitting within a micro‑break.
- **Low intensity:** No external resistance or heavy loading; focus is on mobility and breath.

## Core Gentle Stretches (from [[summaries/session-01_2024-06-15]])
1. **Neck turns** – Slowly rotate the head left‑right, keeping the chin level. Helps release upper‑back and neck tension.
2. **Shoulder rolls** – Lift shoulders toward ears, roll them back and down. Promotes shoulder‑blade mobility.
3. **Seated leg extension** – While seated, straighten one leg a few inches, then lower. Alternates legs to wake hips and hamstrings without bending forward.
4. **Torso swivel (small)** – While seated upright, gently rotate the torso left and right a few degrees, keeping the spine neutral.
5. **Rib‑cage lift (chest opening)** – Sit tall, inhale expanding the ribcage upward, optionally reach arms overhead while the chair supports the back.
6. **Belly‑to‑rib breathing** – Place hands on the lower abdomen, breathe deeply to expand the ribs without arching the lower back.

## How They Fit Into a Routine
Gentle stretches are a primary component of the **[[concepts/micro-breaks]]** approach and are embedded within the **[[concepts/full-day-schedule]]** for a 9 am‑5 pm workday. Each micro‑break can feature one or two of the movements above, ensuring regular variation and preventing over‑use of any single joint.

## Safety & Modifications
- **Avoid deep forward bends** (e.g., classic toe‑touches) as they round the spine and may aggravate lumbar pain.
- **Stop immediately** if any of the [[concepts/lower-back-red-flags]] appear (sharp pain, tingling, catching).
- If standing feels uncomfortable, keep the movement seated or use a stable chair back for support.
- Reduce range of motion or duration if a stretch feels tight; the goal is a gentle, pain‑free reset.
