## Full‑Day Micro‑break Schedule

A **Full‑Day Micro‑break Schedule** is a repeatable, desk‑centric timetable that inserts brief (30‑60 second) **[[concepts/micro-breaks]]** throughout a typical 9 am – 5 pm workday. The goal is to counteract static postures, maintain mobility, and avoid movements that trigger lower‑back discomfort.

### Core Structure
- **Interval:** Every 30 minutes, a micro‑break of 30–60 seconds.
- **Duration:** Two slightly longer seated breaks of 2–3 minutes (mid‑day and late‑afternoon) for deeper breathing and gentle chest opening.
- **Location:** Primarily seated at the workstation; standing is optional for a few seconds only if tolerated.

### Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/gentle-stretches]]** provide the specific movements used during each micro‑break, ensuring spinal neutrality and low impact on the lumbar region.
- **[[concepts/lower-back-red-flags]]** define the sensations that signal a break should be stopped or modified (sharp pain, tingling, catching, sustained tightening).
- **[[concepts/desk-ergonomics]]** underpins the schedule by minimizing baseline strain, making the micro‑breaks more effective.
- The overall pattern aligns with the broader **[[concepts/full-day-schedule]]** idea of structuring work time for health, but focuses specifically on very short, frequent resets suitable for chronic lower‑back pain.

### Implementation Tips
1. **Set a timer** (e.g., smartphone or desktop app) to cue each break.
2. **Keep movements seated** unless standing feels comfortable; even a brief weight shift counts.
3. **Monitor red‑flag cues** from [[concepts/lower-back-red-flags]]; if they appear, abort the movement and return to neutral breathing.
4. **Adjust timings** to fit personal workflow; the 30‑minute cadence is a baseline that can be shortened to 20 minutes if needed.
5. **Pair with ergonomic setup** (see [[concepts/desk-ergonomics]]) to reduce the overall load on the lumbar spine.

The Full‑Day Micro‑break Schedule offers a low‑effort, evidence‑informed framework for maintaining comfort and function throughout a typical office day while respecting the limitations of chronic lower‑back pain.
