# Lower‑Back Red Flags

**Definition**: Specific sensations or reactions that indicate a movement, stretch, or posture is likely to worsen lower‑back discomfort and should trigger an immediate stop or modification.

---

## Common Red‑Flag Signals
| Red‑Flag | Description |
|---|---|
| **Sharp, stabbing pain** | Sudden, localized pain that feels like a jab rather than a gentle stretch. |
| **Catching or locking** | The back feels "stuck" or a movement is abruptly halted by the body. |
| **Tingling or numbness down the leg** | Radiating sensations that suggest nerve irritation. |
| **Persistent tightening** | Muscle tension that remains for more than about 10 seconds after the movement. |
| **Worsening discomfort** | Any feeling that intensifies rather than eases with the activity. |

If any of these occur, **stop the activity immediately**, reduce range or intensity, and consider a simple breathing pause before resuming any work.

## Practical Use
1. **During a micro‑break**: Perform the recommended movement, then quickly assess for any red‑flag sensations.
2. **If a red flag appears**: Stop the movement, return to a neutral seated posture, and breathe.
3. **Modify**: Reduce range, lower intensity, or substitute a gentler alternative from [[concepts/gentle-stretches]].
4. **Track**: Note which movements consistently trigger flags and discuss them with a clinician or physical therapist.

---

By integrating these red‑flag cues, users can maintain the benefits of a structured desk routine while minimizing the risk of aggravating chronic lower‑back pain.
