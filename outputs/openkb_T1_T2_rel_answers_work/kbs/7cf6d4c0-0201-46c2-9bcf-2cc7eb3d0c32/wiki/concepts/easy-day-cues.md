# Easy-Day Cues

**Definition**: Practical signals a runner uses during a workout to verify that the effort stays in the “easy” zone, supporting consistency and reducing injury risk.

## Core Cues from [[summaries/session-40_2025-06-15]]
- **Talk Test** – You should be able to speak in full sentences without gasping. If conversation becomes strained, back off.
- **Heart‑Rate Zone** – Keep the pulse in a comfortable, controlled range (typically 60‑70% of max for many runners). The exact number can be personalized, but the goal is a non‑urgent feel.
- **Pace Caps** – Treat any prescribed pace as a ceiling, not a target. Running slower than the cap is fine; exceeding it means the run is no longer “easy.”
- **Perceived Exertion** – On the Borg scale, aim for a 2–3 (easy) rather than 4–5 (moderate).

## Why Cues Matter
Using these cues helps:
- Maintain **[[concepts/consistent-running]]** by making the workout feel sustainable.
- Detect early signs of potential injury, complementing the broader **[[concepts/post-half-marathon-recovery]]** strategy.
- Build confidence that each session is a step toward a long‑term habit rather than a high‑stakes performance.

## Applying Cues in a Weekly Plan
| Day | Cue Focus |
|-----|-----------|
| Monday, Wednesday, optional Friday | Talk test and perceived exertion during 30‑40 min easy runs |
| Sunday (long run) | Heart‑rate zone and ability to hold a conversation for 60‑80 min |

If any cue signals you are out of the easy zone (e.g., you start to stumble over words), reduce pace or shorten the run.
