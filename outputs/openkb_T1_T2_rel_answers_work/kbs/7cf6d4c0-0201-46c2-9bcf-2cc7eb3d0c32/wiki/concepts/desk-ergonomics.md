# Desk Ergonomics

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Practical workstation adjustments that help maintain a neutral spine, lessen lower‑back load, and complement micro‑break routines.

---

## Core Principles
1. **Lumbar Support** – Use a chair with built‑in lumbar curvature or add a cushion so the natural inward curve of the lower spine is preserved.
2. **Foot Position** – Keep feet flat on the floor or on a footrest; hips should be at or slightly above knee level to avoid excessive lumbar flexion.
3. **Keyboard & Mouse Placement** – Position the keyboard close enough that elbows stay near the body (~90° angle) and forearms remain parallel to the floor. The mouse should sit on the same surface to prevent reaching.
4. **Monitor Height** – The top of the screen should be at eye level, eliminating a forward‑head posture and neck flexion.
5. **Desk Height** – Ensure the desk allows forearms to rest comfortably without elevating shoulders; a slight desk tilt can help keep wrists neutral.
6. **Micro‑Adjustments** – Periodically recline the chair slightly, tilt the seat forward, or shift weight to vary spinal loading throughout the day.

## Practical Checklist
- **Chair:** lumbar cushion, adjustable height, backrest supports the spine.
- **Feet:** flat on ground or footrest; knees at ~90°.
- **Keyboard:** within arm’s reach, wrists straight, elbows close to torso.
- **Monitor:** top edge at eye level, distance ~arm’s length.
- **Desk:** height allows shoulders relaxed; consider a sit‑stand desk for occasional standing if tolerated.
- **Review:** after each week, note any discomfort and tweak one element at a time.
