# Political Engagement Framework

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** A step‑by‑step approach for aligning personal values with concrete local political actions.

---

## Overview
The *Political Engagement Framework* is a practical methodology that helps individuals who feel politically adrift to:
1. **Clarify core values** through reflective exercises.
2. **Translate those values** into actionable decisions such as voting in primaries and choosing volunteer opportunities.
3. **Select two complementary organizations** – a “home‑base” partisan group and an issue‑specific group – using clear effectiveness criteria.
4. **Manage internal disagreements** without falling into purity‑test traps.

The framework was distilled from the dialogue in [[summaries/session-33_2024-06-15]], where the assistant provided concrete questions, checklists, and attitudes for sustainable civic participation.

---

## 1. Values Clarification
- **Reflect on lived experiences**: Identify a personal situation (e.g., housing, work) and ask what you would change and why.
- **Extract underlying values** such as fairness, stability, freedom, responsibility.
- **Assess trade‑offs**: For a policy you like, determine the costs you’re willing to tolerate. This reveals priority hierarchy.

> This step aligns with the broader idea of [[concepts/values_based_orientation]].

---

## 2. From Values to Practical Choices
### Voting & Primaries
- Focus on **stakes**: Does the race affect your prioritized issues?
- If not, choose the candidate whose overall philosophy best matches your core values rather than seeking perfect issue alignment.

### Volunteering & Community Work
- **Two‑track approach**:
  - **Home‑base org** (e.g., [[entities/local_democratic_committee]]) for political updates, campaign coordination, voter outreach.
  - **Issue‑specific org** (e.g., [[entities/tenants_union]], [[entities/environmental_coalition]], [[entities/mutual_aid_group]]) that tackles a concrete problem you care about.
- Prioritize groups that demonstrate **tangible outcomes** over purely rhetorical activity.

> Effectiveness assessment draws on [[concepts/organizational_effectiveness]].

---

## 3. Selecting Organizations – A Simple Framework
| Criterion | Home‑Base Org | Issue‑Based Org |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Primary purpose | Provide political intel, coordinate campaigns, turn out voters. | Directly address a community issue you value. |
| Intro‑meeting questions | How are decisions made? How do volunteers contribute to measurable outcomes? Recent successes? | How is impact measured? Concrete wins last year? Community collaboration? |
| Effectiveness signals | Clear tasks, transparent metrics, documented wins. | Specific goals, progress reports, community partnerships. |
| Red flags | Vague “vibes”, branding over action, no clear outcomes. | Over‑reliance on awareness‑only tactics, lack of measurable milestones. |

---

## 4. Managing Disagreements
- **Reframe** conflicts as opportunities for improvement, not attacks.
- Use **curiosity‑driven questions** (“Why was this decision made?”, “What alternatives exist?”).
- Offer suggestions without insisting; be ready to let some debates go.
- Maintain respectful, consistent engagement to avoid the pitfalls described in [[concepts/avoidance_of_purity_tests]].

---

## 5. Checklist for Ongoing Engagement
1. Reflect on a personal issue → identify core value.
2. Write down acceptable trade‑offs.
3. Apply this hierarchy when voting in primaries.
4. Choose one home‑base org and one issue org using the framework.
5. Evaluate effectiveness after the first meeting (clear tasks + measurable impact).
6. Handle disagreements with curiosity and humility.
