# Moving Checklist

**Concept type:** Concept

## Overview
A Moving Checklist is a phased, time‑boxed plan that helps people organise the essential tasks that arise after relocating to a new residence. It groups actions by priority and typical deadlines, ensuring legal, financial and everyday responsibilities are addressed promptly while reducing the chance of forgotten items.

## Core Phases (as described in [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-15]])

### Week 1 – Identity & Address
- Submit a change‑of‑address form with **[[entities/usps]]**.
- Update address with employer, bank, insurance providers, doctors and any other institutions that send important mail.
- Change the address on your driver’s license or state ID (usually an in‑person visit to the **[[entities/department_of_motor_vehicles]]**; see [[concepts/address_update_requirements]] for typical state windows).
- Register to vote in the new precinct (often a quick online or mail‑in step, but state deadlines vary).

### Week 2 – Utilities & Subscriptions
- Transfer utilities (electric, gas, water, internet) to your name and close accounts at the old address.
- Update recurring delivery services, online shopping accounts, streaming subscriptions, and any other subscriptions that rely on a physical address.

### Week 3 – Vehicle Documentation
- Update vehicle registration and, if moving across state lines, transfer the title. This falls under [[concepts/vehicle_documentation]].
- Arrange any required emissions or safety inspections.
- Notify your auto insurer of the new address; most insurers allow this online or by phone.

### Week 4 – Overlooked Items
- Update payroll/tax address with your employer.
- Register the address change with pet microchip registries, emergency contacts, school records (if you have children), and local memberships such as libraries or gyms.
- Explore the new neighbourhood for primary‑care doctors, pharmacies, grocery stores and other everyday services.

## Required Documents (to gather before starting the checklist)
1. Driver’s license or state ID
2. Passport (optional but useful)
3. Lease agreement or home purchase paperwork (proof of residence)
4. Recent utility bill (proof of address)
5. Vehicle title and current registration
6. Auto‑insurance card or proof of coverage
7. Social Security number (for DMV forms)
8. Lender information if the vehicle is financed
