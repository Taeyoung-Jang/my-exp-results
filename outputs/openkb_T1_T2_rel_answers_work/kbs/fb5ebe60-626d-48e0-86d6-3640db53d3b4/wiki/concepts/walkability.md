# Walkability

## Definition
Walkability refers to how easily residents can travel on foot to essential destinations—work, shops, leisure, and transit—within a safe, pleasant, and time‑efficient environment. High walkability reduces the need for a car, encourages spontaneous social interactions, and underpins a sustainable urban rhythm.

## Why Walkability Matters for City Suitability
- **Supports [[concepts/car_light_living]]**: When daily errands are reachable on foot or by bike, residents can adopt a low‑car lifestyle.
- **Enhances [[concepts/transit]] integration**: Walkable streets provide the first‑mile/last‑mile link to public transport hubs.
- **Improves [[concepts/social_routine]]**: Proximity to cafés, bars, gyms, and parks makes casual weeknight meet‑ups more spontaneous.
- **Buffers [[concepts/weather]] impacts**: In milder climates, walking feels comfortable year‑round, reinforcing routine stability.
- **Contributes to [[concepts/city_suitability]] assessments**: Planners often rank walkability alongside transit, affordability, and climate when recommending cities for newcomers.
