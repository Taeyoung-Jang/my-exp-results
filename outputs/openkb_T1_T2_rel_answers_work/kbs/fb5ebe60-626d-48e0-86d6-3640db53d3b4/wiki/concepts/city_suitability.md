# City Suitability

**Definition**
City Suitability is a framework for assessing how readily a city supports a smooth transition for newcomers, enabling them to establish daily routines, social connections, and a car‑light lifestyle.

**Core Criteria**
| Criterion | Why it matters | Example from session‑06 |
|-----------|----------------|------------------------|
| [[concepts/walkability]] | Dense, pedestrian‑friendly streets let people run errands and meet others without a car. | Neighborhoods like Nørrebro, De Pijp, and the Pearl District are highlighted for their walkable cores. |
| [[concepts/transit]] | Reliable public transport reduces planning friction and expands the area you can comfortably access. | Vancouver’s core transit, Copenhagen’s bike‑first network, and Amsterdam’s metro/bus system are cited. |
| [[concepts/weather]] | Predictable, mild climates help maintain consistent habits and avoid climate shock. | Cities with cool, moderate weather (Copenhagen, Amsterdam, Portland) are favored over hot, humid locales. |
| [[concepts/car_light_living]] | Ability to live without a car streamlines daily logistics and encourages spontaneous outings. | Suggested neighborhoods (Mount Pleasant, Yaletown, Inner Southeast Portland) all offer grocery, work, and leisure within walking or biking distance. |
| [[concepts/social_routine]] | A culture that welcomes newcomers and offers low‑stakes evening activities accelerates friendship formation. | Weeknight routines like brewery visits, fitness classes, or café meet‑ups are described for each city. |
| [[concepts/third_place]] | Regular “third places” (cafés, bars, gyms) act as informal hubs for meeting locals. | Coffee & Coconuts is recommended as a repeat spot in Amsterdam. |
| [[concepts/weeknight_activities]] | Availability of casual, short‑duration evening options supports spontaneous socializing. | Activities such as canal walks, terrace drinks, or climbing gym sessions are highlighted. |

**Application in the Source Document**
The session‑06 discussion uses these criteria to compare cities for a Seattle‑based user. It narrows down recommendations to Copenhagen, Amsterdam, Vancouver, and Portland, then selects Amsterdam as the top choice because it scores highly across all criteria, especially [[concepts/walkability]], [[concepts/transit]], and the presence of abundant [[concepts/third_place]] options.

**Using the Framework**
1. **Identify personal priorities** (e.g., climate tolerance, transit preference).  
2. **Score candidate cities** on each criterion.  
3. **Select neighborhoods** that maximize [[concepts/car_light_living]] and access to [[concepts/third_place]] venues.  
4. **Plan a 30‑day integration strategy** leveraging [[concepts/initial_integration_strategy]] (repeat activities, language classes, etc.) to quickly build a [[concepts/social_routine]].
