# Refractive Surgery

**Type:** other  
**Description:** Surgical procedures that reshape the cornea to correct refractive errors.

## Overview
Refractive surgery refers to a family of ophthalmic procedures—most commonly LASIK and PRK—that alter corneal curvature to reduce or eliminate the need for glasses or contact lenses. It is presented in [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]] as one of the main [[concepts/vision-correction-options]] alongside glasses and contacts.

## Key Points from Session 37
- Intended for people who want decreased dependence on corrective lenses.
- Not a permanent guarantee; many patients still require glasses for certain tasks as they age.
- Candidacy depends on corneal thickness, overall eye health, and stability of the prescription.
- Post‑operative monitoring is essential: annual comprehensive exams, with earlier visits if new symptoms (glare, halos, visual fluctuations) appear.

## Typical Post‑Surgical Follow‑Up
- **Annual comprehensive exam** includes visual acuity, intra‑ocular pressure check ([[entities/intraocular-pressure-test]]) and a dilated retinal assessment ([[entities/dilated-retinal-exam]]).
- **Corneal topography** may be performed every 1–2 years to verify corneal stability ([[entities/corneal-topography]]).
- **Dry‑eye evaluation** is common, especially after LASIK/PRK, and may involve testing for [[entities/dry-eye]].
