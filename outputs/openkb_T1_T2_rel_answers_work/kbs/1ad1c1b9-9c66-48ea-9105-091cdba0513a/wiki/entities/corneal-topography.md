## Corneal Topography

Corneal topography is a diagnostic imaging method that captures a detailed map of the cornea’s surface curvature and shape. It is commonly used to assess the outcome of **refractive surgery** such as [[entities/lasik]] and [[entities/prk]], and to monitor corneal health over time.

### Role in Post‑Surgical Care
- The technique helps eye care professionals detect subtle changes in corneal shape that might affect visual acuity after surgery.  
- In the context of the discussion from [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]], a **corneal topography scan** is suggested **every 1‑2 years** for patients who have undergone LASIK or PRK, unless earlier monitoring is warranted by symptoms.
- Topography data can guide decisions about additional interventions, prescription updates, or the need for further dry‑eye evaluation.

### Connection to Vision‑Correction Options
- By providing an objective measurement of corneal geometry, topography informs the choice among **vision‑correction options** like glasses, contact lenses, or additional surgical procedures.  
- It is part of a broader **post‑surgical eye care** protocol that also includes routine visual acuity checks, intra‑ocular pressure testing, and retinal examinations.

### Related Entities & Concepts
- [[entities/dry-eye]] – often evaluated alongside topography when patients report irritation after surgery.
- [[entities/dilated-retinal-exam]] – another standard component of the yearly comprehensive eye exam.
- [[concepts/post-surgical-eye-care]] – overarching concept that includes topography as a monitoring tool.
- [[concepts/vision-correction-options]] – the broader category of choices that topography helps to assess.

### Practical Takeaways
- Ask your eye doctor whether a corneal topography scan is appropriate at your next check‑up, especially if you notice changes in night vision, glare, or overall sharpness.
- Regular topography, combined with other exams, supports early detection of issues and helps maintain long‑term visual health.
