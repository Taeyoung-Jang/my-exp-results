# Dilated Retinal Examination

A **dilated retinal examination** is a routine part of a comprehensive eye check‑up in which eye‑dropping agents are used to widen the pupils, allowing the clinician to inspect the retina, optic nerve, and macula for signs of disease or post‑surgical changes.

## Role in Post‑Surgical Care
- Listed as a default component of the **annual comprehensive exam** recommended after refractive procedures such as LASIK or PRK. [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]]
- Provides early detection of retinal issues that may manifest as new **glare**, halos, floaters, or flashes – symptoms that should prompt an earlier visit rather than waiting for the yearly schedule.
- Often performed alongside other standard tests, including **intra‑ocular pressure measurement** and a **corneal health assessment**. [[entities/intraocular-pressure-test]]

## Interaction with Other Evaluations
- If the clinician suspects corneal irregularities, a **corneal topography** scan may be ordered in the same visit. [[entities/corneal-topography]]
- Patients reporting dry‑eye symptoms may also receive a **dry‑eye** work‑up during the same appointment. [[entities/dry-eye]]

## Practical Guidance for Patients
1. **When to seek a sooner exam** – persistent night‑time glare/halos lasting more than 1‑2 weeks, sudden floaters, or any rapid change in vision should trigger an earlier dilated exam.
2. **What to mention at the appointment** – any new glare, halos, dry‑eye discomfort, or changes in visual acuity; ask if a topography scan is warranted this year.
3. **What the exam entails** – pupil dilation, retinal imaging (e.g., fundus photography or OCT), intra‑ocular pressure check, and a quick corneal surface review.
