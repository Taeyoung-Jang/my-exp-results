# PRK (Photorefractive Keratectomy)

Photorefractive Keratectomy (PRK) is a **refractive surgery** that uses an excimer laser to remove the surface layer of the cornea and reshape the underlying tissue, thereby correcting nearsightedness, farsightedness, or astigmatism. Unlike LASIK, PRK does **not** create a corneal flap; the epithelium regenerates over several days, resulting in a longer healing period but eliminating flap‑related complications.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]]
- **Post‑surgical eye care**: After PRK, patients are advised to maintain **annual comprehensive eye exams**. These visits typically include vision testing, intra‑ocular pressure checks, and a **dilated retinal exam** to monitor overall eye health. 
- **Corneal monitoring**: A **corneal topography** scan may be recommended every 1‑2 years to track corneal stability and detect any regression.
- **Dry‑eye management**: PRK can temporarily exacerbate dry‑eye symptoms; clinicians often evaluate the **dry‑eye** status during follow‑ups and may prescribe lubricants or other treatments.
- **Vision‑correction options**: PRK is listed alongside glasses, contact lenses, and LASIK under the broader category of [[concepts/vision-correction-options]].
- **Long‑term considerations**: Even after successful PRK, patients should watch for night‑time glare or halos and seek care promptly if these persist beyond a couple of weeks.

## Typical Follow‑Up Schedule
1. **First week** – frequent check‑ins for epithelial healing and comfort.
2. **First month** – assess visual acuity and early signs of regression.
3. **6‑month mark** – optional **corneal topography** and dry‑eye evaluation.
4. **Annual thereafter** – comprehensive exam including **dilated retinal exam**, intra‑ocular pressure test, and discussion of any night‑vision issues.
