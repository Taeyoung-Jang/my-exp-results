# LASIK (Laser‑Assisted In Situ Keratomileusis)

LASIK is a **refractive‑surgery** procedure that uses an excimer laser to reshape the corneal stroma, thereby correcting common refractive errors such as myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism. It is listed under the broader category of [[concepts/vision-correction-options]] and is often considered when patients want to reduce reliance on glasses or contact lenses.

## Key Points from Session 37 (2025‑06‑15)
- **Candidate suitability**: Not everyone qualifies; candidates must have sufficient corneal thickness and stable vision, and must be free of certain ocular diseases.
- **Long‑term expectations**: Even after a successful LASIK procedure, patients may still need glasses for specific tasks (e.g., reading or night driving) as they age. Regular monitoring is essential.
- **Post‑operative care**: The session emphasizes **annual comprehensive eye exams** after the initial healing period, with a focus on:
  - Checking corneal health, optionally via **corneal topography** (see [[entities/corneal-topography]]) every 1–2 years.
  - Assessing tear‑film quality to manage **dry‑eye** symptoms (see [[entities/dry-eye]]).
  - Performing a **dilated retinal exam** (see [[entities/dilated-retinal-exam]]) as part of the yearly visit.
- **Warning signs**: New or worsening glare, halos, flashes, or floaters should prompt an earlier visit rather than waiting for the annual check‑up.
- **Follow‑up schedule**: 
  - Default: once per year after the early post‑op window.
  - Earlier review (within weeks‑months) if symptoms such as blur, irritation, or night‑vision changes appear.

## Relationship to Other Entities & Concepts
- LASIK is one of several procedures under the umbrella of [[entities/refractive-surgery]], alongside [[entities/prk]].
- The need for **intra‑ocular pressure testing** (see [[entities/intraocular-pressure-test]]) may be part of the routine exam to screen for glaucoma.
- Post‑operative guidance aligns with [[concepts/post-surgical-eye-care]], highlighting lifestyle habits (e.g., screen ergonomics) that support visual health.
