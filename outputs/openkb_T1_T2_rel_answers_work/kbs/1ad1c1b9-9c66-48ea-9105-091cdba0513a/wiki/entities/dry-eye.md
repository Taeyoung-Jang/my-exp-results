# Dry Eye

Dry Eye is a common ocular condition where the tear film is inadequate in quantity or quality, leading to symptoms such as burning, itching, blurred vision, and increased sensitivity to light. It can be triggered or worsened by factors like prolonged screen use, contact lens wear, and environmental conditions.

## Relevance in Session 37

In the conversation summarized in [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]], Dry Eye is highlighted as a key consideration for long‑term eye health after refractive procedures such as [[entities/lasik]] or [[entities/prk]]. The assistant advises patients to mention any dryness symptoms during their annual comprehensive exam so that a tear‑film assessment can be performed. This evaluation is part of the broader [[concepts/post-surgical-eye-care]] routine.

## Connection to Vision‑Correction Options

- **Glasses**: Generally low impact on tear film, but may still require lubricating drops if the wearer experiences dryness.
- **Contact lenses**: Frequently exacerbate Dry Eye because lenses can disrupt the natural tear layer and increase evaporation.
- **Refractive surgery**: Procedures like [[entities/lasik]] and [[entities/prk]] can temporarily reduce corneal sensitivity, affecting tear production. Ongoing monitoring for Dry Eye is therefore recommended.

## Management Strategies Mentioned

- Regular use of artificial tears or lubricating eye drops.
- Implementing screen‑work ergonomics such as the 20‑20‑20 rule and conscious blinking, linking to [[concepts/eye-strain-mitigation]] and [[concepts/monitor-ergonomics]].
- Maintaining a humid environment and protecting eyes from wind or air conditioning.
- Scheduling routine follow‑ups where clinicians may perform a tear‑film evaluation alongside other tests like [[entities/corneal-topography]] and [[entities/dilated-retinal-exam]].

## When to Seek Care

Patients should seek prompt evaluation if they notice persistent dryness, fluctuating vision, or new night‑time glare/halos lasting more than one to two weeks, as these can signal worsening Dry Eye or other post‑surgical complications.
