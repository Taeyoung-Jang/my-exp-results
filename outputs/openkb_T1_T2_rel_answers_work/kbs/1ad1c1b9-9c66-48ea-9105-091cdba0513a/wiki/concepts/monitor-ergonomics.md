## Monitor Ergonomics

Monitor ergonomics refers to the optimal positioning and configuration of a computer display to promote visual comfort, minimize eye strain, and support healthy posture during prolonged screen use.

### Key Principles (from [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-15]])
- **Viewing distance**: Position the monitor about an arm’s length away (approximately 50–70 cm). This distance balances focus effort and readability.
- **Vertical placement**: The top edge of the screen should sit at or just below eye level. This encourages a slight downward gaze, reducing neck flexion and eye accommodation.
- **Adjustments for progressive lenses**: Users wearing progressive or multifocal glasses often tilt their heads back because the screen sits too high for the middle of the lens. Lower the monitor a few inches so the eyes look into the central lens zone rather than the upper segment.
- **Fine‑tuning distance**: While arm‑length is a solid baseline, you may move the monitor slightly closer or farther until text appears clear without leaning forward.
- **Complementary ergonomics**: Pair proper monitor placement with appropriate font sizing (large enough to read comfortably) and ambient lighting to avoid glare and contrast issues.

### Updated Guidance from [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]]
- **Regular breaks**: Incorporate the 20‑20‑20 rule—every 20 minutes, look at something ≥20 feet away for about 20 seconds—to give the eyes a rest and reduce fatigue.
- **Blink awareness**: Encourage conscious blinking during screen work to maintain a stable tear film and lessen dry‑eye discomfort.
- **Screen angle**: Slightly tilt the monitor backward (about 10–20°) to minimize reflections and help maintain a comfortable viewing angle, especially in bright environments.
- **Lighting conditions**: Keep ambient lighting moderate and avoid direct glare on the screen; use diffused side lighting if needed.
- **Ergonomic checklist**: At each workstation setup, verify distance, height, tilt, lighting, font size, and blinking habits to ensure a holistic approach to eye‑strain mitigation.

### Interaction with Related Concepts
- **[[concepts/eye-strain-mitigation]]**: Proper monitor ergonomics is a core component of broader eye‑strain mitigation strategies, alongside regular breaks, adequate blinking, and balanced lighting.
- **[[concepts/corrective-screen-eyewear]]**: When corrective lenses (especially progressives) are used, ergonomics must be adjusted so the screen aligns with the lens’s optimal viewing zone, reducing the need for head tilt.

### Practical Tips
1. Measure an arm’s length from your eyes to the screen; adjust the monitor arm or stand accordingly.
2. Use a monitor riser or adjustable stand to set the top edge just below your eye line; for progressive lens users, aim for 2–3 inches lower than the standard position.
3. Slightly tilt the monitor backward (10–20°) to reduce glare and improve comfort.
4. Verify that the screen is centered horizontally to avoid neck rotation.
5. Combine ergonomic placement with a readable font size, the 20‑20‑20 break rule, conscious blinking, and diffused side lighting to complete a comfortable workstation.

By adhering to these guidelines, users can create a workstation that supports visual health and overall comfort throughout the workday.
