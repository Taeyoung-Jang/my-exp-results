# Post‑Surgical Eye Care

**Scope** – This concept captures the recommended long‑term care regimen for patients who have undergone refractive procedures such as [[entities/lasik]], [[entities/prk]] or other forms of [[entities/refractive-surgery]]. It draws primarily from the discussion in [[summaries/session-37_2025-06-15]].

## 1. Follow‑up Schedule
- **Annual comprehensive exam** becomes the default once the immediate post‑op period (first 3‑6 months) is complete.
- **Accelerated visits** (within weeks‑months) are advised if any of the following arise:
  - New or worsening night‑time glare/halos
  - Persistent dryness, irritation, or fluctuating vision
  - Sudden visual loss, flashes, or new floaters
- Patients with a history of dry eye or high‑level corrections may shift to a **semi‑annual** schedule.

## 2. Core Examination Components
| Test | Typical Frequency | Why it matters |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| Visual acuity & refraction | Yearly (or sooner if symptoms) | Detect residual or shifting refractive error |
| [[entities/intraocular-pressure-test]] | Yearly | Screen for glaucoma, which can develop with age |
| Corneal health assessment (including optional [[entities/corneal-topography]]) | Every 1‑2 years, or as clinician advises | Monitor corneal stability after surgery |
| Tear film evaluation (dry‑eye work‑up) | At each visit if symptoms reported; otherwise yearly | Identify and manage dry‑eye, a common post‑op issue |
| [[entities/dilated-retinal-exam]] | Yearly (standard part of comprehensive exam) | Check retinal health, especially important as patients age |

## 3. Symptom Monitoring & Red‑Flag Triggers
- **Night‑time glare or halos** persisting > 1‑2 weeks → schedule an earlier exam.
- **Flashes, new floaters, or sudden loss of sharpness** → seek care promptly.
- **Chronic dryness, burning, or visual fluctuation** during prolonged screen work → request dry‑eye testing.

## 4. Screen‑Work Ergonomics (Eye‑Strain Mitigation)
- Apply the **20‑20‑20 rule**: every 20 minutes, look at something ≥20 feet away for ~20 seconds.
- Consciously blink to maintain tear film stability.
- Position screens slightly **below eye level** and keep ambient lighting moderate to reduce strain.
- Consider **anti‑glare filters** or matte screen protectors.

These practices align with the broader [[concepts/eye-strain-mitigation]] and [[concepts/monitor-ergonomics]] concepts.

## 5. Appointment Checklist (Patient‑Facing)
1. Night‑time vision changes (glare, halos, reduced contrast).
2. Dry‑eye symptoms (itching, burning, fluctuating vision).
3. Any shift in distance or near vision since the last exam.
4. Frequency and severity of eye strain during screen work.
5. Ask whether a **corneal topography** scan is indicated this year.
6. Confirm that a **dilated retinal exam** will be performed.
