# Summary

The conversation provides practical strategies to keep vision comfortable and reduce eye strain during daily screen use, and offers detailed ergonomic recommendations for desk setups, with special attention to users who wear glasses or progressive lenses.

### General eye‑strain mitigation
- Take regular **breaks** (e.g., 20‑20‑20 rule) to let eyes focus on distant objects. [[concepts/eye-strain-mitigation]]
- Ensure good **lighting**: soft, even, side‑light, reduce glare and match screen brightness to ambient light.
- Increase **blink rate** or use artificial tears to prevent dryness.

### Desk‑setup recommendations
- **Monitor distance**: about an arm’s length (~50‑70 cm). [[concepts/monitor-ergonomics]]
- **Monitor height**: top at or slightly below eye level; for progressive lenses, a few inches below eye level to use the middle lens zone. [[concepts/monitor-ergonomics]]
- **Font size**: larger than default, readable without leaning forward.
- **Lighting placement**: diffused side lamp, avoid direct light on screen.

### Considerations for glasses wearers
- Glasses that correct distance vision generally keep the same ergonomics; ensure the screen stays within the clear zone of the lenses. [[concepts/corrective-screen-eyewear]]
- For **progressive lenses**, adjust monitor height lower to avoid head‑tilt; the “sweet spot” of the lens should align with the screen.
- **Computer glasses** (single‑vision for mid‑range) can be beneficial when progressive zones are too narrow or fatigue persists.

### When to seek a dedicated computer prescription
- If head‑tilt or eye fatigue remains despite ergonomic tweaks, a specialized computer prescription provides a wider, optimized viewing area for the typical screen distance. [[concepts/corrective-screen-eyewear]]

These guidelines help create a comfortable visual environment for prolonged screen work, especially for those using corrective eyewear.
