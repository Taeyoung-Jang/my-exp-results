## Levothyroxine

Levothyroxine is a synthetic form of the thyroid hormone thyroxine (T4) prescribed to treat hypothyroidism and to normalize thyroid function tests such as [[entities/tsh]] and [[entities/free-t4]].

### Key facts from [[summaries/session-36_2025-08-15]]
- **Dose adjustments** can cause temporary weight fluctuations and, less commonly, shortness of breath. Monitoring these changes helps clinicians fine‑tune therapy.
- **Administration advice**: take on an empty stomach, wait 30–60 minutes before coffee, calcium, iron, or other medications; record exact timing in a daily tracker. This guidance is summarized in the [[concepts/levothyroxine-adherence]] concept.
- **Tracking**: Patients are encouraged to log daily energy, breathlessness, heart sensations, sleep quality, weight changes, and medication timing, plus a weekly summary of resting pulse and blood pressure. See the [[concepts/health-tracking]] and [[concepts/weight-monitoring]] concepts for template details.
- **Red‑flag symptoms** such as worsening dyspnea, irregular tachycardia, rapid weight change, or swelling should prompt immediate contact with an [[entities/endocrinologist]].
- **Lab follow‑up**: After a dose change, clinicians typically repeat [[entities/tsh]] and [[entities/free-t4]] in 6–8 weeks, with possible addition of free T3 or antibody tests if symptoms are discordant.
