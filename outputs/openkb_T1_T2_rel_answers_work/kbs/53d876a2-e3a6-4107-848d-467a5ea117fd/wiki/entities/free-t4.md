# Free T4 (entity)

Free T4 (free thyroxine) is the biologically active portion of the thyroid hormone thyroxine circulating in the blood without being bound to proteins. It is commonly measured alongside **TSH** to evaluate thyroid function, especially when adjusting **levothyroxine** dosage.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-36_2025-08-15]]
- Used as a follow‑up lab after a levothyroxine dose change; typically rechecked 6–8 weeks after adjustment.
- Combined with TSH to determine whether a patient is in the target therapeutic window.
- May be added to testing when symptoms do not match the TSH result, providing a fuller picture of thyroid hormone availability.
- In the context of **subclinical hypothyroidism**, Free T4 is often normal while TSH is mildly elevated.

## Clinical relevance
Free T4 provides a direct measure of the hormone available to tissues, offering insight when TSH alone is ambiguous. Clinicians use it to:
1. Confirm euthyroid status after medication changes.
2. Differentiate between overt and subclinical thyroid disorders.
3. Guide dosage adjustments for patients experiencing persistent symptoms.
