# Thyroid

The thyroid is a butterfly‑shaped endocrine gland located in the neck. It produces hormones (primarily thyroxine [T4] and triiodothyronine [T3]) that control the body's basal metabolic rate, influencing energy levels, mood, temperature sensitivity, weight, and heart function.

## Key facts from [[summaries/session-36_2025-08-15]]
- **Symptoms of imbalance** are captured in [[concepts/thyroid-symptoms]]: fatigue, anxiety or low mood, sensitivity to heat or cold, unexplained weight change, changes in heart rate, dry skin, hair loss, and peripheral swelling.
- **Diagnostic testing** centers on measuring [[entities/tsh]] as the primary screen, followed by [[entities/free-t4]] (and sometimes free T3) to clarify the hormonal picture.
- **Borderline results** (e.g., mildly elevated TSH with normal free T4) are described as [[concepts/subclinical-hypothyroidism]]. Management depends on symptom severity, TSH trend, antibody status, and patient‑specific factors such as pregnancy plans or cardiac risk.
- **Lifestyle support** includes good sleep, stress reduction, balanced nutrition, and moderate exercise – all part of a broader [[concepts/low-stress-approach]] to symptom mitigation.
- **Medication adjustment**: Levothyroxine is the standard replacement therapy. Proper timing and spacing (e.g., taking on an empty stomach, waiting 30‑60 min before coffee or calcium/iron) are emphasized in [[concepts/levothyroxine-adherence]].
- **Monitoring**: Patients are advised to keep a daily health log covering energy, breathlessness, heart sensations, weight, mood, temperature sensitivity, swelling, and medication timing. This aligns with [[concepts/health-tracking]] and [[concepts/weight-monitoring]].
- **Red‑flag symptoms** prompting immediate contact with an [[entities/endocrinologist]] include worsening shortness of breath at rest, new irregular tachycardia, rapid weight change, or sudden edema.
- **Lab follow‑up** after a levothyroxine dose change typically occurs at 6‑8 weeks for TSH and free T4; earlier testing may be requested if symptoms (e.g., feeling winded) are concerning.
