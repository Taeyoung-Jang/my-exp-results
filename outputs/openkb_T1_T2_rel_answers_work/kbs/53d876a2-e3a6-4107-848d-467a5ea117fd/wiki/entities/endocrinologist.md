# Endocrinologist

**Type:** other

An **endocrinologist** is a medical specialist who focuses on the body’s hormonal systems, including the thyroid gland. In the context of [[summaries/session-36_2025-08-15]], the endocrinologist is the clinician responsible for:

- Prescribing and titrating **[[entities/levothyroxine]]** doses.
- Ordering and interpreting thyroid function tests such as **[[entities/tsh]]**, **[[entities/free-t4]]**, and occasionally total or free **T3**.
- Evaluating symptom patterns like those described in [[concepts/thyroid-symptoms]] and deciding when to move from monitoring to treatment, especially in cases of [[concepts/subclinical-hypothyroidism]].
- Advising on lifestyle measures that support thyroid health, aligning with a [[concepts/low-stress-approach]] and general [[concepts/health-tracking]] practices.
- Providing guidance on medication timing and interactions, a key aspect of [[concepts/levothyroxine-adherence]].

**Key interactions from the session:**
- The user is advised to contact the endocrinologist promptly if red‑flag symptoms appear (e.g., worsening shortness of breath, rapid heart rate, sudden swelling). 
- The endocrinologist may order follow‑up labs 6–8 weeks after a dose change, or sooner if symptoms such as feeling winded arise.
- Recommendations include maintaining a daily symptom and medication log to support the endocrinologist’s monitoring and decision‑making.

**Related concepts**

- [[concepts/levothyroxine-adherence]] – proper timing with food and other meds.
- [[concepts/subclinical-hypothyroidism]] – how the endocrinologist decides on treatment.
- [[concepts/thyroid-symptoms]] – the clinical picture the endocrinologist evaluates.
