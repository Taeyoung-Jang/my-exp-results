# Subclinical Hypothyroidism

**Definition**: A thyroid condition characterized by a mildly elevated serum TSH level while free T4 (and typically free T3) remains within the normal reference range. It is considered a *subclinical* form because overt symptoms may be absent or only mild.

---

## Diagnostic Criteria
- **TSH**: Slightly above the upper limit of the laboratory reference range (usually <5–10 mIU/L, depending on lab standards).
- **Free T4**: Within the normal reference interval.
- **Free T3**: Often not measured unless symptoms are discordant with TSH/T4 results.
- **Antibodies**: May be positive for thyroid peroxidase (TPO) or thyroglobulin (Tg) antibodies, indicating early autoimmune thyroiditis, but not required for the diagnosis.

---

## Why It Matters
Subclinical hypothyroidism sits on a diagnostic gray zone. As highlighted in [[summaries/session-36_2025-08-15]], clinicians balance:
- **Symptom burden** – fatigue, mood changes, temperature sensitivity, weight fluctuation, and subtle cardiac signs.
- **Laboratory trends** – whether TSH is stable, rising, or fluctuating on repeat testing.
- **Risk modifiers** – pregnancy plans, cardiovascular disease, age, and presence of thyroid antibodies.

---

## Management Approaches
| Approach | When Used | Key Considerations |
|----------|-----------|--------------------|
| **Observation / monitoring** | Mild TSH elevation (typically <10 mIU/L) with minimal symptoms | Repeat TSH & free T4 in 3–6 months; lifestyle optimization (sleep, stress, diet) as described in [[concepts/low-stress-approach]]. |
| **Low‑dose levothyroxine** | Persistent symptoms, TSH ≥10 mIU/L, or rising TSH on serial labs, especially with positive antibodies or pregnancy desire | Initiate 25–50 µg daily, titrate per [[concepts/levothyroxine-adherence]] guidelines. |
| **Address comorbidities** | Co‑existing cardiovascular risk, anemia, or dyslipidemia | Manage those conditions alongside thyroid monitoring. |

---

## Lifestyle Support
Even when medication is deferred, patients can mitigate symptom impact through:
- Consistent **sleep hygiene** and stress reduction (see [[concepts/low-stress-approach]]).
- Balanced nutrition avoiding high‑dose iodine supplements.
- Regular **weight monitoring** to detect trends (refer to [[concepts/weight-monitoring]]).
- Routine **health‑tracking** using a daily log (see [[concepts/health-tracking]]).

---

## Red Flags Prompting Earlier Evaluation
- Sudden worsening of fatigue, depression, or cognitive fog.
- New or worsening **shortness of breath**, palpitations, or tachycardia.
- Rapid weight gain or loss over days.
- Development of facial or peripheral **edema**.

If any of these arise, an earlier check of TSH and free T4 (and possibly free T3) is reasonable, as discussed in [[summaries/session-36_2025-08-15]].
