## Weight Monitoring

Weight Monitoring is the practice of regularly recording body weight to observe trends, assess health, and inform fitness decisions. It emphasizes consistency, simplicity, and a low‑stress approach.

### Core Principles
- **Consistency of timing** – Weigh at the same time of day, preferably mornings after waking and using the bathroom, before eating or drinking.
- **Frequency** – For stable weights, weekly weigh‑ins are sufficient; daily tracking is optional for those who prefer more granular data.
- **Single‑point recording** – When weighing weekly, a single number per session is adequate. Averages are only needed when data is collected daily.
- **Acceptable fluctuation** – Variations of up to ~0.5 kg are normal and usually reflect hydration or short‑term factors; even up to 1 kg can be harmless. Patterns over multiple weeks are more meaningful than isolated changes.

### Practical Guidance
1. **Choose one place to log** – Use a notes app, spreadsheet, or dedicated health‑tracking app to keep all entries together.
2. **Keep it quick** – Aim for a check‑in that takes less than a minute to avoid obsessive behavior.
3. **Adjust as needed** – If tracking feels stressful, reduce frequency; if goals change, increase the detail.
4. **Integrate with thyroid health monitoring** – When adjusting levothyroxine dosage, note weight trends alongside other symptoms such as energy, shortness of breath, and heart rate. This helps your endocrinologist discern whether weight changes stem from medication adjustments or other factors.
5. **Combine with symptom tracking** – Pair weight logs with a daily tracker that records energy level, breathlessness, resting pulse, blood pressure, mood, and medication timing. This holistic view supports better clinical decisions, especially during periods of borderline thyroid lab results.
