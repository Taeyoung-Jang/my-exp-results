# Thyroid Symptoms

**Definition**: A collection of physical and subjective signals that often accompany dysfunction of the thyroid gland, whether it is under‑active (hypothyroidism) or over‑active (hyperthyroidism).

## Core Symptom Groups

| Domain | Typical Manifestations |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Energy & Mood** | Fatigue, low stamina, "brain fog," irritability, anxiety, depression. |
| **Thermoregulation** | Increased sensitivity to cold (hypothyroid) or heat (hyperthyroid). |
| **Weight & Appetite** | Unexplained weight gain or loss, changes in appetite that are not proportional to food intake. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Changes in heart rate (brady‑ or tachycardia), palpitations, feeling of a pounding or irregular heartbeat. |
| **Respiratory** | Shortness of breath, especially during activity or when dose adjustments are occurring. |
| **Dermatologic & Hair** | Dry skin, hair thinning or loss, brittle nails, puffiness or swelling (myxedema). |
| **Other** | Muscle aches, joint stiffness, constipation, menstrual irregularities, cognitive slowing. |

These symptoms often appear in clusters, and a single sign alone rarely confirms a thyroid disorder. Clinicians look for patterns and corroborate them with laboratory testing such as [[entities/tsh]] and [[entities/free-t4]].

## Relevance to Session‑36 (2025‑08‑15)

The conversation in [[summaries/session-36_2025-08-15]] highlights several of these signals:
- Persistent fatigue and mood swings.
- Sensitivity to temperature changes.
- Unexplained weight fluctuations after dose adjustments of [[entities/levothyroxine]].
- Episodes of shortness of breath and irregular heart sensations.
- Swelling or a "puffy" feeling.

Understanding these manifestations helps patients articulate their experience, which in turn guides the clinician toward appropriate labs and management decisions.
