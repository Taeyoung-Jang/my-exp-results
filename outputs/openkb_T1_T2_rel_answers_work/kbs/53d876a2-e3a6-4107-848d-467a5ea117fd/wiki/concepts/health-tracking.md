## Health Tracking

Health tracking refers to the practice of regularly recording personal health metrics—such as weight, body measurements, activity, vital signs, and symptom logs—to gain insight into one’s physical condition and support informed decisions about lifestyle, medication, and wellness.

### Key Practices

#### General routine (from [[summaries/session-10_2025-06-15]])
- **Single‑place logging**: Keep all data in one notes app, spreadsheet, or health‑tracking app to avoid fragmentation.
- **Low‑stress approach**: Aim for a routine that takes less than a minute per check‑in, preventing obsessive behavior. [[concepts/low-stress-approach]]
- **Consistent timing**: Weigh yourself at the same time each week (e.g., morning after bathroom use, before food/drink) for comparable readings.
- **Frequency**: For stable weight, weekly weigh‑ins are sufficient; daily tracking is optional.
- **Data granularity**: With weekly weigh‑ins, a single number per session is adequate; averages are more relevant for daily measurements.
- **Normal fluctuation**: Expect up to ~0.5 kg variation week‑to‑week due to hydration, salt, and sleep; larger swings may still be harmless but should be observed for patterns. [[concepts/weight-monitoring]]

#### Condition‑specific tracking (from [[summaries/session-36_2025-08-15]])
- **Symptom logging**: Record daily energy, shortness of breath, heart sensations, mood, temperature sensitivity, swelling, and any unusual observations. This helps identify patterns of [[concepts/thyroid-symptoms]] and informs discussions with an [[entities/endocrinologist]].
- **Medication timing**: For levothyroxine, note exact dose time, spacing from coffee, calcium/iron, or other meds, and any missed doses. Follow the guidance in [[concepts/levothyroxine-adherence]].
- **Vital signs**: Include resting pulse and blood pressure in the daily log to catch early red‑flag changes.
- **Weekly review**: Summarize average resting pulse, blood pressure, overall symptom trend, and any notable days. This mirrors the low‑stress, concise check‑in philosophy.
- **Red‑flag awareness**: While not a formal link, be prepared to contact your [[entities/endocrinologist]] promptly if you notice worsening shortness of breath, irregular heartbeat, rapid weight change, or swelling.
- **Lab follow‑up**: After a levothyroxine dose change, expect repeat [[entities/tsh]] and [[entities/free-t4]] testing at 6–8 weeks; earlier labs may be requested if symptoms are concerning, especially in the context of [[concepts/subclinical-hypothyroidism]].
