# Summary of session-36_2025-08-15

**Main focus** – Practical advice for recognizing thyroid‑related signs, interpreting borderline lab results, and using a concise daily/weekly tracker for levothyroxine management.

## Key Concepts
- **Thyroid symptom patterns** – fatigue, mood changes, temperature sensitivity, weight shifts, heart rate changes, dry skin/hair, swelling. [[concepts/thyroid-symptoms]]
- **Initial lab work** – TSH as primary screen; follow‑up free T4, free T3, and antibodies when indicated. (see [[entities/tsh]], [[entities/free-t4]])
- **Borderline / subclinical results** – Mildly elevated TSH with normal T4 is often “subclinical hypothyroidism.” Decision to treat depends on symptom severity, trend of TSH, antibody status, pregnancy or cardiac risk. [[concepts/subclinical-hypothyroidism]]
- **Lifestyle mitigation** – Sleep hygiene, stress management, balanced diet, moderate exercise; avoid high‑dose iodine or drastic diet changes without clinician guidance. This aligns with a low‑stress approach to health. [[concepts/low-stress-approach]]
- **Medication adjustment effects** – Weight fluctuations and occasional shortness of breath can occur while titrating [[entities/levothyroxine]]; persistent dyspnea warrants prompt evaluation.
- **Tracking & monitoring** – Simple daily log (energy, breathlessness, heart sensations, sleep, weight, mood, temperature, swelling, medication timing) plus a weekly summary (average resting pulse, blood pressure, symptom trend). This supports communication with the [[entities/endocrinologist]] and follows good health‑tracking practice. [[concepts/health-tracking]]
- **Red‑flag symptoms** – Worsening dyspnea at rest, new/irregular tachycardia, rapid weight change, facial/leg edema, faintness. Immediate contact with the endocrinologist is advised.
- **Levothyroxine‑specific considerations** – Take on an empty stomach, wait 30‑60 min before coffee, calcium/iron, or other meds; record exact timing and spacing. [[concepts/levothyroxine-adherence]]
- **Lab timing after dose change** – Typical repeat TSH & free T4 at 6‑8 weeks; free T3 may be added if symptoms don’t align; antibodies usually not repeated unless diagnostic uncertainty. Early labs can be ordered if symptoms (e.g., windedness) are concerning, though interpretation is cautious.

## Practical Output
- Provided a **one‑page printable tracker** (daily + weekly sections) with slots for resting pulse, blood pressure, and levothyroxine timing/spacing.
- Answered the user’s question about requesting earlier labs, confirming it is reasonable when symptoms are out of proportion.
