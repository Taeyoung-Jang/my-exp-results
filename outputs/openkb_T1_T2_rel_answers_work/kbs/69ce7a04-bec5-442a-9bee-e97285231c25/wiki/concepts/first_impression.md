# First Impression

**Description**: The practice of making a positive, authentic impact on new colleagues while avoiding overt formality or effort.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Authenticity** – Show genuine attention through a warm smile, steady eye contact, and relaxed greeting.  
2. **Tone matching** – Align your language and demeanor with the environment’s formality level (casual vs. formal).  
3. **Active listening** – Remember names, follow up on prior remarks, and ask open‑ended questions about the other person’s role.  
4. **Concise self‑intro** – A brief 30‑second “who I am” statement tailored to the audience (manager, peer, cross‑team).  
5. **Contextual adaptation** – Adjust style for in‑person hallway chats versus remote/Slack introductions.

---

## Practical Guidance from [[summaries/session-39_2025-06-15]]

- **First‑impression tip**: Focus on being genuinely attentive and comfortable rather than projecting a perfect professional façade.  
- **Tone matching**: If the setting is casual, keep language relaxed; if more formal, maintain respectful diction while staying natural.  
- **Natural introduction**: Share a light personal detail, ask about the other person’s work, and keep the interaction succinct.  
- **Remote intro examples**: Provide upbeat and low‑key Slack messages that signal openness and availability.
