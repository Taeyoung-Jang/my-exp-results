# Clarity with Warmth

**Definition**: A communication style that pairs precise, unambiguous information with a warm, courteous tone, preventing messages from sounding abrupt, overly formal, or cold.

## Core Elements
- **Clear intent**: State the request or point directly. Example phrasing from the source includes “When you get a moment…” and “Just to clarify…”.
- **Warmth cues**: Add brief friendly elements such as “Thanks”, “Hope you’re well”, or a light‑hearted opener. This softens the directness without diluting the message.
- **Tone check**: Before sending, mentally read the message as if it were spoken; if it could be perceived as sharp, insert a warm phrase or a quick acknowledgment.
- **Context balance**: Provide just enough background for the team to understand the point (see [[concepts/contextual_clarity]]) while avoiding overload that can bury the main idea.

## Why It Matters
- Prevents the **pitfall of being too brief**, which can be interpreted as annoyance or dismissiveness.
- Avoids the opposite extreme of **over‑explaining**, which can obscure the core request.
- Reduces **miscommunication** by ensuring the tone matches the intent, aligning with [[concepts/miscommunication-prevention]].

## Practical Templates (from [[summaries/session-01_2024-06-15]])
- *Status check*: “Hey, quick check‑in on X—how’s it coming along, and anything you need from me?”
- *Issue flag*: “I noticed something that might cause a hiccup with X. Can we take a quick look together before it becomes a problem?”
- *Deadline nudge*: “We’re getting tight on X—can you let me know today where things stand?”

Each template follows the **Clarity with Warmth** pattern: a clear request followed by a courteous, collaborative tone.
