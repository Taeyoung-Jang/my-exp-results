# Summary of session-01_2024-06-15

## Core Communication Habits
- **Provide just enough context** – enough for the team to understand the point, but not so much that the message feels heavy. [[concepts/contextual_clarity]]
- **State the request clearly** and then add a touch of warmth (e.g., “When you get a moment…”, “Just to clarify…”). [[concepts/clarity-with-warmth]]
- **Check tone before sending** – if a message could read as sharp, soften it or add a brief acknowledgment like “Thanks”.  
- **Address confusion early** – ask a clarifying question instead of guessing. [[concepts/miscommunication-prevention]]

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- Being overly brief → sounds annoyed or dismissive.  
- Over‑explaining → buries the main point.  
- Assuming shared understanding of priorities or context → leads to crossed wires.  
- Letting minor misunderstandings linger → creates tension in a small team.

## Ready‑to‑Use Message Templates
### Status Updates
- “Hey, quick check‑in on **X**—how’s it coming along, and anything you need from me?”  
- Casual: “Hey, where are we at with **X**?” / “X update?”

### Flagging Potential Issues
- “I noticed something that might cause a hiccup with **X**. Can we take a quick look together before it becomes a problem?”  
- Casual: “Heads up, I’m seeing a possible snag with **X**—worth a quick look?”

### Disagreeing with a Suggestion
- “I see where you’re coming from, but I’m thinking about it a bit differently. Here’s my take—what do you think?”  
- Casual: “I’m not totally sold on that—mind if I pitch a different angle?”

### Nudging a Deadline
- “Just checking in on the timeline for **X**. Are we still on track, or should we adjust anything?”  
- Direct version for slipping tasks: “We’re getting tight on **X**—can you let me know today where things stand?”

### Handling Confusion
- “Hold up, I think we might be talking about different parts of this—can we reset for a sec?”  
- “Just to make sure we’re aligned, here’s how I’m understanding it… does that match everyone else?”

### Assigning Owner & Next Step
- Casual: “Sounds like **X** is the next move—**[name]**, are you good taking that?”  
- Slightly firmer: “To wrap this up cleanly, let’s have **[name]** take **X** as the next step. Can you confirm?”

## Underlying Principles
- **Balance brevity and warmth** to avoid sounding either abrupt or overly verbose.  
- **Assume shared context** but verify it when ambiguity appears.  
- **Use consistent phrasing** to build a predictable communication style that reduces friction.
