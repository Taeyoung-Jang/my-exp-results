# Hanafi School

The **Hanafi School** is one of the four main Sunni schools of Islamic jurisprudence. It is named after the 8th‑century jurist **Imam Abu Hanifa** and is known for its use of reasoned analogy (qiyās) and a relatively flexible approach to law.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-32_2023-05-02]]
- The document compares the Hanafi view with the other three schools on prayer practices.
- Regarding the first sitting of the prayer, the Hanafi position is that the tashahhud is *necessary for proper structure* but not a pillar that invalidates the prayer if omitted unintentionally.
- For the final sitting, Hanafi scholars generally consider the salawāt (sending blessings on the Prophet) as required and teach it as part of the standard closing.
- Making du‘a after the salawāt is **recommended** but not obligatory in Hanafi fiqh.
- A brief glance at a phone or paper for a reminder is permitted; continuous reading would be discouraged but does not invalidate the prayer.

## Practical relevance
For beginners learning the five daily prayers, understanding the Hanafi stance helps clarify which parts of the prayer are *required* versus *recommended*, especially when attending a mosque that follows Hanafi jurisprudence.
