# Shafi‘i School (Shafi‘ī)

The Shafi‘i (or Shafi‘ī) school is a major Sunni legal tradition founded by Imam al‑Shâfi‘î (767‑820 CE). It is one of the four principal schools of Islamic jurisprudence alongside Hanafi, Maliki, and Hanbali.

## Key Points from [[summaries/session-32_2023-05-02]]
- **Prayer rulings**: The Shafi‘i position holds that the first sitting after two rak‘ahs requires the basic tashahhud, and the final sitting should include both the tashahhud and the full salawāt on the Prophet. The school also recommends making a short du‘a after the salawāt before the concluding taslīm.
- **Use of notes**: According to the document, Shafi‘i scholars consider a brief glance at a phone or paper for memorization aid to be permissible and not invalidating the prayer, provided the glance is brief and not continuous reading.

## Relationship to Other Schools
- Compared with [[entities/hanafi]], [[entities/maliki]], and [[entities/hanbali]], the Shafi‘i school is stricter about the inclusion of salawāt in the final sitting, treating it as effectively obligatory, while the others may regard it as highly recommended.
- All four schools share the view that the core tashahhud (up to the shahada line) is essential in every sitting.
