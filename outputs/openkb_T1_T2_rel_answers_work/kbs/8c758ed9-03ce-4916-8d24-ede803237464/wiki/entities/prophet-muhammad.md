# Prophet Muhammad (person)

Prophet Muhammad (c. 570 CE – 632 CE) is regarded by Muslims as the final messenger and seal of prophecy, delivering the Qur'an and establishing the core practices of Islam.  He is central to the daily ritual guidance discussed in [[summaries/session-32_2023-05-02]], where the required and recommended recitations during the tashahhud and salawāt reference his name and status.

## Key Facts from the Session
- The **tashahhud** includes the testimony: *"ash‑hadu anna Muḥammadan ‘abduhu wa rasūluh"* (I bear witness that Muhammad is God’s servant and messenger), confirming his essential role in the prayer.
- The **salawāt** (sending blessings) explicitly invokes: *"Allāhumma ṣalli ‘alā Muḥammadin…"* and *"Allāhumma bārik ‘alā Muḥammadin…"*, showing the practice of sending blessings on the Prophet during the final sitting of the prayer.
- Different **Islamic schools of law** (Hanafi, Shafi‘i, Maliki, Hanbali) discuss the salawāt’s status, illustrating the theological importance of Muhammad across jurisprudential traditions.  See [[concepts/islamic-schools-of-law]] for a brief comparison.
- The session also outlines a practical **prayer routine** for new converts, emphasizing memorization of the Qur'an’s opening chapter (Al‑Fātiḥah) and short surahs, which are recited in his presence during worship.  See [[concepts/prayer-routine]].
