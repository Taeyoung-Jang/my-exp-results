# Islam

Islam is a monotheistic faith founded on the revelation of the **Qur'an** to **Prophet Muhammad**. It guides believers in all aspects of life, from worship to daily conduct.

## Core Practices Highlighted in Session 32
- **Five daily prayers** form the rhythmic backbone of a modern Muslim routine. See [[concepts/daily-prayer]] and the practical [[concepts/prayer-routine]] outlined in the session.
- New adherents are encouraged to start with **Surah Al‑Fātiḥah** and short Meccan surahs (Al‑Ikhlāṣ, Al‑Falaq, An‑Nās) for memorization, as described in the document.
- The **tashahhud** and **salawāt** are recited while sitting; the required portion is the core tashahhud, while the salawāt is recommended. These details are linked to [[concepts/prayer-routine]].
- A gentle **week‑by‑week plan** helps beginners move from using notes to praying all five rak‘ahs from memory.

## Legal Schools and Prayer Details
The document compares the four major Sunni schools of law regarding prayer components:
- **Hanafi** – first sitting important but not a pillar; salawāt strongly emphasized in the final sitting. [[entities/hanafi]]
- **Shafi‘i** – first sitting required; salawāt treated as obligatory in the final sitting. [[entities/shafi-i]]
- **Maliki** – first sitting important but not invalidating if missed; salawāt highly recommended. [[entities/maliki]]
- **Hanbali** – first sitting essential for completeness; salawāt required. [[entities/hanbali]]
These differences are summarized under [[concepts/islamic-schools-of-law]].

## Community and Conversion
- New Muslims often complete the **shahada** at a local **mosque** to formally declare their faith. [[entities/shahada]] [[entities/mosque]]
- The document advises that a brief glance at a phone for a reminder does **not** invalidate the prayer in any of the four schools.
