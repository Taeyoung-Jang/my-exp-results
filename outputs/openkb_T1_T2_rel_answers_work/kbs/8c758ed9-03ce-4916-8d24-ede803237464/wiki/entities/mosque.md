## Mosque

A **mosque** (Arabic: *masjid*) is the primary place of worship for Muslims, serving as a venue for the five daily prayers, the weekly Friday sermon (*Jumuʿah*), educational activities, and communal events such as the declaration of the *shahada*.

### Role in Daily Practice
- Acts as the focal point for **[[concepts/daily-prayer]]** and the **[[concepts/prayer-routine]]** described in [[summaries/session-32_2023-05-02]], where newcomers are encouraged to visit a local mosque to observe or join the Friday sermon and to find a welcoming person to ask questions.
- Provides a supportive environment for learning the basics of prayer, reciting the **[[entities/quran]]**, and practicing the **[[entities/shahada]]** ceremony.

### Community Functions
- Serves as a hub for social interaction, offering opportunities to connect with fellow believers and to participate in **[[concepts/islamic-schools-of-law]]** discussions that may vary among **[[entities/hanafi]]**, **[[entities/shafi-i]]**, **[[entities/maliki]]**, and **[[entities/hanbali]]** tradition.
- Hosts classes, study circles, and charitable activities that reinforce personal habits of kindness and fairness, as suggested in the guidance for new Muslims.

### Architectural Features (general)
- Typically includes a prayer hall oriented toward the **Qibla** (direction of the **[[entities/prophet-muhammad]]**’s Kaaba), a mihrab (niche indicating Qibla), and a minbar (pulpit for sermons).
- May have separate spaces for men and women, ablution facilities for *wudu*, and sometimes a library of **[[entities/quran]]** translations.

### Significance for New Converts
- The mosque is highlighted as the first community space where a new Muslim can receive guidance, observe communal worship, and eventually take the **[[entities/shahada]]** in a formal setting.
- Engaging with a mosque helps embed the rhythm of prayer into daily life, easing the transition to the full **[[concepts/prayer-routine]]** without feeling overwhelmed.
