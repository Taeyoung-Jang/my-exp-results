# Daily Prayer

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A modern, step‑by‑step framework for incorporating the five daily Islamic prayers into everyday life.

## Overview
Daily Prayer in Islam refers to the ritual performance of the five obligatory prayers (Salat) that structure a Muslim’s day. The practice is intended to create a steady spiritual rhythm rather than a burdensome checklist. For newcomers, the emphasis is on gradual familiarisation with the prayer movements, recitations, and timing.

## Core Elements
1. **Five Daily Prayers** – Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, and Isha. These act as short pauses throughout the day to reconnect with God.
2. **Gradual Learning** – Start with the basics (how to stand, bow, prostrate, and recite Al‑Fātiḥah) and add additional prayers over time.
3. **Essential Surahs & Duʿās** – Memorise Al‑Fātiḥah plus short Meccan surahs such as Al‑Ikhlāṣ, Al‑Falaq, and An‑Nās, along with opening duʿā like “Subḥāna‑ka Allāhumma”.
4. **Tashahhud & Salawāt** – The seated portion of the prayer includes the required tashahhud and the recommended salawāt on the Prophet. Understanding what is required vs. recommended helps avoid overwhelm.
5. **Week‑by‑Week Routine** – A gentle progression:
   - **Week 1:** Perform all five prayers with notes, focusing on physical flow.
   - **Week 2:** Memorise one prayer fully; keep others on paper.
   - **Week 3:** Expand to two‑three prayers from memory.
   - **Week 4:** Complete all five prayers from memory, polishing recitation.

## Cross‑School Nuances
The four major Sunni schools (Hanafi, Shafiʿī, Maliki, Hanbali) agree on the core requirement of the tashahhud but differ on the obligatory nature of the salawāt and duʿā after it. Brief phone glances for reminders are generally permitted across schools.
