# Prayer Routine

**Type:** Concept

## Overview
The *prayer routine* describes a practical, gradual approach to integrating the five daily prayers (Salāh) into everyday life. It emphasizes a steady rhythm rather than a heavy burden and provides a week‑by‑week plan for beginners to move from using notes to praying confidently from memory. The routine is rooted in the broader idea of [[concepts/daily-prayer]] and aligns with the teachings of [[entities/islam]] and the Qur’an.

## Weekly Build‑up Plan (from [[summaries/session-32_2023-05-02]])
| Week | Goal | Details |
|------|------|---------|
| 1 | Perform all five prayers with notes | Focus on the physical flow (standing, bowing, prostrating). |
| 2 | Memorize one prayer fully | Choose a prayer that fits your schedule (e.g., Maghrib). Add a second prayer after a few days. |
| 3 | Pray most rak‘ahs from memory | Use notes only for quick checks before or after movements. |
| 4 | Complete all five prayers from memory | Polish recitation and meanings; the rhythm should feel natural. |

## Essential Surahs & Duas for Beginners
- **Al‑Fātiḥah** – required in every rak‘ah. 
- Short Meccan surahs: **Al‑Ikhlāṣ**, **Al‑Falaq**, **An‑Nās** – easy to memorize and recite after Al‑Fātiḥah. 
- Opening du‘a: *Subḥāna‑ka Allāhumma* (glorify God) used before Al‑Fātiḥah. 
These selections are recommended in the source document and are commonly taught in mosques ([[entities/mosque]]).

## Tashahhud and Salawāt
- **Required portion** (both first and final sitting):
  > *At‑taḥiyyātu lillāhi … ash‑hadu anna Muḥammadan ‘abduhu wa rasūluh.*
- **Recommended addition** (final sitting): full salawāt on the Prophet Muhammad.
- **Du‘a after salawāt** – permissible and encouraged, especially in the Shafi‘ī tradition.

## Differences Among the Four Sunni Schools
The routine notes the varying emphases of the major [[concepts/islamic-schools-of-law]]:
- **Hanafi**: First sitting important but not a pillar; salawāt strongly emphasized in final sitting; brief phone glance for reminder is allowed.
- **Shafi‘ī**: First sitting required; salawāt treated as obligatory in final sitting; du‘a after salawāt strongly encouraged.
- **Maliki**: First sitting important but not invalidating if missed; salawāt highly recommended but not strictly required; du‘a permitted.
- **Hanbali**: First sitting essential for completeness; salawāt required; du‘a recommended.
All schools agree that a quick glance at a phone for a reminder does **not** invalidate the prayer.

## Practical Tips
- Keep a small notebook or phone note with the essential texts for quick reference before the prayer.
- Recite the required tashahhud from memory; if unsure, a brief glance is permissible.
- Join a welcoming community at a local [[entities/mosque]] for support and to observe the Friday sermon (Jumu‘ah).
- Use the week‑by‑week plan as a flexible guide; adjust the pace to avoid burnout.
