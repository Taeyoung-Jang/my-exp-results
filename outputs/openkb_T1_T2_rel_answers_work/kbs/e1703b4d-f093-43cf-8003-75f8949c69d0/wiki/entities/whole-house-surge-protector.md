# Whole‑House Surge Protector

A whole‑house surge protector is a device installed at the main electrical panel that diverts excess voltage caused by lightning strikes, grid fluctuations, or appliance surges, protecting appliances, electronics, and wiring from damage.

## Key Facts from Session 14 (2023‑07‑15)
- **Cost**: Roughly **$300 – $700** installed, depending on brand and complexity of the installation.
- **Purpose**: Prevents costly electrical failures and reduces the likelihood of fire or water‑damage cascades that can result from faulty appliances.
- **Relevance to Cold Climate Homes**: In regions with frequent storms and power surges, a surge protector adds a critical layer of protection alongside other upgrades like [[entities/sump-pump]] and [[entities/heat-trace-cable]].
- **Installation Timing**: Recommended as a **once‑off installation** with a quick visual check during the annual maintenance window (see [[concepts/maintenance-scheduling]]).

## Why It Matters
- Part of a broader **[[concepts/homeownership-disaster-prevention]]** strategy, addressing electrical hazards that can lead to secondary water‑damage (e.g., a failed pump).
- Complements other high‑impact upgrades such as [[entities/automatic-shutoff-valve]] and [[entities/water-leak-sensor]] by ensuring the home’s power infrastructure remains stable.
- Aligns with the **[[concepts/cold-climate-water-risk-mitigation]]** approach, where protecting the electrical system helps maintain heating and de‑icing equipment during winter storms.

## Maintenance Recommendations
- **Annual Check**: During the yearly home‑maintenance review (see [[concepts/maintenance-scheduling]]), verify the indicator light on the protector and ensure no tripped alarms.
- **Replace When Needed**: Surge protectors have a finite lifespan; replace after a major surge event or every 10‑15 years.
