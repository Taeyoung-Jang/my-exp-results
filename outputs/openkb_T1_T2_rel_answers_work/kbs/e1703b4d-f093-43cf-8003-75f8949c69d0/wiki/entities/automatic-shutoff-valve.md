# Automatic Shut‑off Valve

**Type:** product

A residential **automatic shut‑off valve** is a smart plumbing device that detects abnormal water flow or pressure loss—often triggered by a leak sensor—and instantly closes the main water supply to prevent flooding. 

## Key Facts from Session 14
- **Cost:** Roughly **$400–$1,000 installed** (including hardware and labor) as noted in the [[summaries/session-14_2023-07-15]] checklist.
- **Primary Benefit:** Stops water flow during pipe bursts or major leaks, dramatically reducing water‑damage risk especially in cold climates where frozen pipes are common.
- **Recommended Timing:** Install in **Q3 (late summer)**, before the first freeze, as part of the cold‑climate mitigation plan.
- **Complementary Devices:** Works best alongside **[[entities/water-leak-sensor]]** units and a **[[entities/battery-backup]]** to maintain operation during power outages.
- **Risk Indicators:** Consider the valve if your home has:
  - Older or exposed piping in unheated areas (basement, crawlspace, exterior walls)
  - A finished basement where even a small leak can cause costly damage
  - History of frozen pipes or recent pipe repairs

## Relationship to Broader Concepts
- Part of a larger strategy for **[[concepts/homeownership-disaster-prevention]]** and **[[concepts/cold-climate-water-risk-mitigation]]**.
- Fits into a structured **[[concepts/maintenance-scheduling]]** routine: install early in the year, test annually during the “once a year” maintenance window.

## Practical Tips
1. **Installation:** Hire a licensed plumber; ensure the valve is placed at the main water line entry point.
2. **Testing:** After installation, simulate a leak event using the paired sensor to verify automatic shut‑off response.
3. **Maintenance:** Test the system yearly during your annual home‑maintenance checklist; replace batteries in the backup unit as needed.
