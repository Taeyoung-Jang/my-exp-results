# Utility Management

Utility Management refers to the systematic organization, documentation, and transfer of all utility services associated with a residential property. It ensures that the new owner can activate water, electricity, gas, trash, internet, and any specialty services without interruption, while the seller retains records for future reference.

## Why it matters
- Prevents service gaps that can cause damage (e.g., water leaks) or inconvenience.
- Provides clear points of contact for each provider, reducing confusion during move‑in.
- Supports the broader [[concepts/homeownership-handoff]] process by delivering a concise “house info packet”.

## Core Elements (as outlined in [[summaries/session-36_2024-07-15]])
1. **Utility Provider Details** – Name, account number, and contact information for water, electricity, gas, trash/recycling, and internet.
2. **Service Shut‑off Locations** – Physical locations of the main water shutoff, gas shutoff, and electrical panel. Often includes an [[entities/automatic-shutoff-valve]] for water.
3. **Transfer Instructions** – Steps to close the seller’s accounts and open new ones for the buyer, including any required forms or online portals.
4. **Safety and Backup Systems** – Documentation of surge protectors (e.g., [[entities/whole-house-surge-protector]]) and water‑leak detection devices (e.g., [[entities/water-leak-sensor]]) that may need re‑programming or reset.

## Interaction with Related Concepts
- **[[concepts/maintenance-scheduling]]** – Knowing utility shut‑off points aids routine maintenance and emergency repairs.
- **[[concepts/smart-home-transfer]]** – Smart thermostats, cameras, and garage door openers often rely on utility connections; their handoff must be coordinated with utility changes.
- **[[concepts/homeownership-handoff]]** – Utility Management is a foundational sub‑section of the overall handoff packet.

## Best Practices
- Create a digital copy of all utility contracts and store it with other closing documents.
- Take photos of shut‑off valves and label them for the buyer.
- Verify that any transferable warranties (e.g., for a water‑heater) include utility‑related service terms.
- Reset or remove personal logins from any smart devices that interface with utilities.

By following these steps, both seller and buyer can ensure a smooth transition of essential services, minimizing risk and enhancing the overall homeownership experience.
