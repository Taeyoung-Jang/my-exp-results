# Vehicle Title Management

Vehicle title management is the process of verifying, maintaining, and transferring the legal document that proves ownership of a motor vehicle. It ensures that titles are correctly recorded, that any liens are addressed, and that transfers comply with state regulations.

---

## Core Elements

- **Title Verification**: Confirm the title is in the seller’s name and physically in hand before any sale. In California, this is the **Certificate of Title**.
- **Duplicate Title**: If the title is lost, request a replacement using **REG 227**.
- **Lien Resolution**: Any outstanding loan must be paid off and the lienholder’s claim removed before a clean transfer.
- **Accurate Completion**: Fill out buyer name, sale price, date, mileage, and required signatures exactly as prescribed on the title.
- **Release of Liability**: File the **Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability** (form **REG 138**) online within the state‑mandated window (typically 5 days) to protect the seller from future tickets or tolls.

---

## Typical Workflow (California Example)

1. **Pre‑Sale Checks**
   - Verify ownership and obtain a duplicate title if needed (REG 227).
   - Gather maintenance records, smog inspection proof, and confirm any loan payoff details.
2. **Meet‑up Preparation**
   - Bring the title, a simple bill of sale, driver's license, and buyer’s insurance proof.
3. **Title Transfer**
   - Complete the title fields accurately: buyer name, mileage, price, and signatures.
   - Provide the buyer with the signed title and bill of sale.
4. **Post‑Sale Filings**
   - Submit the Notice of Transfer/Release of Liability via the **California DMV** website (REG 138) on the same day or within five days.
   - Cancel the seller’s insurance after the DMV confirms the transfer.
   - Keep copies of the online filing confirmation, bill of sale, and the old registration for personal records.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- **Failing to file the release of liability** – can result in tickets or tolls being billed to the former owner.
- **Incorrect mileage or sale price on the title** – leads to rejection of the transfer.
- **Handing over the title before confirming payment** – exposes the seller to fraud risk.
- **Neglecting to cancel insurance promptly** – may cause duplicate coverage or liability issues.
