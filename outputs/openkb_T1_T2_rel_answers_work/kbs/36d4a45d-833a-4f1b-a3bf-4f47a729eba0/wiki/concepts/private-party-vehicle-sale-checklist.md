# Private‑Party Vehicle Sale Checklist

A concise, repeatable process for individuals selling a car directly to another person. It captures the essential phases—pre‑listing, meetup preparation, paperwork execution, and post‑sale actions—while highlighting common pitfalls and the documentation required.

---

## 1. Pre‑Listing
- Verify that the **[[entities/certificate-of-title]]** is in your name and physically in your possession. If it is lost, request a duplicate with **[[entities/reg-227]]**.
- Clean the vehicle, take clear daylight photos, and gather maintenance records.
- Check your state’s smog or inspection requirements (e.g., California’s emissions test).
- Optionally draft a simple **[[entities/bill-of-sale]]** to record the transaction.

## 2. Meet‑up Preparation
- Choose a safe, public location.
- Bring:
  - The title (or duplicate).
  - Printed **[[entities/bill-of-sale]]**.
  - Your driver’s license.
  - Proof of the buyer’s insurance and a valid driver’s license.
  - Any required release‑of‑liability forms (see **[[concepts/notice-of-transfer-release-of-liability]]**).
- Accompany the buyer on any test‑drive; verify insurance before allowing it.

## 3. Paperwork Execution
- Complete the title with exact buyer name, sale price, date, mileage, and signatures.
- Fill the **[[entities/bill-of-sale]]** with matching details to avoid discrepancies.
- Hand the buyer the title, bill of sale, and any additional documents required by your state.
- For California, the license plates stay on the vehicle; surrender them only for specialty plates, junking, or if the DMV requests them.

## 4. After‑Sale Actions
- File the **Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability** online (or via **[[entities/reg-138]]**) within five days; same‑day filing is safest. This links to **[[concepts/notice-of-transfer-release-of-liability]]**.
- Keep a screenshot or PDF of the filing confirmation.
- Retain a copy of the bill of sale and the old registration for your records.
- Cancel your auto‑insurance after the DMV processes the transfer.

## 5. Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Forgetting to file the release‑of‑liability, leading to tickets or tolls appearing under your name.
- Incorrect mileage, price, or missing signatures on the title.
- Accepting unsecured payment methods; prefer verified cashier’s checks or bank transfers.
- Handing over the title before confirming receipt of funds.
- Not notifying the lienholder when a loan is still on the title; the loan must be paid off before a clean title can be transferred.
