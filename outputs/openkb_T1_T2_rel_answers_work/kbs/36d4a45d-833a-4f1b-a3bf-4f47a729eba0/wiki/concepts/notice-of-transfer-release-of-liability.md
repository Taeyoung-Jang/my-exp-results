# Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability

**Definition**

The Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability (often referred to by its form number **REG 138**) is a filing with the **[[entities/california-dmv]]** that formally informs the state that a vehicle has been sold or otherwise transferred.  The filing releases the seller from future responsibility for tickets, tolls, or fees that accrue after the transfer date.

---

## Why It Matters

- Protects the seller from post‑sale violations (speeding tickets, tolls, parking citations).  
- Provides a documented timestamp showing when ownership changed hands.  
- Required by California law for private‑party sales; failure can result in the seller receiving unwanted notices and potential fines.

---

## Filing Process (California)

1. **Obtain the Form** – The official form is **[[entities/reg-138]]**.  It can be completed online through the **[[entities/california-dmv]]** website or on paper.
2. **Provide Required Information** – Vehicle VIN, license plate number, seller’s name and address, buyer’s name and address, sale date, and odometer reading.
3. **Submit the Notice** – Online submission is immediate and generates a confirmation page; paper forms can be mailed or delivered in person.
4. **Timing** – The DMV advises filing **within five days** of the sale, but filing the same day is safest to avoid any liability gaps.
5. **Keep Proof** – Save a screenshot or PDF of the online confirmation, or retain the stamped receipt if filing on paper. This proof is essential for disputing any later tickets.

---

## Relation to Private‑Party Vehicle Sales

The notice is a core step in the **[[concepts/private-party-vehicle-sale-checklist]]**.  After the buyer signs the title (**[[entities/certificate-of-title]]**) and receives the **[[entities/bill-of-sale]]**, the seller must promptly file the Notice of Transfer to finalize the transaction and protect themselves.

---

## Interaction with Vehicle Title Management

- If the title is **lost**, the seller must first request a duplicate using **[[entities/reg-227]]** before completing the sale and filing the notice.
- When a lien exists, the lender typically holds the title.  The seller must ensure the loan is paid off and the clean title is in hand before filing the notice.
- The notice does **not** affect license plates in California; plates remain on the vehicle unless surrendered for specialty plates or vehicle disposal.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- **Delayed filing** – leads to the seller being held liable for violations that occur after the sale.
- **Incomplete or incorrect information** – the DMV may reject the filing, requiring resubmission.
- **Missing proof of filing** – without a confirmation, disputes become harder to resolve.
