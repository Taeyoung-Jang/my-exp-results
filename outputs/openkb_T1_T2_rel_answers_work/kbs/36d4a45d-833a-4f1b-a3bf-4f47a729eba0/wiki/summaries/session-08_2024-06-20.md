# Summary of Session 8 (2024-06-20)

**Main focus:** How to decide whether to keep a 5‑year‑old hatchback with ~120k miles or replace it, and how to allocate a $1,500–$2,500 repair budget.

## Prioritization & Budget Breakdown
- **Top priority:** Resolve suspension clunks (diagnosis → replace worn shocks/struts, control‑arm bushings, or sway‑bar end links). Typical cost: $600–$800 for front struts, $200–$400 for bushings/end links. See [[concepts/suspension-noise-diagnosis]].
- **Next:** Overdue fluid services (coolant, brake fluid, transmission fluid). Each service: $100–$150 (transmission $150–$300). This follows a [[concepts/cost-benefit-analysis-car-maintenance]] approach.
- **Then:** New tires (mid‑range set for a compact hatchback): $450–$700 installed.
- **Last:** Brakes (pads only or pads + rotors). Pads per axle: $200–$300; full replacement both axles: $500–$700.
- **Overall range:** $1,700–$2,400, fitting the user’s budget. The order reflects [[concepts/budget-prioritization-vehicle-repairs]].

## Diagnostic Approach
- Request a **"suspension noise diagnosis"** (or "clunk diagnosis"). Expect the shop to lift the car, perform a pry‑bar test on bushings/joints, do a bounce test on shocks, and check for leaks. Diagnostic fee norm: **$50–$120**, often waived if you proceed with repairs.

## Sanity‑Checking Recommendations
- Ask the technician to **show the worn part on the lift** and explain why it’s out of spec.
- Separate **"must‑fix now"** items from **"can wait"** items.
- Request an **itemized estimate** before any work begins.
- Ask for the **old parts back** after replacement.
- If the list seems excessive, get a **second opinion**.

## Script for the Service Counter
```
Hi, I’m hearing a clunk from the suspension and would like a suspension noise diagnosis.
Before any repairs, could you show me the exact part that’s causing the noise while the car is on the lift and explain why it needs replacement?
Please separate anything that’s a must‑fix from anything that can wait.
Also, I’d like an itemized estimate before work starts and to have the old parts returned after the repair.
```
