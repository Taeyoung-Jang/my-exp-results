# Certificate of Title (work)

The **Certificate of Title** is the official legal document that proves ownership of a motor vehicle. In California it is commonly referred to as the *Certificate of Title* and is required for any private‑party vehicle sale.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-18]]
- The seller must have the title in their possession before listing the car; a missing or incorrect title halts the sale.
- If the title is lost, a duplicate must be requested using **REG 227** (see [[entities/reg-227]]).
- When a loan exists, the lienholder typically holds the title; the seller must coordinate payoff and title release before the buyer can receive a clean title.
- The title must be filled out accurately with buyer name, sale price, date, mileage, and required signatures; errors can cause the DMV to reject the transfer.
- After the sale, the seller files the **Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability** (REG 138) online through the [[entities/california-dmv]] within five days to protect against future tickets or tolls.

## Practical Guidance
1. **Before listing**: Verify you hold the original Certificate of Title or obtain a duplicate via REG 227.
2. **During sale**: Complete the title carefully; match information on the optional bill of sale.
3. **After sale**: Submit the Notice of Transfer (REG 138) through the [[entities/california-dmv]] website and retain a copy of the confirmation.

Keeping the Certificate of Title accurate and properly transferred is essential to avoid future liability, title disputes, or registration issues.
