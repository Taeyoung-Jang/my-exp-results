# REG 138 – Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability

**Type:** work (California Department of Motor Vehicles form)

**Purpose**
- Used to officially notify the California DMV that a vehicle has been sold or transferred to a new owner.
- Releases the seller from future liability for tickets, tolls, or other violations associated with the vehicle after the transfer date.

**Key Facts from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-18]]**
- Filing is required **within five days** of the sale, but filing the same day is strongly recommended to avoid post‑sale surprises.
- The form can be submitted **online** via the [[entities/california-dmv]] website, providing a quick, paper‑less option.
- A **confirmation screenshot or PDF** serves as proof that the release was filed and should be retained by the seller.
- Completing [[concepts/notice-of-transfer-release-of-liability]] correctly protects the seller from later tolls, tickets, or registration fees.

**When It’s Used**
- Private‑party vehicle sales in California (see [[concepts/private-party-vehicle-sale-checklist]]).
- Situations where a vehicle is being taken off the road, junked, or otherwise removed from the seller’s ownership.

**Related Entities & Concepts**
- [[entities/california]] – the state jurisdiction governing the form.
- [[entities/california-dmv]] – the agency that administers REG 138.
- [[concepts/notice-of-transfer-release-of-liability]] – the broader procedural concept.
- [[concepts/private-party-vehicle-sale-checklist]] – checklist that references filing this form.

**Practical Tips**
1. **Gather required information**: vehicle VIN, license plate number, sale price, buyer’s name and address, and the exact date of transfer.
2. **Submit online**: Visit the California DMV’s “Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability” portal, fill out the fields, and submit.
3. **Save confirmation**: Download or screenshot the confirmation page; store it with your bill of sale and other transaction records.
4. **Follow up**: Verify that the DMV updates the vehicle record within a few days; you can check the status via the DMV’s online services.

**If the Form Is Missed**
- The seller may still be held responsible for violations incurred after the sale until the DMV processes the transfer.
- Filing retroactively is possible, but delays can lead to disputes and additional fees.
