# Collaborative Communication

**Definition**: A style of interaction that emphasizes open, respectful dialogue and frames household routines or boundaries as shared goals rather than imposed rules.

**Key Characteristics**
- **Joint framing** – Suggestions are presented as “let’s make things easier for everyone,” promoting a sense of partnership.
- **Gentle rhythms** – Routines are described as flexible habits, reducing the perception of rigidity.
- **Inclusive listening** – Everyone’s needs (quiet, privacy, clean‑up) are invited and validated before agreements are set.
- **Positive conflict handling** – Slip‑ups are addressed kindly, focusing on solutions instead of blame.

**Application in [[summaries/session-02_2024-07-15]]**
- The assistant guides a four‑person household to discuss kitchen duties, quiet hours, and privacy using collaborative language.
- Phrases like “what makes things easier for all of us?” illustrate the approach.
- The resulting “house agreement” is framed as a shared understanding, not a top‑down rule set.

**Related Concepts**
- [[concepts/flexible-habits]] – The underlying habit‑based structure that supports collaborative communication.
- [[concepts/house-agreement]] – A concrete artifact that emerges from collaborative communication.

**Benefits**
- Reduces resistance and feelings of being policed.
- Encourages mutual respect across different family dynamics (e.g., parent‑adult‑child).
- Creates a living document that can be revisited and adjusted together.
