# Flexible Habits

**Definition**

Flexible Habits are small, low‑friction routines that can be easily adopted by all house members, allowing the household to run smoothly while remaining adaptable to changing schedules and needs.

**Key Characteristics**
- **Low effort:** Actions like rinsing dishes immediately or wiping counters after cooking require minimal time.
- **Adaptable:** Habits can be adjusted (e.g., quiet windows can shift) without breaking the overall flow.
- **Collaborative:** They are framed as shared goals rather than top‑down rules, supporting [[concepts/collaborative-communication]].
- **Gentle enforcement:** Slip‑ups are handled with kind reminders, not blame, fostering a supportive environment.

**Examples from [[summaries/session-02_2024-07-15]]**
- **Kitchen routine:** "Rinse dishes after use and leave counters clear" – a habit that keeps the kitchen ready for the next person.
- **Quiet hours:** Agree on a general quiet window and use headphones when someone is resting, rather than imposing a strict curfew.
- **Privacy norm:** Knock before entering closed doors, treating it as basic courtesy.
- **Check‑in practice:** Brief, periodic reviews to tweak habits as needed.

**Relationship to Other Concepts**
- Works together with a [[concepts/house-agreement]] to provide concrete, low‑stakes actions that embody the agreement’s principles.
- Reinforces [[concepts/collaborative-communication]] by encouraging open, non‑authoritative dialogue about daily routines.

**Why They Matter**
Flexible Habits reduce friction points (mess, noise, privacy breaches) while preserving a sense of autonomy and respect among housemates. By keeping routines simple and adjustable, households can maintain calm and functional living spaces without feeling constrained.
