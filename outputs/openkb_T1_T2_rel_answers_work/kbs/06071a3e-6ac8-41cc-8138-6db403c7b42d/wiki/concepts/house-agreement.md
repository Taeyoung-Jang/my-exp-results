# House Agreement

A **House Agreement** is a brief, collaboratively created list of household guidelines that clarifies expectations while remaining adaptable. It serves as a living document posted in a shared space (e.g., on the fridge) to support calm, functional co‑living without imposing rigid rules.

## Core Principles

- **Collaborative communication** – The agreement is framed as a shared goal rather than a top‑down rule set, encouraging open dialogue and mutual respect. [[concepts/collaborative-communication]]
- **Flexible habits** – Guidelines focus on simple, repeatable actions that can be adjusted as schedules change, keeping the environment adaptable. [[concepts/flexible-habits]]
- **Respectful tone** – Language is inclusive and considerate of family dynamics (e.g., parent‑adult‑child relationships), avoiding a commanding voice.

## Typical Content (derived from [[summaries/session-02_2024-07-15]])

1. **Kitchen / dishes** – Rinse dishes right after use and leave counters cleared; keep overnight messes minimal unless coordinated.
2. **Quiet hours / noise** – Agree on a general quiet window (e.g., rest times) and use headphones when someone is resting.
3. **Privacy & knocking** – Treat closed doors as a signal for privacy; knock and wait for a response before entering.
4. **Handling slip‑ups** – When a guideline is missed, discuss it kindly, focus on solutions, and avoid blame.
5. **Periodic check‑ins** – Schedule brief reviews every few weeks to adjust the agreement as needed.
6. **Family‑specific wording** – Phrase items as shared understandings (“let’s…”, “we’ll…”) to respect parent‑adult‑kid dynamics.

## Benefits

- Reduces friction points (kitchen mess, noise, unknocked doors).
- Encourages a sense of shared responsibility and ownership.
- Provides a clear reference that can be quickly reviewed, keeping expectations transparent.
- Supports a respectful atmosphere, especially in multigenerational households.

## Creating Your Own House Agreement

1. **Gather all housemates** for a short, calm conversation.
2. **Identify the top friction points** (e.g., dishes, noise, privacy).
3. **Draft 6–8 bullet points** using collaborative‑communication language.
4. **Post the list** in a common area.
5. **Set a recurring check‑in** to refine the agreement.
