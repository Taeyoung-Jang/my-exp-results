# Winter Preparation

**Related concepts:** [[concepts/morning-routine]], [[concepts/neighbourhood-exploration]]

**Source:** [[summaries/session-34_2025-02-13]]

---

## Overview
Winter preparation for a newcomer to the **Annex/Koreatown** area of [[entities/toronto]] focuses on three pillars:
1. **Personal gear** – staying warm and dry.
2. **Commute safety** – handling TTC delays, icy sidewalks, and extra travel time.
3. **Bag essentials** – small items that keep you comfortable during short morning outings.

These pillars support the broader [[concepts/morning-routine]] and the weekly [[concepts/neighbourhood-exploration]] walks detailed in the source conversation.

---

## 1. Personal Gear Checklist
| Item | Why it matters |
|------|----------------|
| Waterproof insulated boots | Prevents slipping and keeps feet dry on icy sidewalks |
| Heavy insulated coat (down or parkas) | Core warmth for sub‑zero temperatures |
| Gloves (inner liner + waterproof outer) | Keeps hands functional for phone, transit card, and bag |
| Warm hat that covers ears | Reduces heat loss from the head |
| Base layers (merino, fleece) | Moisture‑wicking and easy to add/remove |
| Scarf or neck gaiter | Protects face from wind and cold |

Optional but useful for the season:
- Reflective accessories for low‑light visibility
- A compact **snow brush** for the car or bike frame

---

## 2. Commute Considerations
- **TTC delays**: During snow or freezing rain, streetcars run slower and may be cancelled. Load extra buffer time (5‑10 min) into your morning schedule.
- **Sidewalk ice**: The Annex area freezes unevenly. Favor well‑plowed routes (e.g., Bloor, Harbord) and watch for black ice near intersections.
- **Transit payment**: Keep your [[entities/presto]] card topped up via the **PRESTO** app; it works on TTC, GO, and local **Bike Share Toronto** stations.
- **Apps for live updates**: Use [[entities/transit-app]] for real‑time TTC service alerts, and [[entities/weather-network]] (or Environment Canada) for weather warnings that may affect travel.
- **Alternate routes**: On severe ice days, consider a shorter loop around your block with a café stop, or shift the walk indoors (light‑therapy, stretching) as a backup.

---

## 3. Bag Essentials for Winter Mornings
- Lip balm and hand cream (dry air can crack skin)
- Pack of tissues (runny noses are common)
- One pair of dry socks (store in a small waterproof pouch)
- Portable phone charger / power bank (cold batteries drain faster)
- Spare gloves (in case the outer pair gets wet)
- Small reusable water bottle (helps with hydration even in cold weather)

---

## 5. Integrating with Daily Routines
- **Morning habit anchor**: Turn on a warm light, drink water, and step outside for a minute of fresh air – this triggers the body’s wake‑up response even before sunrise.
- **Nightly preparation**: Lay out coat, boots, and coffee mug the night before; this reduces friction when leaving the house in the dark.
- **Exploration loop timing**: Each of the seven weekday walks (see the source document) should include a brief check of [[entities/weather-network]] for ice warnings and a quick glance at [[entities/transit-app]] for any service disruptions.

---

By following this checklist and using the recommended tools, you can turn Toronto’s harsh winter mornings into manageable, even enjoyable, part of your routine.
