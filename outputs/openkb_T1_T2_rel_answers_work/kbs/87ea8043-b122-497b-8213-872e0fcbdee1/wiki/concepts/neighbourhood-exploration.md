# Neighbourhood Exploration

**Definition**: A repeatable, time‑boxed method for learning the layout, amenities, and character of a new urban area by walking short loops and making focal stops.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Purpose‑driven routes** – each walk has a theme (e.g., cafés, parks, quiet streets) that gives the outing direction and prevents aimless wandering.
2. **Time boxing** – clear start and end times (typically 30‑60 minutes) that fit around a work schedule, ensuring the activity feels manageable.
3. **Looped paths** – begin and finish at the same home base, reducing complexity and guaranteeing a safe return before work begins.
4. **Strategic checkpoints** – a single stop for coffee, a brief sit‑down, or a quick observation point that anchors the walk.
5. **Fallback options** – short block loops or indoor alternatives for icy conditions, preserving the habit without risking safety.

## Benefits

- **Rapid familiarisation** – tenable for newcomers needing to locate cafés, transit stops, and safe routes.
- **Habit formation** – regular, predictable outings reinforce a sense of belonging and reduce the intimidation of a new city.
- **Flexibility** – the structure accommodates weather changes; ice‑day alternatives keep the routine alive.
- **Efficiency** – time‑boxed loops ensure the activity never conflicts with work commitments.

---

## Implementation Tips

1. **Map the loops** using a digital map (e.g., Google Maps) before heading out.
2. **Set reminders** on a phone calendar with the exact start/end times.
3. **Track progress** in a simple journal or note‑taking app to note favourite spots.
4. **Adjust weekly** – after a few cycles, modify routes based on discovered preferences (new cafés, quieter streets, better lighting).
