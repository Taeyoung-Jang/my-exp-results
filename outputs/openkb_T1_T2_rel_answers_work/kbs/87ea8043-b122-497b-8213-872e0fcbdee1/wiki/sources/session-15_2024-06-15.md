**User:** I’m trying to get better at coordinating plans with friends who live in different places—do you have a good system for managing time zones and picking meetup times that won’t accidentally land in someone’s “way too late” hours?

**Assistant:** A simple approach is to pick one “home” time zone as the reference—usually yours or the one most people share—and always convert from that instead of juggling everyone’s zones at once. Before suggesting a time, quickly check each friend’s local hour using a world‑clock app or by adding their cities to your phone’s clock. It helps to remember each person’s general waking window so you can mentally flag anything that drifts too early or too late.

If you want something more automatic, tools like When2Meet, Calendly, or Google Calendar’s “time zone” option can show overlap without you doing the math. The key is to start with a small range of possible times, check the overlaps, and then float only the options that fall into everyone’s reasonable hours.

**User:** Could you recommend one specific tool or workflow (ideally something simple) that works well for a small group of 4–6 people across 2–3 time zones, and explain how you’d set it up step by step?

**Assistant:** When2Meet is probably the easiest option for a small group, and you don’t need accounts or any setup beyond creating a single link.

The workflow is basically: you create a new availability grid on the site, choose your own time zone as the reference, give the event a name, and set the days you’re considering. When2Meet automatically displays everyone else’s availability in their own local time once they click the link and mark their free hours, so you never have to think about conversions yourself. Each person just drags across the blocks of time they’re available, and the site builds a shared heat map that shows the best overlapping hours.

In practice, it feels like: you make the link, drop it into the group chat, everyone marks their times whenever they get around to it, and then you look at the darkest overlapping section and pick a slot from there. That’s it—no math, no juggling zones, and no back‑and‑forth guessing.

**User:** Nice—when you create the When2Meet grid, how wide of a time window would you set (like 9am–9pm), and do you have any tips for handling someone who doesn’t fill it out until the last minute?

**Assistant:** For most groups, a window like 9am to 9pm in the event’s reference time zone works well because it comfortably spans everyone’s typical waking hours without making the grid huge. If your friends are spread farther apart, you can stretch it a little, but it’s usually better to keep it narrow so you don’t end up with a giant sheet of unusable early‑morning or late‑night blocks.

For the person who fills it out late, the easiest trick is to set a soft deadline when you share the link—something like “fill this out by tomorrow evening so we can lock a time.” Most people respond better when there’s a clear expectation. If they still don’t get to it, you can either nudge them once or just pick the best slot based on the people who did respond and ask if they can make that time. Usually they’ll go with it, and if not, you can adjust, but at least you avoid getting stuck waiting forever.
