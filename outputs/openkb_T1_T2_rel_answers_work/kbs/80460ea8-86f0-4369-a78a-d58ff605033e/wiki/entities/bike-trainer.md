# Bike Trainer

A **bike trainer** is a stationary cycling apparatus that lets athletes ride their own road bike indoors, providing a controlled environment for consistent mileage and intensity.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-12]]
- Used daily to log roughly **25 miles** of trainer work, forming the core of the user’s endurance volume.
- Serves as the **weekday “steady rhythm”** in a periodized plan that balances cardio and strength.
- Integrated with a **Monday lower‑body strength session**; the lift is performed *after* the trainer ride to preserve ride quality and protect Tuesday’s easy spin.
- Acts as a low‑stress anchor that allows the weekend **long run** to remain the primary high‑intensity block.

## Relationships to Other Concepts & Entities
- Supports the concept of **[[concepts/training_periodization]]** by providing a repeatable, moderate‑intensity stimulus each weekday.
- Enables **[[concepts/stress_clustering]]** by grouping leg‑intensive work (trainer ride + lift) early in the week.
- Facilitates **[[concepts/recovery_feedback_loops]]**; the rider monitors leg fatigue on Tuesday’s spin to decide whether to dial back Monday’s strength load.
- Often paired with equipment such as **[[entities/barbell]]**, **[[entities/dumbbells]]**, and **[[entities/cable-machine]]** during concurrent strength sessions.

## Typical Use in the Weekly Schedule
| Day | Activity |
|-----|----------|
| Monday | Trainer ride (≈25 mi) → lower‑body lift |
| Tuesday | Easy spin (recovery) |
| Wednesday | Trainer ride + core/upper‑body work |
| Thursday | Steady trainer ride |
| Friday | Full recovery (rest or light movement) |
| Saturday | Long outdoor run |
| Sunday | Optional easy spin or rest |

The bike trainer thus acts as the backbone of the endurance component, offering consistent volume while allowing strategic placement of strength work and recovery.
