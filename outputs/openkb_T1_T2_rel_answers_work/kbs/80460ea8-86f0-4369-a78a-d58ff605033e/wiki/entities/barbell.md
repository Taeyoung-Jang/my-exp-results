## Barbell

A **barbell** is a piece of strength‑training equipment consisting of a long steel rod onto which weight plates are loaded. It is a core product for a wide range of resistance exercises.

### Key facts from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-12]]
- **Primary lifts**: Used for back squats and hip thrusts in the Monday lower‑body workout designed for a half‑Ironman preparation.
- **Load management**: The session specifies an RPE of ~7 (moderate effort) and integrates the barbell work into a **stress clustering** approach, grouping leg‑intensive work early in the week.
- **Periodization**: The barbell routine fits within a weekly **training periodization** plan that balances bike volume, strength, and recovery.
- **Recovery feedback**: A simple **recovery feedback loop** is suggested – if legs feel dead on Tuesday’s easy spin, the barbell load should be reduced by ~10 % or a set removed.
- **Effort metrics**: RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) is used as the primary **effort metric** to gauge intensity.

### Typical exercises (from the session)
1. **Barbell back squat** – warm‑up sets followed by 3 working sets of 5‑6 reps.
2. **Barbell hip thrust** – 2 sets of 8‑10 reps for posterior‑chain strength.

These lifts are paired with complementary equipment:
- **[[entities/dumbbells]]** for Bulgarian split squats.
- **[[entities/cable-machine]]** for hip activation work.
- Integrated into a broader training schedule that includes a **[[entities/bike-trainer]]** for daily cycling and a long weekend run supporting a **[[entities/half-ironman]]** goal.

### Relevance
The barbell is central to the strength component of the weekly plan, enabling targeted lower‑body development while respecting the overall endurance focus. Its use exemplifies how classic strength tools can be harmonized with high‑volume endurance training through thoughtful periodization, effort metrics, and recovery feedback loops.
