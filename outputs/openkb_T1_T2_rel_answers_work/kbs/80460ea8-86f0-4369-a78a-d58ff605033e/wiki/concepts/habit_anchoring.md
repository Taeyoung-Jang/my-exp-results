# Habit Anchoring

Habit anchoring is the practice of tying a new habit to an already established, reliable daily cue. By anchoring a behavior to a predictable event—such as running immediately after waking up—you reduce the decision load and increase the likelihood of automatic execution.

## How it appears in the source

In [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-15]], the assistant recommends running right after waking as an anchor for a five‑mile morning jog. This cue makes the run part of the morning routine, turning it into a low‑effort decision.

## Key mechanisms

- **Cue‑behavior link**: The cue (e.g., getting out of bed) consistently triggers the behavior (the run).
- **Consistency over intensity**: The focus is on showing up rather than on speed or distance, which aligns with the principle of low [[concepts/effort_metrics]] effort for habit formation.
- **Minimal disruption**: Variations (tempo, strides, route changes) are introduced within the anchored run without altering the anchor itself, supporting sustainable [[concepts/running_variation]].

## Practical tips

1. Identify a stable daily event that already occurs without fail.
2. Attach the new running habit immediately before or after that event.
3. Keep the initial effort easy (RPE 3‑4) to reinforce the cue‑habit loop.
4. Introduce small internal variations (tempo segment, strides, surges) while preserving the external anchor.

By using habit anchoring, runners can maintain a consistent schedule, reduce burnout, and create a foundation for gradual performance gains.
