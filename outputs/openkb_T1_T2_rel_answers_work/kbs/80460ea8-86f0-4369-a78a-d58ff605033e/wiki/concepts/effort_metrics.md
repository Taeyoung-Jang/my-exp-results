# Effort Metrics (RPE & Talk Test)

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Practical ways to quantify running effort using the Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE) scale and the conversational "talk test".

---

## Overview

Effort metrics give runners an intuitive, equipment‑free method to monitor how hard they are working. Two common, complementary tools are:

1. **Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE)** – a numeric scale (typically 1‑10) where the runner rates how intense the effort feels. In the context of the 5‑mile plan from [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-15]], easy runs sit at **RPE 3‑4** (easy, full‑conversation), tempo work at **RPE 6** (comfortably hard, short phrases), and short bursts (strides or surges) at **RPE 6‑7** (hard but controlled).
2. **Talk Test** – a qualitative check of breathing and speech. It aligns closely with RPE levels:
   - **Full conversation** (easy) → matches RPE 3‑4.
   - **Short phrases** (moderate) → matches RPE 6.
   - **Breath‑caught, limited talk** (hard) → matches RPE 6‑7.

Both metrics are easy to apply during any run and help ensure the training stays within a low‑injury‑risk zone.

---

## Why Use Both?

- **RPE** provides a precise numeric reference that can be logged and compared over weeks.
- **Talk Test** offers a quick, real‑time cue without needing to think about numbers, especially useful when the runner’s attention is on the environment or form.
- Together they reinforce each other; a runner can verify that an RPE 6 feeling also corresponds to being able to speak in short phrases, creating a reliable internal gauge.

---

## Application in the 7‑Day Plan

| Day | Segment | RPE | Talk Test |
|-----|---------|-----|-----------|
| 1,3,5,7 | Easy 5‑mi | 3‑4 | Full conversation |
| 2 | Mid‑mile tempo | 6 | Short phrases |
| 4 | Strides (last 0.5 mi) | 6‑7 | Breath‑caught, limited talk |
| 6 | Surges (mid‑mile) | 7 | Hard, brief talk |

By following these targets, the runner adds enough stimulus to avoid plateaus while keeping overall injury risk low.
