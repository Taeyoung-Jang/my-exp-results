# Recovery Feedback Loops

**Definition**: A systematic process of monitoring short‑term recovery cues (e.g., perceived fatigue, performance on the next session) and using them to adjust training variables such as volume, intensity, or exercise selection for the upcoming workouts.

---

## Core Elements
1. **Signal Collection** – Simple, actionable metrics (RPE, how the legs feel in the first 10 minutes of a ride, heart‑rate variability, etc.).
2. **Interpretation Rule** – Pre‑defined thresholds that trigger a specific response (e.g., reduce load by ~10 % if legs feel "dead").
3. **Adjustment Action** – Concrete change to the next session’s plan: lower weight, drop a set, insert an extra easy day, etc.
4. **Feedback Loop Closure** – Re‑evaluate the effect of the adjustment in the subsequent session to confirm the rule’s validity.

---

## Application in Session‑38 (2025‑02‑12)
In the half‑Ironman preparation plan, a clear **dial‑back rule** was introduced:
- If, on Tuesday’s easy spin, the legs feel noticeably dead within the first ten minutes, the athlete should **reduce the following Monday’s squat load by ~10 % or trim one set**.
- This rule directly ties a perceptual recovery cue to a quantifiable training modification, creating a rapid feedback loop that protects the weekend run while still providing strength stimulus.

The approach exemplifies a **low‑tech, RPE‑based feedback loop** that can be applied across sports without needing sophisticated monitoring tools.

## Practical Tips
- **Keep it simple**: Choose one or two easy‑to‑measure signals.
- **Define triggers** clearly before training starts.
- **Document outcomes** after each adjustment to refine the rule over time.
