# Summary of session‑07_2024‑06‑15

- Discussed **habit formation** for consistent running and how to keep a fixed 5‑mile morning routine fresh.  
- Emphasized **habit anchoring** [[concepts/habit_anchoring]], **manageable effort**, and **variation without breaking consistency**.  
- Presented a concrete **7‑day plan** that keeps the 5‑mile distance and timing unchanged while adding:
  - **Tempo segment** (mid‑mile, effort measured with RPE or talk test) – a type of **running variation** [[concepts/running_variation]].
  - **Strides** (last 0.5 mi, 4‑6 × 15‑20 s, higher effort) – a **running variation** [[concepts/running_variation]].
  - **Surges** (mid‑week, three 20‑30 s bursts, higher effort) – a **running variation** [[concepts/running_variation]].
  - **Route or focus changes** for mental freshness – another form of **running variation** [[concepts/running_variation]].
- All other days are **easy recovery runs** (RPE 3‑4, full conversation) to limit injury risk – effort described using **effort metrics** [[concepts/effort_metrics]].
- Uses **RPE** and **talk test** as effort metrics – see **effort metrics** [[concepts/effort_metrics]].
- Aims to avoid **plateau** and **burnout** by providing small, periodic stressors while preserving the core habit – achieved through the described **running variation** [[concepts/running_variation]].
- Highlights the role of **mental focus** (breathing, form, scenery) as a low‑impact variation – also covered under **running variation** [[concepts/running_variation]].

The plan can be adapted by swapping the order of tempo/strides/surges or adjusting effort based on personal fitness.
