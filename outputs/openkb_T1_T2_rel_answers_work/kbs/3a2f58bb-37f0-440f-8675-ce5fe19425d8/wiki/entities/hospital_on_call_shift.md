# Hospital On‑Call Shift

A **hospital on‑call shift** is an event‑type work schedule where medical staff must be ready to respond to emergencies at any hour, often resulting in highly irregular sleep, meal, and activity patterns.

## Key Characteristics (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-01-09]])
- **Irregular timing**: Shifts can start at any hour, e.g., a phone may ring at 2 a.m. one night and at noon the next.
- **Fragmented sleep**: Staff often sleep in short “scraps” and must fall asleep quickly.
- **Unpredictable meals**: Eating windows shift, leading to missed meals or the need for rapid nutrition.
- **High‑adrenaline moments**: Late‑night calls trigger adrenaline spikes that can hinder returning to sleep.

## Related Anchor Strategies
- **[[concepts/anchor_habits]]**: Tiny, repeatable cues (pre‑sleep wind‑down, morning reset, emergency snack, adrenaline de‑escalation) that help maintain physiological stability.
- **[[concepts/pre_sleep_wind_down]]**: A quick sequence (dim lights, warm water splash, slow breathing, phone out of reach) to fall asleep fast.
- **[[concepts/morning_reset]]**: Post‑wake micro‑ritual (water, light exposure, gentle stretch) to re‑orient the body.
- **[[concepts/emergency_snack]]**: Portable nutrition options like a **[[entities/protein_bar]]**, **[[entities/instant_oatmeal_cup]]**, or mixed nuts kept near the **[[entities/nurses_station]]**.
- **[[concepts/adrenaline_deescalation]]**: Breathing and muscle‑relaxation steps to counter post‑call adrenaline.

## Typical Resources on‑Shift
- **[[entities/stethoscope]]** and **[[entities/pen_light]]** are kept within easy reach for rapid response.
- Snack stash often stored alongside the **[[entities/stethoscope]]** or in the **[[entities/nurses_station]]** for quick access.

## Impact on Well‑Being
Implementing the above anchor habits supports **[[concepts/emotional_stability]]** and overall health despite the chaotic nature of the on‑call schedule.
