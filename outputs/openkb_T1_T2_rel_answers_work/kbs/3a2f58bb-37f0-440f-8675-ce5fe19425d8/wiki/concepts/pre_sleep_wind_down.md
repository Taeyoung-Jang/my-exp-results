# Pre‑Sleep Wind‑Down

**Definition**: A short, repeatable sequence of actions that signals the nervous system to transition into sleep, regardless of the clock time.

---

## Core Principles
- **Consistency over timing** – the same steps each time train the brain to recognize a shutdown cue. [[concepts/anchor_habits]]
- **Minimal duration** – designed to fit into 5‑10 minutes, ideal for unpredictable schedules.
- **Sensory modulation** – dim lighting, warm water, and controlled breathing reduce arousal.
- **Device detachment** – removing visual and auditory stimuli prevents cognitive engagement.

---

## Typical 5‑Minute Routine (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-01-09]])
1. **Dim the lights** or move to a low‑light area to lower visual input.
2. **Splash the face with warm water** – a quick temperature cue that signals the body to relax.
3. **Breathing sequence**: six slow breaths, inhaling for ~3 seconds and exhaling for ~6 seconds. This lengthens the exhale, activating the parasympathetic system.
4. **Phone placement**: put the phone face‑down and out of reach (e.g., pocket, drawer). This removes the expectation of incoming alerts.
5. **Slip into bed** immediately, avoiding additional steps that could re‑stimulate the mind.

---

## Why It Works
- **Physiological anchoring**: Repeating the same stimuli creates a conditioned response, linking the routine to melatonin release and reduced cortisol.
- **Adrenaline mitigation**: When combined with a brief [[concepts/adrenaline_deescalation]] after a late‑night call, the wind‑down helps dissipate residual sympathetic activation.
- **Mental decluttering**: The routine serves as a mental “off‑switch,” preparing the brain for the dream state.

---

## Connections to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader set of [[concepts/anchor_habits]] that include [[concepts/morning_reset]] and [[concepts/emergency_snack]] for overall schedule resilience.
- Complements [[concepts/grounding_techniques]] by providing a bodily anchor at the end of the day.
- Supports [[concepts/emotional_stability]] by reducing sleep‑related stress and anxiety.

---

## Practical Tips
- Keep a small **lamp** or **night‑light** with adjustable brightness near the bed.
- Store a **warm water bottle** or have a quick‑fill kettle to avoid delays.
- Place a **notepad** next to the bed for the brief brain‑dump step (optional) before the wind‑down.
