# Greek Yogurt

**Type:** product

Greek yogurt is a strained, high‑protein dairy product that retains the creamy texture of regular yogurt while delivering roughly twice the protein per serving. In the context of the **session‑35_2025‑03‑13** morning routine, it serves as a core component of a [[concepts/steady-energy-breakfast]] because it provides a quick, low‑sugar source of protein that helps maintain stable blood‑sugar levels throughout the day.

## Key facts from [[summaries/session-35_2025-03-13]]
- **Portion recommendation:** 1 cup (≈ 240 ml) of plain Greek yogurt.
- **Protein contribution:** ~15–17 g per cup; when paired with 2 Tbsp of [[entities/nuts]] the total reaches ~22–24 g, matching the target 20–25 g protein per breakfast.
- **Role in the 5‑day rotation:** Used on Monday (Greek yogurt + nuts) and Thursday (as a side with avocado toast & eggs) to keep the morning meal quick, low‑sweetness, and nutritionally balanced.
- **Energy impact:** The high protein content supports steady energy and reduces the likelihood of an afternoon crash, tying into [[concepts/energy-crash-troubleshooting]].

## Practical considerations
- **Convenience:** Comes in single‑serve containers that are ready‑to‑eat; no cooking or reheating required.
- **Flavor:** Plain version is preferred to avoid excess sugar; optional toppings include [[entities/nuts]], a drizzle of honey (if desired), or a small spoonful of [[entities/cottage-cheese]] for extra protein.
- **Allergy note:** As a dairy product, it is unsuitable for those with lactose intolerance or dairy allergies; alternatives could include fortified plant‑based yogurts, though they were not part of the original plan.
