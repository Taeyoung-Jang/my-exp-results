## Caffeine (other)

Caffeine is a naturally occurring psychoactive compound that stimulates the central nervous system. It is the primary active ingredient in many popular beverages such as [[entities/coffee]], [[entities/decaf-coffee]] (in reduced amounts), and [[entities/tea]].

### Role in [[summaries/session-01_2024-09-14]]
- The conversation focuses on distinguishing a comforting **caffeine ritual** from **caffeine dependence**.
- The user experiences withdrawal headaches when skipping the second cup of coffee, indicating a physiological dependence on caffeine.
- An incremental **caffeine tapering** plan is proposed to reduce intake while preserving the ritual and protecting sleep.

### Associated Concepts
- [[concepts/caffeine-ritual]]: The habitual, sensory enjoyment of consuming caffeinated drinks.
- [[concepts/caffeine-dependence]]: The body's adapted need for caffeine, evidenced by headaches and fog when missed.
- [[concepts/caffeine-tapering]]: A gradual reduction strategy (≈10‑20 % every 3‑5 days) to lower caffeine consumption safely.

### Related Entities
- [[entities/coffee]] – the primary source of caffeine discussed.
- [[entities/decaf-coffee]] – used as a tool in the tapering plan to maintain ritual with less caffeine.
- [[entities/tea]] – suggested as a milder caffeine source or alternative.

### Key Facts from the Session
- Timing is crucial: caffeine should be stopped by early afternoon to avoid interference with sleep hygiene.
- A step‑wise reduction, changing one variable at a time (amount, strength, timing), helps minimize withdrawal symptoms.
- If a headache occurs during a step‑down, a temporary increase in caffeine is acceptable to alleviate the symptom before resuming the taper.
