# Hard‑Boiled Egg

**Type:** product

## Overview
A hard‑boiled egg is a boiled egg that has been cooked until the white and yolk are fully set, then cooled and peeled for convenient, ready‑to‑eat consumption. It provides a high‑quality source of protein, essential vitamins, and minerals with minimal preparation time.

## Role in Session 35 (2025‑03‑13)
- Featured as a core component of the **morning breakfast rotation** aimed at delivering **~20–25 g protein** per day.
- Paired with other items such as [[entities/cottage-cheese]], [[entities/greek-yogurt]], [[entities/nuts]], and whole‑grain toast to meet protein targets without a large meal volume.
- Recommended on **Tuesday** (Eggs + cottage cheese) and **Thursday** (Avocado toast + eggs) in the checklist that supports steady energy throughout the day.
- Highlighted as a quick, no‑cook, low‑sugar option that helps prevent **[[concepts/energy-crash-troubleshooting]]** caused by insufficient protein intake.

## Nutritional Snapshot (per large egg, ~50 g)
- **Protein:** ~6 g
- **Calories:** ~78 kcal
- **Fat:** ~5 g (mostly unsaturated)
- **Key micronutrients:** Vitamin B12, riboflavin, selenium, choline.

## Practical Tips
- **Portioning for protein goals:** Two eggs contribute ~12 g protein; combine with ½ cup [[entities/cottage-cheese]] (≈12–14 g) to reach the 24–26 g target for a balanced breakfast.
- **Storage:** Keep refrigerated; they stay fresh for up to a week.
- **Variations:** Slice on top of [[entities/whole-grain-toast]] with avocado, or serve alongside [[entities/turkey]] or [[entities/chicken]] for a savory snack.
