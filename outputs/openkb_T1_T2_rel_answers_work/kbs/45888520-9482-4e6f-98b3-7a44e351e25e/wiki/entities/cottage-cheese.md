# Cottage Cheese

**Type:** product  
**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-35_2025-03-13]]

Cottage cheese is a fresh cheese curd product known for its high protein content and low fat (when low‑fat varieties are chosen). In the discussed morning routine it is used as a quick, no‑cook breakfast component to help reach a target of 20‑25 g protein.

## Key facts from the session

- **Portion for ~20 g protein:** 1 cup (≈ 225 g) provides about 22–24 g protein.
- **Alternative portion:** ¾ cup (≈ 170 g) + a small handful of [[entities/nuts]] can raise protein to the 20–22 g range.
- **Meal role:** Paired with other items (e.g., hard‑boiled eggs, Greek yogurt, avocado toast) to meet daily protein goals without heavy cooking.
- **Energy impact:** Contributes to the [[concepts/steady-energy-breakfast]] strategy, helping prevent mid‑afternoon energy crashes identified in [[concepts/energy-crash-troubleshooting]].

## Typical pairings in the rotation

- With [[entities/hard-boiled-egg]] (Tuesday) – 2 eggs + ½ cup cottage cheese → 24–26 g protein.
- Stand‑alone (Wednesday) – 1 cup cottage cheese → 22–24 g protein.
- Added to toast & eggs (Thursday) – small side of cottage cheese to boost protein.

## Nutrition snapshot (per 1 cup, approx.)

- Protein: 22–24 g
- Calories: ~200 kcal (varies by fat content)
- Calcium: ~15 % DV
- Low sugar, high calcium, moderate sodium.

Cottage cheese fits easily into a 10‑15 minute morning routine, requiring no cooking or reheating, and aligns with the overall goal of maintaining steady energy throughout the day.
