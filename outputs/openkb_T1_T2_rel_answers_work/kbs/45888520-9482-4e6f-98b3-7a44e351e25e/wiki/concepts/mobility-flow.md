# Mobility Flow

**Definition**: A short, low‑impact mobility routine designed to wake up the joints, support steady morning energy, and help prevent mid‑day crashes.

## Core Structure (5 minutes total)
| Time | Movement & Form Cue |
|------|----------------------|
| 0:00–1:00 | Neck circles & shoulder rolls – keep ribs relaxed, shoulders loose, stay within a pain‑free range. |
| 1:00–2:00 | Cat‑cow (floor or standing) – soft elbows, let the belly drop on the “cow,” gently round the spine on the “cat.” If knees are uncomfortable, perform the motion standing with a slight bend. |
| 2:00–3:00 | Hip circles or gentle leg swings – core lightly engaged, hold a wall or countertop for balance if needed. |
| 3:00–4:00 | Forward fold – deep knee bend, head hangs heavy, avoid pulling down. Optional back‑of‑chair hamstring stretch if the fold irritates the back. |
| 4:00–5:00 | Standing twist – hips stay forward, twist originates from the upper back, move slowly and keep the motion small if tightness is felt. |

**Alternatives** are provided for each segment to accommodate back or knee sensitivities.

## Purpose & Benefits
- **Energy stability**: Gentle joint activation primes circulation without triggering a stress response, supporting the steady‑energy goals outlined in [[summaries/session-35_2025-03-13]].
- **Crash prevention**: By incorporating movement early, the routine helps mitigate the factors discussed in [[concepts/energy-crash-troubleshooting]].
- **Time‑efficiency**: Fits within a 5‑minute window, making it easy to adopt consistently.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works hand‑in‑hand with a high‑protein, low‑sugar morning meal as described in [[concepts/steady-energy-breakfast]].
- Forms part of a broader habit‑stacking approach that can include caffeine‑related rituals (see [[concepts/caffeine-ritual]]) for those who use caffeine strategically.

## Implementation Tips
1. Perform the flow **after** a brief morning walk or hydration (e.g., a glass of water).  
2. Keep movements **smooth** and **controlled**; avoid bouncing or forcing range of motion.  
3. Use a timer or a simple playlist with 5‑minute length to stay on schedule.  
4. If any movement causes pain, switch to the suggested alternative or skip that segment.
