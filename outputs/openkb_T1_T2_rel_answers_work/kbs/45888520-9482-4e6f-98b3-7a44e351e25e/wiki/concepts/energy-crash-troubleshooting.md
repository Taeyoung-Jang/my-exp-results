# Energy Crash Troubleshooting

## Definition
A systematic approach to pinpointing and remedying the routine factors that cause a sudden dip in energy during the afternoon.

## Core triggers identified in [[summaries/session-35_2025-03-13]]
- **Insufficient protein at breakfast** – skipping or eating a low‑protein meal can leave blood‑sugar without a stable fuel source.
- **Carb‑heavy lunch without balance** – meals high in refined carbs can create a rapid glucose spike followed by a crash.
- **Dehydration** – even mild fluid loss reduces circulation and cognitive alertness.
- **Delayed eating** – waiting too long after the morning walk before fueling can lead to an energy dip.
- **Over‑intense early exercise** – jumping into high‑intensity activity before the body is warmed up can deplete glycogen stores.

## Step‑by‑step troubleshooting checklist
1. **Assess breakfast protein**
   - Aim for 20–25 g protein (see [[concepts/steady-energy-breakfast]] for portion ideas).
2. **Review lunch composition**
   - Pair carbs with protein and healthy fats to avoid rapid glucose swings.
3. **Hydration check**
   - Drink water consistently; a good baseline is ~2 L spread throughout the day.
4. **Evaluate morning activity intensity**
   - Keep the post‑walk routine gentle; refer to [[concepts/mobility-flow]] for low‑impact movements.
5. **Monitor caffeine use** (if applicable)
   - Excessive reliance can mask underlying fuel gaps; consider [[concepts/caffeine-dependence]] for context.
6. **Adjust if symptoms persist**
   - If crashes continue, experiment with a small mid‑morning snack containing protein and fiber.
