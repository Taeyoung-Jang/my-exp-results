# Follow‑up Cadence

**Definition**: A structured timing plan for sending follow‑up communications after submitting a job application, aimed at maintaining interest without appearing overly eager.

## Core timing recommendations
- **First follow‑up** – Approximately **one week** after the application submission. A brief, polite check‑in to reaffirm interest and offer additional information.
- **Second (final) follow‑up** – Around **two weeks** after the original application (or 1.5–2 weeks after the first follow‑up). This serves as a last inquiry about the hiring timeline.
- **Beyond two attempts** – If there is still no response, shift focus back to new applications while keeping the current one in the pipeline for possible later contact.

## Message tone and content
- Keep the language courteous and succinct.
- Reference the specific role and company name.
- Offer to provide any missing information or answer questions.
- Avoid repetitive language; each follow‑up should add a slight new value (e.g., a recent project relevance).

## Channel considerations
- **Company portal (no direct contact)**: Send the same template to the generic hiring inbox or listed recruiting address. Expect slower, less personal replies.
- **Direct recruiter or hiring manager**: Address the individual by name; the personalized message typically yields higher response rates.

## Relationship to other job‑search concepts
- Works in tandem with a **[[concepts/daily-job-search-schedule]]** to ensure follow‑up tasks are allocated in the afternoon light‑work block.
- Complements the **[[concepts/cover-letter-checklist]]** by reminding users to verify contact details before sending follow‑ups.
- Relies on an organized **[[concepts/application-tracking-spreadsheet]]** to track dates of application, planned follow‑up, and status updates.
