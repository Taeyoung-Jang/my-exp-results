# Quality Assurance

**Quality Assurance** (QA) in the context of daily blueprint work is a focused set of checks that guarantees technical drawings meet project requirements while allowing room for creative thinking. The concept is outlined in the [[summaries/session-10_2024-08-14]] and is central to the **Daily Blueprint Workflow**.

## Core Principles
- **Simplicity:** A short, repeatable checklist that can be executed quickly at the end of each work segment.
- **Coverage:** Key attributes of a drawing that must be verified before release.
- **Speed vs. Perfection:** QA stops when the drawing satisfies all functional criteria; further tweaks that only affect appearance trigger the *stop‑polishing rule*.

## Quick QA Checklist (from the template)
- **Dimensions** – clear, consistent, and accurate.
- **Scale** – correctly marked and applied.
- **Alignment** – major elements positioned properly.
- **Annotations** – readable, relevant, and up‑to‑date.
- **Revision History** – complete and correct.

These items address the most common blueprint errors captured in [[concepts/common_blueprint_mistakes]].

## Relationship to Workflow Rhythm
The QA steps are placed at the **End‑of‑Day Handoff** stage of the [[entities/daily-blueprint-workflow]], following a **creative warm‑up** in the morning that nurtures the [[concepts/creative_rhythm]] of alternating between imagination and discipline.

## When to Stop Polishing
- All QA items are satisfied.
- The drawing meets the stated project requirements.
- Any additional changes would only affect aesthetics without improving function or clarity.

Adhering to this rule prevents endless revisions and ensures timely delivery.
