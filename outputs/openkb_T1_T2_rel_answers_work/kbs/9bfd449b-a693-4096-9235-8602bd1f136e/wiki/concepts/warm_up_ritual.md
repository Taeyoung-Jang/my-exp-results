# Warm‑Up Ritual

A **warm‑up ritual** is a short, repeatable activity performed at the very beginning of a protected focus period to cue the brain that work time has begun. It helps transition from personal mode to professional mode, reduces friction, and builds momentum for the ensuing [[concepts/deep_work]] session.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]] and [[summaries/session-34_2024-10-15]])
- **Timing:**
  - Typical morning window: 5 minutes (e.g., 9:00‑9:05).
  - When a day begins with a multi‑time‑zone meeting window, the ritual can be extended to 10 minutes (e.g., 7:50‑8:00) to include a quick planning step before the standups.
- **Typical actions:** making coffee or tea, a quick stretch, reviewing the day's primary outcome, or a brief glance at the plan. In the pre‑standup version, you also define the top priority and what success looks like for the day.
- **No inbox:** During the ritual you keep notifications off and avoid opening messages.

## Why it matters
- Signals a mental shift, making it easier to enter a state of flow.
- Establishes a **protected focus time** routine that can be reliably repeated each day ([[concepts/protected_focus_time]]).
- Reduces decision fatigue by having a pre‑planned starter activity.
- When placed before a [[concepts/meeting_window]], it primes you for focused participation and prevents early‑day energy drain.

## Interaction with other concepts
- After the ritual you launch into the first deep‑work block ([[concepts/deep_work]]).
- The ritual precedes the **message sweep** and any triage activities later in the day ([[concepts/message_sweep]], [[concepts/triage_checklist]]).
- It supports the use of scripted responses for urgent interruptions by creating a clear boundary period ([[concepts/urgent_message_scripts]]).
- A brief **reset break** after meetings (e.g., 2‑10 minutes) helps maintain the momentum built by the warm‑up before diving into deep work ([[concepts/reset_break]]).

## Practical checklist
1. Choose a consistent activity (brew coffee, stretch, review goal, define success).
2. Perform it for 3‑10 minutes immediately after the workday start or before the meeting window.
3. Keep digital devices muted; do not check email or Slack.
4. Finish by stating the single outcome you will tackle first.

Implementing a warm‑up ritual can make your morning routine more predictable, boost productivity, and create a solid foundation for both meeting windows and deep‑work blocks.
