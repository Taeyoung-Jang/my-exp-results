# Knee‑Friendly Strength Conditioning

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** A set of low‑impact lower‑body exercises designed to strengthen the hips, glutes, and supporting musculature while minimizing stress on the patellofemoral joint.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Hip‑dominant loading** – Emphasize movements that drive from the hips rather than forcing the knee through a deep flexion.
2. **Controlled eccentric phase** – Slow, deliberate lowering (eccentric) reduces impact forces on the kneecap.
3. **Limited knee angle** – Keep the knee bend at roughly 90° or less during step‑ups and sit‑backs.
4. **Stable alignment** – Use light hand or rail support only for balance, never to pull the body upward.
5. **Progressive volume** – Start with modest sets/reps and increase only when pain‑free.

---

## Recommended Exercise Set (used in [[summaries/session-33_2025-03-11]])

| Exercise | Sets | Reps / Time | Key Cue |
|----------|------|------------|---------|
| Supported split‑stance sit‑backs | 2 | 8‑10 each side | Push hips back, keep torso tall, avoid deep knee bend |
| Low step‑ups (6‑10 in) | 2 | 8 each leg | Step up to a knee angle ≤ 90°, control the descent, optional light rail hold |
| Glute bridges | 2 | 12‑15 | Squeeze glutes at top, keep spine neutral |
| Core hold (plank or dead‑bug) | 1‑2 | 20‑30 s | Maintain neutral spine, avoid hip sagging |

### Adjustments for Knee Comfort
- If any anterior knee pain appears, lower the step height or reduce range of motion.
- Replace step‑ups with a hip‑dominant move (e.g., single‑leg dead‑lift) on flare‑up days.
- Keep the weight light; body‑weight or a light dumbbell is sufficient.

---

## Integration in a Training Cycle
The routine is performed **weekly on Tuesdays** as part of an 8‑week stair‑climb preparation plan. It pairs with low‑impact cardio on Mondays and non‑impact treadmill/bike sessions on Thursdays, supporting the overall progression outlined in [[concepts/stair-climb-training-progression]] and the interval design described in [[concepts/treadmill-interval-design]].

---

## Red‑Flag Indicators
- Sharp or lingering pain at the front of the knee during or after the exercise.
- Swelling or a feeling of the knee “giving way”.
- Inability to complete the prescribed range without compensatory gait changes.

If any of these occur, pause the strength work and replace with gentle cardio until the knee feels normal.
