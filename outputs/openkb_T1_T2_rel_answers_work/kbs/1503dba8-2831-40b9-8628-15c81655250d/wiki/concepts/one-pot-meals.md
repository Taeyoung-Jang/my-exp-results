# One-Pot Meals

**Definition** – One‑pot meals are dishes prepared in a single vessel (typically a pot, sometimes a pot + mug) so that cooking, serving, and cleaning stay confined to one piece of equipment.  This approach is especially valuable in ultra‑small living spaces where countertop area, cookware, and storage are limited.

---

## Core Benefits
- **Space efficiency** – no need for multiple pans, baking sheets, or stovetop burners.
- **Low mess** – only one pot to scrub, reducing cleanup time.
- **Low‑smoke** – many one‑pot recipes rely on simmering rather than high‑heat frying, aligning with [[concepts/low-smoke-cooking]].
- **Meal‑planning simplicity** – easy to portion for a single serving or a small batch, supporting [[concepts/meal-planning]] and the “cook‑once, eat‑once” mindset.
- **Flavor integration** – ingredients cook together, allowing starches, proteins, and sauces to meld in the same liquid, which can reduce the need for additional seasoning containers.

## Tips for Effective One‑Pot Cooking in Tiny Spaces
- Keep the pot covered whenever possible to trap steam and limit smell diffusion.
- Choose ingredients that cook in similar time frames to avoid over‑ or under‑cooking.
- Use gentle heat settings on the hot‑plate to maintain a simmer rather than a vigorous boil.
- Incorporate quick‑finish add‑ins (butter, cream‑cheese, lemon) at the end of cooking for flavor without lingering odor.
