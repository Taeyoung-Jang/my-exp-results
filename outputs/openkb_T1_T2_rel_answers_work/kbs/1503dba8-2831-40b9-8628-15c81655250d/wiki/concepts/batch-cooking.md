# Batch Cooking

**Definition**: Batch cooking is the practice of preparing sizable portions of core ingredients—such as grains, proteins, legumes, and vegetables—in a single dedicated session so they can be combined into multiple meals throughout the week.

## Core Principles
- **Single prep day**: Allocate a short, focused window (e.g., Sunday) to cook all base components.
- **Overlapping ingredients**: Use the same cooked items in varied combinations to keep meals interesting while minimizing waste.
- **Time efficiency**: Once the batch is ready, daily meals can be assembled in 10‑15 minutes.
- **Cost control**: Bulk cooking of inexpensive staples reduces per‑meal cost.

## Example from [[summaries/session-06_2024-07-10]]
The session outlines a practical batch‑cooking routine for one person:
1. **Roast vegetables** – about 2 cups of carrots, broccoli, and onions.
2. **Cook grains** – ½ cup dry [[entities/rice]] (≈1.5 cups cooked).
3. **Simmer legumes** – ½ cup dried [[entities/lentils]] (≈1 cup cooked).
4. **Pan‑sear protein** – ½ block of [[entities/tofu]] seasoned with salt or soy sauce.

All four components are prepared on Sunday, then mixed and matched for lunches and dinners across the week, keeping each meal under 15 minutes of reheating.

## Benefits Highlighted
- **Meal planning**: The batch‑cooked items fit neatly into a [[concepts/meal-planning]] framework, allowing pre‑designed combos for each day.
- **Budget‑friendly**: By focusing on cheap staples like rice, lentils, and tofu, the approach aligns with [[concepts/budget-friendly-meals]].
- **Reduced decision fatigue**: With core components ready, daily cooking requires only minimal seasoning or sauce adjustments.
