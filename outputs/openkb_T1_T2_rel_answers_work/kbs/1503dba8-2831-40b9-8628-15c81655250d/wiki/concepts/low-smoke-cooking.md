# Low-Smoke Cooking

**Definition**: Low-smoke cooking focuses on techniques that generate little to no visible smoke, making it ideal for compact living situations with strict ventilation limits.

## Core Principles
- **Gentle heat**: Favor simmering, steaming, and low‑temperature poaching over high‑heat frying or sautéing.
- **One‑pot focus**: Using a single pot (or pot + mug) reduces surface area for vapor to escape and keeps cleanup minimal. See [[concepts/one-pot-meals]].
- **Lid usage**: Keeping a lid on the pot traps steam, which condenses back into the dish instead of drifting into the room.
- **Ingredient selection**: Choose foods that release little aroma when cooked—e.g., pasta, rice, lentils, frozen vegetables, and pre‑washes greens.
- **Mild flavor enhancers**: Citrus (e.g., [[entities/lemon]]), butter, cream cheese, and parmesan add flavor without lingering strong odors.

## Techniques Highlighted in [[summaries/session-33_2025-03-09]]
1. **One‑Pot Tomato Pasta** – Dry pasta ([[entities/dry-pasta]]) simmered with water, cherry tomatoes or frozen spinach, finished with butter or cream cheese. No frying, just gentle simmer.
2. **Ten‑Minute Lentil Soup** – Broth (or bouillon) with canned [[entities/lentils]], frozen vegetables, and a pinch of thyme or lemon; simmered until hot.
3. **Egg‑Rice Bowl** – Instant rice ([[entities/instant-rice]]) cooked with water, then an [[entities/egg]] stirred in at the end for a silky texture.

All three recipes rely on a [[entities/hot-plate]] and a single pot, avoiding oil‑heavy sauté steps that create smoke.

## Smell‑Reduction Integration
Low-smoke cooking pairs naturally with [[concepts/smell-reduction-techniques]]:
- **Cover the pot** to contain aromas.
- **Ventilation**: Use a small fan toward a window if possible.
- **Immediate cleanup**: Wipe the pot and surrounding surfaces right after cooking.
- **Mild aromatics**: Lemon juice, butter, and parmesan provide flavor without lingering in the air.

## Relationship to Meal Planning
Because each dish can be prepared in a single portion with minimal leftovers, low-smoke cooking supports [[concepts/meal-planning]] strategies that emphasize “cook‑once, eat‑once” or very small leftovers, fitting tightly into a [[entities/mini-fridge]].

## Typical Equipment & Staples
- **[[entities/hot-plate]]** – primary heat source.
- **[[entities/mini-fridge]]** – for storing greens, dairy, and eggs.
- **Dry staples**: [[entities/dry-pasta]], [[entities/instant-rice]], canned [[entities/lentils]].
- **Frozen vegetables** – [[entities/frozen-vegetables]].
- **Dairy**: [[entities/butter]], [[entities/cream-cheese]], [[entities/parmesan]].
- **Fresh add‑ins**: [[entities/lemon]], [[entities/prewashed-greens]], [[entities/egg]].

## Benefits
- **Safety**: Reduces fire‑hazard risk in small kitchens.
- **Compliance**: Meets lease restrictions that prohibit smoke‑heavy cooking.
- **Convenience**: Minimal cleanup and limited equipment.
- **Flavor control**: Allows seasoning without overpowering aromas.
