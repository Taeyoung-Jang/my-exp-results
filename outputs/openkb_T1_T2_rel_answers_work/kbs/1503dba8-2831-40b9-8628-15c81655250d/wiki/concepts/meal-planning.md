# Meal Planning

Meal planning is the process of designing a weekly (or longer) menu and coordinating the required ingredients and cooking steps so that meals can be prepared efficiently, economically, and with minimal waste. The approach described in [[summaries/session-06_2024-07-10]] illustrates a practical, low‑cost strategy built around a small set of core recipes.

## Core Principles
- **Define a few versatile recipes** that share ingredients. In the example, the core recipes are roasted vegetables, a pot of [[entities/rice]], a batch of [[entities/lentils]], and pan‑seared [[entities/tofu]].
- **Batch‑cook on a dedicated prep day** (often called a "prep day" or "Sunday prep"). This aligns with the concept of [[concepts/batch-cooking]].
- **Use overlapping ingredients** across meals to reduce grocery spend and storage needs, supporting [[concepts/budget-friendly-meals]].
- **Allocate meals to specific days** (lunch and dinner) so that each day’s menu can be assembled in ~15 minutes.

## Example Weekly Structure
| Day | Lunch | Dinner |
|-----|-------|--------|
| Monday | Rice + roasted vegetables + tofu (bowl) | Same bowl with a splash of [[entities/soy-sauce]] or lemon |
| Tuesday | Warm lentils + vegetables | Lentil soup with broth and veggies |
| Wednesday | Tofu over lentils | Skillet rice‑vegetable‑tofu mix |
| Thursday | Rice + vegetables | Tofu‑vegetable stir‑fry |
| Friday | "Clean‑out" bowl or soup combining leftovers | — |
| Weekend | Flexible: repeat favorites, simple salads, scrambled egg, etc. | — |

## Benefits
- **Time efficiency:** Most meals require only reheating or a quick stir‑fry, keeping weekday cooking under 15 minutes.
- **Cost control:** By purchasing a limited set of staples (rice, lentils, tofu, mixed vegetables) and using them in multiple ways, grocery bills stay low.
- **Reduced waste:** Portion sizes (e.g., ½ cup dry rice, ½ cup dry lentils, ½ block tofu) are calibrated for one person, preventing excess leftovers.
- **Flexibility:** Small variations—different sauces, fresh add‑ons like cucumber or greens—keep the menu interesting without extra prep.
