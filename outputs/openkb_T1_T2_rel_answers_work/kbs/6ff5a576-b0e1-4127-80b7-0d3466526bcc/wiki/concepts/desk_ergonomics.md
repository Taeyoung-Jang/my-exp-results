# Desk Ergonomics

Desk ergonomics refers to the arrangement of a workstation to promote physical comfort, reduce strain, and support sustained focus. The concept emphasizes a balance between functionality and visual calm, enabling users to work for long periods without fatigue or distraction.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-35_2025-02-13]])
- **Designated spots**: Every frequently used item (pen, charger, headset, badge) should have a permanent location to avoid visual clutter.
- **Open work surface**: Keep the primary area of the desk clear; store less‑used tools in drawers or out‑of‑sight containers.
- **Lighting & chair**: Adequate, glare‑free lighting and an ergonomic chair are essential for posture support during extended sessions.
- **Cable management**: Route cables behind or under the desk to minimise visual noise and prevent accidental tugging.
- **Minimalist organizers**: Use a small tray, a pen cup, and a shallow drawer insert to keep essential items tidy without sacrificing desk space.

## Interaction with Related Concepts
- A tidy workspace supports the [[concepts/end_of_day_reset]] routine by making it easy to return items to their homes each evening.
- The approach aligns with [[concepts/minimalist_organizing]], encouraging the use of only a few purpose‑built tools rather than numerous, redundant accessories.

## Practical Tips
1. **Assign a home for each tool** – label or visually group spots so you instinctively place items back.
2. **Maintain an open zone** – keep only the monitor, keyboard, and mouse on the surface; stash everything else.
3. **Adjust lighting and chair** – ensure the screen is at eye level and the chair supports lumbar curvature.
4. **Bundle cables** – use clips or a cable sleeve to keep cords together and out of sight.
5. **Regular micro‑cleanups** – a brief surface wipe at the end of each day reinforces the ergonomic setup.

By applying these principles, a desk becomes a calm, efficient hub that sustains productivity and protects physical wellbeing.
