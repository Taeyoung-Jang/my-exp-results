## Time Blocking

Time Blocking is a productivity technique that divides the day into discrete, scheduled blocks for specific activities such as deep‑focus work, breaks, chores, exercise, and administrative tasks. By allocating fixed start and end times, it reduces decision fatigue, creates clear boundaries between work and rest, and helps maintain consistent energy levels throughout the day.

### Core Elements
- **Defined intervals:** Each block has a start and end time (e.g., 8:00–10:00 for deep‑focus work). 
- **Purpose‑driven blocks:** Blocks are assigned a specific purpose—deep work, light admin, workout, or tidy‑up.
- **Transition buffers:** Short breaks or transition periods (e.g., 10:00–10:15) separate intensive blocks, aiding mental reset.

### Example from [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]]
The schedule proposed for a typical work‑from‑home day uses Time Blocking to:
1. Protect a **morning deep‑focus window** (8:00–10:00 and 10:15–12:00) when energy is highest.
2. Insert a **mid‑day break** for lunch (12:00–13:00).
3. Allocate a **short daily tidy** (13:00–13:20) and a **light admin** slot (13:20–14:00).
4. Schedule a **workout** (14:00–14:45) to combat post‑lunch slump.
5. End with a **lighter work period** (15:30–17:30) for wrap‑up tasks.

### Benefits
- **Improved focus:** By dedicating blocks to deep work, distractions are minimized.
- **Energy management:** Aligns high‑cognitive tasks with peak energy times.
- **Habit formation:** Repeating consistent blocks builds routine and reduces procrastination.
