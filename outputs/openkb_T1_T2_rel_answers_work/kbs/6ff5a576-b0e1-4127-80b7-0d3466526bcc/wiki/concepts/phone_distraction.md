# Phone Distraction Management

## Overview
Phone distraction management refers to practical methods for keeping a mobile device from breaking concentration during dedicated work intervals. It is especially relevant for people who follow a structured [[concepts/time_blocking]] schedule and aim to achieve [[concepts/deep_work]].

## Key Strategies from the Session
The document **session-14_2024-06-15** outlines two simple, low‑tech rules that can be applied immediately:

1. **Physical distance** – Place the phone in another room or far enough away that you must stand up to retrieve it. Access is limited to scheduled break periods.
2. **Invisible screen** – Enable *Do Not Disturb*, flip the phone face‑down, and treat the hidden screen as “off‑limits” until the work block ends.

These rules create friction that discourages impulsive checking while requiring minimal setup.

## Integration with Time Blocking and Deep Work
When a day is divided into clear blocks (e.g., 8:00‑10:00 and 10:15‑12:00 deep‑focus sessions), phone rules can be tied to the start and end of each block. By aligning phone placement with the boundaries of [[concepts/time_blocking]], the habit reinforces the mental cue that it is time to focus, supporting the conditions needed for [[concepts/deep_work]].

## Example Implementation
Refer to the timeline in [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]] for a concrete schedule that incorporates these phone‑distraction rules alongside work, a daily tidy, and a workout. The schedule demonstrates how a short break (10:00‑10:15) can serve as a safe window to retrieve the phone if needed, while the rest of the deep‑work periods remain phone‑free.

## Benefits
- Reduces context‑switching overhead
- Improves task completion speed
- Enhances overall work‑day satisfaction

Adopting these straightforward practices can make a noticeable difference without requiring additional apps or complex configurations.
