# Conversation session 10

Date: 2024-10-13 19:00

**User:** I’m trying to get more organized with my studying and stop feeling like I’m always behind. I usually carve out about ninety minutes each evening to get through my course content, but I’m not sure I’m using that time well. Can you help me figure out a simple routine for those 90 minutes (like what to do first, how to take notes, when to review, etc.) that actually sticks?

**Assistant:** You can make those ninety minutes feel a lot more productive if you give them a clear flow. A simple routine that works for many people is to divide the time into three phases while keeping the whole thing flexible enough that it doesn’t feel rigid.

Start by spending the first few minutes setting your focus for the session. Look at what you need to cover, choose one or two specific goals, and clear away anything distracting. This helps your brain switch into “study mode” faster.

Once you begin working through the material, aim for concentrated effort in short bursts. Many people like something close to a 25–30 minute work block followed by a very short break. During the work block, read or watch your course content and take notes in your own words rather than copying. Try to capture only the key ideas and any questions that pop up, not every detail. After a brief pause, do another work block in the same style.

Save the last part of the session for a quick review. Look back over what you covered and test yourself on it without looking at your notes. Even five minutes of recall makes the information stick better. Add anything you realized you didn’t fully understand to a “fix this later” list so it doesn’t linger in your head.

If you follow that general rhythm—set intention, do two focused blocks with light notes, and finish with a short review—the routine becomes predictable, easy to repeat, and surprisingly effective.

**User:** That structure sounds doable. Can you lay it out as an exact 90‑minute schedule with timestamps (like 0–5, 5–35, etc.) and include a simple note-taking template I can reuse? Also, how should I handle it if the lesson is a video vs a textbook chapter?

**Assistant:** Here’s a clear 90‑minute flow written out in plain text so you can follow it easily.

From 0 to 5 minutes, set your plan for the session. Pick the exact section or lesson you will finish and jot down what “done” looks like.

From 5 to 35 minutes, do your first focused work block. Move steadily through the material and take light, in‑your‑own‑words notes. Don’t pause the flow unless you need to mark a question or confusion.

From 35 to 40 minutes, take a short break. Stand up, get water, reset.

From 40 to 70 minutes, start your second focused block. Continue the same style of active reading or watching. Capture only the strongest ideas, examples, or questions.

From 70 to 80 minutes, review what you learned. Close the material and try to recall the main points from memory, then check your notes to see what you missed.

From 80 to 90 minutes, tie things up. Clarify any confusing points, note what to cover next session, and put everything away so you finish with a sense of closure.

A simple note‑taking template you can reuse each day might look like this:

Topic:
Key ideas (short sentences):
Examples or details I might need later:
Questions to resolve:
Quick summary in two or three lines:

If the lesson is a video, let it play naturally during your work blocks and pause only when you need to write a brief idea or question. Avoid stopping for every detail so you don’t lose momentum. If it’s a textbook chapter, skim the section headers first, then read in small chunks and summarize each chunk in a short line. The routine stays the same; only the pacing shifts slightly.

**User:** If I’m doing this every night, when should I fit in spaced review of older material without blowing up the 90 minutes? Could you tweak the schedule so it includes a small daily review, and tell me what to do on nights when I’m exhausted and can’t manage two full focus blocks?

**Assistant:** You can slip daily spaced review into the routine without adding extra time by taking it from the start of the session rather than tacking it on at the end. Here’s a revised 90‑minute flow that keeps everything intact.

From 0 to 10 minutes, do your spaced review. Look back at material from a few days ago and from about a week ago. Quickly test yourself from memory, then glance at your notes to fill gaps. This keeps old content alive without requiring its own study block.

From 10 to 40 minutes, start your first focused block on new material.

From 40 to 45 minutes, take your short reset break.

From 45 to 75 minutes, do your second focused block.

From 75 to 85 minutes, do your end‑of‑session recall of today’s new material.

From 85 to 90 minutes, decide what you’ll tackle tomorrow and write down any unresolved questions.

On nights when you’re exhausted, shrink the structure but keep the basic rhythm. Aim for a short ten‑minute review of older content, then choose just one focused block of about twenty minutes on new material. After that, do a brief two‑minute recall of what you covered and stop. This lets you maintain the habit without draining yourself, and you can pick up the full routine the next night.

**User:** Could you give me a super simple “spaced review menu” for that 0–10 minute slot (like exactly what to review on Day 1, Day 2, Day 4, Day 7, etc.) and how to track it without an app? I’d love something I can do with just a notebook page or index cards.

**Assistant:** You can keep a spaced‑review system very simple by using one notebook page as a running log. Each new lesson gets its own line. Next to it, you write the dates you plan to revisit it: the next day, two days later, four days later, and one week later. That’s enough spacing to make it stick without getting complicated.

It might look like this in plain text: write the lesson name, then four future dates. For example, if you learned something on March 16, you’d write March 17, March 19, March 23, and March 30. Each day, during your 0–10 minute review slot, check today’s date and see which items on your list match. Review them quickly, then put a check mark next to the date. Once a line has all four dates checked, you’re done with that item.

If you prefer index cards, put the lesson title on the front and the same four review dates on the back. Keep all cards in a stack. Each evening, flip through and pull out only the ones that match today’s date. After you review a card, cross out that date and slide the card to the back of the stack.

Either version keeps the system lightweight, low‑tech, and easy to maintain in just a few minutes.
