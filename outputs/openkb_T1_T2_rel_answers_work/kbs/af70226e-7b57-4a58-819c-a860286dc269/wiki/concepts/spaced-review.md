# Spaced Review

**Definition**: A low‑effort, regularly scheduled recall practice that revisits previously learned material at expanding intervals (e.g., 1 day, 2 days, 4 days, 7 days) to strengthen long‑term memory.

## Core Elements from [[summaries/session-10_2024-10-13]]
- **Daily 0–10 minute slot**: Use the first ten minutes of a 90‑minute study session for quick spaced review.
- **Review schedule**: 
  - Day 1 – material from the previous day
  - Day 2 – material from two days ago
  - Day 4 – material from four days ago
  - Day 7 – material from a week ago
- **Active recall**: Test yourself from memory, then glance at notes only to fill gaps.
- **Low‑tech tracking**: 
  - **Notebook log** – one line per lesson with four future dates; check off each date after review.
  - **Index cards** – front holds the lesson title, back lists the four review dates; pull cards matching today’s date.

## How it Fits into a [[concepts/study-routine]]
Spaced review is inserted at the very start of the routine, replacing any warm‑up time. By allocating only ten minutes, it integrates seamlessly without extending the overall session length.

## Relationship to Note‑Taking
While the review itself does not require new notes, having a consistent [[concepts/note-taking-template]] makes it easy to locate the key ideas you need to recall during the ten‑minute window.

## Practical Tips
- Keep the review **brief** (30–60 seconds per item) to stay within the time budget.
- Focus on **retrieval** rather than rereading; the goal is to pull information from memory.
- Adjust dates slightly if you miss a review day, but maintain the expanding‑interval pattern.
- Use the same notebook or index‑card system across all subjects for simplicity.
