# Hidden Ingredient Names

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-10_2023-10-31]]

## Overview
Food manufacturers often disguise common ingredients behind friendlier or technical terms. Recognizing these hidden names helps consumers avoid unwanted sugars, unhealthy fats, and flavor enhancers, especially when managing allergies or dietary restrictions.

## Common Hidden Sugar Names
- **Cane juice**
- **Malt syrup** – see [[entities/malt_syrup]]
- **Dextrose** – see [[entities/dextrose]]
- **Fructose**
- **Barley malt**
- Any ingredient ending in “‑ose” (e.g., glucose, galactose)

## Concealed Fat and Oil Terms
- **Soybean oil**, **canola oil**, **safflower oil** (often marketed as “healthy” oils)
- **Arachis oil** – see [[entities/arachis_oil]]
- **Groundnut oil**
- **Cold‑pressed** or **expeller‑pressed** versions of the above

## Masked Flavor Enhancers
- **Monosodium glutamate (MSG)** – may appear as **yeast extract**, **hydrolyzed protein**, or simply “natural flavors” (see [[entities/msg]])
- **Artificial sweeteners** such as sucralose, acesulfame potassium, erythritol (usually not labelled as “artificial”) 

## Why It Matters
Understanding hidden ingredient names is essential for:
- **Allergy safety** – e.g., detecting peanut derivatives like [[entities/peanut]] hidden under “natural flavors”.
- **Nutritional control** – avoiding added sugars and unhealthy fats.
- **Ingredient transparency** – making informed choices when labels are vague.

## Related Concepts
- **Label scanning tips** – strategies for quickly spotting hidden names: focus on the first three ingredients and look for “‑ose” suffixes. See [[concepts/label_scanning_tips]].
- **Peanut allergy checklist** – a practical checklist that includes hidden peanut terms. See [[concepts/peanut_allergy_checklist]].
