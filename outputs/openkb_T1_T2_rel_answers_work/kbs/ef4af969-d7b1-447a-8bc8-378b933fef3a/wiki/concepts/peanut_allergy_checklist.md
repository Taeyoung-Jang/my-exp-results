# Peanut Allergy Red‑Flag & Ambiguous Checklist

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-10_2023-10-31]]

## Overview
This concept defines a two‑tiered checklist used by individuals with a severe peanut allergy to evaluate packaged food labels quickly. It separates **automatic put‑back (red‑flag) terms**—ingredients or precautionary statements that require immediate rejection—from **ambiguous terms** that merit further verification with the manufacturer or a closer look at the allergen statement.

The checklist builds on general label‑reading strategies such as the **first‑three‑ingredients rule** and searching for suffixes like “‑ose” described in [[concepts/label_scanning_tips]], and it complements the broader discussion of hidden ingredient names in [[concepts/hidden_ingredients]].

## Automatic Red‑Flag (Put‑Back) Terms
Any of the following appearing in the ingredient list or allergen statement should trigger an immediate discard of the product:

- **Peanut** and all direct derivatives: `peanut`, `peanuts`, `peanut butter`, `peanut paste`, `peanut flour`, `peanut powder`, `peanut extract`.
- **Latin or alternative names**: `arachis oil`, `groundnut oil`, `groundnuts`.
- **Specific oil descriptors**: `cold‑pressed peanut oil`, `expeller‑pressed peanut oil`.
- **Precautionary statements** indicating likely cross‑contact:
  - “may contain peanuts”
  - “contains peanuts”
  - “processed on shared equipment with peanuts”
  - “made in a facility that also processes peanuts”

These items are linked to the entity page for the allergen itself: [[entities/peanut]] and its oil variant [[entities/arachis_oil]].

## Ambiguous Terms – Verify Further
The following phrases do **not** guarantee the presence of peanuts but should prompt a deeper review or a direct inquiry with the manufacturer:

- `natural flavors`
- `mixed nut flour`
- `vegetable protein`
- `hydrolyzed plant protein`
- `protein blend`
- `generic nut flavoring`
- Facility statements that mention “nuts” without specifying which nuts (e.g., “may contain tree nuts only”).

When any ambiguous term appears, the consumer should:
1. Check the dedicated allergen box for a peanut declaration.
2. If the box is silent, examine the full ingredient list for hidden peanut synonyms.
3. Contact the producer if uncertainty remains.

## Practical Use in Shopping
1. **Scan the first three ingredients** – they represent the bulk of the product.
2. **Look for red‑flag terms** – if found, put the item back.
3. **If only ambiguous terms appear**, follow the verification steps above before deciding.
4. **Maintain a personal copy** of this checklist for quick reference while shopping.
