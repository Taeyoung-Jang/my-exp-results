## Label‑Scanning Strategies

**Definition**: A set of practical steps for rapidly evaluating packaged‑food ingredient lists to identify hidden sugars, fats, flavor enhancers, and allergen risks, especially peanuts.

### Core Steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-10_2023-10-31]])
1. **First‑Three‑Ingredients Rule** – The first three ingredients typically make up the bulk of the product; concentrate your initial scan here.
2. **Suffix Scan** – Look for any ingredient ending in “‑ose” (e.g., dextrose, malt syrup) or containing the word “syrup” to flag added sugars. See [[entities/dextrose]] and [[entities/malt_syrup]] for common examples.
3. **Fat & Oil Check** – Spot oil names that may hide less‑healthy fats (e.g., [[entities/arachis_oil]] for peanut oil) or “seed oils” such as canola, soybean, safflower.
4. **Flavor Enhancer Scan** – Identify MSG equivalents like yeast extract, hydrolyzed protein, or “natural flavors” (see [[entities/msg]]). These often fall under the broader [[concepts/hidden_ingredients]] umbrella.
5. **End‑of‑List Review** – Preservatives, colorings, and other additives are typically listed toward the bottom; a quick skim can reveal them.
6. **Allergen & Cross‑Contact Flags** – For severe allergies, treat any peanut‑related ingredient or warning statement (see [[concepts/peanut_allergy_checklist]]) as an automatic put‑back.
7. **Category Awareness** – Certain product categories—granola/protein bars, baked goods, candies, trail mixes, Asian sauces, flavored cereals, and plant‑based snacks—are high‑risk for hidden peanuts.

### Practical Tips
- **Visual Keywords**: Keep an eye out for the words “syrup,” “concentrate,” “‑ose,” “oil,” and “extract.”
- **Allergen Box First**: Check the allergen statement before diving into the full list; it often summarises the most critical risks.
- **Use a Checklist**: Maintain a personal red‑flag list (see [[concepts/peanut_allergy_checklist]]) to speed decision‑making while shopping.
