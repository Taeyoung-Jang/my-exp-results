# Energy Budgeting

**Definition**: Energy budgeting is the practice of allocating a limited amount of personal energy to daily tasks by setting quantitative caps (e.g., maximum scheduled hours or a limit on high‑energy tasks) and deliberately reserving buffer time.

---

## Core Elements (as outlined in [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-16]])

1. **Cap Setting**
   - Choose a concrete limit such as "max 3 hours of structured activity" or "no more than 2 high‑energy tasks".
   - The cap is a *kindness* to your body, preventing the physical symptoms of overload (e.g., shaking, shortness of breath).

2. **Energy‑Cost Rating**
   - For each candidate task, assign a simple rating: **low**, **medium**, or **high**.
   - Use the rating when selecting which tasks fit under the cap.

3. **Buffer Protection**
   - Reserve explicit free time (e.g., a one‑hour slot) or limit back‑to‑back high‑energy tasks.
   - Buffers act as safety nets for unexpected requests or needed breaks.

4. **Task Prioritization Integration**
   - Energy budgeting works hand‑in‑hand with the [[concepts/daily-scheduling]] framework: after identifying the non‑negotiable, week‑easing, and wellbeing tasks, apply the cap and energy‑cost filter to decide which of those make the final list.

5. **Mid‑Day Adjustments**
   - When a new request arises, compare its importance and energy cost against the tasks already selected.
   - If it outranks an existing task, swap; otherwise, defer to the next day.

---

## Benefits
- **Physical health protection** – reduces stress‑induced symptoms.
- **Clarity** – a clear numerical or categorical limit simplifies decision‑making.
- **Consistency** – repeated use builds a habit of respecting personal limits.
