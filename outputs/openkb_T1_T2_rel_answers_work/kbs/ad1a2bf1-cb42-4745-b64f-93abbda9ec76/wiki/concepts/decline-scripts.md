## Decline Scripts

**Definition**: A set of concise, respectful statements used to say “no” or “not today” when new requests conflict with a pre‑set daily energy cap.

### Origin
These scripts were introduced in [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-16]] as part of a broader framework for managing personal workload and preventing stress‑induced symptoms like shaking and shortness of breath.

### Core Scripts
| Situation | Script |
|-----------|--------|
| General request (work or peer) | “Today’s already fully booked for me, so I’ll need to handle this tomorrow.” |
| When bandwidth is limited | “I don’t have the bandwidth right now, but I can revisit it later.” |
| Maintaining a strict cap | “I’m at my limit for the day, so I have to say no to anything new. Thanks for understanding.” |
| Close friends / family | “I really want to help, but I’m at my limit today and need to stick to it so I don’t crash. Can we talk about it tomorrow when I have more breathing room?” |

### Usage Guidelines
1. **Align with your daily cap** – Refer to the energy limit set in your [[concepts/energy-budgeting]] plan before responding.
2. **Keep it short** – A brief statement reduces decision fatigue and reinforces the boundary.
3. **Adjust tone for relationship** – Use the softer version for people you are close to, otherwise the neutral scripts work well.
4. **Follow‑up** – Offer a concrete alternative time or date to show you are still supportive.

### Integration with Daily Planning
The scripts complement the [[concepts/daily-scheduling]] checklist. After you have defined your non‑negotiable, week‑easing, and wellbeing tasks, the scripts give you a ready‑made way to protect the buffer time you scheduled.

### Benefits
- Prevents over‑commitment and associated physical stress.
- Maintains professional and personal relationships through clear communication.
- Reinforces the habit of respecting personal energy limits.
