# Caffeine Timing

**Definition**: The practice of strategically planning caffeine consumption to maximize alertness while minimizing disruption to sleep, especially for people with irregular or rotating schedules.

## Core Principles
- **Afternoon cut‑off**: Stop substantial caffeine intake by early afternoon (around 2:00 pm) to protect subsequent nap or sleep windows.
- **Pre‑shift boost**: If needed, take a small amount of caffeine shortly before a night shift (e.g., ~10:00 pm) to aid performance, ensuring it tapers off before the next sleep block.
- **Avoid late‑night caffeine**: No caffeine after midnight unless a very short, low‑dose sip is essential; this prevents bleed‑over into the post‑shift wind‑down.
- **Consistency with cues**: Pair caffeine timing with other regular cues such as [[concepts/floating-anchors]] and [[concepts/meal-timing]] to reinforce the body’s internal rhythm.

## Application in Irregular Schedules
The recommendations stem from the strategies discussed in [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-16]], which address a patchwork day that includes:
- An overnight hotel shift (11 pm–7 am)
- Morning class (8:30–10 am)
- Mid‑day shop shift (11 am–3 pm)
- Evening committee call (≈8 pm)

In that context, the suggested caffeine plan is:
1. **Morning caffeine (optional)** – A light dose before class if needed, finished before 10 am.
2. **Afternoon cut‑off** – No caffeine after the shop shift ends (≈2 pm). This preserves the ability to nap or sleep during the 4:30–7:30 pm anchor.
3. **Evening/Pre‑shift caffeine** – A small, timed dose around 10 pm if alertness is required for the night shift, but no larger servings.
4. **Hydration** – Pair caffeine with water to mitigate jitteriness and support overall energy budgeting.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/floating-anchors]]**: Caffeine timing works best when sleep and activity blocks are defined as flexible windows rather than fixed clock times.
- **[[concepts/meal-timing]]**: Align caffeine with meals (e.g., after the “first fuel” breakfast) to avoid spikes in blood sugar and to leverage food‑based buffering.
- **[[concepts/daily-scheduling]]**: Incorporating caffeine cut‑offs into a broader daily schedule ensures the strategy is repeatable.
- **[[concepts/energy-budgeting]]**: Proper caffeine placement helps allocate mental energy efficiently across the day’s demands.

## Practical Tips
- Use a simple log to track the time and amount of each caffeine dose.
- Choose lower‑caffeine options (e.g., tea) for the pre‑shift boost.
- Pair caffeine with a brief walk or light stretch to enhance alertness without over‑stimulating.
- Adjust the cut‑off time gradually if you notice lingering wakefulness during intended rest periods.

By following these guidelines, individuals with non‑standard work hours can maintain clarity and performance while safeguarding the quality of their essential sleep blocks.
