# Meal Timing

**Definition**: Meal Timing is the practice of scheduling eating windows as flexible *anchors* that align with a person’s irregular daily rhythm, prioritizing consistent patterns over fixed clock times.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Floating Anchors for Meals**
   - Treat meals as “first fuel,” “mid‑shift fuel,” “pre‑sleep fuel,” and an optional overnight snack.
   - Each anchor is a time *range* rather than a precise hour, allowing the schedule to shift while preserving the order of intake.
   - Consistency in the *pattern* (fuel → activity → wind‑down → sleep) helps the body maintain metabolic stability.

2. **Alignment with Sleep and Activity**
   - Meal windows are placed around the main sleep block and key activity periods to support energy budgeting and circadian cues.
   - Example from [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-16]]: 
     - First meal ~10:30 am after class (first fuel)
     - Second meal ~3:30 pm after shop shift (mid‑shift fuel)
     - Third meal ~9:00 pm after committee call (pre‑sleep fuel)
     - Fourth snack ~2:00 am during overnight shift (light fuel)

3. **Interaction with Caffeine**
   - Early‑afternoon cut‑off (around 2 pm) avoids interference with the afternoon sleep anchor.
   - A small boost may be taken around 10 pm if needed for the night shift, but no substantial caffeine after the cut‑off.
   - See [[concepts/caffeine-timing]] for detailed guidelines.

4. **Energy Budgeting**
   - Balanced meals and regular hydration keep the body’s energy budget steady, preventing mood swings and mental fog.
   - Aligning larger meals with periods of higher activity maximizes utilization of calories.
   - Related concept: [[concepts/energy-budgeting]].

---

## Practical Template (Floating Anchors)

| Anchor | Approx. Time (flexible) | Purpose |
|--------|------------------------|---------|
| First Fuel | ~10:30 am (post‑class) | Replenish after morning wake‑up, kick‑start metabolism |
| Mid‑Shift Fuel | ~3:30 pm (post‑shop) | Sustain energy for afternoon tasks |
| Pre‑Sleep Fuel | ~9:00 pm (post‑call) | Light meal to avoid hunger before sleep block |
| Overnight Snack | ~2:00 am (during shift) | Small digestible snack to prevent crash |

When a window is compressed (e.g., a long call), slide the subsequent anchors forward while preserving the order. This maintains the *float* principle of [[concepts/floating-anchors]].

## Takeaway

By treating meals as floating anchors rather than rigid clock times, individuals with patchwork schedules can maintain metabolic health, stable energy levels, and better overall well‑being.
