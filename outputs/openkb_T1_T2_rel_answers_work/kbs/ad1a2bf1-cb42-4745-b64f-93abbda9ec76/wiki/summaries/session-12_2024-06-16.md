# Summary of session-12_2024-06-16

**Main contribution**: A practical framework for creating a realistic daily schedule that respects personal energy limits, includes built‑in buffers, and provides scripts for declining additional requests.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/daily-scheduling]]** – Identify a non‑negotiable task, a week‑easing task, and a wellbeing task each morning.  
- **[[concepts/energy-budgeting]]** – Rate each task as low/medium/high energy and set a cap (e.g., max 3 hours scheduled or only 2 high‑energy tasks).  
- **[[concepts/decline-scripts]]** – Short, polite phrases for saying “no” or “not today,” with a softer version for friends/family.  
- **Buffer time** – Reserve explicit free time (e.g., one hour) or limit back‑to‑back high‑energy tasks to prevent overload.  
- **Morning checklist** – A reusable fill‑in template that captures tasks, energy ratings, caps, and buffers.  

## Practical Steps
1. **Morning routine (≈5 min)**  
   - Fill in the template:  
     ```markdown
     Today’s non‑negotiable task: _______
     One task that would make the rest of the week easier: _______
     One task that supports my wellbeing: _______
     Energy cost (low/medium/high) for each: _______
     My cap for today (e.g., max 3 h or 2 high‑energy tasks): _______
     Built‑in buffer (e.g., one free hour): _______
     Tasks I’m postponing without guilt: _______
     ```
2. **Apply the three‑gate filter**  
   - **Consequence**: Does the task have real fallout if delayed?  
   - **Wellbeing**: Does it support health or daily functioning?  
   - **Energy cost**: Choose the lower‑cost option when both meet the first two gates.  
3. **Enforce the cap**  
   - Treat the cap as a kindness, not a failure. Anything beyond it is automatically postponed.  
4. **Mid‑day new requests**  
   - Evaluate against existing tasks. If it outranks one, swap; otherwise, defer.  
5. **Polite decline scripts**  
   - General: “Today’s already fully booked for me, so I’ll need to handle this tomorrow.”  
   - Bandwidth: “I don’t have the bandwidth right now, but I can revisit it later.”  
   - Limit: “I’m at my limit for the day, so I have to say no to anything new. Thanks for understanding.”  
   - Warm (friends/family): “I really want to help, but I’m at my limit today and need to stick to it so I don’t crash. Can we talk about it tomorrow when I have more breathing room?”  

## Takeaways
- **Set a clear, quantifiable cap** (hours or high‑energy tasks) to protect against physical stress signals like shaking or shortness of breath.  
- **Build buffers** into the schedule to allow for unexpected events and genuine breaks.  
- **Use a concise morning checklist** to streamline decision‑making and reduce decision fatigue.  
- **Communicate limits** with short, respectful scripts, adapting tone for close relationships.  

This framework can be adapted across contexts where energy management and task overload are concerns, forming the basis for cross‑document concepts such as [[concepts/daily-scheduling]] and [[concepts/energy-budgeting]].
