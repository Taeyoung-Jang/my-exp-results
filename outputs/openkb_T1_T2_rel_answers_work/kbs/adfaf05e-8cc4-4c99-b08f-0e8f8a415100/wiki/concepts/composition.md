# Composition

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Fundamental visual arrangement guiding subject focus and viewer direction in photography.

---

Composition is the backbone of intentional photography. It determines how elements are placed within the frame to create a clear visual path for the viewer, highlight the main subject, and convey the photographer’s intention. The concept is repeatedly emphasized in the **four‑week leveling‑up plan** from [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]], which uses it as the starting point for skill development.

## Core Elements Highlighted in the Source

1. **Leading Lines** – Use streets, hallways, or any linear element to draw the eye toward the subject.
2. **Framing** – Incorporate natural or architectural borders (doorways, branches, shadows) to isolate the subject.
3. **Negative Space** – Provide breathing room around a simple subject to emphasize its importance.
4. **Shape & Pattern** – Build themes around geometric forms (circles, triangles) or repetitive motifs to give visual cohesion.
5. **Balance & Rhythm** – Ensure the arrangement feels stable and the eye moves smoothly across the image.

## Practical Application from the Session

- **Week 1** of the plan focuses exclusively on practicing distinct compositional ideas each day (leading lines, framing, negative space).  
- **Week 2** expands composition into thematic series (shapes, reflections, repetition), reinforcing the habit of purposeful framing.
- **Week 4** merges composition with lighting, reminding photographers that strong compositional intent amplifies the effect of light.

## Scoring Checklist Connection

The three‑point critique checklist ties directly to composition:
- **Subject Clarity** – A direct result of effective framing and placement.
- **Composition Direction** – Checks that the arrangement guides the eye without distraction.
