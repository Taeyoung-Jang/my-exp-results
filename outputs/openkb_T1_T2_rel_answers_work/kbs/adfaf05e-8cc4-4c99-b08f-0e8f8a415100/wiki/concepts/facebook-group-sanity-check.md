## Facebook group sanity check

A **Facebook group sanity check** is a quick evaluation you run before requesting to join a group, aimed at confirming that the community is active, well‑moderated, and welcoming to newcomers. It helps you avoid wasting time on stale or problematic groups and complements the broader [[concepts/low-effort-meetup-discovery]] process.

### What to inspect
| Area | What to look for |
|------|------------------|
| **About / purpose** | Clear description of the group’s focus (e.g., “walking group in [city]”). vague or promotional blurbs are a red flag. |
| **Rules** | Simple, respectful guidelines (e.g., no spam, stay on‑topic). Aggressive or overly restrictive rules suggest a hostile environment. |
| **Admin activity** | Recent posts or comments from the organizer(s) within the last few weeks. An admin list that looks empty or full of generic‑looking profiles may indicate abandonment or bots. |
| **Recent content** | At least a handful of posts, comments, or photos dated within the past month. Dusty threads or a single post from years ago signal inactivity. |
| **Privacy setting** | Public vs. private isn’t decisive, but for private groups you should still see a preview of recent activity before joining. |
| **Member engagement** | Varied members posting, not just the same one or two accounts repeatedly. |

### Red flags
- No recent posts or all posts older than 3 months.
- Rules that forbid “new members” or require fees not mentioned elsewhere.
- Predominantly promotional links or sales pitches.
- Admin list comprised of incomplete names or profile pictures that look like placeholders.
- Overly aggressive language in the About section.
