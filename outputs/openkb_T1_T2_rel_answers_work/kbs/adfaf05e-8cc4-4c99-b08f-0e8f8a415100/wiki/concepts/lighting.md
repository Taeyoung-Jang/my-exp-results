# Lighting

Lighting in photography describes how light interacts with the subject, influencing mood, texture, and three‑dimensional form. It encompasses **quality** (soft vs. hard), **direction** (front, side, back), and **control** (natural, artificial, or mixed).

## Core Elements
- **Quality** – Soft window light creates gentle gradients and wraps around subjects, while harsh midday sun produces strong shadows and high contrast.  
- **Direction** – The angle of light determines where highlights and shadows fall, shaping the subject's form.  
- **Source** – Natural light (sun, sky) and artificial light (lamps, phone LEDs) can be combined or used separately to achieve desired effects.

## Practical Application from [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]]
The four‑week plan introduces lighting in **Week 3**, giving concrete exercises:
1. **Soft window light** – Position a subject near a window and shoot from multiple angles to explore how the light wraps the form.
2. **Harsh midday sun** – Embrace strong shadows, using them intentionally rather than trying to eliminate them.
3. **Artificial home light** – Use a lamp or phone light to control direction and intensity for portraits or object studies.

These exercises are tied to a simple **three‑point scoring system** that evaluates whether the light **enhances** the image (one point for lighting support). The scoring feeds into the weekly [[concepts/photographic-review-workflow]], where low‑scoring shots are analyzed for lighting weaknesses.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Mastery of [[concepts/composition]] provides a framework for placing light deliberately within the frame.
- Effective lighting informs the broader [[concepts/photographic-review-workflow]] by offering clear criteria for critique and improvement.

## Why It Matters
Good lighting can turn an ordinary subject into a compelling story, reveal texture, and guide the viewer’s eye. By practicing varied lighting conditions and reflecting on the scoring feedback, photographers develop an intuitive sense for choosing and shaping light to serve their creative intent.
