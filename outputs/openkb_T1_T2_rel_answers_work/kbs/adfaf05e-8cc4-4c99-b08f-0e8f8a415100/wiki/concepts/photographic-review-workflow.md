## Photographic Review Workflow

**Definition**: A systematic, low‑friction method for critiquing, scoring, and choosing the most effective images from a weekly shooting batch.

### Core Steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]])
1. **First Pass – Flag promising shots**
   - Quickly skim all images and mark any that catch your eye.  No deep analysis yet; just a gut reaction.
2. **Side‑by‑side comparison**
   - Group similar frames and compare them directly.  Look for the image that best communicates the intended idea.
3. **Score‑driven tie‑breaker**
   - Apply the three‑point scoring system (subject clarity, compositional direction, lighting support).  The higher‑scoring image wins when visual preference is ambiguous.
4. **Select keepers**
   - Aim for **3‑5** final images per week.  This forces selectivity while preserving variety.
5. **Low‑score debrief**
   - For each discarded or low‑scoring image, write a brief note (one sentence) identifying the main weakness – unclear subject, messy framing, or unhelpful light.
6. **Archive or delete**
   - After noting, move the image out of the active pool.  Over time these notes reveal recurring problem areas.

### Why it works
- **Efficiency**: Minimal time is spent on each image, preventing the process from feeling like homework.
- **Feedback loop**: The debrief notes turn quantitative scores into qualitative insights, guiding focused practice.
- **Progress tracking**: Weekly keeper counts and scores provide a simple metric for improvement.
