## Overview
A concise coaching guide for photographers looking to move from random snapshots to intentional projects. It outlines a **four‑week leveling‑up plan** that begins with [[concepts/composition]] fundamentals, introduces thematic work, adds [[concepts/lighting]] practice, and finally merges both into cohesive mini‑projects. The guide also provides a **quick three‑point scoring system** and a **streamlined weekly review workflow** to turn scores into actionable learning.

## 4‑Week Plan
### Week 1 – Composition Foundations
- **Day 1:** Capture leading lines (e.g., streets, hallways) – 5 images.
- **Day 2:** Use natural frames (doorways, branches) – 5 images.
- **Day 3:** Explore negative space with a simple subject – 5 images.

### Week 2 – Thematic Composition
- **Day 1:** Focus on a shape (circles, triangles) throughout the frame.
- **Day 2:** Shoot reflections (windows, puddles, metal).
- **Day 3:** Create a series of repeating patterns or objects.

### Week 3 – Lighting Practice
- **Day 1:** Soft window light – vary angle and distance.
- **Day 2:** Harsh midday sun – emphasize intentional shadows.
- **Day 3:** Artificial home light (lamp, phone) – control direction and intensity.

### Week 4 – Combined Projects
- **Day 1:** One subject in varied light with strong compositional intent.
- **Day 2:** A short narrative (3‑5 images) where composition + lighting set mood.
- **Day 3:** People‑focused candid or posed shots, managing both composition and available light.

## Scoring Checklist (3‑Point System)
1. **Subject Clarity** – Is the main subject immediately obvious? (1 pt)  
2. **Composition Direction** – Does the framing guide the eye without distraction? (1 pt)  
3. **Lighting Support** – Does the light enhance rather than hinder the image? (1 pt)

- **3 points:** Photo ready to showcase.  
- **2 points:** Good, but note a single improvement area.  
- **1 point:** Re‑shoot or study the specific weakness.

## Weekly Review Workflow
1. **First Pass – Flag Promising Shots**: Quickly mark images that catch your eye.  
2. **Side‑by‑Side Comparison**: For similar frames, choose the one that communicates most cleanly.  
3. **Score‑Driven Tie‑Breaker**: Use the three‑point score to decide when aesthetics are close.  
4. **Select Keepers**: Aim for **3‑5** final images per week.  
5. **Low‑Score Debrief**: Write a one‑sentence note on why a shot scored low (subject, framing, or light issue).  
6. **Archive or Delete**: After noting, move the low‑scoring images out of active view.

Over time the debrief notes reveal recurring patterns, turning scoring into targeted practice.
