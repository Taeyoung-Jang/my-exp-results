# Summary of session-04_2024-06-15

**Main idea** – The assistant proposes a concrete, low‑effort evening routine that helps the user curb doomscrolling, signal the brain for sleep, and keep the phone out of reach while still allowing it to serve as an alarm.

## Key concepts
- **[[concepts/nighttime-routine]]** – A short (30‑45 min) wind‑down sequence anchored to a consistent cue (e.g., brushing teeth).  
- **[[concepts/phone-management]]** – Put the phone on Do Not Disturb, charge it outside the bedroom, or place it across the room so the alarm can be heard but not accessed.  
- **[[concepts/cue-based-habit]]** – Tie the routine to an existing habit (teeth‑brushing, tea‑making) to automate the start.  
- Sleep hygiene – Dim lights, avoid screens, use low‑stimulation activities (reading, stretching, journaling, herbal tea).  
- Doomscrolling – Reducing screen time before bed to prevent mental over‑stimulation.  
- Low‑friction habit – Design the “last check” (alarm set, phone placed) as the final phone interaction, eliminating empty moments that tempt reuse.  
- Relaxation techniques – Slow breathing or simply lying still for the final ten minutes.

## Step‑by‑step routine (≈30‑45 min)
1. **9:45‑10:00** – Put phone on Do Not Disturb, move it to a charger outside the bedroom, dim lights.  
2. **Cue** – Wash face, brush teeth, or make herbal tea to signal wind‑down.  
3. **10‑20 min activity** – Read a physical book, stretch lightly, journal, or sip tea; keep it quiet and predictable.  
4. **Last 10 min** – Get into bed, practice slow breathing or simply let the day fade.  
5. **Alarm placement** – If the phone must serve as an alarm, place it on a dresser across the room; set alarm before the final cue (around 10:15‑10:20).

## Practical tweaks
- **Cut‑off time** – Perform any final phone checks by 10:15‑10:20, then immediately move the phone and start the wind‑down activity.  
- **Out‑of‑sight benefit** – The physical distance forces the user to stand up to turn off the alarm, reducing snooze‑button usage.

## Expected outcomes
- Reduced evening doomscrolling  
- More consistent bedtime (≈10:30 pm)  
- Automatic, low‑effort wind‑down that becomes a habit  
- Easier wake‑up thanks to a “stand‑up‑to‑turn‑off” alarm
