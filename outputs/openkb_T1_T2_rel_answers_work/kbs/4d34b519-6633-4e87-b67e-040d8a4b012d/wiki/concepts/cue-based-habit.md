# Cue‑Based Habit

**Definition**: A cue‑based habit is a behavior that is triggered automatically by pairing it with an existing, reliable cue, reducing the need for conscious willpower.

## Core Elements
- **Cue** – A pre‑existing action or environmental signal (e.g., brushing teeth, making tea) that consistently occurs at the same time each day.
- **Routine** – The new habit you want to establish, such as a calming wind‑down activity.
- **Automaticity** – Over repeated pairings, the cue alone initiates the routine, making it feel effortless.

## Why It Works
Cue‑based habits leverage the brain’s habit loop, where the cue triggers a routine followed by a reward. By anchoring a new nightly activity to a well‑established cue, the new behavior inherits the cue’s reliability, which helps the habit stick.

## Application in Evening Routines
In [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-15]], the assistant recommends using an existing bedtime cue—like brushing teeth or making herbal tea—to launch a 30‑45 minute [[concepts/nighttime-routine]]. This cue signals the brain that it’s time to transition toward sleep, making the subsequent low‑stimulation activities (reading, stretching, journaling) more automatic.

## Interaction with Phone Management
The concept also ties into [[concepts/phone-management]]: the final phone‑related step (setting an alarm and moving the phone out of reach) can be treated as the cue’s “last check.” By performing this as the very last phone interaction, the user creates a clear, repeatable signal that the wind‑down phase is beginning, reinforcing the overall habit loop.

## Steps to Build a Cue‑Based Habit
1. **Identify a reliable cue** that occurs each night (e.g., turning off the main light, brushing teeth).
2. **Define a simple, pleasant routine** that follows the cue (e.g., reading a page, gentle stretching).
3. **Start with a short duration** (5‑10 minutes) to ensure feasibility.
4. **Repeat consistently**; over time the cue will automatically trigger the routine.
5. **Add incremental elements** (e.g., herbal tea, breathing exercises) once the habit feels stable.

## Benefits
- Reduces decision fatigue.
- Increases consistency of bedtime, supporting better sleep quality.
- Aligns with broader [[concepts/nighttime-routine]] strategies for healthier sleep hygiene.
