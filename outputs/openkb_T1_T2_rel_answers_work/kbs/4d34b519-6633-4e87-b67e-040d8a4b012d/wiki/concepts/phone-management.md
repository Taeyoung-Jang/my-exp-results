# Phone Management

**Definition**: Strategies for handling a mobile device in the evening to minimise distraction, support sleep hygiene, and still allow it to function as an alarm.

## Core Practices (from [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-15]])
- **Do Not Disturb / Sleep Mode**: Activate the phone’s DND or sleep mode at the start of the wind‑down period so notifications are silenced while the alarm remains allowed.
- **Charging Outside the Bedroom**: Plug the phone into a charger placed in another room. This removes the temptation to check the screen and creates a physical barrier.
- **Alarm Placement Across the Room**: If the phone must serve as an alarm, set it on a dresser or shelf away from the bed. The user must get out of bed to turn it off, reducing snoozing.
- **Last‑Check Cut‑off**: Perform any final phone interactions (setting the alarm, confirming DND) about ten minutes before the intended bedtime (e.g., 10:15 pm for a 10:30 pm lights‑out). Immediately after, move the phone and transition to the next wind‑down cue.
- **Low‑Friction Setup**: Make the phone‑related steps the final action in the routine, so there is no “empty moment” that could trigger a re‑check of the device.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Supports a **[[concepts/nighttime-routine]]** by providing a concrete, low‑effort step that signals the start of the routine.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with **[[concepts/cue-based-habit]]**: the phone‑placement action becomes a cue that triggers the subsequent calming activities.

## Benefits
- Reduces evening doomscrolling and screen‑induced mental arousal.
- Encourages a consistent bedtime (e.g., 10:30 pm) by removing a common source of delay.
- Improves wake‑up quality by requiring a physical movement to silence the alarm.
