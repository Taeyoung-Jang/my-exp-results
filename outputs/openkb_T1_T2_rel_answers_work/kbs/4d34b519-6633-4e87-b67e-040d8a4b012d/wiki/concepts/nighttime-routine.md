## Nighttime Routine

A **nighttime routine** is a short, repeatable set of low‑stimulation activities performed before sleep to signal the brain that it is time to wind down.  The goal is to make the routine easy to start, pleasant to follow, and automatically triggered by a consistent cue, so it becomes a habit that sticks.

### Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-15]])
1. **Cue‑based start** – Anchor the routine to an existing habit (e.g., brushing teeth, making tea) to create a reliable trigger.  This links to the broader idea of a [[concepts/cue-based-habit]].
2. **Phone management** – Put the phone on Do Not Disturb, charge it outside the bedroom, or place it across the room.  This reduces screen exposure and prevents doomscrolling, connecting to the [[concepts/phone-management]] concept.
3. **Low‑stimulation activities** – Dim lights and engage in quiet tasks such as reading a physical book, gentle stretching, journaling, or sipping herbal tea.  These actions support good sleep hygiene (though not a listed wikilink, it is described in plain text).
4. **Relaxation phase** – In the final ten minutes, lie in bed and practice slow breathing or simply let thoughts drift, helping the mind transition to sleep.
5. **Alarm placement (optional)** – If the phone must serve as an alarm, set it across the room before the cue so the user must stand up to turn it off, reinforcing wake‑up.

### Typical Timeline (30‑45 minutes)
- **9:45‑10:00** – Phone management and dim lights.
- **Cue** – Wash face, brush teeth, or make tea.
- **10‑20 min** – Quiet activity (reading, stretching, journaling, tea).
- **Last 10 min** – Bed, breathing, settle.
- **Alarm** – Place phone across the room if needed.

### Benefits
- Reduces evening doomscrolling and screen‑induced arousal.
- Promotes consistent sleep onset around 10:30 pm.
- Creates a low‑friction habit that becomes automatic over time.
- Supports a smoother wake‑up by requiring a stand‑up to silence the alarm.
