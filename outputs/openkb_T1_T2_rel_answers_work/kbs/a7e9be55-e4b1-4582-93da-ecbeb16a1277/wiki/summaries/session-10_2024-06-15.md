# Summary of session-10_2024-06-15

**Main topic**: How to develop *high‑impact, tiny habits* that help people remain steady when faced with ongoing stress.

## Key ideas
- **Steadiness is largely trainable** – personality provides a baseline, but consistent habits shape how we react to chaos.
- **High‑impact habits are simple** – sleep hygiene, short daily “reset” practices, and a fixed piece of daily structure act as anchors. [[concepts/high_impact_habits]]
- **Start tiny** – begin with actions that take seconds (e.g., go to bed 10 min earlier, 2‑minute breathing break) to lower the will‑power barrier.
- **Habit anchoring / stacking** – attach the new tiny habit to an existing, non‑skippable routine (brushing teeth, closing a laptop, getting into bed) so it becomes automatic. [[concepts/habit_anchoring]]
- **Consistency over motivation** – low‑effort, repeatable cues beat ambitious routines; missing a day is okay as long as you resume the next cue.

## Recommended anchor habits for chronic stress
1. **Breathing pause** – three slow breaths with long exhales (~20 s). Attach to:
   - Morning: after brushing teeth.
   - Midday: after closing the laptop.
   - Night: after pulling the blankets on.
2. **Transition moment** – a tiny physical reset (shoulder roll, stretch, sip of water). Anchor to natural shifts such as:
   - Arriving home.
   - Switching tasks.
   - Lying down for sleep.

## Implementation examples (If‑Then cues)
- **Morning**: *If* I put my toothbrush down, *then* I plant my feet and take three slow breaths.
- **Midday**: *If* I close my laptop, *then* I stand, roll shoulders, and take a single deep breath.
- **Bedtime**: *If* I settle under the covers, *then* I place a hand on my chest and breathe calmly for a few seconds.
