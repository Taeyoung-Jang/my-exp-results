# Summary of Session 34 (2024‑12‑16)

**Topic:** Helping the user monitor and understand subtle factors that improve daily calmness.

## Key Ideas
- Adopt a **curiosity** mindset rather than trying to avoid “jinxing” calm periods. [[concepts/curiosity_framing]]
- Use a **simple daily tracking template** (5–7 prompts, 1–10 calm/tension rating) to collect data without burden. [[concepts/calm_tracking]]
- Refine the template to a **sub‑minute version** with minimal prompts for rapid entry.
- Identify **quiet changes** that often go unnoticed but boost morning calm: hydration, screen timing, phone use, consistent meal timing, bedtime shifts, pre‑planned clothing, brief predictable morning routine. [[concepts/quiet_changes]]
- Modify **phone usage** in the morning to be less stimulating (e.g., open weather/notes, delay notifications).  
- Conduct a **2‑week review** using a short checklist of guiding questions to spot patterns without spreadsheets.  
- Translate observed patterns into **1–2 tiny experiments** for the following week (e.g., delay caffeine, add 5‑min movement). [[concepts/simple_experiments]]

## Tracking Templates
### Full Template (7 prompts)
1. Calm/Tension (1–10)  
2. Sleep quality (quick note)  
3. Caffeine (what + when)  
4. Movement or exercise (anything)  
5. Body feeling through the day (tight, loose, steady, jittery)  
6. Notable boosts or drains (one sentence)  
7. Anything that felt different today

### Ultra‑Quick Template (<60 s, 6 prompts)
- Calm/Tension 1–10
- Sleep (good/ok/rough)
- Caffeine (yes/no + time)
- Movement (yes/no)
- Body feel (one word)
- Big boost/drain (one phrase)

## Common Quiet Changes (overlooked morning calm boosters)
- Slightly increased water intake
- Turning off screens 30‑60 min before bed
- Avoiding phone use the first few minutes of the morning
- Eating breakfast at a consistent time
- Going to bed 10‑15 min earlier
- Laying out clothes the night before
- Keeping the first 5 minutes of the morning predictable

## Phone‑Use Tip
- Open a neutral app (weather, notes, calming playlist) instead of diving into notifications.
- Enforce a “no notifications for the first 5 minutes” rule.

## 2‑Week Review Checklist (≈10 min)
1. Which entries have the highest calm scores? Note any common antecedents (sleep, caffeine timing, movement, etc.).
2. Are there repeatable cues on calmer days (e.g., later caffeine, a brief walk)?
3. Do lower calm scores share any consistent factor (early phone use, tight body feel, etc.)?
4. Look for recurring descriptive words for body feel; do they cluster with specific habits?
5. Highlight any surprising notes that may hint at hidden influences.

### Turning Insights into Experiments
- Choose the most evident pattern and design a tiny test for the next week.
  - Example 1: *Delay caffeine by 30 minutes* if calmer days follow later caffeine.
  - Example 2: *Add five minutes of any movement* if movement correlates with higher calm scores.
- Keep the experiment simple, track using the same quick template, and observe if the calm rating shifts consistently. [[concepts/simple_experiments]]
