# Curiosity Framing

**Definition**: Curiosity framing is the mental shift of approaching well‑being changes as a neutral inquiry—observing what works—rather than a performance goal aimed at preserving a calm state.

## Core Idea from Session 34
In the conversation on 2024‑12‑16, the assistant suggested that the user adopt a *curiosity* mindset instead of fearing to “jinx” their recent calm. By treating the calm streak as data to explore, the user reduces pressure, stays open to subtle influences, and can more easily notice patterns.

## Why It Helps
- **Reduces anxiety**: Viewing calm as a subject of study removes the fear of losing it.
- **Encourages observation**: Prompts the user to notice small factors (sleep, caffeine, movement) that might otherwise be ignored.
- **Supports tracking**: Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/calm_tracking]] by framing daily entries as observations rather than performance scores.
- **Facilitates experimentation**: Sets the stage for [[concepts/simple_experiments]] where the user tests tiny adjustments without feeling they must maintain a perfect state.

## Practical Steps
1. **Adopt a neutral tone** when logging daily data – “What happened?” instead of “Did I break my calm?”
2. **Ask open‑ended questions** (e.g., “What might have contributed to today’s calm rating?”) to keep the focus on discovery.
3. **Separate observation from judgment**; treat each entry as a clue, not a verdict.
4. **Link observations to quiet changes** such as hydration, screen timing, or bedtime shifts – see [[concepts/quiet_changes]].
5. **Review patterns** with a curiosity lens, looking for recurring cues rather than blaming deviations.
