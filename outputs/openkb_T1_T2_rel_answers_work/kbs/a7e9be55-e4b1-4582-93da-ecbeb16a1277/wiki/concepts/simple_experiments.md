# Simple Experiments

**Definition**: Simple Experiments are brief, low‑commitment tests designed to isolate and evaluate the effect of a single habit or environmental tweak on perceived calmness or stress levels.

## Core Principles
- **Minimal friction** – the experiment should be easy to start and stop, requiring no special tools or extensive planning.
- **Single variable focus** – change only one factor at a time (e.g., caffeine timing, brief morning movement) to keep causal inference clear.
- **Short duration** – typically run for one week after a two‑week observation period to verify patterns quickly.
- **Feedback loop** – use the same quick tracking template from [[concepts/calm_tracking]] to record outcomes, then review with the 2‑week checklist.

## Process (as described in [[summaries/session-34_2024-12-16]])
1. **Identify a repeatable pattern** from the two‑week review (e.g., calmer days followed later caffeine or a brief walk).  
2. **Formulate a concise hypothesis** (e.g., “Delaying caffeine by 30 minutes will raise my calm rating”).
3. **Design the experiment** – decide the exact change and keep everything else constant.
4. **Track daily** using the ultra‑quick template:
   - Calm/Tension 1–10
   - Sleep (good/ok/rough)
   - Caffeine (yes/no + time)
   - Movement (yes/no)
   - Body feel (one word)
   - Big boost/drain (one phrase)
5. **Review after a week** – look for consistent shifts in the calm rating and note any supporting observations.
6. **Iterate** – either adopt the change, tweak the variable, or move to a new experiment.

## Example Experiments from the Source
- **Delay caffeine**: Shift the first coffee/tea by 30 minutes.
- **Add micro‑movement**: Perform any 5‑minute movement (stretching, short walk) in the morning.

Both examples follow the principle of being **tiny** and **measurable**, fitting into everyday routines without feeling like a project.
