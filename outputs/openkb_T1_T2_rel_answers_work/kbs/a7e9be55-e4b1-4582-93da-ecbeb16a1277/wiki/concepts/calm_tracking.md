# Calm Tracking

**Definition**: Calm Tracking is the practice of recording daily subjective calmness (often on a 1–10 scale) alongside key contextual variables (sleep, caffeine, movement, body feel, etc.) to surface patterns that affect overall stress levels.

---

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-34_2024-12-16]])
- **Calm/Tension rating** – a quick numeric self‑assessment (1 = very tense, 10 = very calm).
- **Sleep note** – simple quality indicator (good/ok/rough) or brief comment.
- **Caffeine** – presence and timing (yes/no + time).
- **Movement** – whether any exercise or physical activity occurred (yes/no).
- **Body feel** – a one‑word descriptor (tight, loose, steady, jittery, etc.).
- **Boost/Drain** – a short phrase capturing the most noticeable factor that lifted or lowered calmness that day.

These prompts are designed to be answered in under a minute, making the habit easy to sustain for weeks.

---

## Purpose and Benefits
- **Pattern discovery** without spreadsheets; just skim entries and look for recurring combinations.
- **Low friction** encourages consistent data collection, increasing the reliability of insights.
- Supports a **curious mindset** rather than performance pressure, aligning with [[concepts/curiosity_framing]].
- Highlights **quiet changes** (e.g., hydration, screen timing) that can have outsized impact on morning calm, linking to [[concepts/quiet_changes]].
- Provides a factual basis for designing **simple experiments** (e.g., delay caffeine) as described in [[concepts/simple_experiments]].

---

## Typical Workflow
1. **Morning or evening entry** – fill the ultra‑quick template (6 lines) in a notes app.
2. **Two‑week review** – read through entries, ask guiding questions (see the review checklist in the source) to note which factors co‑occur with high calm scores.
3. **Experimentation** – pick 1–2 repeatable habits that seem beneficial and test them for a week, continuing the tracking to confirm effects.

---

## Implementation Tips
- Keep the template **visible** (e.g., pinned note) to reduce friction.
- Use **plain language** for body‑feel descriptors; consistency helps spotting patterns.
- Resist rating fatigue by **limiting entries** to once per day.
- Treat the review as a **reading exercise**, not a statistical analysis.
