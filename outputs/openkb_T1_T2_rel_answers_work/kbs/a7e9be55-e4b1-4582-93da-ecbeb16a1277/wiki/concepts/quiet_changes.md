# Quiet Changes

**Definition**: Quiet changes are small, often overlooked adjustments to daily routines that can have an outsized effect on stress levels and overall calmness. They are typically low‑effort, high‑impact habits that work in the background to support a more relaxed state.

## Key Details from [[summaries/session-34_2024-12-16]]
- **Hydration** – Drinking a bit more water than usual, especially in the morning.
- **Screen Timing** – Turning off screens 30–60 minutes before bed and avoiding phone use for the first few minutes after waking.
- **Phone Use** – Starting the day with a neutral app (weather, notes, calming playlist) rather than scrolling notifications.
- **Meal Timing** – Eating breakfast at a consistent time each day.
- **Bedtime Adjustments** – Going to bed 10–15 minutes earlier or shifting bedtime slightly.
- **Prep Work** – Laying out clothes the night before and keeping the first five minutes of the morning predictable.
- **Micro‑Movement** – Adding brief, light movement (e.g., a short walk or stretch) can reduce tension without feeling like a formal workout.

These quiet changes were highlighted as common contributors to the user’s recent increase in calm, suggesting that tracking them alongside other variables can reveal patterns.
