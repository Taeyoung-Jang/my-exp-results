## Stress Management

**Definition**: Stress management encompasses practices and habits that help individuals keep their nervous system balanced, reduce perceived pressure, and sustain emotional steadiness during challenging or chaotic circumstances.

### Core Elements from session‑10_2024‑06‑15
- **High‑impact, tiny habits** are the foundation of effective stress management. Small actions like a brief breathing pause, a short transition stretch, or a consistent sleep cue provide outsized benefits while requiring minimal willpower.
- **Habit anchoring** (or habit stacking) is the key implementation strategy: the new micro‑habit is attached to an existing, non‑skippable routine (e.g., after brushing teeth, after closing a laptop, after pulling the blankets). This automatic cue‑response loop reduces reliance on motivation.
- **Consistency over motivation**: By keeping the effort tiny—seconds rather than minutes—people can maintain the practice even on the busiest days, allowing the habit to compound over time.
- **Anchor examples**:
  - *Morning*: After putting the toothbrush down, take three slow breaths.
  - *Midday*: After closing the laptop, stand, roll shoulders, and breathe.
  - *Bedtime*: After settling under the covers, place a hand on the chest and breathe calmly.

### Practical Takeaway
To start managing stress effectively, pick one tiny habit, attach it to a daily routine you never miss, and repeat it consistently. Over weeks, this small anchor can become a reliable cushion against chronic stress.
