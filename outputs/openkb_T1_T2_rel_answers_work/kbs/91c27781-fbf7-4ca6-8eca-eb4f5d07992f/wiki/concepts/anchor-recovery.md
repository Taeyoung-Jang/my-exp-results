# Anchor Recovery

**Definition** – Anchor Recovery refers to the set of lightweight strategies used to address missed routine anchors (e.g., a skipped workout or a postponed chores/admin block) so that the overall weekly rhythm stays intact and does not become overloaded.

## Why It Matters
- Anchors give a week its **rhythm**; missing one can feel like a setback.
- Without a recovery plan, people tend to **stack** missed tasks later, leading to stress and burnout.
- A deliberate recovery approach keeps the routine sustainable, especially on weeks with unexpected disruptions.

## Core Principles (derived from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]])
1. **Treat the miss as a skip, not a failure.**
2. **Slide, don’t stack.** Move the task to the next light‑weight slot rather than cramming it into an already‑busy day.
3. **Scale down if needed.** A reduced‑duration version (e.g., 30 min instead of 45 min) preserves the habit without overwhelming the schedule.
4. **Accept occasional gaps.** If a task cannot be rescheduled within the week, simply let it go and resume the normal pattern the following week.

## Example Recovery Tactics
- **Missed Tuesday chores/admin** – Shift a 30–40 minute version to the next evening that feels light (e.g., Wednesday 7:30 pm). Prioritize only the top one or two items to avoid backlog.
- **Missed workout** – Keep the next scheduled workout unchanged. If you want a bridge, add a brief 10–15 minute walk on the same day, but do not add an extra full session.

## Practical Checklist
- [ ] Identify the missed anchor.
- [ ] Choose the next appropriate light slot.
- [ ] Reduce the duration if the slot is already partially occupied.
- [ ] Execute the scaled‑down task.
- [ ] Return to the regular anchor schedule.

- **[[concepts/weekly-routine-design]]** – Effective recovery is a key component of a resilient weekly routine design, allowing the system to absorb disruptions without breaking.
