# Time Blocking

**Definition**: Time Blocking is a scheduling method that divides the day into distinct blocks, each dedicated to a specific activity or category of work. Rather than a fluid to‑do list, you pre‑assign time slots for tasks such as exercise, chores, focused work, or personal growth.

## Why It Works
- **Predictable anchors** create a rhythm that the brain can anticipate, reducing decision fatigue.
- By fixing start and end times, it protects activities from being crowd‑ed out by urgent items.
- It makes it easier to see where gaps or overloads exist, supporting intentional balance.

## Key Elements from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]]
1. **Anchor Selection** – Identify a few recurring moments (e.g., early‑morning workouts, evening chores) and lock them in as blocks.
2. **Duration Rules** – Keep blocks short enough to survive a low‑energy day: 30‑45 min for workouts, up to 60 min for chores/admin, 30‑60 min for reading/learning.
3. **Flexibility Layer** – Offer a fallback evening slot when mornings are low energy, preserving the block without forcing a rigid schedule.
4. **Recovery Strategy** – If a block is missed, shift a reduced version to the next open slot rather than stacking multiple blocks; see [[concepts/anchor-recovery]].

## Structured vs. Flexible Templates
- The **structured template** uses the same time‑block each week (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri 6:30–7:15 am workout) with an evening fallback.
- The **flexible template** presents a “pick‑4‑blocks” menu, letting the user choose any four blocks that fit their weekly energy, illustrating the core idea that blocks are *anchors*, not strict appointments.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- [[concepts/weekly-routine-design]]: Time Blocking is a core tool for building a weekly routine that balances productivity, rest, and personal growth.
- [[concepts/anchor-recovery]]: Provides guidance on how to handle missed blocks without over‑loading the remaining schedule.

## Practical Tips
- **Start Small** – Begin with 1–2 blocks and gradually add more as the habit solidifies.
- **Buffer Time** – Leave small gaps between blocks to handle overruns or transition.
- **Review Weekly** – At the end of each week, assess which blocks were consistently kept and adjust durations or times accordingly.
