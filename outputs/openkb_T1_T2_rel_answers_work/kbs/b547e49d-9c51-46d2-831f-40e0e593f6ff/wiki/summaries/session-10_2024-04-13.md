# Summary of session-10_2024-04-13

**Main focus**: How to test whether the cosmic dark sector is explained by a new particle (dark matter) or by a modification to gravity.

## Preferred mystery
- The assistant chooses **dark matter** as the most impactful unsolved mystery because its nature underpins galaxy formation, large‑scale structure, and the ultimate fate of the universe.

## Core observational test: the Bullet Cluster
- In merging galaxy clusters like the **Bullet Cluster**, the separation between baryonic gas (seen in X‑rays) and the gravitational potential (mapped by **gravitational lensing**) provides a clean discriminator.
- Under **modified‑gravity** scenarios the lensing mass should trace the visible matter, while the observed offset strongly supports the presence of an unseen mass component → a point in favor of particle dark matter.

## Additional priority observations
- **Weak lensing surveys** and **large‑scale‑structure** measurements that track how matter clumps over cosmic time.
- **Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)** acoustic peaks, which encode early‑universe conditions that are tightly predicted by ΛCDM with cold dark matter.

## Sharpest discriminating observables
| Observable | What it probes | ΛCDM + particle dark matter prediction | Typical modified‑gravity signature |
|------------|----------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **E_G** E G | Combination of galaxy motions, clustering and lensing; tests the relation between the potentials that bend light and the force that moves matter. | Near‑GR value, weak scale/redshift dependence. | Scale‑ or redshift‑dependent drift due to altered force law; Φ ≠ Ψ leads to measurable deviation. |
| **Gravitational slip (Φ vs Ψ)** gravitational slip | Direct comparison of the two metric potentials. | Φ ≈ Ψ after anisotropic stresses decay. | Φ ≠ Ψ, causing mismatched lensing vs dynamical mass estimates. |
| **Redshift‑space distortions fσ₈** redshift space distortions | Growth rate of structure. | Specific growth curve tied to cold dark matter and a cosmological constant. | Enhanced or suppressed growth, especially on large scales, shifting the fσ₈‑z curve. |
| **ISW cross‑correlations** ISW cross correlation | Evolution of gravitational potentials during cosmic acceleration. | Modest, well‑predicted correlation between CMB temperature and foreground galaxies. | Altered potential decay can amplify, diminish, or flip the sign of the correlation. |
| **Cluster mass function** cluster mass function | Abundance of massive clusters as a function of redshift. | Steep, well‑measured evolution consistent with ΛCDM. | Too many early massive clusters unless screening keeps gravity GR‑like on small scales. |

## Synthesis
- A consistent picture across **E_G**, **gravitational slip**, **fσ₈**, **ISW**, and the **cluster mass function** tightly constrains modified‑gravity models.  They can sometimes match one observable but usually fail to reproduce the full suite without reverting to behaviour that mimics particle dark matter.
- Therefore, the combination of **weak lensing**, **galaxy clustering**, **CMB** acoustic peak analysis, and **cluster counts** provides the most robust stress‑test for any alternative to dark matter.
