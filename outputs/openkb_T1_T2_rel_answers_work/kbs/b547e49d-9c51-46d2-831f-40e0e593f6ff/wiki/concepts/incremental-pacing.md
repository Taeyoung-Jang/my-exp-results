# Incremental Pacing

**Definition** – A structured approach to scaling study effort over time, where daily hours, question volumes, and practice intensity are raised in small, controlled steps to maintain steady progress while minimizing the risk of burnout.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Start Small** – Begin with a modest daily commitment (1–2 hours) and low‑volume untimed practice.
2. **Weekly Checkpoints** – Review performance on MBE questions and essay outlines; adjust load if sessions feel draining.
3. **Gradual Volume Growth** – Increase total MBE questions by ~10‑20 % every one to two weeks and lengthen timed sets incrementally.
4. **Introduce New Tasks Slowly** – Add mixed‑subject sets and MPT practice only after a stable rhythm is established (around week 4 in the bar‑exam plan).
5. **Preserve Recovery** – Keep one full rest day each week untouched, using it as a mental reset.

---

## How It Appears in Practice

The concept is detailed in the **8‑week bar‑exam preparation plan** from [[summaries/session-36_2025-01-11]].  The plan demonstrates incremental pacing by:

- Starting with ~90 untimed MBE questions in week 1 and scaling to ~200 by week 6‑8.
- Expanding timed mixed sets from a modest 30‑question block to full‑length timed sets in later weeks.
- Introducing the first relaxed MPT in week 4, then moving to timed MPTs only after several weeks of stable routine.
- Maintaining a consistent 1–2 hour daily schedule with alternating input (review) and output (practice) days, which aligns with the broader [[concepts/learning-rhythm]].

## Practical Takeaway

Apply incremental pacing by mapping out a baseline routine, setting weekly and monthly checkpoints, and then modestly raising the scope of practice each cycle.  This ensures continuous improvement without overwhelming the learner, making it especially effective for high‑stakes exams like the [[entities/bar-exam]].
