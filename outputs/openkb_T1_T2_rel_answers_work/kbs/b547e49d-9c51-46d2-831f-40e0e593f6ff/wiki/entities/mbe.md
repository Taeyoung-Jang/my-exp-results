# Multistate Bar Examination (MBE)

**Entity type:** work

The **Multistate Bar Examination (MBE)** is the multiple‑choice portion of the U.S. bar exam, consisting of 200 questions that test knowledge of core legal subjects such as contracts, torts, evidence, and constitutional law. It is administered as part of the broader **[[entities/bar-exam]]** and is a key focus for candidates preparing for the bar.

## Role in the bar‑prep plan (session‑36_2025-01-11)

The session‑36 summary outlines a sustainable weekly rhythm for integrating MBE practice into an overall study schedule. Key recommendations include:
- Beginning with untimed question blocks (≈20‑25 questions) on input days and gradually increasing volume.
- Introducing timed mixed sets on Fridays, scaling from 30 to 50 questions as the exam approaches.
- Using weekly checkpoints that track MBE accuracy and comfort level rather than raw hour counts.
- Maintaining a **[[concepts/burnout-prevention]]** focus by protecting a full rest day each week.

These practices embody the principles of **[[concepts/learning-rhythm]]** (alternating input and output days) and **[[concepts/incremental-pacing]]** (slowly raising question volume and timed‑practice intensity).

## Study recommendations derived from the document

| Phase | MBE activity | Typical load |
|-------|---------------|--------------|
| Weeks 1‑2 | Untimed subject‑specific question sets | ~90‑110 questions total per week |
| Weeks 3‑4 | Add a mixed untimed set; lengthen timed Friday set | ~140‑160 questions |
| Weeks 5‑6 | Introduce larger timed mixed sets; begin occasional timed **[[entities/mpt]]** practice | ~170‑200 questions |
| Weeks 7‑8 | Maintain volume; add a second short timed block if stable | ~200‑220 questions |

Progress is measured by improvements in accuracy on the timed sets and the speed of outlining or writing essays, which are used as complementary checkpoints.
