# Summary of session-31_2023-09-06

**Document:** Conversation session 31 (2023‑09‑06) – user asks about how to assess whether a food allergy has been outgrown, reliable testing, and post‑challenge care.

## Key Points

1. **Evaluation of suspected change in allergy**  
   - Start with a detailed clinical history taken by an allergist.  
   - Skin‑prick test or specific‑IgE blood test can show *sensitisation* but not necessarily clinical reactivity.  
   - The gold‑standard confirmation of tolerance is a **supervised oral food challenge** performed in a medical setting.

2. **Distinguishing allergy, intolerance, and oral‑allergy syndrome (OAS)**  
   - **Food allergy**: IgE‑mediated, risk of anaphylaxis; diagnosed via history, testing, and challenge.  
   - **Food intolerance**: non‑immune mechanisms (e.g., lactase deficiency); diagnosis relies on symptom patterns and exclusion diets.  
   - **Oral‑allergy syndrome**: cross‑reactivity between pollen allergens and raw fruits/vegetables; identified by characteristic oral itching after ingestion, especially in patients with seasonal allergic rhinitis.

3. **Post‑challenge management**  
   - Decision to discontinue epinephrine auto‑injectors is individualised; factors include severity of prior reactions, confidence in tolerance, and comorbid asthma.  
   - **Regular inclusion** of the tolerated food is recommended to maintain tolerance – typically **once a week** (or a few times weekly) rather than monthly.  
   - Long gaps (several months) may allow tolerance to wane, particularly in children or recent tolerizers.

4. **When to re‑evaluate / resume epinephrine**  
   - New or reproducible immediate‑type symptoms after eating the food:  
     - Oral itching, throat tightness, hives, or angio‑edema.  
     - Rapid onset gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, vomiting).  
     - Light‑headedness, feeling “off,” or any systemic reaction.  
   - Even mild, repeatable symptoms merit a follow‑up with the allergist and temporary reinstatement of epinephrine.

## Practical Recommendations

- **Step‑wise approach**: history → targeted IgE testing (if helpful) → supervised oral challenge → individualized post‑challenge plan.  
- **Maintenance dosing**: aim for a normal serving of the food about **once per week**; no strict schedule needed, just avoid multi‑month avoidance.  
- **Monitoring**: keep a brief log of reactions; any pattern of symptoms triggers a re‑evaluation.
