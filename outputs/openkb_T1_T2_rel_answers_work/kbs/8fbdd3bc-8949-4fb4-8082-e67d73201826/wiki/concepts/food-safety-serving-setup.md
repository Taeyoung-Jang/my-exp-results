# Food Safety Serving Setup

**Definition**: A set of practical arrangements for presenting and serving food that reduce the risk of accidental exposure to allergens, especially peanuts, while also supporting regular inclusion for those who have recently outgrown an allergy.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-08_2021-09-06]] and updated guidance from [[summaries/session-31_2023-09-06]])

1. **Separate serving tools**
   - Use a dedicated scoop for each ice‑cream flavor.
   - Provide individual spoons or tongs for each topping.
2. **Clear visual separation**
   - Place desserts, snacks, and main dishes in distinct zones.
   - Keep nut‑free items on a dedicated platter away from other foods.
3. **Signage**
   - Small cards next to each bowl or container stating, e.g., “nut‑free – use this scoop only.”
   - Labels can also note the brand of the product when relevant (e.g., [[entities/frontera]] salsa, [[entities/sabra]] hummus).
4. **Pre‑service checks**
   - Verify that all packaged items are from trusted, peanut‑free brands such as [[entities/simple-mills]] crackers, [[entities/triscuit]] crackers, [[entities/ithaca]] hummus, [[entities/haagen-dazs]] ice cream, or [[entities/tillamook]] ice cream.
5. **Guest communication**
   - Pair the setup with a brief, friendly reminder about allergens (see [[concepts/allergen-communication]]).
   - When a guest has recently passed a supervised oral food challenge, inform them that regular inclusion (e.g., weekly servings) helps maintain tolerance, referencing [[concepts/maintenance_of_food_tolerance]].
6. **Support for regular inclusion**
   - Offer the previously allergenic food in an accessible, appealing way at least once a week to reinforce tolerance.
   - Keep the food on a clearly labeled, dedicated platter to encourage consistent consumption without cross‑contamination.
7. **Re‑evaluation cues**
   - Encourage guests to report any new immediate‑type symptoms (mouth itching, hives, gastrointestinal upset, light‑headedness) after eating the food.
   - Such symptoms should trigger a follow‑up with an allergist and possibly a repeat [[concepts/food_allergy_evaluation]] and reconsideration of epinephrine auto‑injector use.

## Why It Matters

When guests have severe peanut allergies, even trace contamination can be dangerous. By structuring the serving area with dedicated utensils, clear labeling, brand awareness, and ongoing communication, hosts create a safer environment, reduce anxiety, and support individuals who are re‑introducing foods after an allergy has been outgrown.
