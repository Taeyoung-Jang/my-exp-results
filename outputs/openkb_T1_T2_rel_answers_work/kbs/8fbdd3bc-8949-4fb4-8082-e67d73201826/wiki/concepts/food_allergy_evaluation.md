# Food Allergy Evaluation Process

**Concept type:** Concept

The *Food Allergy Evaluation Process* outlines the standard clinical workflow used by allergists to determine whether a person has a true IgE‑mediated food allergy, an intolerance, or an oral‑allergy syndrome, and to verify if tolerance has been acquired.

---

## Core Steps

1. **Comprehensive History**
   - Detailed symptom chronology, timing, and severity.
   - Relationship to specific foods, preparation methods, and co‑existing conditions such as asthma or pollen allergies.

2. **Targeted IgE Testing**
   - **Skin‑prick test** or **specific‑IgE blood test** to detect sensitisation.
   - Results indicate the presence of IgE antibodies but do **not** confirm clinical reactivity; they must be interpreted alongside the history.

3. **Differential Diagnosis**
   - **Food allergy**: IgE‑mediated, risk of anaphylaxis.
   - **Food intolerance**: Non‑immune mechanisms (e.g., enzyme deficiency); diagnosis relies on symptom patterns rather than IgE testing.
   - **Oral‑allergy syndrome (OAS)**: Cross‑reactivity between pollen and raw fruits/vegetables, identified by oral itching and throat discomfort in pollen‑allergic individuals.

4. **Supervised Oral Food Challenge**
   - Considered the **gold standard** for confirming tolerance.
   - Performed in a medical setting with incremental dosing and continuous monitoring.
   - Only undertaken when the anticipated risk is low enough for a safe challenge.

5. **Post‑Challenge Management**
   - Decision on continued epinephrine auto‑injector use is individualized.
   - Encourage **regular inclusion** of the tolerated food (usually **once a week** or a few times per week) to maintain tolerance, linking to [[concepts/maintenance_of_food_tolerance]].
   - Monitor for any new or reproducible immediate‑type symptoms; these trigger re‑evaluation and possible reinstatement of epinephrine.
