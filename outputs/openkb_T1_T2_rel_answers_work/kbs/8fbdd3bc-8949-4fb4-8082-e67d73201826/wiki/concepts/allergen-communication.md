# Allergen Communication

**Definition**: A set of polite, low‑pressure strategies for gathering information about guests' food allergies or dietary restrictions before a social event, and for confirming that any disclosed allergies are still relevant.

## Core Principles
1. **Timing** – Ask early, preferably when inviting guests, so they have time to respond.
2. **Tone** – Keep the request light and framed as part of normal planning (e.g., “I’m planning the menu and want to make sure everyone can enjoy everything—any allergies or no‑go foods I should know about?”).
3. **Clarity** – Use simple language and give guests a chance to list any specific allergens (peanuts, nuts, dairy, gluten, etc.).
4. **Documentation** – Note the responses in a quick checklist so you can cross‑reference it with your ingredient watch list.
5. **Follow‑up** – If a guest mentions an allergy, confirm any questions about hidden ingredients or cross‑contamination.
6. **Verification** – When a guest indicates they may have outgrown an allergy, encourage a brief discussion about any recent [[concepts/food_allergy_evaluation]] (e.g., allergist review or supervised oral food challenge) to ensure the allergy is truly resolved.
7. **Maintenance** – Remind guests that continued, regular inclusion of a tolerated food (typically weekly) helps maintain tolerance, as described in [[concepts/maintenance_of_food_tolerance]].

## Practical Steps (from [[summaries/session-08_2021-09-06]])
- Include the question in the event invitation or a brief follow‑up message.
- Phrase it as part of routine planning rather than a special exception.
- Offer examples of common allergens to jog memory, but let guests add anything else.
- Reassure guests that you’ll take steps to keep the menu safe (linking to [[concepts/peanut-allergy-watch-list]] and [[concepts/food-safety-serving-setup]]).
- If a guest reports a past allergy that may have changed, note whether they have undergone a recent evaluation; if not, suggest they consult an allergist before assuming safety.
- Keep an eye out for any new or repeatable symptoms after eating a previously problematic food; such signals may warrant the guest to carry an [[entities/epinephrine_auto_injector]] again and seek re‑evaluation.

## Example Script
> *“I’m putting together the menu for the get‑together and want to make sure everyone can enjoy everything. Do you have any allergies or foods you can’t have? If you’ve had an allergy in the past that you think you’ve outgrown, let me know so we can be sure the menu is still safe for you.”*

Using this approach helps create an inclusive atmosphere while gathering the critical information needed to plan a safe, enjoyable gathering.
