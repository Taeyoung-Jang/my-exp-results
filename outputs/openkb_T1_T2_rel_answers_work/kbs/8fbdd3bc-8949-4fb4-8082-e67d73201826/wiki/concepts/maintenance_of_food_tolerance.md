# Maintenance of Food Tolerance

**Concept definition**: A set of practical recommendations that help individuals keep a previously confirmed food tolerance over time after passing a supervised oral food challenge.

---

## Why maintenance matters

Even after a **supervised oral food challenge** demonstrates that a person can safely ingest a food, the immune system may revert to a reactive state if exposure is too infrequent. The source conversation (see [[summaries/session-31_2023-09-06]]) notes that long gaps—especially several months—can allow tolerance to drift, particularly in children or recent tolerizers.

## Core recommendations

1. **Regular inclusion**
   - Aim for a normal serving **about once a week** (or a few times per week).  This frequency is enough to keep the immune system “familiar” with the antigen without imposing a strict schedule.
2. **Avoid prolonged avoidance**
   - Periods of several months without the food increase the risk of tolerance loss.  If a long break is unavoidable, a repeat oral challenge may be needed.
3. **Flexible real‑life eating**
   - Incorporate the food in typical meals rather than as a prescribed medication dose.  The goal is natural, steady exposure.
4. **Symptom monitoring**
   - Keep a simple log of any reactions after eating the food.  New or repeatable immediate‑type symptoms (oral itching, hives, gastrointestinal upset, light‑headedness) should prompt a re‑evaluation.
5. **Re‑evaluation triggers**
   - Any of the following after inclusion suggests a need to see an allergist and possibly resume carrying an epinephrine auto‑injector:
     - Oral or throat itching/tightness
     - Hives or angio‑edema
     - Rapid‑onset nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain
     - Sudden light‑headedness or a “wrong” feeling

## Connection to broader allergy care

- This maintenance strategy is part of the overall **food allergy evaluation** process, which begins with a detailed history and may involve skin‑prick or IgE testing before the challenge (see [[concepts/food_allergy_evaluation]]).
- Consistent maintenance supports the long‑term goal of reducing the need for emergency medication, aligning with best practices for **allergen communication** and patient education.
