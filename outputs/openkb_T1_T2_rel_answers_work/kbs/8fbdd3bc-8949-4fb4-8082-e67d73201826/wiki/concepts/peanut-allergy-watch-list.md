# Peanut Allergy Watch List

**Purpose** – Provide a quick reference of common label language and food categories where peanuts can appear unintentionally, helping anyone with a peanut allergy avoid accidental exposure.

## Typical Label Terms
- **Peanut oil**
- **Peanut flour**
- **Ground nuts** / **mixed‑nut products**
- Generic descriptors such as **"nut paste"**, **"nut meal"**
- Phrases indicating possible contamination: **"may contain peanuts"**, **"processed on shared equipment"**

## Food Categories to Scrutinize
| Category | Why it’s risky | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Packaged snacks** | Often contain hidden nuts or are produced in shared facilities | Cookies, granola bars, trail mixes |
| **Candies & confectionery** | Nut‑based fillings or processing lines | Chocolate bars, toffee |
| **Asian‑style sauces & condiments** | Satay, hoisin, some soy sauces may include peanut derivatives |
| **Baked goods** | Recipes may call for nut meals or be made in facilities with peanuts |
| **Processed foods** | Labels may list peanuts in the ingredient list or as an allergen warning |

## Practical Checking Tips
1. **Read the ingredient list** – Look for the terms above.
2. **Inspect the allergen statement** – Even if peanuts aren’t listed as an ingredient, the warning *may contain peanuts* is a red flag.
3. **Prefer whole‑food options** where you control the ingredients (e.g., fresh veggies, homemade dips).
4. **Choose trusted brands** that explicitly label products as peanut‑free; still verify each batch.
