# Nutrition for Nurses

## Overview
Nutrition is a cornerstone for nurses working long, physically demanding shifts. Proper fueling helps maintain energy, focus, and recovery, reducing burnout and supporting patient care.

## Core Principles
- **Balanced macronutrients**: combine protein, complex carbs, and healthy fats each meal.
- **Frequent small refueling**: snack every 2‑3 hours to avoid energy crashes.
- **Hydration**: aim for at least 2 L of water per shift.
- **Caffeine management**: follow a spaced schedule as described in [[concepts/caffeine-timing]].

## Meal & Snack Planning (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-06-15]])
### Default lunches
1. **Chicken‑and‑rice bowl** – rotisserie chicken, microwavable rice, frozen veggies; stays fresh cold.
2. **Turkey‑cheese wrap** – whole‑grain tortilla, sliced turkey, cheese, spinach, with a side of ranch or mustard.

### Snacks (compact, non‑soggy)
- **Yogurt** – [[entities/chobani]] or [[entities/oikos]] cup.
- **Protein bars** – [[entities/kind]] bar or [[entities/rxbar]].
- **Veggie & dip** – baby carrots with [[entities/sabra]] hummus singles.
- **Cheese stick** – [[entities/blue-diamond]] cheese stick.
- **Nuts** – Blue Diamond almonds.
- **Fruit** – a small bunch of grapes or a clementine.
- **Meat stick** – [[entities/slim-jim]] or [[entities/chomps]] beef stick.

## Hydration & Caffeine
Carry a large water bottle and schedule caffeine: first dose with breakfast (6‑7 am), second around 10‑11 am, optional third small dose 2‑3 pm. This spreads stimulation and avoids late‑day crashes.

## Timing & Micro‑breaks (integrated with [[concepts/shift-routine]])
During the shift, use micro‑breaks: sip water while walking, 20‑second breathing pauses, and eat a bite of snack while charting. When a lull appears, take a 10‑minute reset – sit, hydrate, eat, and relax shoulders.

## Emergency Options
If lunch is forgotten, choose cafeteria items that are filling but low‑fat: grilled‑chicken sandwich, rice‑and‑protein bowl, or a cup of chili. In vending machines, pair a protein bar with nuts and a piece of fruit.

## Practical Tips
- Pack food in a small insulated bag to keep temperature stable.
- Rotate snack varieties to avoid monotony.
- Keep a list of quick scripts for requesting coverage (see [[concepts/shift-routine]]).

**Result:** By following these nutrition strategies, nurses can sustain energy, reduce stress, and improve overall well‑being throughout demanding 12‑hour shifts.
