# Rotating Chore Schedule

**Definition**: A rotating assignment system for shared household chores that promotes fairness and reduces conflict.

---

## Overview
A **Rotating Chore Schedule** is a simple, predictable method for distributing household responsibilities among multiple occupants. Rather than assigning permanent duties, the schedule cycles participants through primary chores (e.g., kitchen reset, living‑room tidy) on a regular basis—typically weekly. This rotation helps avoid feelings of resentment, prevents any single person from becoming the default "clean‑up" monitor, and keeps the shared space feeling cooperative.

---

## Key Elements
| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Periodicity** | Usually weekly, but can be adapted to any regular interval that suits the household. |
| **Primary vs. Light Tasks** | Primary tasks are the core cleaning actions (e.g., wiping counters, resetting the living‑room). Light tasks are smaller, opportunistic actions (e.g., putting away stray items) that anyone can handle as they arise. |
| **Rotation Logic** | Participants shift forward one spot each period, ensuring each person experiences each primary task over a full cycle. |
| **Flexibility** | The schedule can be adjusted on the fly; if someone is unavailable, another member can cover the spot without breaking the overall rhythm. |

---

## Benefits
- **Fairness** – Everyone gets an equal share of the more demanding chores.
- **Reduced Policing** – No single resident is responsible for enforcing standards; the schedule itself sets expectations.
- **Clear Expectations** – With a set rotation, each person knows exactly when they are responsible for which space.
- **Ease of Conversation** – Introducing the schedule can be framed as a cooperative plan rather than a criticism, supporting smoother [[concepts/shared-household-communication]].

---

## Example from Session 14 (2022‑06‑15)
The conversation in [[summaries/session-14_2022-06-15]] proposes a three‑week rotation for a family of four:

1. **Week 1** – You handle the kitchen reset; your brother handles the living‑room tidy; parents manage light tasks.
2. **Week 2** – Brother takes the kitchen; a parent takes the living‑room; you take light tasks.
3. **Week 3** – The other parent takes the kitchen; you take the living‑room; brother handles light tasks.
4. **Repeat** – The cycle starts again, keeping the workload balanced.

---

## Implementation Tips
1. **Co‑create the Schedule** – Involve all house members when drafting the rotation to increase buy‑in.
2. **Keep It Visible** – Post the current week’s assignments on a fridge board or shared digital note.
3. **Use Gentle Reminders** – If a task is missed, a light, private check‑in works better than public scolding.
4. **Review Periodically** – Every month, briefly discuss whether the rotation feels fair and tweak as needed.
