# Baseline Expectations

**Definition**: A set of light, mutually agreed‑upon standards for how shared household areas are used and maintained. They are created collaboratively, framed as habits rather than rigid rules, and aim to keep the home comfortable while minimizing the need for policing.

## Key Elements (from [[summaries/session-14_2022-06-15]])
- **Collaborative creation** – All house members discuss what matters most (noise, cleanliness, guest frequency) and co‑author the expectations.
- **Habit framing** – Language such as “let’s leave the kitchen ready for the next person” replaces prescriptive commands.
- **Simple, concrete actions** – Examples include wiping counters after cooking, not leaving personal items in common areas for more than a day, and a quick visual check of shared spaces.
- **Consistency over perfection** – The goal is a reliable baseline that feels natural, not an unattainable standard.
- **Periodic check‑ins** – Brief, friendly reviews help adjust the baseline before small annoyances become larger conflicts.

## Benefits
- Reduces the perception of “policing” because expectations feel co‑owned.
- Encourages a sense of shared responsibility and mutual respect.
- Provides a clear, low‑effort reference point for everyday interactions.
- Supports smoother communication, especially in family settings where tone matters.
