# Reading Ritual

**Definition** – A reading ritual is a small, repeatable ceremony that surrounds the act of opening a book, turning it into a moment of presence rather than a task. It typically involves a brief pause, sensory cues, and a simple note‑taking habit to anchor the experience.

## Core Elements (as suggested in [[summaries/session-36_2025-06-23]])
- **Pre‑reading pause** – notice the book’s age, smell, marks, or any personal connection before turning a page.
- **Sensory cue** – a warm drink, a candle, or a brief stretch to signal the transition into reading mode.
- **Micro‑journal** – a few handwritten lines about mood, setting, or a line that resonated, without aiming for analysis.
- **Closing gesture** – a tiny act such as slipping a postcard inside, lighting a candle for a minute, or offering a quiet thank‑you when the volume is finished.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- It supports an [[concepts/intentional-reading-habit]] by providing structure that feels nurturing rather than checklist‑driven.
- A simple [[concepts/tracking-template]] can be used to record the ritual’s moments, keeping the log lightweight and sustainable.

## Benefits
- Encourages mindfulness and emotional connection with the text.
- Reduces pressure, making long‑term reading projects feel sustainable.
- Creates a personal archive of feelings and sensory experiences that can be revisited later.
