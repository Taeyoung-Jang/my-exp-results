## Color Versatility

**Definition**
- Color versatility describes how easily a garment’s colour pairs with a broad range of other items, allowing the piece to be used across different looks, seasons, and contexts without feeling repetitive.

**Why it matters in an outerwear rotation**
- When building a minimal wardrobe, each outer layer should maximize outfit permutations. A versatile hue reduces the need for multiple colour‑specific pieces and supports a streamlined, interchangeable system.
- In [[summaries/session-40_2024-10-15]], navy was highlighted as the optimal colour for a mid‑weight [[entities/harrington-jacket]] because it complements every denim wash, neutral tee, and most sneaker colours.

**Key insights from the source session**
- **Navy** was chosen over black (too stark) and olive (more niche) for its universal compatibility.
- The colour choice enables the Harrington to fill a distinct visual niche between a rugged [[entities/denim-jacket]] and a soft [[entities/hoodie]], preventing redundancy while keeping the overall palette cohesive.
- By selecting a versatile colour, the user can rotate the three outer pieces—denim jacket, hoodie, and navy Harrington—across mild‑to‑cool days without needing additional colour variations.

**Relation to other concepts**
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/outerwear-rotation]]: a versatile colour expands the functional reach of each rotation piece.
- Supports [[concepts/layering-strategy]]: interchangeable colours make layering under the outer layer seamless.
- Complements [[concepts/clothing-keepers]] by reducing the total number of garments needed for a functional wardrobe.

**Practical application**
1. Choose a base colour (e.g., navy, charcoal, beige) for core outerwear pieces.
2. Pair that colour with varied inner layers—plain tees, knit polos, hoodies—to create distinct looks.
3. Evaluate new outerwear additions for colour compatibility before purchase to maintain a versatile palette.
