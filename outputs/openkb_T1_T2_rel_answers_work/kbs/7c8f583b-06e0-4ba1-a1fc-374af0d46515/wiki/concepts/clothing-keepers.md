# Clothing Keepers

**Definition**

Clothing keepers are garments that remain in a wardrobe far beyond their typical lifespan because they offer more than basic utility. Their continued use is driven by a combination of functional fit, emotional attachment, and the distinctive character that develops through wear.

**Key Drivers**
- **Fit & Comfort**: Items that mold to the body over time and feel "like yours".
- **Memory & Meaning**: Pieces associated with important moments, people, or personal milestones.
- **Unique Wear Patterns**: Signs of age such as softening leather, faded denim, or patina that give the item character.

These factors overlap; a single piece can persist for all three reasons.

**Decision Framework**

When considering whether to keep, repair, or replace a keeper, the criteria outlined in [[concepts/repair-decision-criteria]] apply: assess the garment’s structural integrity, the feasibility of repair, and the emotional cost of letting it go.

The conversation in [[summaries/session-07_2023-10-15]] illustrates a classic keeper: an old brown [[entities/leather-jacket]]. The jacket's value stems from its fit, the memories attached, and the softened leather. Maintenance decisions focus on functional repairs (e.g., fixing a sticky [[entities/zipper-slider]] and refreshing the lining) while preserving the jacket’s character.

**Practical Tips**
1. Prioritize repairs that restore wearability without altering the garment’s feel.
2. Use inexpensive, low‑risk fixes (e.g., graphite on a sticky zipper) before opting for major work.
3. Evaluate material choices (e.g., synthetic‑satin linings) for durability while maintaining the original aesthetic.

Understanding clothing keepers helps balance sentimental value with practical stewardship, extending the life of beloved pieces while making informed repair choices.
