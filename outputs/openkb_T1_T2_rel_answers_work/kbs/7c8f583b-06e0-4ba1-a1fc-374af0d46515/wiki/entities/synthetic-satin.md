# Synthetic Satin

**Synthetic Satin** is a man‑made fabric with a glossy, smooth surface that mimics the feel of traditional satin while offering greater durability and easier care. It is lightweight, slides easily against skin, and does not stretch, making it a popular choice for garment linings.

## Key characteristics
- **Material**: Usually polyester or a polyester‑blend, sometimes blended with nylon for extra strength.
- **Feel**: Silky, smooth, and cool to the touch.
- **Durability**: Resistant to wrinkling and shrinking, holds up well under repeated wear.
- **Care**: Machine‑washable on gentle cycles; quick‑drying.

## Role in leather‑jacket maintenance
In the context of [[concepts/leather-jacket-maintenance]], synthetic satin is frequently recommended as the lining material of choice because it:
- Allows the jacket to glide on and off smoothly.
- Adds minimal bulk, preserving the jacket’s original silhouette.
- Offers a cost‑effective, long‑lasting alternative to natural fibers.

The advice from [[summaries/session-07_2023-10-15]] suggests opting for a synthetic satin or similar poly‑blend when requesting a full lining replacement, especially if the goal is to retain the jacket’s classic feel while improving durability.

## Related concepts
- The decision to use synthetic satin ties into [[concepts/repair-decision-criteria]]—evaluating the balance between cost, longevity, and the emotional value of keeping the original garment.
- While not directly a "keeper" item itself, the choice of lining material can influence whether a leather jacket remains a long‑term [[concepts/clothing-keepers]] piece.

## Practical tips
- Ask the leather shop to show swatches of synthetic satin before confirming the replacement.
- Verify that the stitching will use existing holes to avoid new needle damage to the leather.
- Ensure the selected satin matches the desired weight and sheen to keep the jacket’s aesthetic consistent.
