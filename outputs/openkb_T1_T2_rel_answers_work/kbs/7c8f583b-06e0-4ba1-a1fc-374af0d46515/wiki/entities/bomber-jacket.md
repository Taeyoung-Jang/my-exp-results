## Bomber Jacket

A **bomber jacket** is a mid‑weight, often nylon or leather, outerwear piece characterized by a front zipper, ribbed cuffs, collar, and hem. It is traditionally associated with a sporty, street‑wear aesthetic.

### Role in the outerwear rotation (as discussed in [[summaries/session-40_2024-10-15]])
- The session suggested a three‑piece rotation: lightweight shell, mid‑weight jacket, and heavy coat.
- While the bomber was mentioned as one of the possible mid‑weight options, the **Harrington jacket** was recommended over it for a mostly casual, minimal wardrobe because it offers cleaner lines and a slightly more grown‑up look.
- Nevertheless, a bomber can still serve as a viable alternative within the same [[concepts/outerwear-rotation]] framework, especially for those who prefer a sportier vibe.

### Styling considerations
- **Color versatility**: The conversation highlighted navy as a universally flexible colour for mid‑weight jackets. Although navy works for bombers too, the discussion noted black can feel stark and olive more niche; navy hits the sweet spot for minimal wardrobes. See [[concepts/color-versatility]].
- **Layering**: Like other mid‑weight pieces, a bomber can be worn over a tee or thin sweater and under a heavier coat, fitting the [[concepts/layering-strategy]] described in the session.
- **Compatibility with existing pieces**: In the user's current closet the bomber would occupy a different aesthetic niche compared to the [[entities/denim-jacket]] (rugged) and [[entities/hoodie]] (soft). It adds a sport‑inspired layer rather than the cleaner, smart‑casual look of a [[entities/harrington-jacket]].

### Practical tips
1. Choose a bomber in a versatile colour (navy or muted earth tones) to maximize pairing with denim, tees, and sneakers.
2. Use it on mild‑to‑cool days when you want a bit more edge than a Harrington but less bulk than a heavy coat.
3. Pair with simple inner layers—plain tee, henley, or lightweight sweater—to keep the look balanced.
