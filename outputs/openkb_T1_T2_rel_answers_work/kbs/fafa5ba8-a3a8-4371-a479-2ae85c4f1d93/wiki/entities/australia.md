# Australia

Australia is a country and continent in the Southern Hemisphere, known for its stable democratic institutions and use of various electoral reforms. In the conversation of **session-11_2022-06-15**, Australia was cited as an example where ranked‑choice voting (also called instant‑runoff voting) has been adopted and observed to soften candidate rhetoric and encourage broader coalitions.

## Relevance to the session
- **Electoral reform example**: Ranked‑choice voting in Australia provides early evidence that the system can reduce polarization by rewarding candidates who appeal beyond their base. This aligns with the discussion of [[concepts/voting_reforms]] and the broader theme of mitigating political tribalism.
- **Comparative context**: The document contrasts Australia’s experience with other jurisdictions such as New York City ([[entities/nyc]]) and New Zealand ([[entities/new_zealand]]), illustrating how different electoral designs influence political dynamics.

## Key points from the session
- Ranked‑choice voting “has some early evidence of softening candidate rhetoric” in Australia.
- The system “pushes campaigns to appeal to voters who might rank them second or third,” encouraging broader appeal.
- While not a complete solution, Australia’s experience supports the argument that **structural drivers** like voting methods can moderate the underlying **psychological drivers** of tribalism.
