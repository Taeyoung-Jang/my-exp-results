# United States

**Type:** place

## Overview
The United States is a federal republic located in North America, comprising 50 states and a federal district. It operates under a constitutional democracy with a strong tradition of civil liberties and a market‑based economy.

## Political Context in Session 11
In the conversation *session-11_2022-06-15*, the United States is examined as a case where political identity has become highly binary, creating a “two‑team” dynamic.

Key points from the discussion:
- The U.S. electoral system features **winner‑take‑all voting** and often **gerrymandered districts**, which amplify tribal tendencies.
- Media ecosystems driven by outrage and algorithmic amplification further reinforce division.
- Psychological instincts such as group‑sorting and threat sensitivity are identified as core drivers of polarization.

## Structural Drivers
The document highlights several structural factors that intensify polarization in the United States:
- **Winner‑take‑all voting** (single‑member districts) magnifies partisan battles.
- **Gerrymandering** creates artificially safe seats, reducing incentives for moderate candidates.
- **Outrage‑focused media** and social‑media algorithms reward extreme content, shaping public discourse.

These align with the broader concept of [[concepts/structural_drivers]].

## Psychological Drivers
Underlying the structural mechanisms are deep‑seated human tendencies:
- **Identity‑based group sorting** leads individuals to view politics as a reflection of self.
- **Status and threat sensitivity** make polarizing cues especially salient.

These factors correspond to the concept of [[concepts/psychological_drivers]].

## Reform Proposals Mentioned
The session discusses voting‑system reforms that could mitigate the two‑team dynamic in the United States:
- **Approval voting** – a simple ballot that lets voters endorse all acceptable candidates, encouraging broader‑appeal winners.
- **Ranked‑choice voting** – used in places like **New York City** (see [[entities/nyc]]), showing early evidence of softer rhetoric.
- **Open primaries** – intended to dilute the power of highly ideological party bases.

All these reforms fall under the umbrella of [[concepts/voting_reforms]].
