## Value‑Based Non‑Negotiable vs Nice‑to‑Have Prioritization Framework

**Purpose**: Provides activists and organizers a concrete method to distinguish policies or actions that are core to their underlying values (non‑negotiables) from those that are desirable but adaptable (nice‑to‑haves). This helps maintain strategic focus, avoid burnout, and keep collaborative momentum.

### Core Components
1. **Core Value Identification**
   - List the fundamental values driving the movement (e.g., fairness, safety, economic security).  
   - A policy is a *non‑negotiable* if removing it would break one of these values.
2. **Urgency Assessment**
   - Determine if immediate implementation is required to prevent real harm. Immediate‑need items are stronger candidates for non‑negotiables.
3. **Impact Projection**
   - Imagine the outcome without the item. If the overall vision remains intact, the item is likely a *nice‑to‑have*.
4. **Prioritization Label**
   - Tag each item as **Non‑Negotiable** or **Nice‑to‑Have** based on the above checks.

### Week‑to‑Week Checklist (derived from [[summaries/session-37_2024-06-15]])
- **Value Alignment** – Does the item protect a core value?
- **Urgency Check** – Is immediate action essential?
- **Future‑Fit Test** – Would delayed or phased implementation still feel like meaningful progress?
- **Label** – Assign **Non‑Negotiable** or **Nice‑to‑Have**.

### How It Relates to Other Concepts
- Builds on the broader [[concepts/priority_framework]] used for organizing tasks and goals.
- Supports the sense of [[concepts/personal_agency]] by clarifying where individuals can have decisive impact.
- Encourages focus on small, achievable wins, aligning with [[concepts/incremental_progress]].
- Addresses the motivational aspect of activism described in [[concepts/psychological_drivers]], helping activists manage impatience versus genuine red‑lines.

### Practical Example (Session 37)
In the conversation, a volunteer wanted to push an entire local party platform at once. Applying the framework:
- **Core Values**: fairness, community safety.
- **Policy Check**: A housing affordability clause directly protects fairness → **Non‑Negotiable**.
- **Policy Check**: A specific zoning amendment is a route to fairness but not the only one → **Nice‑to‑Have**.
- The volunteer then communicated this distinction in meetings, acknowledging incremental steps while emphasizing the essential values.

### Benefits
- **Clarity**: Reduces internal conflict by making trade‑offs explicit.
- **Sustainability**: Prevents burnout by allowing flexible pacing on nice‑to‑haves.
- **Collaboration**: Keeps allies engaged; they see respect for shared goals rather than a purity test.
