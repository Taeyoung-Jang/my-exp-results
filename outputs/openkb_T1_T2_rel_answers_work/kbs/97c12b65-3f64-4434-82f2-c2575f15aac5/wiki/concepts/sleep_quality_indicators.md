# Sleep Quality Indicators

**Definition**: Observable metrics and subjective feelings that signal how restorative and consolidated a night’s sleep is.

## Core Indicators (from [[summaries/session-37_2024-10-20]])
1. **Sleep onset latency** – how quickly you fall asleep after getting into bed. A noticeable reduction (e.g., falling asleep within 15‑20 minutes) is a positive sign.
2. **Number and length of nocturnal awakenings** – fewer interruptions and shorter wake‑times during the night indicate better continuity.
3. **Perceived sleep depth** – feeling that the sleep you got was “solid” or “unbroken,” even if total hours vary.
4. **Morning grogginess** – reduced need for prolonged stretching or lingering fatigue upon waking.
5. **Pre‑sleep tension** – lower anxiety or muscle tension before bedtime, making the transition to sleep smoother.
6. **Daytime energy and mood** – consistent alertness, stable mood, and the ability to concentrate without excessive daytime sleepiness.

## How These Indicators Fit Together
- Consistent **[[concepts/sleep_hygiene]]** (regular bedtime, dark/cool room, screen curfew) supports improvements in all the above indicators.
- When these metrics stay stable over several days, they reflect **[[concepts/sleep_stability]]** – the sleep system’s ability to maintain a healthy pattern.
- If multiple indicators deteriorate together, the **[[concepts/decision_rule_sleep_monitoring]]** suggests applying a decision rule (e.g., three bad nights in a week) and possibly consulting a professional.
- Persistent daytime symptoms that align with the red‑flag criteria in **[[concepts/clinician_red_flags]]** may warrant clinical assessment.

## Practical Use
- Track the indicators in a simple sleep diary or app.
- Look for trends rather than isolated incidents; a single night of longer latency is normal, but a pattern of three or more nights with increased latency plus daytime fatigue signals a need to adjust habits or seek help.
- Use the indicators to evaluate the effectiveness of **[[concepts/sleep_hygiene]]** changes or interventions such as **[[concepts/light_therapy]]** or **[[concepts/exercise_and_sleep]]**.
