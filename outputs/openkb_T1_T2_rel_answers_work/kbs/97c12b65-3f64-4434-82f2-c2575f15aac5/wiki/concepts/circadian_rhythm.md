## Circadian Rhythm

The **circadian rhythm** is a roughly 24‑hour internal time‑keeping system that regulates physiological processes, most notably the sleep‑wake cycle. It is driven by a central pacemaker in the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the brain and is entrained by external cues called *zeitgebers*, the most powerful of which is natural light.

### How the rhythm shapes sleep timing
- **Wake‑time anchor**: Keeping a consistent wake‑up time each day provides a strong cue that synchronises the whole rhythm. In the 14‑day reset plan from [[summaries/session-11_2024-01-20]], the wake‑time anchor is the single non‑negotiable element.
- **Morning light exposure**: Light received within the first hour after waking shifts the rhythm earlier, promoting earlier melatonin release the following night. This is the core of [[concepts/light_therapy]].
- **Evening dimming**: Reducing bright light in the evening signals the body to prepare for sleep, reinforcing the rhythm.

### Practical implications for habit redesign
| Habit | Role in circadian alignment | Related concept |
|-------|----------------------------|-----------------|
| Fixed wake time | Sets the daily phase reference | — |
| Morning sunlight (10‑30 min) | Strong zeitgeber that advances the clock | [[concepts/light_therapy]] |
| Caffeine cutoff (≥6 h before bedtime) | Prevents caffeine‑induced phase delay | [[concepts/caffeine_and_sleep]] |
| Light‑to‑moderate exercise (late morning/early afternoon) | Provides secondary timing cue, boosts daytime alertness | [[concepts/exercise_and_sleep]] |
| Consistent wind‑down routine | Signals upcoming sleep, smoothes the transition | [[concepts/sleep_hygiene]] |

### Interaction with sleep disorders
Even when a sleep disorder disrupts sleep architecture, the underlying circadian rhythm can still be strengthened through the above cues. A robust rhythm can reduce nighttime awakenings and improve daytime alertness, but clinicians should monitor for any worsening of disorder‑specific symptoms. See [[concepts/clinician_red_flags]] for safety thresholds.

### Troubleshooting and adjustments
If the rhythm seems misaligned (e.g., difficulty falling asleep within 45 min), the plan suggests **if/then** actions such as adjusting light exposure or temporarily shortening exercise intensity. These steps are part of [[concepts/troubleshooting_sleep_reset]].

### Key take‑aways
- A stable **wake‑time anchor** is the cornerstone of circadian alignment.
- **Morning light** is the most potent cue; even brief exposure yields measurable phase shifts.
- Supporting habits (caffeine timing, exercise, wind‑down) reinforce the rhythm without over‑complicating the routine.
- Monitoring simple daily metrics (sleep onset latency, awakenings, morning restfulness) can indicate whether the rhythm is stabilising.
