# Light Therapy for Sleep

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Light therapy uses strategically timed exposure to bright light—natural sunlight or artificial light boxes—to shift or reinforce the body's internal clock, thereby enhancing sleep onset, duration, and overall quality.

## How It Works
Light is the strongest zeitgeber (time‑giver) for the [[concepts/circadian_rhythm]]. When the eyes receive adequate illumination in the early morning, melatonin production is suppressed, body temperature rises, and alertness improves. This cascade sets a robust phase reference that carries through the day, making it easier to feel sleepy at night.

## Practical Guidelines from the 2‑Week Reset
The sleep‑reset plan in [[summaries/session-11_2024-01-20]] recommends:
- **Morning exposure** within the first hour after waking.
- **Duration:** 10 minutes on clear days, 20‑30 minutes on overcast days; week 2 may extend this by a few minutes if tolerated.
- **Location:** Outdoors when possible; otherwise a 10,000‑lux light box positioned at eye level.
- **Consistency:** Same exposure time each day to reinforce the rhythm.

These steps are treated as a **must‑do** element of the reset because they directly support the circadian anchor.

## Interaction with Other Sleep Factors
- **Sleep Hygiene:** Light therapy complements a dim‑evening environment (low‑blue light) to promote melatonin release later in the day. See [[concepts/sleep_hygiene]].
- **Exercise Timing:** Morning light paired with late‑morning/early‑afternoon activity (see [[concepts/exercise_and_sleep]]) amplifies daytime alertness without impairing evening sleep.
- **Caffeine Management:** Limiting caffeine (see [[concepts/caffeine_and_sleep]]) ensures that the alerting effect of light is not countered by stimulant residues.
- **Troubleshooting:** If sleep onset takes >45 minutes despite adequate light, refer to [[concepts/troubleshooting_sleep_reset]] for step‑by‑step adjustments.
- **Clinical Safety:** Users with sleep disorders should monitor for red‑flag symptoms (see [[concepts/clinician_red_flags]]) and pause the protocol if issues arise.

## Monitoring Effectiveness
A minimal daily log—tracking time to fall asleep, night awakenings, morning restfulness, daytime energy, and any symptom changes—provides feedback on whether light exposure is achieving the desired phase shift without requiring detailed sleep‑stage data.

## When to Adjust
- **Insufficient benefit:** Increase morning exposure by 5‑10 minutes.
- **Excessive alertness later in the day:** Shorten exposure or shift it slightly later within the first hour.
- **Discomfort or eye strain:** Reduce intensity or use a softer light source.

## Summary
Consistent, timed morning light exposure is a low‑cost, high‑impact tool for aligning the circadian system, supporting other sleep‑hygiene practices, and enhancing overall sleep quality, especially when integrated into a structured reset like the one described in [[summaries/session-11_2024-01-20]].
