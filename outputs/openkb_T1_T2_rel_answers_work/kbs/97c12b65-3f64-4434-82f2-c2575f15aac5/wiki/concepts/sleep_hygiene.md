# Sleep Hygiene Practices

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A set of routine behaviors and environmental adjustments that support optimal sleep quality, regularity, and overall restorative function.

---

## Overview
Sleep hygiene encompasses the habits, environmental factors, and pre‑sleep routines that facilitate the body's natural sleep‑wake cycle. Good sleep hygiene does not require sophisticated tracking of sleep stages; instead, it focuses on consistent, low‑effort actions that align with the body’s circadian rhythm and promote relaxation.

## Core Elements Highlighted in [[summaries/session-11_2024-01-20]]

| Aspect | Practical Recommendation | Rationale |
|--------|--------------------------|-----------|
| **Consistent Wake‑time Anchor** | Wake at the same time every day, even on weekends. | Reinforces the internal clock and stabilises [[concepts/circadian_rhythm]]. |
| **Morning Light Exposure** | Get outside light within the first hour of waking (10‑30 min). | Light is the strongest zeitgeber, boosting melatonin regulation and daytime alertness. [[concepts/light_therapy]] |
| **Caffeine Management** | Stop caffeine at least six hours before intended bedtime. | Reduces stimulant interference with sleep onset. [[concepts/caffeine_and_sleep]] |
| **Exercise Timing** | Light‑to‑moderate activity late morning or early afternoon, adjusted for individual symptoms. | Supports daytime alertness without raising evening arousal. [[concepts/exercise_and_sleep]] |
| **Wind‑down Routine** | A short, predictable sequence (dim lights, screen‑off, calming activity) before bed. | Signals to the brain that sleep is approaching, easing transition. [[concepts/sleep_hygiene]] |
| **Flexible Bedtime Window** | Allow bedtime to float within a 30‑60 min range, going to sleep when naturally sleepy. | Prevents the stress of forcing a fixed bedtime, especially important for those with sleep disorders. |
| **Environment** | Keep room dark, cool, and quiet; consider comfortable bedding. | Optimises physiological conditions for sleep continuity. |

### Monitoring and Decision Rules (from [[summaries/session-37_2024-10-20]])
- **Threshold for concern:** Three or more bad nights within a single week, especially when they feel similar, should trigger a review of the routine. This aligns with the practical [[concepts/decision_rule_sleep_monitoring]] used to detect deteriorating [[concepts/sleep_stability]].
- **Daytime red‑flags:** Persistent fatigue, irritability, trouble concentrating, or uncontrollable sleepiness lasting more than a week or two are signals to consider professional consultation. These symptoms reflect declining [[concepts/sleep_quality_indicators]] and may meet [[concepts/clinician_red_flags]].
- **Action steps:** If the threshold is met, tighten sleep‑hygiene practices (e.g., stricter wind‑down, earlier caffeine cutoff) and monitor for a short period. If no improvement, seek a clinician’s advice.

## Why These Practices Matter
- **Circadian Alignment:** Consistency in wake time and light exposure anchors the circadian rhythm, making it easier to fall asleep and wake up refreshed.
- **Reduced Arousal:** Limiting caffeine, scheduling exercise appropriately, and employing a calming wind‑down lower physiological and cognitive arousal before bedtime.
- **Feedback Loop:** Observing early improvement signs—faster sleep onset, fewer awakenings, smoother mornings—helps reinforce the habit loop and signals that sleep stability is returning.
- **Adaptability for Sleep Disorders:** The flexible bedtime window and optional wind‑down modifications accommodate variable sleep propensity, reducing frustration and the risk of exacerbating symptoms.

## Practical Checklist (derived from the 2‑week reset plan)
1. **Wake‑time anchor** – set a fixed wake‑up time.
2. **Morning light** – 10‑30 min within the first hour.
3. **Caffeine cutoff** – stop ≥ 6 h before bed.
4. **Exercise** – light‑to‑moderate activity late morning/early afternoon.
5. **Wind‑down** – dim lights, no screens, calm activity 20‑30 min before bed.
6. **Bedtime flexibility** – aim for sleep within a 30‑60 min window.
7. **Environment** – dark, cool, quiet room.
8. **Monitor** – apply the decision rule: ≥3 bad nights/week or persistent daytime red‑flags → adjust routine or consult a professional.

## Connection to Troubleshooting
If difficulties arise (e.g., prolonged sleep onset, early awakening), the plan advises simple **if/then** adjustments—such as shortening morning light or altering the wind‑down—while monitoring red‑flag symptoms that warrant clinician review.
