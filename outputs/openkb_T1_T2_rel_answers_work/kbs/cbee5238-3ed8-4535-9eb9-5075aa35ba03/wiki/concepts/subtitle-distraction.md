# Subtitle Distraction

**Definition** – The phenomenon where on‑screen text (subtitles) competes for the viewer’s visual attention, potentially reducing the perception of acting, facial expressions, or cinematographic details.

## Origins in the source
The discussion in [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-15]] highlights that subtitles may cause a brief “eye‑dip” away from the frame, leading to missed subtle expressions or visual cues. However, it also notes that most viewers quickly adapt, turning the distraction into a rhythm that can even *enhance* nuance.

## Key aspects
- **Momentary visual shift** – Readers glance down to decode text, which can interrupt the flow of visual storytelling.
- **Cognitive adaptation** – Repeated exposure trains the brain to process subtitles and visual information simultaneously, reducing the impact over time.
- **Trade‑off** – While a minor distraction exists, subtitles often provide richer linguistic nuance than dubbing, potentially deepening emotional engagement.

## Mitigation strategies (see also [[concepts/subtitle-settings]])
1. **Optimise subtitle size and contrast** – Large enough to read at a glance, with high contrast outlines, so the eyes can return quickly to the scene.
2. **Screen distance** – Sitting farther back (especially on a TV) integrates subtitles into the visual field rather than pulling focus away.
3. **Training through exposure** – Regular viewing of subtitled content builds a natural rhythm, lessening the perception of chasing text.
4. **Viewing habits** – Encourage a loose gaze that alternates between faces and text, rather than constantly flipping back and forth.

## Relationship to other concepts
- [[concepts/foreign-film-experience]] – Subtitle distraction is one factor influencing the overall experience of foreign films.
- [[concepts/subtitle-settings]] – Proper subtitle configuration directly reduces the degree of distraction.
