# Audio Description

**Definition**

Audio description (AD) is a supplemental narration track that verbally conveys on‑screen visual information—actions, settings, facial expressions, and other visual cues—to make visual media accessible to blind or low‑vision audiences.  It is typically mixed as a separate audio channel that can be turned on or off in streaming apps and TV settings.

**Why it matters for half‑watching**

- When viewers are multitasking (e.g., cooking, cleaning), the visual component may be missed.  AD provides the missing narrative so the story remains understandable.  This aligns with [[concepts/half-watching]], where users prefer content that can be followed without full visual attention.
- Pairing AD with subtitles further reinforces comprehension, reducing the need to constantly rewind.  See also [[concepts/subtitle-distraction]] for the trade‑off between subtitle use and visual focus.

**Quality cues from the source document**
- Premium streaming services tend to offer higher‑quality AD because they treat it as part of production rather than an afterthought.  Notable platforms include [[entities/netflix]], [[entities/max]], [[entities/prime-video]], and [[entities/apple-tv-plus]].
- Genres that benefit most from AD are those with visually‑rich storytelling—mysteries, thrillers, character dramas, and animation.  Examples highlighted in [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-15]]: *Stranger Things* (Netflix), *The Crown* (Netflix), *Arcane* (Netflix), *Succession* (Max), *Station Eleven* (Max), *The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel* (Prime Video), *Reacher* (Prime Video), *Ted Lasso* (Apple TV+), and *Severance* (Apple TV+).

**Technical setup tips**
- Enable the separate AD track and optionally raise its volume relative to the main mix.
- Use TV or soundbar settings that prioritize speech, such as [[concepts/dialogue-enhancement]] modes, “voice boost,” “night mode,” or “dynamic range compression.”  These settings flatten loud‑quiet swings and keep the narration audible across a room.
- Combining AD with subtitles creates a dual‑track reinforcement, useful when the visual feed is only glanced at.

**Recommendations for easy‑to‑follow titles**
- Titles noted for clear AD and low visual noise: *Reacher* (Prime Video) and *Ted Lasso* (Apple TV+).
- Titles that can be busy (dense mixes) but still have good AD: *Stranger Things* and *Severance*; viewers may need stronger dialogue‑enhancement settings.

**Relation to other concepts**
- AD complements [[concepts/dialogue-enhancement]] by providing an audible description layer when dialogue alone is insufficient.
- For viewers who watch foreign films, AD can help bridge language gaps alongside subtitles, linking to [[concepts/foreign-film-experience]].

**Summary**
Audio description is essential for making visually‑dense media accessible during multitasking, especially when paired with thoughtful device settings and subtitles.  Its effectiveness is demonstrated across multiple platforms and titles, as discussed in [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-15]].
