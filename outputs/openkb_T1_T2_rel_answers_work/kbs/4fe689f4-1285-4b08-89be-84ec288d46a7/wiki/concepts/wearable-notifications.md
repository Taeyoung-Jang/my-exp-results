# Wearable Notifications

**Wearable Notifications** refers to the set of alert settings on a [[entities/smartwatch]] or [[entities/fitness-tracker]] that determine which information reaches the user’s wrist. Properly configured notifications help the device act as a **passive health tracker** rather than a constant source of distraction.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-02_2024-06-15]])
- **Prioritize essential alerts** – enable only phone calls and, if needed, texts from a few high‑priority contacts.
- **Disable all other app notifications** – emails, social media, promotional messages, and most third‑party app alerts should be turned off.
- **Leverage quiet modes** – activate Do Not Disturb during sleep or set custom silent hours to prevent unwanted vibrations.
- **Adjust haptic feedback** – lower intensity or switch off vibration for non‑essential alerts.
- **Keep the screen dormant** – prevent automatic wake‑on‑hand‑raise unless you need a quick glance.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Effective notification management is a key component of [[concepts/distraction-management]], as it reduces the cognitive load and prevents the wearable from becoming a “tiny screen tugging” at the user.
- By limiting alerts, users can focus on the most actionable **[[concepts/health-metrics]]** (steps, total sleep time, resting heart rate) without being overwhelmed by secondary data.

## Practical Steps for a 30‑Day Reset
1. **Week 1‑2:** Allow *only* phone call alerts; block everything else.
2. **Week 3:** Add text notifications from 2–3 priority contacts; keep all other alerts off.
3. **Week 4:** Evaluate whether a single, low‑key reminder (e.g., scheduled workout vibration) adds value; keep everything else minimal.

By following these guidelines, a wearer can keep their device useful for health monitoring while minimizing interruptions, striking a balance between insight and distraction.
