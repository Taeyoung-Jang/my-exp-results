# Health Metrics

Health metrics are the measurable data points that wearables capture to reflect a user’s physiological state and daily activity.  They range from simple, broadly useful numbers—such as step count, total sleep duration, and resting heart rate—to more complex, often less actionable scores like heart‑rate variability, stress, and readiness.

## Core metrics for beginners
- **Step count** – provides a straightforward view of daily movement.
- **Total sleep time** – indicates overall recovery without requiring interpretation of sleep stages.
- **Resting heart rate (RHR)** – a baseline indicator of cardiovascular health that can be tracked over time.

These three metrics were highlighted in the **[[summaries/session-02_2024-06-15]]** discussion as the most useful for someone starting with a smartwatch or fitness tracker.

## Advanced metrics often considered noise
- **Heart‑rate variability (HRV)** – while valuable for deep analysis, it can be confusing for new users.
- **Stress scores** – derived from HRV and other signals, but rarely actionable without context.
- **Readiness scores** – composite indices that blend sleep, activity, and HRV, often ignored by beginners.
- **Detailed sleep stage breakdowns** – provide granular insight but require interpretation that most new users lack.

The conversation recommended that users initially ignore these advanced metrics and focus on the core trio to avoid information overload.

## Relationship to other concepts
- This concept is tightly linked to **[[concepts/distraction-management]]**, as reducing exposure to noisy metrics helps keep the wearable experience low‑distraction.
- It also connects with **[[concepts/wearable-notifications]]**, since the choice of which metrics to monitor can influence what alerts or reminders the device generates.

## Practical guidance from the source
- **Prioritize** step count, total sleep time, and resting heart rate for daily checks.
- **Limit** attention to advanced metrics until a solid baseline of the core metrics is established.
- **Use** the 30‑day quiet‑mode reset plan (described in the summary) to experiment with focusing on these core metrics and assess their impact on habits.

By concentrating on these essential health metrics, users can gain meaningful insight without the cognitive load of unnecessary data, supporting a smoother, less distracting wearable experience.
