## Distraction Management

**Definition**: Distraction Management is the practice of configuring wearable‑device notifications and related settings so that the device delivers valuable information without becoming a source of frequent, unwanted interruptions.

### Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-02_2024-06-15]])
1. **Limit notification types** – Only allow essential alerts such as phone calls (and, optionally, texts from a few priority contacts). All other app, email, and social notifications should be turned off.
2. **Quiet or Do‑Not‑Disturb modes** – Enable sleep‑time quiet periods and set specific “focus” hours where the watch stays silent.
3. **Minimize haptic feedback** – Reduce vibration intensity or disable automatic screen wake to keep the wrist experience unobtrusive.
4. **Periodic review** – After a set period (e.g., a 30‑day reset), reassess which alerts truly add value and re‑enable only those.

### Practical Steps
- **Week 1‑2**: Enable only call alerts; disable all other notifications; use DND during sleep.
- **Week 3**: Add text alerts from 2‑3 priority contacts if needed; keep everything else silent.
- **Week 4**: Evaluate and optionally add a single, low‑frequency reminder (e.g., workout reminder) if it aids your routine.

### Benefits
- **Reduced cognitive load** – Fewer interruptions help maintain focus throughout the day.
- **Higher adherence** – When the device is less intrusive, users are more likely to wear it consistently.
- **Clearer insight** – By limiting noise, the core health metrics become easier to interpret.

### When to Apply
Anyone using a **smartwatch** or **fitness‑tracker** who wants to balance the benefits of real‑time data with a low‑distraction experience can apply Distraction Management techniques.
