# Wearable Device

A **wearable device** is a category of electronic product—typically worn on the wrist—that combines fitness‑tracking sensors with smart‑phone‑like notifications and health‑metric reporting.  Modern examples include smartwatches and fitness‑trackers that monitor steps, sleep, heart rate, and provide alerts for calls or messages.

## Key facts from the session‑02 discussion
- The primary value of a wearable device is **reliable activity and sleep tracking** (step count, total sleep time, resting heart rate) while remaining comfortable and having good battery life.
- Users are advised to manage **[[concepts/wearable-notifications]]** tightly: enable only calls (and possibly texts from a few priority contacts) and disable all other app alerts to prevent distraction.
- Many advanced **[[concepts/health-metrics]]** such as heart‑rate variability, stress scores, or detailed sleep stages are considered noisy for beginners and can be turned off.
- The conversation outlines a **[[concepts/distraction-management]]** strategy via a 30‑day "quiet mode" reset, focusing on minimal notifications and a simple daily metric check.
