## Summary of Session 2 (2024‑04‑10)

This document provides a practical, low‑maintenance roadmap for establishing a self‑sufficient garden on a temperate, heavy‑clay sloped yard, emphasizing a **soil‑first** approach, simple **layout/design**, and a clear **month‑by‑month checklist**.

### Core Concepts
- **[[concepts/soil-improvement]]** – soil testing, compost addition, and organic matter building are the foundational steps.  
- **[[concepts/garden-layout-design]]** – early observation of sun, drainage, and accessibility drives the design.  
- **[[concepts/contour-bed-slope-management]]** – beds built level along the natural slope to minimize erosion and water pooling, and guidance on marking contours and sizing paths for stability.

### Year‑One Checklist (March – November)

| Month | Must‑Do Tasks | Plant/Seed |
|-------|---------------|------------|
| **March** | Soil test, observe drainage & sun, add first round of compost | Spinach, peas, radish |
| **April** | Build raised beds in heavy‑clay spots, prep in‑ground areas, mulch paths | Lettuce, kale, onions, potatoes |
| **May** | Finalize layout, finish bed prep, set up simple watering plan | Tomatoes, peppers, beans, zucchini after frost passes |
| **June** | Mulch beds, steady watering, fill gaps with quick crops | Basil, lettuce, cilantro, more beans |
| **July** | Light pruning/trellising, keep beds moist, remove failing plants | Second sowing of beans or greens |
| **August** | Harvest steadily, suppress weeds, prep spaces for fall crops | Kale, arugula, spinach for fall |
| **September** | Remove spent plants, add light compost, tidy beds | Fast‑green plantings early in month |
| **October** | Final cleanup, add heavy compost or leaves, protect soil | Garlic before freeze |
| **November** | Tool cleanup, check slope drainage, notes for next year | *No new planting* |

**Timing notes:** The schedule assumes a generic last‑frost around **[[entities/mid-may-last-frost]]** and a first‑frost around **[[entities/mid-october-first-frost]]**.

### Bed Placement & Slope Strategies
- **Mix of raised and in‑ground beds:** place raised beds on the most compacted, low‑lying parts of the slope; keep in‑ground beds in higher, better‑draining zones.
- **Running level across the slope:** each raised‑bed frame is leveled so water does not pool at the low end; the long side follows the contour line.
- **Contour‑bed marking:** use a homemade A‑frame level or a long board with a bubble level to locate level points, stake them, and connect the stakes to form the contour line.
- **Path width:** 2–3 feet wide, mulched, to stay usable when damp and to accommodate a wheelbarrow.

### Recommendations for Beginners
- Stick with **contour beds** rather than full terracing or swales to keep labor low.
- Leverage the **2–3 hours/week** time budget by spreading tasks across months rather than front‑loading.
- Continuously add **compost** and **mulch** each season to gradually improve the heavy clay.

Overall, the plan balances immediate, achievable actions with long‑term soil health, ensuring a manageable path toward a self‑sufficient garden on a challenging slope.
