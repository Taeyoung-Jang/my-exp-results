**Assistant:** You can think of a “mobile living” system as two layers: what stays with you all the time, and what stays packed but doesn’t travel unless you decide it does.

Your go‑bag should basically be your everyday-life kit—things you’d be annoyed to replace on the road. A small set of clothes that mix and match, toiletries you genuinely use every day, your work gear, chargers, and a few comfort items. Treat this bag like it’s always ready to walk out the door: restock toiletries right when they run low, launder clothes before you stash them back in, and keep all cables permanently assigned to this bag so you’re never hunting.

Your storage bin is for things you don’t need every week but still want available—seasonal clothes, backup supplies, larger items that aren’t worth carrying, and anything “sometimes useful” but not mission‑critical. The key is to only open this bin with intent; if you find yourself pulling something out regularly, it belongs in the go‑bag instead.

To minimize duplicates, create one permanent set of travel-only items—chargers, toiletries, small office supplies—so you don’t take things from your home setup and forget to return them. It’s easier to buy a second cheap charger than to constantly re-pack the same one.

A simple routine helps a lot. After each trip, do a quick reset: empty the go‑bag completely, toss laundry in one pile, refill consumables, and repack immediately rather than “later.” Before leaving, do the reverse: move through the bag once to check clothing, once to check work gear, once to check personal items. That’s it. The fewer decisions you have to make, the faster and cleaner it feels.
