# Garden Layout & Design

**Overview**

Garden layout and design is the process of organizing the physical space of a garden to optimize sunlight, water drainage, accessibility, and long‑term productivity while keeping weekly labor low. It builds on the foundation of [[concepts/soil-improvement]] and informs the placement of raised versus in‑ground beds, pathways, and contour structures.

**Key Steps from the Year‑One Plan**

1. **Site Observation** – Early in the season (March) walk the yard to note:
   - Where the sun hits most of the day.
   - How water collects on the slope.
   - Areas that are easiest to reach during a 2–3 hour weekly work window.
2. **Sketch a Simple Layout** – Draw a basic map showing:
   - A few manageable beds (1–2 m wide) with clear, level surfaces.
   - Paths that are 2–3 ft wide to stay usable when damp.
   - Placement of raised beds in the poorest‑draining, heavy‑clay spots and in‑ground beds where natural drainage is better.
3. **Contour Alignment** – Align beds along the natural contour of the slope so each bed is level from end to end, preventing water pooling. See [[concepts/contour-bed-slope-management]] for practical marking methods.
4. **Iterative Adjustment** – After each growing season, use observations (e.g., plant performance, soil moisture) to tweak bed locations, width, or orientation.

**Design Considerations**

- **Sunlight Exposure** – Prioritize the sunniest quadrant for heat‑loving crops (tomatoes, peppers) and reserve shadier zones for cool‑season greens.
- **Water Management** – On a slope, direct runoff toward raised beds and use mulch on paths to reduce erosion.
- **Path Width** – Two to three feet accommodates a wheelbarrow and provides stable footing on a slope.
- **Flexibility** – Start with 2–3 beds; expand gradually as soil health improves.

**Seasonal Timing**

The layout plan is synchronized with the planting calendar based on a generic last frost of [[entities/mid-may-last-frost]] and a first frost of [[entities/mid-october-first-frost]]. Early‑season tasks focus on observation and soil amendment; bed construction peaks in April–May; planting follows the frost dates.
