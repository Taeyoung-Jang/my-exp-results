# Contour Bed & Slope Management

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Simple, low‑effort techniques for creating level beds that follow a slope’s natural contour, aimed at beginners with limited time.

---

## Why Contour Beds?
- Reduce erosion by slowing water runoff.
- Keep soil moisture more uniform across the bed.
- Provide a stable planting surface on heavy‑clay, sloped yards.
- Offer a manageable alternative to full terracing or swales.

## Core Steps (as described in [[summaries/session-02_2024-04-10]])

1. **Identify the Desired Bed Line**
   - Walk the slope and note where you want the bed to sit.
   - The bed should *run level across the slope*; its long side follows the natural contour, while the surface remains horizontal from end to end.

2. **Mark the Contour Without Fancy Tools**
   - **A‑frame level**: Build two long sticks into an “A” shape, attach a crossbar, and hang a string with a weight. When the string aligns with a mark on the crossbar, the two feet are level. Walk the A‑frame, pivoting one foot at a time, and drive a small stake at each level point.
   - **Board & bubble level**: Lay a straight board on the ground, adjust until the bubble is centered, then mark the spot. Slide the board and repeat to create a series of level marks.
   - Connect the stakes with a string or garden hose to visualize the contour line.

3. **Build the Bed**
   - **Raised beds**: Place frames on the contour line, adding soil and compost to reach a level top. Use them in the heaviest‑clay sections where drainage is poorest.
   - **In‑ground beds**: Dig along the contour line in better‑draining spots, amending with compost as you go.
   - Ensure the finished surface is level; water will then spread evenly rather than pooling at the low end.

4. **Path Width and Surface**
   - Aim for **2–3 feet** wide paths on the slope. This width provides stable footing, accommodates a wheelbarrow, and reduces mud.
   - Mulch paths with wood chips or straw to improve traction and limit erosion.

5. **Integrate with Overall Garden Design**
   - Align contour beds with the broader [[concepts/garden-layout-design]] to maintain clear access routes and sunlight exposure.
   - Incorporate soil health practices from [[concepts/soil-improvement]]—regular compost additions and mulching—to gradually improve heavy clay.

---

## Practical Tips for Beginners
- Start with **one or two small beds** to master the leveling technique before expanding.
- Use inexpensive materials (e.g., reclaimed lumber) for bed frames.
- Schedule the work during the early season (March‑April) when the ground is workable but not frozen.
- Record observations of drainage and plant performance to refine bed placement in subsequent years.

---

## Seasonal Context
- Planting windows are guided by the generic **mid‑May last frost** (`[[entities/mid-may-last-frost]]`) and **mid‑October first frost** (`[[entities/mid-october-first-frost]]`).
- Early‑season tasks (soil testing, compost incorporation) should be completed before the last frost, while bed construction can continue into late spring.
