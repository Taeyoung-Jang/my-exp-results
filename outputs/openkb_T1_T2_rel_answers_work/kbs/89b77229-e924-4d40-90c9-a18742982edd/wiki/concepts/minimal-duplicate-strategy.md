## Minimal Duplicate Strategy

**Definition**: A pragmatic approach that limits the number of repeat items you own, keeping only those that save time, reduce packing errors, or are cheap to replace, while avoiding unnecessary duplication of bulky or expensive belongings.

### Core Principles
1. **Identify travel‑only essentials** – items such as chargers, cables, adapters, a full toiletry kit, and a compact first‑aid/meds pouch should exist in duplicate (one in the go‑bag, one in a storage bin) because they are cheap, small, and critical for uninterrupted workflow.
2. **Avoid duplicating bulky or high‑value items** – shoes, jackets, work hardware, specialty tools, and grooming appliances stay in a single location (either the carry‑on, a bin, or the home base). Re‑purchasing these is more costly than a brief inconvenience.
3. **One‑and‑only items** – documents (passport, ID, company badge), prescription medications, and personal identifiers are never duplicated; they reside in the go‑bag only.
4. **Cost‑vs‑Time trade‑off** – evaluate each item: if the time saved by having a duplicate outweighs its purchase cost (e.g., a second cheap USB‑C charger), keep the duplicate.
5. **Periodic review** – every few months, audit your inventory (see the bin inventory method) to ensure duplicates remain justified.

### Application in a Mobile Living System
In the context of the [[concepts/mobile-living-system]] described in [[summaries/session-37_2025-04-10]], the Minimal Duplicate Strategy underpins the organization of the backpack go‑bag and the carry‑on clothing module:
- **Tech duplicates**: a spare phone charger, laptop charger, and universal adapter are kept in the backpack and a spare set in the storage bins.
- **Toiletry duplicates**: a complete travel‑toiletry bag lives permanently in the go‑bag; a refill pack sits in the “Seasonal + Clothing Swaps” bin.
- **First‑aid/meds**: a small kit remains in the backpack; a larger backup is stored in the “Home‑Base Utilities + Backups” bin.
- **Non‑duplicates**: bulky items like jackets, extra shoes, and specialty tools are stored only in the carry‑on or bins, not in both locations.

### Benefits
- **Reduced repacking errors** – fewer decisions about where an item belongs.
- **Faster turn‑around** – when a bag is gate‑checked, you still have all mission‑critical items.
- **Cost efficiency** – cheap duplicates replace expensive ones lost or forgotten.
- **Simplified inventory** – the one‑page lid inventory (see the bin section in the source) makes it easy to track what is "In" or "Out."
