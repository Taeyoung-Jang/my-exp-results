# Mid‑May Last Frost (Temperate Climate)

**Type:** Event

**Definition**

Mid‑May Last Frost refers to the typical timing of the final frost of spring in temperate climates. Gardeners use this date as a benchmark for when it becomes safe to plant frost‑sensitive crops.

**Relevance in Session 02**

The planning guide in [[summaries/session-02_2024-04-10]] structures its month‑by‑month checklist around a **mid‑May last‑frost** date. Tasks such as finalizing the garden layout, completing bed preparation, and planting warm‑season vegetables (e.g., tomatoes, peppers, beans, zucchini) are scheduled for May after this date passes.

**Connection to Key Concepts**

- Proper **[[concepts/soil-improvement]]** is emphasized before the last frost so that the soil is ready for planting.
- A clear **[[concepts/garden-layout-design]]** is advised once the frost risk has passed, allowing the garden to be arranged for optimal sunlight and access.
- For sloped sites, the timing of the last frost informs when to install **[[concepts/contour-bed-slope-management]]** features without risking damage from early cold snaps.

**Practical Implications**

- **Planting Window:** Warm‑season crops should be sown **after mid‑May**, once the risk of frost is minimal.
- **Soil Work:** Soil testing and compost incorporation are recommended **before** the last frost to ensure the soil is fertile when planting begins.
- **Bed Construction:** Raised or in‑ground beds can be built **pre‑frost**; however, any structures that rely on soil stability should be completed before the ground freezes for winter.

**Related Entity**

- The counterpart date for the onset of autumn freeze is **[[entities/mid-october-first-frost]]**, which marks the end of the growing season.
