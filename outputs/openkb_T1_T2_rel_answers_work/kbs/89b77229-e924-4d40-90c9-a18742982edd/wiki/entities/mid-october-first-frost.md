# Mid‑October First Frost (event)

Mid‑October First Frost is a seasonal marker indicating the average time when nighttime temperatures consistently drop below freezing in temperate climates. In **session‑02_2024‑04‑10**, this date serves as the key endpoint for the garden’s planting and harvesting schedule.

## Role in the Garden Plan
- Acts as the cutoff for **soil‑improvement** activities that can be performed before the ground freezes.
- Determines the latest month for planting cool‑season crops (e.g., kale, arugula) and the final planting of garlic before the soil hardens.
- Aligns with the **mid‑may‑last‑frost** to define a safe growing window (mid‑May to mid‑October).
- Influences the design of **garden‑layout‑design**, ensuring beds and pathways are prepared for winter protection.
- Guides decisions on **contour‑bed‑slope‑management**, such as adding a heavy layer of compost or leaf mulch before the frost to improve soil structure over the winter.

## Key Facts from the Session Document
