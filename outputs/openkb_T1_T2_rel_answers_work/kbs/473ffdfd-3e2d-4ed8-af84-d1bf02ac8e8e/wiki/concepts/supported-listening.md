# Supported Listening

**Definition**: A scaffolded listening approach that pairs slowed‑down audio (e.g., 0.9× speed) with limited, strategic use of transcripts or subtitles to help learners bridge the gap between comfortable reading comprehension and native‑speed auditory input.

**Purpose**
- Reduce the cognitive overload that arises from jumping straight to full‑speed speech.
- Provide an incremental “support ladder” that can be gradually removed as the learner’s ear adapts.
- Keep sessions short (20–30 min) to avoid burnout while still delivering regular exposure.

**Core Elements**
1. **Speed Adjustment** – Begin with a modest slowdown (typically 0.9×) and later transition to 1.0× as competence improves. This ties directly into [[concepts/progressive-speed-adjustment]].
2. **Selective Text Support** – Use transcripts or subtitles only for the first pass (preview) or after the initial listen, never as a continuous crutch.
3. **Repeated Listening** – After the first exposure, replay the same segment to consolidate auditory patterns before consulting the text.
4. **Brief Speaking Integration** – Pair listening with short shadowing drills, linking to [[concepts/shadowing-practice]].

**Implementation in the 4‑Week German Routine (session‑07_2025‑06‑15)**
- **Week 1**: All weekday sessions run at 0.9× speed. Learners listen once, skim the transcript for missed words, then listen again at the same speed. Weekend sessions use subtitles for the first half of a video/scene, then repeat without subtitles.
- **Week 2**: Speed moves to 1.0× for most sessions; transcripts are consulted *only after* a complete listen. Subtitles are kept to a brief introductory segment.
- **Week 3**: Transcripts become purely diagnostic—listen fully at native speed, then review only the problematic portions.
- **Week 4**: Pure native‑speed listening with minimal text support; learners shorten clips rather than slow them if needed.

**Benefits**
- Accelerates the transition from reading‑based comprehension to authentic listening.
- Encourages active attention to prosody, rhythm, and natural phrasing.
- Provides a measurable scaffold: speed and text support are reduced week by week.
