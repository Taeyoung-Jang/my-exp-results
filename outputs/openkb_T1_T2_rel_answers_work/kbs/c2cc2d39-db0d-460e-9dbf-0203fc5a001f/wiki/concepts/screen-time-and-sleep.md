# Screen Time and Sleep

## Definition
Screen Time and Sleep refers to the relationship between the amount, timing, and visual characteristics of electronic screen exposure in the evening and the quality, latency, and architecture of subsequent sleep.

## Biological Mechanisms
Evening exposure to short‑wavelength (blue) light suppresses melatonin and shifts the circadian clock, making it harder to fall asleep.  The effect is described in detail in [[concepts/blue-light-and-circadian-rhythm]].

## Practical Mitigation Strategies
The recommendations distilled from [[summaries/session-03_2025-07-21]] focus on three pillars: adjusting device output, managing ambient lighting, and structuring the pre‑sleep routine.

### 1. Wind‑down routine
Allocate a 30–60 minute window before bedtime with reduced visual stimulation.  This is the core of a [[concepts/wind-down-routine]] and helps the brain transition to sleep mode.

### 2. Device display settings
- **Brightness** – keep at the lower‑third of the slider on phones and ~20‑30 % on computers.
- **Night‑mode / Night Shift / Night Light** – set to the *warmest* (deep amber) setting.
- **Dark mode** – enable to lower UI contrast.
- **Platforms** – specific entities:
  - [[entities/iphone]] (iOS Night Shift, lowest‑quarter brightness)
  - [[entities/android]] (Night Light, max warmth, low brightness)
  - [[entities/macos]] (Night Shift, warmest, dark mode)
  - [[entities/windows]] (Night Light, near‑max warmth, low brightness)

### 3. Ambient room lighting
Use a single warm lamp rather than bright overhead lights.  A bulb in the 2200‑2700 K range, emitting 200‑450 lumens, placed off‑axis (side or behind) provides enough illumination to reduce screen contrast without sending a “daytime” signal.

### 4. Blue‑light‑blocking glasses
Amber lenses (e.g., [[entities/amber-blue-light-blocking-glasses]]) block more blue light than clear lenses and give a modest additional benefit when software warmth cannot be maximized.

### 5. Timing & environment
If you must use a screen for the final hour, keep the dim warm lamp on throughout; total darkness makes the screen’s contrast more alerting.

## Summary of Evidence
The session‑03 conversation emphasizes that the biggest gains come from **maximizing software warmth and lowering brightness**, followed by **ambient warm lighting** and, optionally, **amber glasses**.  Consistency in sleep schedule (same wake‑time) often outweighs any single screen tweak.
