# Wind‑Down Routine

A **wind‑down routine** is a short, low‑stimulus period (typically 30–60 minutes) before bedtime that prepares the brain and body for sleep. The goal is to minimize alerting cues—especially from screens and bright lighting—so the circadian system can shift into a “night mode.”

## Core Elements

1. **Screen Management**
   - Lower brightness to the lower third of the slider on phones and to ~20‑30 % on computers.
   - Enable the warmest night‑mode setting (Night Shift on [[entities/iphone]] and macOS, Night Light on [[entities/android]], Night Light on [[entities/windows]]). 
   - Use dark mode to reduce bright UI elements.
   - If a screen must be used for the last hour, keep it at arm’s length and avoid stimulating content.

2. **Ambient Lighting**
   - Replace overhead lights with a single warm lamp (2200‑2700 K, 200‑450 lumens) placed off‑axis or behind the user. 
   - Keep the lamp on throughout the wind‑down period; complete darkness can make the screen’s contrast more alerting.

3. **Timing & Consistency**
   - Aim for a consistent start time each night, ideally 30–60 minutes before the intended sleep time.
   - Align the routine with a regular wake‑time to reinforce the circadian rhythm.

4. **Optional Enhancements**
   - **Amber blue‑light‑blocking glasses** (e.g., [[entities/amber-blue-light-blocking-glasses]]) can provide a modest additional reduction in blue light, especially if device night‑mode cannot be set very warm.
   - Light stretching, gentle breathing, or reading on a dim device can replace fast‑paced scrolling.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- The routine directly mitigates the impact of **blue light** on the circadian system, linking to [[concepts/blue-light-and-circadian-rhythm]].
- It is a key strategy within broader **screen‑time and sleep** guidance, see [[concepts/screen-time-and-sleep]].
