# Summary of Session 32 (2025‑10‑19)

**Main contribution:** A realistic, day‑long eye‑care routine for heavy computer users, especially those who must avoid screens after work, plus ergonomic tips for long video calls and a short manager‑communication draft.

## Core Eye‑Strain Concepts
- **20‑20‑20 rule** – every 20 minutes look ~20 feet away for ~20 seconds. 20 20 20 rule
- **Lighting ergonomics** – match screen brightness to ambient light, use soft, even lighting, avoid harsh overhead or direct sunlight on the monitor. lighting ergonomics
- **Screen distance & height** – keep monitor an arm’s length away, top edge slightly below eye level. screen distance
- **Text size & contrast** – increase font size enough to avoid squinting; use warm colour temperature for long sessions. monitor settings
- **Conscious blinking & lubricating drops** – blink fully and use doctor‑approved artificial tears to prevent dryness. eye blinking
- **Movement breaks** – brief neck/shoulder stretches or a short walk each hour to reduce overall tension. movement breaks

## Hourly Workday Plan
1. **Top‑of‑the‑hour reset (≈60 s)** – look across the room or out a window, roll shoulders, blink deliberately. hourly reset routine
2. **During the hour**
   - Maintain arm‑length screen distance, slight downward gaze.
   - Use larger text, warm colour temperature, and brightness that blends with the room.
   - Apply lubricating drops as needed.
3. **Micro‑breaks** – whenever a task ends or you feel you’re staring, glance to a distant object for 10‑15 s.
4. **Avoid** – dark room with bright screen, direct sunlight on monitor, long uninterrupted staring periods.

## Adjustments for Post‑Work Screen‑Free Evenings
- Keep the workday as gentle as possible to prevent carry‑over strain.
- In the evening, rely on audiobooks/radio, keep lighting dim but not pitch‑black, and give eyes a visual rest.

## Video‑Call Ergonomics
- **Camera placement** – slightly above or beside the screen to allow a natural off‑screen gaze.
- **Window size** – keep video participants in a smaller screen area to reduce high‑contrast motion.
- **Off‑screen glances** – look away briefly (appears as “thinking”) to give eyes a break.
- **Note‑taking** – use a notepad or secondary window at a comfortable distance as an alternate focal point.
- **Audio‑only segments** – switch to audio‑only when possible, framing it as a productivity or bandwidth choice. video call ergonomics

## Draft Manager/Team Message
```
I wanted to give a quick heads‑up that I’ve been making a few adjustments to reduce eye strain during the workday. For longer video calls, I may switch to audio‑only for parts of the meeting or briefly look away from the screen while still listening and engaged. This shouldn’t affect my participation, but I wanted to mention it so it doesn’t feel unexpected. Thanks for understanding.
```
manager communication

*Key take‑away:* Consistent micro‑resets, proper lighting, comfortable screen settings, and subtle meeting adaptations together minimize eye fatigue and help avoid post‑work headaches.
