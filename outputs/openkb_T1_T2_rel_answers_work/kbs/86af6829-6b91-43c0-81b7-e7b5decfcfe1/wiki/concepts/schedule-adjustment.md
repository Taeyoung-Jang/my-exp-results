# Schedule Adjustment

**Definition**: The process of intentionally aligning sleep, meals, and daily anchors to a new work schedule—especially when shifts are longer, irregular, or fall on different days—so that the body’s circadian rhythm and energy levels stay stable.

---

## Core Principles
1. **Treat the shift like a time‑zone change** – Gradually move wake‑up and bedtime times by no more than an hour to keep the internal clock near its previous state.
2. **Prioritize sleep consistency** – Aim for the same sleep window each night, even on days off, with only modest adjustments (≤1 hour).
3. **Insert anchor routines** – Small, repeatable actions (e.g., a short walk before work, a wind‑down ritual after work) that signal the brain the start and end of a workday.
4. **Structure days off** – Use a light template (see [[summaries/session-34_2024-06-15]]) that keeps wake‑up times close to the work‑day schedule while providing restorative activities.
5. **Manage naps wisely** – Short, early‑afternoon naps (20–30 minutes) prevent deep sleep and avoid disrupting bedtime.

## Benefits
* Faster acclimation (typically 1–2 weeks) to new shift patterns.
* More stable energy levels and mood.
* Reduced risk of burnout by treating recovery as part of the schedule.

---

## Tips for Implementation
* **Shift in ≤1‑hour increments** until the new pattern feels natural.
* **Maintain a “sleep anchor”** – same bedtime and wake‑time on workdays and most days off.
* **Use brief, consistent anchor routines** to bookend the day.
* **Plan a light day‑off structure** that mirrors work‑day rhythms (see the bullet lists in the source summary).
* **Set two alarms for naps** and keep the nap environment semi‑upright to stay in the light‑sleep stage.
