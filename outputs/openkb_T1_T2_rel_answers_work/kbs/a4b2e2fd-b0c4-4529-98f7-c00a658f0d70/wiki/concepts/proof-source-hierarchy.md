# Proof Source Hierarchy

**Definition**: A prioritized list of evidence types used to confirm the official move‑in and move‑out dates when documenting personal address history.

## Why it matters
Accurately recording address timelines is essential for rental applications, background checks, and personal record‑keeping. Different documents provide varying levels of precision; a clear hierarchy helps users choose the most reliable source first and resolve conflicts efficiently.

## Hierarchy (most precise → least precise)
| Rank | Source | Best for | Typical date reflected | Notes |
|------|--------|----------|------------------------|-------|
| 1 | **Lease or rental agreement** | Both move‑in & move‑out | Official contract dates | Highest authority; use when any conflict arises |
| 2 | **Landlord or property‑manager emails/texts** | Both | Exact day of key pickup, move‑out confirmation | Often includes timestamps and explicit instructions |
| 3 | **Rent‑payment records** (bank statements, Venmo, Zelle, checks) | Both (lean toward move‑in) | First payment after move‑in, last payment before move‑out | Payments usually align closely with occupancy but may lag a few days |
| 4 | **Utility start/stop notices** | Both | Service start ≈ move‑in, service stop ≈ move‑out | Utility companies record the date service is activated or terminated |
| 5 | **USPS change‑of‑address confirmation** | Move‑in | Forward‑mail start date | Useful for sequencing; less precise for move‑out |
| 6 | **Account address updates** (bank, employer, delivery apps like Amazon) | Move‑in | Date the address was changed in the account | Provides a timestamp but not an official move‑out record |

## Conflict resolution
When two sources report different dates, the hierarchy dictates that the **lease or landlord confirmation** should be treated as the authoritative record for both move‑in and move‑out dates.
