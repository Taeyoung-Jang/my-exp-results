# Nicotine Reduction

**Definition**: A set of practical methods for decreasing dependence on nicotine, especially cigarettes, by adjusting timing, frequency, and replacing cues with brief physical or sensory breaks.

## Core Principles
1. **Identify low‑priority cigarettes** – Usually the mid‑morning or early‑afternoon smoke that feels least essential.
2. **Delay before skipping** – Start by pushing the chosen cigarette back 10‑15 minutes, using the wait time for a short reset activity.
3. **Replacement breaks** – Substitute the delay with a 1‑minute movement break, fresh‑air step‑outside, stretching, or chewing gum. These act as micro‑stimulants without nicotine.
4. **Gradual escalation** – After a few days of successfully delaying one cigarette, try skipping it entirely once or twice a week, then repeat the process with a second low‑priority cigarette.
5. **Maintain baseline energy** – Keep caffeine timing stable (see [[concepts/caffeine-timing]]) and support basics: consistent sleep, hydration, and small protein snacks to blunt cravings.

| Week | Goal | Action Steps |
|------|------|--------------|
| 1 | Baseline – keep current nicotine routine, focus on coffee adjustments. | No change to cigarettes.
| 2 | First reduction | Choose one low‑priority cigarette, delay 10‑15 min, replace with a movement break. Optional: skip it 1‑2 times this week.
| 3 | Expand reduction | Apply the same delay/skip pattern to a second low‑priority cigarette. Continue using brief physical resets for any missed cues.

## Monitoring
- **Positive signs**: mild urge that fades after a short break, stable concentration.
- **Over‑reduction signs** – Persistent irritability, strong cravings, headaches, or a noticeable drop in focus. If these appear, pause progress and hold the current level a few extra days.
