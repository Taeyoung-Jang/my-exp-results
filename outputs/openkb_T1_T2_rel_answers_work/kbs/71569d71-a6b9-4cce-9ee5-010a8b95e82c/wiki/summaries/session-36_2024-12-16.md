# Summary of Session 36 (2024‑12‑16)

**Main focus:** Practical preparation for the first day of a rehab or residential treatment stay, covering intake conversations, property handling, allowed vs. restricted personal items, communication with family, and coping tools such as journaling.

## Intake Procedure
- Initial intake involves a conversational health and safety check (medications, daily needs, goals).  
- Staff collect sharp or metal objects (wallet, keys, belt) in a sealed property bag with a barcode.  
- This is standard for most rehab programs and serves safety and loss‑prevention purposes.  
- [[concepts/intake-procedure]]

## Allowed vs. Restricted Items
- **Typically allowed:**
  - Clothing, shoes, socks, underwear, light jacket/hoodie
  - Non‑glass toiletries (toothbrush, toothpaste, deodorant, shampoo, body wash)
  - Phone charger, wired headphones, paperback books, journal, pen/pencil
  - Simple personal comfort items (small blanket, pillow, eye mask)
- **Commonly restricted/held:**
  - Anything sharp or metallic (wallet, keys, belt, lanyard, shoelace strings if policy‑specific)
  - Glass containers, aerosols, vapes, unapproved medications, alcohol‑based products
  - High‑value items (jewelry, cash) stored in property
- [[concepts/allowed-items]]

## Communication Protocols
- If the phone is held, facilities usually provide:
  - Scheduled calls from a landline or staff‑supervised phone
  - Daily/bi‑daily check‑ins for family updates
- The goal is structured contact, not total isolation.
- [[concepts/communication-protocols]]

## Packing Checklist (Most Likely Allowed)
1. **Clothing** – 5‑7 days of casual wear, pajamas, underwear, socks  
2. **Footwear** – Comfortable shoes, flip‑flops for showers  
3. **Outerwear** – Light jacket or hoodie (no metal accessories)  
4. **Toiletries** – Toothbrush, non‑alcohol toothpaste, deodorant, shampoo, body wash, hairbrush/comb  
5. **Personal comforts** – Paperback book, small journal, pen/pencil, ear‑plugs, eye mask  
6. **Electronics** – Phone charger, possibly a basic phone (subject to check), wired headphones  
7. **Miscellaneous** – Reusable plastic water bottle, zip‑top bag for laundry  

### Items Usually Stored in Property
- Wallet, keys, belts, metal lanyards, sharp tools, glass bottles, aerosols, vapes, unapproved meds, high‑value jewelry.  
- (These are considered restricted items.)

## Coping Strategies
- Keeping a journal and writing tools is generally permitted and encouraged to manage anxiety.  
- Staff are receptive to patients requesting such tools and may provide alternatives if a specific pen is disallowed.  

**Takeaway:** Knowing the typical intake flow, item policies, and communication setup reduces first‑day anxiety; a focused packing list ensures you bring only what’s needed while leaving high‑risk or valuable items at home.
