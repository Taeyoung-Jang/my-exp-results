# Communication Protocols

**Definition**: The set of structured procedures that residential treatment facilities use to manage phone and other communications between new residents and their external contacts during the initial admission period.

## Core Elements (as described in [[summaries/session-36_2024-12-16]])
1. **Property Hold on Personal Phones** – Phones are often collected on day 1 for safety. The facility retains the device in a sealed property bag until the resident is cleared to have it back.
2. **Scheduled Calls** – Residents are allowed to make outbound calls from a landline or a staff‑supervised phone at predetermined times (e.g., once per day or every other day). These calls are typically limited in duration to ensure fairness and security.
3. **Check‑in Windows** – Many programs set brief daily or bi‑daily windows where staff facilitate short check‑ins with family members. This provides reassurance without unrestricted phone use.
4. **Supervised Use** – When a resident is permitted to use their own phone, it may be monitored or logged by staff to prevent misuse of the device.
5. **Emergency Access** – Facilities usually have a protocol for urgent calls, allowing residents to request immediate contact with a designated emergency contact.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The communication setup is part of the broader **[[concepts/intake-procedure]]**, which includes health screening, property collection, and orientation.
- Allowed personal items such as a phone charger or a small notebook are covered under **[[concepts/allowed-items]]**, ensuring residents know what they can keep for communication purposes.

## Practical Implications for Residents
- **Prepare a contact list** in advance, noting the preferred times for scheduled calls.
- **Ask staff** about the exact schedule and any limits on call length to avoid surprises.
- **Bring non‑electronic alternatives** (e.g., a paper notebook) for note‑taking during check‑ins, as these are typically permitted.
- **Understand the property policy**: items like wallets, keys, and phones are stored securely and returned when the resident’s status allows.
