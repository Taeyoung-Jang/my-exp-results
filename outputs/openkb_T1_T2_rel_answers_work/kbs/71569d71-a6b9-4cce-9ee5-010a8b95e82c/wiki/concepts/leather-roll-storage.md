# Leather Roll Knife Storage

Storing knives in a leather roll is convenient for transport and protection, but the leather can trap moisture and accelerate corrosion, especially on high‑carbon blades. The following practices, distilled from [[summaries/session-02_2024-09-15]], keep knives in optimal condition.

## Key Principles
- **Dry the blade completely** before placing it in the roll. Even a thin film of water can promote rust.
- **Apply a light coat of food‑safe mineral oil** periodically (e.g., weekly or after a long hiatus) to create a barrier against oxidation. See [[concepts/high-carbon-knife-care]] for details.
- **Use a thin blade guard** or separator inside the roll. This keeps the steel from contacting the leather directly and reduces moisture retention.
- **Vent the roll** after returning home. Open it in a well‑ventilated area and allow any residual moisture to evaporate before resealing.
- **Avoid stacking wet knives** together. Separate each blade with a soft cloth or parchment if they were used in a wet environment.

## Routine Checklist
1. **Hand‑wash** the knife immediately after use, rinse quickly, and *dry with a clean towel*.
2. **Inspect** the blade for water droplets; wipe any spots you missed.
3. **Light oil coating** (optional but recommended for high‑carbon steel).
4. **Insert the blade guard**, then place the knife in the roll.
5. **Close the roll loosely**; do not compress tightly, which can keep moisture trapped.
6. **Store the roll** in a dry, temperature‑stable location, away from direct sunlight.

## Benefits
- Minimizes rust and patina formation on high‑carbon or stainless steel knives.
- Extends edge life by reducing the need for frequent heavy sharpening.
- Preserves the leather’s integrity by preventing it from becoming damp and stiff.
