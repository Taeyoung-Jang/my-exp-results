# High‑Carbon Knife Care

**Overview**

High‑carbon knives offer exceptional edge retention and sharpness, but they require more attentive maintenance than most stainless‑steel kitchen blades. Proper care prevents rust, maintains a bright edge, and extends the knife’s useful life.

---

## Immediate Post‑Use Routine

1. **Wipe dry immediately** – As soon as you finish using the knife, wipe the blade with a clean kitchen towel until it is completely bone‑dry. Moisture left on the surface accelerates oxidation.
2. **Hand‑wash gently** – If the blade is heavily soiled, wash quickly with warm water and a mild detergent, then dry thoroughly. Avoid soaking the knife.
3. **Apply a thin coat of food‑safe mineral oil** – Periodically (e.g., weekly or after several uses) rub a light layer of mineral oil onto the blade. This creates a protective barrier against humidity.

These steps are highlighted in the source [[summaries/session-02_2024-09-15]] and echo the recommendations for high‑carbon steel care.

---

## Storage Considerations

- **Leather roll** – When using a leather roll, ensure the blade is completely dry before insertion. Moisture trapped in leather can cause localized rust.
- **Blade guard** – A slim, non‑metallic guard placed between the blade and the leather adds an extra moisture barrier.
- **Air circulation** – Store the roll in a well‑ventilated area and avoid sealing it shut for long periods.

For broader storage advice, see [[concepts/leather-roll-storage]].

---

## Sharpening Routine

High‑carbon steel responds well to frequent, light touch‑ups rather than infrequent heavy grinding.

| Task | Stone Grit | Frequency |
|------|------------|-----------|
| Light touch‑up (maintain edge) | 3000‑6000 grit (fine) | After each few uses, before dullness is noticeable |
| Full sharpening (reset edge) | 1000 grit (medium) to establish geometry, then 3000‑6000 grit to polish | When edge becomes blunt or after several months of regular use |

Refer to the detailed grit guidance in [[concepts/sharpening-stone-grits]].

---

## Traits to Avoid

- **Excessive heat** – Do not place a high‑carbon knife in a dishwasher or expose it to high cooking temperatures for extended periods.
- **Prolonged moisture** – Even brief soaking can lead to pitting; always dry promptly.
- **Neglecting oil** – Skipping regular oiling speeds up rust formation, especially when stored in leather.

**Conclusion**

Consistent drying, light oiling, proper storage, and a disciplined sharpening schedule keep high‑carbon knives performing at their best, allowing home cooks to enjoy the superior sharpness and feel these blades provide.
