## Intake Procedure

The **intake procedure** is the initial, structured interaction that a new resident experiences on the first day of a rehab or residential treatment program. Its primary goals are to gather essential health and safety information, establish expectations, and secure personal property for a safe environment.

### Core Components
1. **Health & Safety Interview**
   - Staff ask about current medical conditions, medications, allergies, and any recent injuries.
   - Questions cover daily needs (e.g., sleep patterns, dietary restrictions) and safety concerns (e.g., past self‑harm, triggers).
   - Residents are also asked about their goals and what they hope to achieve during their stay.
2. **Property Collection**
   - All metal or potentially hazardous items (wallet, keys, belts, jewelry, sharp tools) are placed in a sealed *property bag* with a barcode for tracking.
   - The bag is stored securely and items are returned upon discharge or when the resident is permitted to have them again.
3. **Explanation of Rules**
   - Staff outline what personal items are allowed versus restricted, referencing the facility’s **allowed items** policy.
   - Residents receive a brief on communication expectations, such as scheduled phone check‑ins.

### Why It Matters
- **Safety:** Reduces the risk of accidental injury or contraband.
- **Accountability:** Ensures staff know each resident’s health profile for proper care.
- **Predictability:** Gives residents a clear picture of the environment, easing first‑day anxiety.
