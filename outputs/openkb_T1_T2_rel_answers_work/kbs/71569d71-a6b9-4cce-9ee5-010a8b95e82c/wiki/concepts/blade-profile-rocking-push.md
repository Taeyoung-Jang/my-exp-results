# Blade Profile for Rocking and Push Cutting

A **blade profile** that works well for mixed rocking and push‑cutting combines two key geometric features:

1. **Gentle curve toward the tip** – this curvature allows a smooth rocking motion, keeping the edge in continuous contact with the food when you pivot the knife.
2. **Relatively flat section near the heel** – a flatter heel provides the stability and direct thrust needed for efficient push cuts, giving the blade a firm feel when you drive it straight through ingredients.

This hybrid profile is especially valuable for Japanese **gyuto** knives used as a daily driver. In the source conversation, the recommended length for such a gyuto is 210 mm, which pairs naturally with this shape, offering reach without sacrificing control.

## Why the Profile Matters
- **Rocking cuts** (e.g., chopping herbs, mincing garlic) benefit from a curved tip that stays engaged with the board, reducing the need to lift the knife repeatedly.
- **Push cuts** (e.g., slicing meat or vegetables) require a straight, stable edge; a flat heel minimizes flex and delivers clean slices.
- The balance of curvature and flatness prevents the blade from feeling "chattery" or overly flexible when used for both techniques.

## Practical Guidance
- When selecting a gyuto, look for a description mentioning a *gentle curve* or *slight belly* toward the tip and a *flat or slightly sloped heel*.
- For a Western chef’s knife, the same principle applies: a modest belly with a flat heel offers versatility across tasks.
- Avoid knives with an ultra‑flat profile and a spine that is too thin, as these can flex during rocking motions and feel unstable for push cuts.
