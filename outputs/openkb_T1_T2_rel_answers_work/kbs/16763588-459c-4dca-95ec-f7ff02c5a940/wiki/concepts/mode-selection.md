# Mode Selection

**Mode Selection** is the process of deciding between walking, cycling, public transit, or rideshares for a specific trip by evaluating **distance**, **cost**, **time**, and **stress** factors.

## Core Decision Factors
1. **Distance**
   - *Walk* for very short trips where walking feels quick.
   - *Bike* for the middle‑distance zone (longer than a comfortable walk but shorter than the point where transit wait times dominate).
   - *Transit* for longer trips where the bike route is indirect or waiting time is outweighed by speed.
2. **Cost‑Time‑Stress** – see [[concepts/cost-time-stress]] for the matrix that balances monetary expense, travel time, and perceived stress.
3. **Urban Infrastructure** – the presence of protected bike lanes, safe intersections, and secure bike parking expands the viable range for cycling. Details are in [[concepts/urban-infrastructure]].

## Practical Checklist (derived from [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-15]])
- **Is the trip short enough to walk?** If yes → Walk.
- **Is the distance in the middle zone?** If yes → Prefer Bike, unless conditions (weather, night, cargo) push you toward Transit.
- **Is the trip long?** If yes → Evaluate Transit, but consider Bike if infrastructure is excellent.
- **Check conditions:** severe weather, low lighting, or heavy cargo shift the choice to Transit.

## Gear & Prep to Extend Bike Viability
Investing in a rain shell, fenders, bright lights, and panniers can move many borderline cases back into the “Bike” column, effectively widening the mode‑selection decision space.
