# One‑Handed Cooking

One‑handed cooking refers to strategies, tools, and kitchen layouts that enable a person to prepare food using a single functional hand, often because of injury or disability. The goal is to minimize reliance on wrist pressure, bracing, or the need for a second hand while maintaining safety and efficiency.

## Core Principles
- **Stabilization**: Use non‑slip surfaces (e.g., [[entities/dycem]]) and spiked cutting boards (e.g., [[entities/swedish-cutting-board]] or [[entities/sammons-preston]]) to keep bowls, plates, and food steady.
- **Wrist‑friendly design**: Choose tools that avoid downward wrist force, such as a rocker knife ([[entities/rocker-knife]]) and one‑handed can openers (e.g., [[entities/kohn-rikon]]).
- **Hands‑free mechanisms**: Install under‑cabinet jar openers like [[entities/ez-off]] and use pump‑style dispensers for soap or lotion.
- **Layout optimization**: Arrange frequently used items within easy reach and mount devices (e.g., EZ Off) near the front underside of an upper cabinet to reduce twisting.

## Typical One‑Handed Kitchen Kit
1. **Dycem non‑slip mat** – provides friction without stickiness.
2. **EZ Off under‑cabinet jar opener** – allows jar opening with a single hand.
3. **Spiked cutting board** – Swedish or Sammons Preston models hold food in place.
4. **Rocker knife** – cuts by rocking motion rather than pressing down.
5. **One‑handed can opener** – models from OXO Good Grips or [[entities/kohn-rikon]].
6. **Rubber‑base mixing bowl** – stays put on a non‑slip mat.
7. **Pump‑style soap dispenser** – enables one‑handed cleaning.
