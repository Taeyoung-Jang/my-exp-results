# Summary of session-39_2025-03-14

**Main contribution**: A concise guide to setting up a one‑handed kitchen, focusing on tools and layout that avoid putting weight on an injured wrist.

## Key concepts
- **One‑handed cooking** – stabilizing surfaces, grip aids, and wrist‑friendly tools [[concepts/one-handed-cooking]].
- **Non‑slip mat** – [[entities/dycem]] provides friction without a sticky feel, useful for bowls, cutting boards, and appliances.
- **Jar opener** – [[entities/oxo-good-grips]] rubber‑cone opener for manual use; **EZ Off** under‑cabinet opener enables completely hands‑free twisting [[entities/ez-off]].
- **Cutting board with spikes** – [[entities/swedish-cutting-board]] or [[entities/sammons-preston]] boards keep food steady via spikes or corner guards, eliminating the need for wrist pressure.
- **Rocker knife** – cuts by rocking motion, reducing downward wrist force [[entities/rocker-knife]].
- **One‑handed can opener** – models from OXO Good Grips or [[entities/kohn-rikon]] clamp and turn with a single hand.
- **Pump‑style dispenser** – allows one‑handed soap or lotion dispensing without bracing.
- **Mounting strategy** – place EZ Off near the front underside of an upper cabinet for easy reach; keep the non‑slip mat movable with the good hand; position the cutting board near the counter edge for body support. These ideas relate to [[concepts/kitchen-setup-optimization]] and [[concepts/wrist-friendly-tool-design]].

## Recommended “set‑it‑and‑forget‑it” kitchen kit (5‑7 items)
1. **Dycem non‑slip mat** – universal grip surface.
2. **EZ Off under‑cabinet jar opener** – mount under a cabinet close to the prep area.
3. **Swedish (or Sammons Preston) spiked cutting board** – holds food without wrist pressure.
4. **Rocker knife** – efficient slicing with one hand.
5. **One‑handed can opener (OXO Good Grips or Kohn‑Rikon)** – clamps and turns.
6. **Rubber‑base mixing bowl (OXO)** – stays put on the mat.
7. **Pump‑style soap dispenser** – hands‑free washing.

## Setup tips to protect the injured wrist
- Mount the EZ Off on the front underside of an upper cabinet so the V‑shaped teeth are reachable without twisting.
- Lay the Dycem mat where you can slide it with your good hand; it secures bowls, boards, and containers.
- Position the spiked cutting board near the counter edge; rest your forearm or elbow (not wrist) for balance.
- Keep the rocker knife and can opener within easy reach on the mat to avoid extra movement.

These suggestions enable most kitchen tasks—cutting, opening jars or cans, mixing, and cleaning—without needing to brace or press down with the injured left wrist.
