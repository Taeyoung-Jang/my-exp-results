## Overview
This conversation outlines a practical framework for deciding between **cycling**, **walking**, and **public transit** (with occasional rideshares) in urban daily travel. It emphasizes evaluating **cost**, **time**, and **stress** and provides a quick decision checklist plus gear recommendations to expand biking applicability. See the full source in [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-15]].

## Key Decision Factors [[concepts/mode-selection]]
- **Distance**:  
  - *Walk* for very short trips where walking feels quick.  
  - *Bike* for the middle‑distance zone—longer than a comfortable walk but shorter than the point where transit wait times dominate.  
  - *Transit* for long trips or when the bike route is indirect.  
- **Cost‑Time‑Stress Matrix** [[concepts/cost-time-stress]]:  
  - Walking costs nothing, zero traffic stress, but can be time‑inefficient over distance.  
  - Cycling is low‑cost, predictably fast, and low‑stress when infrastructure is supportive.  
  - Transit is cheapest for long distances, modest stress if service is frequent, but adds waiting time.  
  - Rideshares are high‑cost, time‑responsive, but can be stress‑heavy due to traffic.

## Urban Infrastructure Impact [[concepts/urban-infrastructure]]
- Continuous, protected bike lanes  
- Calm intersections and bike‑friendly traffic laws  
- Secure bike parking and lock‑up points  
When these elements are present, cycling often becomes the fastest door‑to‑door option.

## Contextual Adjustments
### Weather  
- Light rain: manageable with a waterproof jacket and fenders.  
- Heavy rain, snow, strong wind: switch to transit.  

### Nighttime Safety  
- Well‑lit, familiar routes → keep cycling.  
- Dark, isolated, or unpredictable streets → consider transit.  

### Cargo Load  
- Small backpack or pannier → bike.  
- Bulky or heavy groceries → transit or rideshare.

## Simple Decision Checklist
1. **Distance?**  
   - < 0.5 km → Walk  
   - 0.5–3 km → Assume Bike  
   - > 3 km → Evaluate Transit  
2. **Weather & Lighting?**  
   - Severe conditions or unsafe night → Transit  
   - Manageable conditions → Continue Bike  
3. **Load?**  
   - Light (≤ 10 kg) → Bike  
   - Heavy → Transit  
4. **Personal Comfort?**  
   - Feel safe and prepared? → Bike  
   - Not comfortable? → Transit  

## Gear & Prep to Expand Bike Viability
- Rain shell & fenders – handle wet conditions.  
- Bright front/rear lights – improve nighttime safety.  
- Panniers or basket – increase cargo capacity.  
- Reflective clothing – boost visibility.  
- Lock and secure storage – reduce theft anxiety.  

By treating cycling as the default and using the checklist above, you can quickly decide when to **bike**, **walk**, or **transit** while minimizing stress and maximizing efficiency.
