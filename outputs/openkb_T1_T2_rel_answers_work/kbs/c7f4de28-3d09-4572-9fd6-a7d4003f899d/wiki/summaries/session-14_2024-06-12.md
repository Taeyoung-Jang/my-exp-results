# Summary of session-14_2024-06-12

**Main focus**: Providing a concrete, low‑drama one‑month experiment for two working adults to rebalance household chores, plus a quick weekly check‑in script and an automatic fallback rule for unexpected work nights.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/chore_rotation]]** – Swapping tasks on specific days (Tuesday cooking/dishes, Friday cooking/dishes) while keeping the usual laundry and vacuuming responsibilities.
- **[[concepts/fairness_perception]]** – Emphasizing how chores feel rather than tallying hours; the experiment is designed to surface perceived fairness.
- **[[concepts/low_drama_negotiation]]** – Using clear, pre‑agreed rules and a simple fallback to avoid on‑the‑spot renegotiation.
- fallback rule – If the assigned cook is delayed, the other partner prepares a quick meal and the original cook takes dishes; if both are delayed, a minimal‑effort “neutral” meal is made and cleanup is split 5 minutes each.
- weekly check‑in – A five‑minute script with prompts:  
  1. *What felt easier this week?*  
  2. *Anything heavier or surprising?*  
  3. *Do we keep, tweak, or change anything for next week?*  
  4. *Anything you appreciated?*
- experiment design – Small, time‑boxed swaps (twice‑weekly) with clear “if‑then” contingencies to keep the plan lightweight.
- workload perception – The experiment targets current feelings of balance rather than historical counts.

## Concrete Plan (Option 1)
1. **Tuesday**: You cook dinner; partner does all dishes.  
2. **Friday**: Partner cooks dinner; you do all dishes.  
3. **Other days**: Maintain existing split – you handle laundry & vacuuming; partner handles cooking & dishes.

### Fallback Rules
- **Late‑work cook** → Other partner prepares a quick meal (frozen, take‑out, or ultra‑quick) and the delayed cook handles dishes.  
- **Both delayed** → Choose the simplest meal; each spends 5 minutes on cleanup; the night is recorded as “neutral” and does not affect the experiment count.

## Expected Outcome
- Short‑term insight into how each partner experiences the other’s core tasks.  
- A neutral, scripted method to adjust the division without score‑keeping or blame.  
- Data for a future, larger‑scale chore redistribution if needed.
