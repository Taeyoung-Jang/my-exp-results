# Low‑Drama Negotiation for Chore Sharing

**Definition**: A low‑drama negotiation approach provides pre‑established, simple rules and scripts that let partners adjust household chore responsibilities without on‑the‑spot debates, blame, or tedious tally‑keeping.

---

## Core Principles
1. **Pre‑Agreed Fallback Rules**
   - If the designated cook is delayed, the partner prepares a quick fallback meal and the delayed person handles dishes.
   - If both partners are delayed, a “neutral” minimal‑effort meal is made and each spends a brief, fixed time (e.g., 5 minutes) on cleanup.
   - These "if‑then" rules remove the need for real‑time renegotiation.
2. **Feel‑Based Evaluation**
   - The focus is on how tasks *feel* rather than counting hours, aligning with [[concepts/fairness_perception]].
3. **Short, Structured Check‑Ins**
   - A five‑minute weekly script with prompts such as:
     - *What felt easier this week?*
     - *Was anything heavier or surprising?*
     - *Do we keep, tweak, or change anything for next week?*
     - *Anything you appreciated?*
   - This keeps conversations future‑oriented and prevents blame. The script can be extended with a two‑week trial check‑in, e.g., “Let’s review the last two weeks – did this version feel fair? Any swaps needed?”
4. **Limited Scope Swaps**
   - Small, time‑boxed swaps (e.g., twice‑weekly cooking/dish duties) make the experiment manageable and concrete, tying into [[concepts/chore_rotation]].
5. **Point‑Person Assignment**
   - Each week partners agree on a *point person* for each core chore (cooking, cleaning, laundry, errands). The point person leads execution while the other provides support as needed. This flexible role is outlined in [[concepts/point_person]].
6. **Flexible Chore Adjustment**
   - When one partner’s schedule suddenly gets heavier, the partner with more bandwidth temporarily takes on extra tasks, with the understanding that the balance will be reevaluated at the next weekly check‑in. See [[concepts/flexible_chore_adjustment]].

---

## Why It Works
- **Clarity**: Everyone knows exactly what to do each day and what the fallback is.
- **Predictability**: Simple rotation patterns and point‑person roles reduce mental load.
- **Emotion‑Focused**: By discussing feelings rather than numbers, partners stay empathetic.
- **Low Overhead**: The brief weekly (or bi‑weekly) check‑in replaces endless ad‑hoc negotiations.
- **Adaptability**: Flexible adjustment and point‑person shifts let the system evolve with changing schedules.

## Example from Source Documents
- **Session‑14 (2024‑06‑12)** – One‑month experiment using the principles:
  - Tuesday: Partner cooks, you do dishes.
  - Friday: You cook, partner does dishes.
  - Fallback: If the cook is late, the other makes a quick meal and the original cook does dishes.
  - Weekly 5‑minute check‑in script to gauge perceived fairness. [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-12]]

The meeting follows a three‑part structure (check‑in, look‑ahead, assign point‑person) and uses a concise checkbox list to verify completion without score‑keeping.
