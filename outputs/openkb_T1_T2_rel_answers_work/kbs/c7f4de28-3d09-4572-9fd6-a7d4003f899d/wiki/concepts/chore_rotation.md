# Chore Rotation

**Definition**: A structured, time‑based swapping of household tasks aimed at keeping the division of labor feeling fair and reducing the need for continual negotiation.

## Core Elements
- **Predictable Schedule**: Specific days are assigned for each partner to take on the other's primary task (e.g., cooking on Tuesdays, dishes on Fridays).
- **Limited Scope**: Only a few tasks are swapped at a time, keeping the system simple and low‑maintenance.
- **Fallback Rules**: Clear "if‑then" contingencies handle late‑work nights or mutual delays without renegotiation.
- **Short‑Term Experimentation**: Implemented for a set period (typically one month) to gauge how the swaps feel before deciding on longer‑term changes.

## Why It Works
- **Perceived Fairness**: By rotating chores, each partner experiences the other's workload, fostering empathy and a sense of balance [[concepts/fairness_perception]].
- **Low Drama**: Pre‑agreed schedules and fallback rules avoid on‑the‑spot arguments, supporting smoother communication [[concepts/low_drama_negotiation]].
- **Feedback Loop**: Weekly check‑ins provide brief, structured reflection, helping adjust the rotation without formal score‑keeping.

## Example from Source Document
The experiment outlined in [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-12]] proposes a **twice‑weekly swap**:
- **Tuesday**: Partner A cooks, Partner B does dishes.
- **Friday**: Partner B cooks, Partner A does dishes.
- All other chores (laundry, vacuuming) remain unchanged.

If a partner has a late work night, the other prepares a quick meal and the original cook handles the dishes—a simple "if‑then" fallback that keeps the rotation intact.
