# Point‑Person Model

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** A rotating responsibility system where one partner is the designated lead for a chore each week.

## Overview
The Point‑Person Model is a flexible framework for dividing household chores. Instead of assigning permanent ownership, partners agree on a *point person* for each task during a defined period (usually a week). The point person is responsible for planning, executing, and ensuring the task is completed, while the other partner can provide support as needed.

## Key Elements
| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Timeframe** | Typically a weekly cycle, resetting each week to avoid score‑keeping. |
| **Task Scope** | Can include cooking, kitchen cleanup, laundry, errands, or any household duty. |
| **Support Role** | The non‑point person may help (e.g., doing dishes after a late‑night meal) but is not the primary accountable party. |
| **Flexibility** | When schedules shift, the point‑person can be reassigned without guilt; the model emphasizes *flexible chore adjustment* [[concepts/flexible_chore_adjustment]]. |
| **Fairness Check** | Regular check‑ins assess whether the split feels balanced, linking to *fairness perception* [[concepts/fairness_perception]]. |

## How It Connects to Other Concepts
- **Chore Meeting Template**: The model is often introduced during a brief agenda that reviews the past week and assigns point‑persons for the upcoming week [[concepts/chore_meeting_template]].
- **Low‑Drama Negotiation**: By framing changes as temporary adjustments rather than accusations, the point‑person approach supports low‑drama conversations [[concepts/low_drama_negotiation]].
- **Chore Rotation**: Over time, partners can rotate point‑person duties across different chores to maintain equity [[concepts/chore_rotation]].

## Implementation Steps (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-11]])
1. **Check‑in** – Each partner briefly shares what felt heavy or smooth the previous week.
2. **Look Ahead** – Discuss upcoming schedule changes or energy levels.
3. **Assign Point‑Person** – Decide who will lead each core chore for the next week (cooking, cleaning, laundry, errands).
4. **Set Reflection Questions** – Ask if the split feels fair, if any hidden dislikes exist, and whether any minor tweaks are needed.
5. **Track Lightly** – Use a shared note with a weekly snapshot of point‑person assignments; reset every week to keep it low‑stakes.
6. **Review** – After two weeks, run a brief script to evaluate fairness and adjust accordingly.

## Benefits
- **Dynamic Balance**: Adapts quickly to changing work schedules or energy levels.
- **Reduced Resentment**: By making adjustments explicit and temporary, partners avoid the buildup of hidden grievances.
- **Clarity without Score‑Keeping**: Everyone knows who is leading a task, but the weekly reset prevents long‑term tallies.
- **Empowers Negotiation**: Encourages open, low‑drama dialogue about workload distribution.
