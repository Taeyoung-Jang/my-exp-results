# Chore Meeting Template

**Definition**: A lightweight, recurring agenda that guides partners through a brief conversation about household chores, ensuring fairness, flexibility, and low‑drama negotiation.

---

## Purpose
- Turns chore division into an ongoing dialogue rather than a one‑off negotiation.
- Helps both partners surface feelings of overload, resentment, or satisfaction.
- Creates a predictable rhythm for checking in, adjusting, and preventing small issues from escalating.

---

## Core Structure (as used in [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-11]])
1. **Check‑in** – Each partner shares:
   - What went smoothly.
   - What felt heavy or stressful.
   - Any tiny annoyances that need tweaking.
2. **Look Ahead** – Discuss upcoming schedule changes, events, or energy drains that could affect chore capacity.
3. **Assign Point‑Person** – For the coming week, decide who is the *point person* for each core chore (cooking, cleaning, laundry, errands). See [[concepts/point_person]] for the role definition.
4. **Reflection Questions** – Quickly answer:
   - Does the split feel fair based on current time and energy?
   - Is anyone doing a task they quietly hate and want to trade?
   - Are there any small issues to address before they grow?
   
---

## Key Features
- **Weekly cadence**: Typically every 1–2 weeks, keeping the process nimble.
- **Low‑drama negotiation**: Focuses on feelings and practical constraints rather than blame. Linked to [[concepts/low_drama_negotiation]].
- **Flexibility**: If one partner’s schedule spikes, the other can temporarily take on extra tasks, following the principle in [[concepts/flexible_chore_adjustment]].
- **No score‑keeping**: Uses a shared note with a simple weekly snapshot rather than a tally system.

---

## Example Agenda (text you can paste into a shared note)
```
## Weekly Chore Check‑In
1. How did last week feel? (smooth / heavy / tense)
2. Any schedule changes next week?
3. Point‑person assignments:
   - Cooking: __________
   - Kitchen reset: __________
   - Laundry: __________
   - Errands: __________
4. Quick fairness check:
   - Does the split feel fair?
   - Any hidden dislikes?
   - Any tiny tweaks needed?
```

## When to Use
- When couples notice growing resentment around chores.
- When one partner’s work schedule changes unpredictably.
- As a preventive routine for any shared household.
