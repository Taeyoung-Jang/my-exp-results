# Flexible Chore Adjustment

**Definition**: A set of practices that let partners temporarily shift chore responsibilities to accommodate sudden changes in work or personal schedules, while preserving a sense of fairness and preventing resentment.

## Core Principles (derived from [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-11]])
- **Pre‑agree on a fallback rule**: The partner with more available time takes on extra tasks without expecting “pay‑back” later. This creates a safety net and avoids guilt.
- **Use the point‑person model**: Identify a *point person* for each chore each week. When a schedule spike occurs, the point‑person role can be reassigned quickly. See [[concepts/point_person]].
- **Re‑evaluate at the next check‑in**: During the regular [[concepts/chore_meeting_template]] (the weekly chore meeting), ask a simple “Is this still temporary?” question. If the answer is “no,” adjust the baseline distribution.
- **Communicate offsets**: Pair the increased load with offset tasks that don’t depend on the partner’s presence (e.g., grocery ordering, weekend deep‑cleaning). This keeps the overall workload balanced.

## Practical Steps
1. **Establish the flexible clause** during the first chore meeting:
   - *“If either of us has a week with unexpectedly high work hours, the other will temporarily cover extra chores, and we’ll revisit the split next week.”*
2. **Identify which chores are schedule‑dependent** (cooking, kitchen clean‑up) versus *behind‑the‑scenes* tasks (groceries, errands) that can absorb the extra load.
3. **Update the point‑person list** for the affected week, noting the temporary shift.
4. **Document the change** in a shared note (no score‑keeping, just a weekly snapshot).
5. **Check‑in after two weeks** using a brief script (see [[summaries/session-37_2025-02-11]] for wording) and decide whether the shift becomes the new baseline or reverts.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/point_person]] to designate who leads each chore for a given period.
- Supports [[concepts/fairness_perception]] by making temporary imbalances explicit and time‑boxed.
- Can be integrated into a broader [[concepts/chore_rotation]] schedule for long‑term equity.
- Aligns with a [[concepts/low_drama_negotiation]] style: focus on needs, not blame.

## Example Scenario
*Partner A begins working late nights. Partner B takes on weeknight dinners and kitchen reset. Partner A temporarily assumes weekend errands and grocery stock‑up. After two weeks, they review: if late nights persist, the new baseline shifts to give Partner A additional weekend responsibilities, keeping the overall load fair.*
