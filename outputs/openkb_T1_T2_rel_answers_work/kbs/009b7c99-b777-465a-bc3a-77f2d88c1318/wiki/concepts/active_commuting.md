## Active Commuting

Active commuting refers to using physically exerting modes—primarily biking, but also walking or a combination of bike‑to‑transit—to incorporate exercise into the daily travel routine.

### Why Consider Active Commuting?
- **Health benefits**: Built‑in aerobic activity reduces sedentary time.
- **Cost savings**: Once a bike is owned, daily expenses are minimal compared with fuel, parking, or transit fares.
- **Environmental impact**: Lower emissions and reduced traffic congestion.

### Key Evaluation Dimensions (from [[summaries/session-15_2025-06-15]])
1. **Safety** – Availability of protected bike lanes, lighting at stops, and personal comfort on the route.
2. **Cost** – Purchase, maintenance, and storage of a bike versus fuel, parking, and fares.
3. **Practicality** – Door‑to‑door time, weather resilience, and the need for bike storage at origins/destinations.
4. **Reliability** – Consistency of travel time compared with public transit schedules.

### Measuring Active Commuting
The session proposes a systematic [[concepts/commute_tracking]] template that logs:
- Baseline bike travel time and cost
- Time and cost deltas when testing alternative modes
- Separate AM/PM observations
- Safety/comfort notes

By collecting data over a two‑week period, commuters can compare the **active** option against [[concepts/mode_comparison]] alternatives (public transit, driving) and decide which mode best balances health, expense, and predictability.

### When Active Commuting Is Most Advantageous
- Safe, well‑maintained bike infrastructure exists.
- Weather conditions are moderate or storage facilities are available.
- The bike baseline travel time is competitive with other modes.
