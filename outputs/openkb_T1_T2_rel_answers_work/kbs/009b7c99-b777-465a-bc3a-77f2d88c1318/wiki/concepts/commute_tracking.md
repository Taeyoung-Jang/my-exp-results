# Commute Tracking

## Definition
Commute Tracking is the practice of collecting structured, day‑to‑day data about one’s travel between home and work (or other regular destinations). It focuses on quantifying **time**, **cost**, **reliability**, and **safety/comfort** in order to make evidence‑based decisions about transportation mode choices.

## Core Elements (as outlined in [[summaries/session-15_2025-06-15]])
- **Baseline bike metrics** – the established door‑to‑door time and cost for a pure bike commute.
- **Mode‑specific entries** – separate records for each test mode (public transit, bike‑to‑transit, driving, etc.).
- **AM/PM breakdown** – capturing differences in reliability and conditions between morning and evening trips.
- **Delta calculations** – time and cost differences compared to the bike baseline, highlighting the impact of each alternative.
- **Safety/comfort notes** – qualitative observations about lighting, crowding, bike storage, and perceived risk at stops or on routes.

Duplicate this block for each day of a two‑week trial to gather sufficient data for analysis.

## How It Supports [[concepts/active_commuting]]
By providing concrete metrics, Commute Tracking lets users evaluate whether a more active option (e.g., biking or bike‑to‑transit) truly saves time, reduces cost, and maintains acceptable safety compared to less active alternatives.

## Relationship to [[concepts/mode_comparison]]
The collected data feed directly into a systematic [[concepts/mode_comparison]]: after the tracking period, users can compute averages, variance, and qualitative scores for each mode, enabling a clear side‑by‑side assessment.

## Practical Benefits
- **Objective decision‑making** – moves beyond intuition to data‑driven choices.
- **Identifies hidden costs** – such as parking fees, bike‑storage charges, or occasional rideshare expenses.
- **Highlights reliability gaps** – by tracking missed connections and wait times.
- **Improves safety awareness** – through consistent notes on lighting and crowding.
