# Mode Comparison

**Definition**: A systematic approach to evaluate different commuting modes—biking, public transit (including bike‑to‑transit), and driving—by assessing safety, cost, reliability, and day‑to‑day practicality.

## Core Dimensions
1. **Safety**
   - Physical safety on routes (bike lanes, traffic conditions).
   - Security at transit stops (lighting, visibility, crowding).
   - Weather exposure considerations.
2. **Cost**
   - Direct expenses: fares, fuel, parking, bike maintenance, storage fees.
   - Indirect expenses: wear‑and‑tear, insurance, opportunity cost of time.
3. **Reliability & Practicality**
   - Door‑to‑door travel time, including wait times and transfers.
   - Consistency of travel times (variance across days).
   - Availability of secure bike storage at origins/destinations.
   - Flexibility for AM vs. PM trips.

## Application in Session 15
The conversation in [[summaries/session-15_2025-06-15]] uses a **baseline bike commute** to benchmark other modes. It proposes a two‑week tracking exercise that captures:
- Door‑to‑door time and cost for each mode.
- Time and cost deltas versus the bike baseline.
- Safety/comfort notes per trip segment.
- Separate AM and PM entries to account for directional reliability differences.

These data points feed directly into a **Mode Comparison** analysis, allowing a clear side‑by‑side view of each option’s trade‑offs.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- [[concepts/active_commuting]]: Mode Comparison often highlights biking (or bike‑to‑transit) as the most active option, weighing health benefits against safety and cost.
- [[concepts/commute_tracking]]: Effective Mode Comparison relies on systematic data collection; the tracking template outlined in the source document is an example of such a tool.

## Practical Steps
1. **Establish Baseline** – Record your regular bike commute’s time and cost.
2. **Collect Data** – Use a [[concepts/commute_tracking]] template for each test day, noting AM/PM trips.
3. **Calculate Deltas** – Compute time and cost differences relative to the baseline.
4. **Assess Safety** – Add qualitative notes on route conditions and stop environments.
5. **Synthesize Findings** – Summarize average deltas, variance, and safety scores to decide which mode best aligns with your goals.
