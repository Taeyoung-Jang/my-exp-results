# Mobility Aid Optimization

**Definition:** Optimizing the selection, placement, and use of mobility aids to reduce energy expenditure, improve safety, and streamline daily routines.

## Overview
Mobility‑aid optimization focuses on arranging supports—such as a walking boot, handrail, reacher, or travel mug—in a way that minimizes unnecessary movements, especially during morning routines. By planning the environment and tools ahead of time, users can conserve energy, avoid pain, and maintain stability.

## Key Strategies from [[summaries/session-35_2025-09-15]]
- **Night‑before stationing** – place the boot, water bottle, phone, and first‑layer clothing by the bed; keep bathroom essentials together; create a kitchen “launch pad” with a travel mug and pre‑portioned food.
- **Single‑chair placement** – a stable, armless chair in the bedroom enables seated dressing, the biggest time sink when wearing a walking boot.
- **Tool use** – a long‑handled shoehorn or [[entities/reacher]] helps pull pant legs over the boot without bending; a [[entities/travel_mug]] with a leak‑proof lid allows hands‑free carrying of drinks.
- **Safety aids** – a sturdy [[entities/handrail]] on stairs and in the bathroom provides reliable support while transitioning from sitting to standing.

## Practical Set‑ups
| Location | What to Keep Within Reach |
|----------|--------------------------|
| **Bed** | [[entities/walking_boot]] (or removable insert), water bottle, phone, next‑day outfit (pants, socks, underwear). |
| **Bathroom** | Toothbrush, face wash, deodorant, any grooming items placed together for seated use. |
| **Kitchen** | [[entities/travel_mug]] near coffee maker, pre‑portioned breakfast, bag/keys by the exit. |

## Energy‑Saving Tips
- Break tasks into short, seated steps with brief rests.
- Prioritize “good enough” over perfection; skip non‑essential steps on higher‑soreness days.
- Use the handrail on stairs, moving one step at a time, and avoid carrying items until fully stable.
