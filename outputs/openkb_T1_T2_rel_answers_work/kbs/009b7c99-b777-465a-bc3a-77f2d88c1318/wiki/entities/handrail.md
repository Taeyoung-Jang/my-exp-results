# Handrail

A handrail is a sturdy, fixed railing installed alongside stairs, ramps, or other vertical pathways to provide balance support and safety. In the context of the **walking boot** routine discussed in [[summaries/session-35_2025-09-15]], the handrail plays a critical role in early‑morning stair navigation when the user is still groggy and the boot limits stability.

## Key Facts from Session 35
- **Stair Safety**: The handrail should be used on every ascent and descent, with the stronger, non‑boot side leading on the way up and the booted side leading on the way down.
- **One‑Step-at-a-Time**: Coupling handrail use with a slow, deliberate step pattern reduces the risk of falls.
- **No Carrying**: The guidance advises avoiding carrying items (phone, coffee, etc.) while using the handrail on the first stair climb.
- **Integration with Routine**: The handrail is part of the broader [[concepts/mobility_aid_optimization]] strategy, ensuring the boot user can safely transition from the bedroom to the bathroom and kitchen.

## Practical Tips
1. **Grip the rail firmly** with the hand opposite the boot side when climbing.
2. **Place the handrail within comfortable reach** from the bedroom chair where the user starts dressing.
3. **Keep the area around the rail clear** of clutter to allow smooth movement.
4. **Combine handrail use with a seated pause** after reaching the top to re‑stabilize before proceeding.
