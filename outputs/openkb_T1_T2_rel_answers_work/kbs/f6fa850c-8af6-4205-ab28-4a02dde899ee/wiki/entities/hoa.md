# Homeowner Association (HOA)

**Type:** organization

## Overview
A Homeowner Association (HOA) is a governing body that sets and enforces rules, collects fees, and manages common areas for a residential community. HOAs often maintain shared amenities, dictate architectural standards, and handle community-wide concerns such as landscaping, security, and external noise.

## Mention in [[summaries/session-03_2022-05-15]]
In the checklist template from the session‑03 discussion, the **FOLLOW‑UP QUESTIONS FOR AGENT/SELLER** section specifically advises prospective buyers to ask about "HOA rules or fees." This highlights the HOA’s relevance when evaluating both the neighborhood and the long‑term cost and maintenance implications of a property.

## Relationship to key concepts
- The HOA is one of the factors examined in the **[[concepts/home-selection-checklist]]**, where understanding community regulations can affect the overall suitability of a home.
- When assessing a neighborhood, the presence and policies of an HOA influence the **[[concepts/neighborhood-assessment]]** criteria such as maintenance standards and future development plans.
- HOA fees and restrictions may weigh into the **[[concepts/weighted-scoring-method]]**, especially for buyers prioritizing stability and predictable expenses.

## Practical considerations for buyers
- **Fees:** Review the amount, payment schedule, and what the fees cover (e.g., landscaping, security, insurance).
- **Rules:** Obtain a copy of the governing documents (CC&Rs, bylaws) to ensure they align with your lifestyle and any future plans (e.g., home extensions, rentals).
- **Enforcement:** Understand how the HOA enforces rules and resolves disputes, as this can impact property value and resident satisfaction.
