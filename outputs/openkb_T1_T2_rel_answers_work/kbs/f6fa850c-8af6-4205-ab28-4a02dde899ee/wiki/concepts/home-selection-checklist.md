# Home Selection Checklist

**Definition** – A systematic, printable framework that guides prospective buyers through the evaluation of both a neighborhood and an individual home, helping them capture sensory impressions, identify dealbreakers, ask targeted questions, and apply a weighted scoring method to compare options.

---

## Overview
The checklist originates from the conversation in [[summaries/session-03_2022-05-15]], where a user sought a practical tool for home‑search decisions. It combines qualitative prompts with a simple quantitative scoring system, emphasizing long‑term stability while remaining lightweight enough for on‑the‑go use.

---

## Core Components

### 1. Neighborhood Assessment
- Walk‑feel and safety after dark
- Ambient noise levels and sources
- Smells from traffic, restaurants, or waste
- Walkability to transit, groceries, parks, cafés
- Personal bodily response while strolling
- Potential resale value over the next decade

This section aligns with the broader [[concepts/neighborhood-assessment]] concept.

### 2. Home‑Tour Assessment
- First‑five‑second impression upon entry
- Direction and quality of natural light throughout the day
- Noise transmission from outside, neighbors, and interior
- Airflow when windows are opened
- Odors in corners, closets, bathrooms, plumbing areas
- Practicality of storage and layout for daily life
- Parking or bike‑storage access and daily route
- Long‑term maintenance considerations
- Flexibility for future work, family, or hobby changes

### 3. Dealbreakers (Red Flags)
- Safety concerns, hidden moisture or mold
- Persistent loud noises, temperature irregularities
- Signs of poor maintenance or evasive sellers

### 4. Follow‑Up Questions for Agent/Seller
- Repair history and age of major systems
- Average utility costs and upcoming construction
- Neighborhood development plans and HOA rules (see [[entities/hoa]])
- Reasons previous occupants moved out

---

## Weighted Scoring Method
The checklist incorporates a simple scoring matrix based on the [[concepts/weighted-scoring-method]]:
1. **Select five priority qualities** (e.g., noise control, safety, maintenance burden, commute, natural light).
2. **Assign importance** to each (1 = low, 2 = medium, 3 = high).
3. **Rate the property** for each quality on a 1‑5 scale during the visit.
4. **Multiply importance × rating** and sum the results for a total score.

A default weight set for stability‑focused buyers is provided:
- Noise control = 3
- Neighborhood safety/feel = 3
- Long‑term maintenance burden = 3
- Commute & daily convenience = 2
- Natural light & layout = 2

---

## Usage Tips
- Print the checklist on a single sheet for easy reference while walking.
- Use the sensory prompts to capture immediate, gut‑level reactions.
- Record red‑flag items separately to avoid overlooking critical issues.
- After each visit, complete the scoring table to generate a comparable numeric value across multiple properties.
