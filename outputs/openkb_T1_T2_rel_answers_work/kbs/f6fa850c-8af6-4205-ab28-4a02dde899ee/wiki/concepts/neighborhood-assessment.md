# Neighborhood Assessment

**Definition** – A systematic set of sensory and practical prompts used to gauge how well a neighborhood matches a home‑buyer’s lifestyle, safety expectations, and long‑term investment goals.

## Core Components (from [[summaries/session-03_2022-05-15]])
1. **Atmosphere on a slow walk** – Observe the street’s vibe, visual appeal, and how welcoming it feels.
2. **Safety after dark** – Assess perceived security and lighting at night.
3. **Ambient sounds** – Note background noise levels at ground level and from a distance (traffic, pedestrians, nearby venues).
4. **Smell profile** – Detect odors from traffic, restaurants, waste collection, or green spaces.
5. **Walkability to essentials** – Test the ease of reaching transit stops, grocery stores, parks, cafés, and other daily‑use destinations.
6. **Physical response** – Pay attention to how your body feels while moving through the area (stress, comfort, fatigue).
7. **Resale & value trajectory** – Consider the neighborhood’s potential to hold or increase value over the next decade.

## How It Fits in Home‑Selection
Neighborhood assessment is one half of the broader [[concepts/home-selection-checklist]]. Together with a structured home‑tour assessment, it provides a holistic picture before scoring properties.

## Integration with Scoring
The prompts feed directly into the [[concepts/weighted-scoring-method]]: after gathering observations, assign importance (1‑3) to each factor (e.g., safety, noise, walkability) and rate the specific neighborhood (1‑5). Multiply and sum to produce a comparative score.

## Practical Use
- Print the checklist on a single page for on‑site use.
- Record brief notes or tick boxes next to each prompt.
- Highlight any **red flags** (e.g., unsafe lighting, persistent foul odors) as deal‑breakers.
- Use the collected data to inform follow‑up questions for agents or sellers.
