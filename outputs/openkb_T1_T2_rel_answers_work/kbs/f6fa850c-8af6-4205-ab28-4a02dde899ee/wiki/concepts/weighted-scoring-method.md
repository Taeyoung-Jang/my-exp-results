# Weighted Scoring Method

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A systematic way to evaluate and compare housing options by assigning importance weights to selected criteria, rating each property on those criteria, and calculating a total score.

---

## Overview
The weighted scoring method originates from the checklist created in [[summaries/session-03_2022-05-15]]. It helps buyers translate qualitative impressions (e.g., light quality, noise level) into a quantitative comparison that reflects personal priorities.

## Steps
1. **Select five primary criteria** that matter most for the purchase. Typical examples from the source document include:
   - Noise control
   - Neighborhood safety and feel
   - Long‑term maintenance burden
   - Commute and daily convenience
   - Natural light and layout
2. **Assign an importance weight** to each criterion on a scale of 1 to 3 (1 = low importance, 3 = high importance). The document suggests a default stability‑focused set where noise, safety, and maintenance each receive a weight of 3.
3. **Rate each property** on the same criteria using a 1 to 5 scale (1 = poor, 5 = excellent) during the neighborhood walk‑through and home tour.
4. **Calculate the weighted score** for each criterion:
   
   `Weighted score = importance × rating`
5. **Sum the weighted scores** across all five criteria to obtain a total score for the property. The highest total indicates the best overall fit based on the user's weighted priorities.

## Why It Works
- **Personalization:** Users explicitly encode what matters to them, making the comparison tailored rather than generic.
- **Clarity:** Numeric results simplify decision‑making, especially when visiting multiple homes.
- **Flexibility:** Weights can be adjusted as priorities shift (e.g., from short‑term comfort to long‑term stability).

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The method is a core component of the broader [[concepts/home-selection-checklist]] used for systematic home hunting.
- It operationalizes the qualitative prompts from [[concepts/neighborhood-assessment]] and the home‑tour questions, turning observations into comparable data points.

| Criterion                      | Importance (1‑3) | Rating (1‑5) | Weighted Score |
|--------------------------------|------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Noise control                  | 3                | 4            | 12             |
| Neighborhood safety & feel    | 3                | 5            | 15             |
| Long‑term maintenance burden  | 3                | 3            | 9              |
| Commute & daily convenience    | 2                | 4            | 8              |
| Natural light & layout         | 2                | 5            | 10             |
| **Total Score**                |                  |              | **54**         |

In this example the property scores 54 out of a possible 75, indicating a strong fit for a buyer prioritizing stability.
