# Summary of session-03_2022-05-15

**Main contribution** – a structured, printable checklist for assessing potential homes and neighborhoods, complete with prompts, red‑flag indicators, follow‑up questions, and a weighted scoring system that emphasizes long‑term stability.

## Core ideas
- **Neighborhood assessment** – sensory and experiential prompts (walk feel, safety after dark, ambient noise, smells, walkability to transit/amenities, bodily response, resale potential). See [[concepts/neighborhood-assessment]].
- **Home‑tour assessment** – first‑impression cues, natural‑light direction, noise transmission, airflow, odors, storage practicality, parking/bike access, maintenance burden, adaptability for future work/family changes.
- **Dealbreakers (red flags)** – safety concerns, moisture/mold, chronic noise, temperature swings, poor upkeep, evasive sellers.
- **Follow‑up questions for agents/sellers** – repair history, system ages, utility costs, upcoming construction, HOA rules or fees (see [[entities/hoa]]), prior occupant motives, long‑term neighborhood development plans.
- **Weighted scoring framework** – user selects five priority qualities, assigns importance (1‑3), rates each property (1‑5), multiplies and sums for a total score. A default weight set for stability‑oriented buyers is provided (noise = 3, safety = 3, maintenance = 3, commute = 2, light/layout = 2). See [[concepts/weighted-scoring-method]].

## Usage
The template is designed to be printed on a single page, allowing prospective buyers to walk through neighborhoods and homes, note observations, flag issues, ask targeted questions, and quantitatively compare options without excessive paperwork.
