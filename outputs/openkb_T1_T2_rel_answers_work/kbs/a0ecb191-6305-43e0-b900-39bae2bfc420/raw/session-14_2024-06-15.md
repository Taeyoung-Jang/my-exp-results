**Assistant:** You can usually make a big difference with a mix of small gear tweaks and routine shifts. For noise, a good pair of over‑ear noise‑canceling headphones is often the simplest fix. Even mid‑range models block out most household noise, and using them with a little background sound—like soft ambient noise—can make calls feel a lot cleaner. If you use an external mic, try switching it to a cardioid or “voice‑only” mode if it has one, which naturally cuts down on sound coming from behind or around you.

On the routine side, blocking your day into quiet and noisy periods helps a lot. Many people do their calls or deep‑focus work early, before things get hectic, then keep lighter tasks for louder times. If your environment has predictable noisy windows, scheduling apps or calendar alerts can help you organize around them without thinking too hard about it.

You can also train people around you with subtle cues. Something as simple as the same “on a call” light or headphones on your head can signal that you’re in work mode, which reduces random interruptions over time.

**User:** Sure—my current setup is pretty basic: I’m using a laptop on a desk in a shared space and I usually just use the built-in mic and speakers (sometimes cheap earbuds). Calls are the hardest part because the background noise gets picked up and I end up getting distracted too.

If I can only buy one thing first, would you prioritize noise-canceling headphones or a dedicated mic? And are there specific settings in Zoom/Teams I should change to cut down background noise?

**Assistant:** If you can only upgrade one thing, noise‑canceling headphones are usually the best first purchase. They help you in two ways at once: they block out noise so you can stay focused, and the built‑in mics on most decent ANC headphones do a better job at ignoring background sounds than a laptop mic. A dedicated external mic is great, but it won’t solve the problem of you hearing the chaos around you.

For Zoom, turn on the “High” or “Auto” background noise suppression setting. In Teams, make sure noise suppression is set to “High.” Both apps also have an option to automatically adjust mic sensitivity—turn that on so sudden loud sounds don’t get picked up as strongly.

If you want to push it a bit further, using “push to talk” on calls can help during really noisy moments, but headphones plus those noise‑suppression settings usually make a noticeable difference right away.

**User:** Can you recommend a few specific noise-canceling headphones that have good mic quality for calls (ideally not super expensive), and tell me what I should look for in the specs? Also, if I’m stuck using my laptop mic sometimes, are there any quick positioning tricks or cheap add-ons that help it pick up less background noise?

**Assistant:** If you want solid noise canceling and reliable mic quality without spending top‑tier money, a few models tend to hit the sweet spot. The Sony WH‑CH720N and the Anker Soundcore Space Q45 both give you noticeably better isolation than budget earbuds and have mics that are clear enough for everyday calls. They’re usually much cheaper than the flagship Sony or Bose models but still feel like a big upgrade from built‑in laptop audio. If you’re okay spending a bit more, the Sony WH‑XM4 often goes on sale and still has excellent ANC and call quality.

When you look at specs, the most useful clues are terms like “hybrid ANC,” multiple mic arrays for voice pickup, and Bluetooth multipoint so you can switch between your laptop and phone easily. You don’t need to obsess over frequency ranges; what matters more is reviewers saying the ANC handles voices and household noise well.

If you’re stuck with the laptop mic, a couple of small tweaks help. Position yourself so the noise source is behind the laptop instead of behind you, because laptop mics pick up what’s in front of them most strongly. Raising the laptop a bit—like on a stand or even a stack of books—gets the mic closer to your mouth and farther from desk noise. And if you want a super‑cheap helper, a simple foam pop filter meant for small mics can sometimes be cut or placed in front of the laptop’s mic area to soften harsh sounds and reduce the sharpness of background noise. None of these tricks are magic, but together they make the built‑in mic noticeably less chaotic.

**User:** Between the WH‑CH720N and the Soundcore Q45, which one tends to do better specifically with cancelling voices/TV noise in the background and keeping my voice clear on calls?

Also, can you give me the exact steps/menus for the Zoom and Teams noise suppression settings (desktop apps), and any other “must change” audio toggles you’d recommend?

**Assistant:** Between the two, the Soundcore Q45 usually does a better job at canceling voices and TV noise. Its ANC is stronger overall, especially with mid‑range sounds like people talking. The Sony WH‑CH720N has decent cancellation, but the Q45 tends to feel quieter in a shared space. For call clarity, they’re fairly close, but the Sonys sometimes keep your voice a little more natural. If your priority is blocking out other people, the Q45 is the safer pick.

For Zoom on desktop, open Settings, go to Audio, and look for the Noise Suppression section. Set it to High if your room is lively. While you’re in the Audio menu, make sure “Automatically adjust microphone volume” is on, and if you see an option called “Suppress background noise for calls only,” keep it enabled.

For Microsoft Teams on desktop, click your profile picture, open Settings, go to Devices, then look for Noise Suppression. Switch it to High. If you see an option labeled “High fidelity music mode,” keep that off because it reduces background filtering. You can also quickly adjust noise suppression right before a meeting by clicking the device settings panel on the join screen and choosing High there as well.

If you tend to get echo or feedback, using the headphone output instead of speakers and muting your mic when you’re not talking will keep things sounding cleaner without much effort.
