# Sleep Hygiene

**Definition**: Sleep hygiene encompasses habits and environmental controls that support the quality, continuity, and timing of sleep.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Consistent Sleep Schedule**  
   - Go to bed and wake up at the same times each day, even on weekends, to reinforce the body's circadian rhythm.
2. **Optimized Bedroom Environment**  
   - Keep the room cool, dark, and quiet. Use curtains, rugs, or wall hangings to dampen echoes and external sounds.
3. **Controlled Light Exposure**  
   - Limit bright screens before bedtime; expose yourself to natural light during the day to support melatonin regulation.
4. **Pre‑Sleep Routine**  
   - Engage in relaxing activities (e.g., reading, gentle stretching) and avoid stimulating tasks or intense conversations close to bedtime.
5. **Mindful Substance Use**  
   - Restrict caffeine, nicotine, and alcohol in the hours leading up to sleep, as they can fragment sleep architecture.
6. **Physical Activity**  
   - Regular exercise improves sleep depth, but aim to finish vigorous workouts at least a few hours before bedtime.
7. **Screen‑Free Bed**  
   - Reserve the bed for sleep (and intimacy) only, avoiding work, gaming, or scrolling to strengthen the mental association between the bed and rest.

- **Background Sound Masking**: Introducing a steady, low‑volume sound (fan, brown or pink noise) creates an acoustic anchor that prevents the abrupt silence of early mornings from jolting the sleeper awake. This aligns with the principle of a *controlled bedroom environment* and a *consistent auditory backdrop*.
- **Volume & Placement**: Positioning the sound source across the room and pointing it toward a wall ensures the noise is diffuse and unobtrusive, supporting a calm atmosphere without disturbing a noise‑sensitive partner.
- **Gradual Fade‑In**: Using a timed fade‑in at night eases the transition from wakefulness to sleep, reducing the likelihood of sudden awakenings caused by a stark quiet.
- **Daytime Acclimation**: Embracing daytime birdsong as a natural cue helps the brain habituate to the new soundscape, making nighttime quiet feel less jarring.

## Practical Tips for Implementation

1. **Select a Soft Noise**: Brown or pink noise is generally less intrusive than white noise. Set the volume so that you can still hear gentle morning birdsong.
2. **Strategic Placement**: Place a small fan or speaker on a nightstand or shelf a few feet from the bed, angled away from you and your partner.
3. **Use a Timer**: Program the device to start at a low level and optionally fade out after several hours, ensuring it doesn’t run all night.
4. **Combine with Other Hygiene Practices**: Pair the sound strategy with a consistent bedtime, dim lighting, and a screen‑free routine for maximal benefit.
