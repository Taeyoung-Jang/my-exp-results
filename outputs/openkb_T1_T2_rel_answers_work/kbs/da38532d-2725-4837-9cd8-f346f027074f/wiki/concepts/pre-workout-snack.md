# Pre‑Workout Snack Fundamentals

**Scope**: This concept outlines the principles of selecting and timing pre‑workout nutrition for early‑morning exercise, with a focus on light, quickly digestible options that avoid gastrointestinal discomfort and energy crashes.

## Core Principles

1. **Timing**: Eat 5–15 minutes before the workout to allow minimal digestion while still providing a quick energy source. For ultra‑early sessions (e.g., swimming within 20‑30 minutes of waking), the window can be as short as a few sips or bites.
2. **Portion Size**: Keep the snack small enough to prevent heaviness. Typical portions include:
   - Half a banana
   - A thin slice of toast
   - 2–3 crackers
   - A sip of fruit juice
   - A glucose chew or small sports gel
3. **Macronutrient Focus**: Prioritize simple carbohydrates for rapid glucose availability. Protein and fat are generally avoided in the immediate pre‑session snack because they slow gastric emptying.
4. **Digestibility**: Choose foods with low fiber and low water content to minimize stomach upset, especially important for activities like swimming where a heavy stomach can feel uncomfortable.
5. **Hydration**: Pair the snack with a few sips of water to aid digestion and prevent dehydration during the workout.

## Relation to Early‑Morning Workouts

- The snack strategy aligns with the broader [[concepts/early-morning-workout-routine]] by supporting energy levels without compromising sleep quality or causing a mid‑morning crash.
- When combined with the caffeine guidelines from [[concepts/caffeine-timing]], a modest snack can smooth the transition from sleep to activity, ensuring both mental alertness and physical stamina.

## Practical Examples (from [[summaries/session-34_2025-10-15]])

- **Half a banana**: Provides ~15 g of carbs, easy on the stomach.
- **Small piece of toast**: Offers quick carbs and a familiar texture.
- **A couple of crackers**: Light, low‑fiber carbohydrate source.
- **Sip of juice**: Quick glucose boost with minimal volume.
- **Glucose chew**: Concentrated carbs for those who prefer not to eat solid food.

## Benefits

- **Energy boost**: Supplies immediate glucose to muscles and brain.
- **Reduced fatigue**: Prevents the low‑energy feeling that can follow an empty‑stomach workout.
- **Improved performance**: Allows for slightly higher intensity or longer duration without gastrointestinal distress.

## When to Skip or Adjust

- If you feel fully awake and energetic upon waking, you may forgo a snack and rely on a small coffee (see [[concepts/caffeine-timing]]).
- For extremely short or low‑intensity sessions, a sip of water alone might suffice.
- Individuals with specific medical conditions (e.g., diabetes) should tailor carbohydrate amounts accordingly.
