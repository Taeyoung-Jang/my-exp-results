# Caffeine Timing for Early Exercise

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-34_2025-10-15]]

## Overview
[[concepts/caffeine-timing]] refers to the optimal point in the morning routine to ingest caffeine so that its stimulant effects align with an early‑morning workout, especially when the workout begins within 20–30 minutes of waking.

## Key Recommendations from Session 34 (2025‑10‑15)
- **Immediate intake after waking:** Drink a small amount of caffeine (≈40–80 mg, roughly half a normal cup of coffee) as soon as you get out of bed. This timing allows the caffeine to start acting within 10–15 minutes, which matches the short window before a pool session.
- **Split dosing:** Keep the pre‑workout dose modest to avoid jitters and a mid‑morning crash. After the workout, you can consume the remainder of your usual coffee to maintain steady energy.
- **Effect duration:** The initial boost helps with focus and alertness during the swim, while the later dose smooths out energy levels for the rest of the morning.

## Practical Tips
1. **Measure the dose:** 40 mg is about a single espresso shot; 80 mg is roughly a half‑cup of brewed coffee.
2. **Timing checklist:**
   - Wake up → hydrate → take caffeine → 10‑15 min wait → light stretch → start workout.
3. **Adjust for sensitivity:** If you’re caffeine‑sensitive, start at the lower end (≈40 mg) and assess how you feel.

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- The timing strategy supports an [[concepts/early-morning-workout-routine]] by reducing perceived fatigue and improving consistency.
- Pairing caffeine with a light [[concepts/pre-workout-snack]] (e.g., half a banana) can further stabilize blood‑sugar levels and enhance performance.

## Summary
Effective caffeine timing for early exercise involves a small, immediate dose upon waking, followed by a post‑workout supplement if needed. This approach maximizes alertness during the workout while minimizing the risk of an energy crash later in the day.
