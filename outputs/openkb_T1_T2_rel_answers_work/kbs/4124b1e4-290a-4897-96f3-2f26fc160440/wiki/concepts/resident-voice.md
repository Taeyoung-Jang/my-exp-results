# Resident Voice

**Definition**: Resident Voice is the perspective and advocacy of long‑term neighborhood inhabitants, emphasizing stability and continuity when communicating with municipal bodies such as the city council.

**Key aspects** (from [[summaries/session-03_2004-06-15]]):
- Emphasizing personal tenure (e.g., fifteen‑year residence) to underline commitment.
- Requesting that long‑term residents be heard in policy decisions.
- Balancing firmness with respect in tone.

**Related concepts**:
- [[concepts/communication-tone]] – selecting a tone that is firm yet courteous.
- [[concepts/neighborhood-change]] – context of rapid changes that motivate residents to speak up.

**Typical usage** in civic letters:
> "I’m writing as a long‑time resident who’s noticed how quickly things have been shifting... I hope my perspective is heard."

This illustrates how a resident frames their voice to influence the [[entities/city-council]] while highlighting stability.

**Implications**:
- Strengthens community representation.
- Provides a counter‑balance to transient viewpoints.
