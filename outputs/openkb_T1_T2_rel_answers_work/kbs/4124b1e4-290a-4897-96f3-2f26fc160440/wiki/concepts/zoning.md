# Zoning (Functional Cues Over Walls)

**Definition**: Zoning is the practice of creating distinct functional areas in a living space through the strategic placement of objects, lighting, and small visual cues rather than relying on physical walls or partitions.

**Key Principles**
- **Cue‑Based Boundaries**: Use items such as lamps, trays, or a folded blanket to signal a specific activity (e.g., reading, work, wind‑down). These cues act as low‑effort markers that the brain interprets as zone delimiters.
- **Clear Pathways**: Maintain at least one unobstructed walk‑through to foster a sense of order and spaciousness.
- **Anchor Integration**: Pair zoning with an anchor item (e.g., a favorite chair or sofa) that anchors the primary zone; secondary zones branch off from this focal point.
- **Micro‑Routines**: Reinforce zones by pairing them with simple daily habits, such as a morning tea at a designated table, which strengthens the mental association of the space with a purpose. See [[concepts/routine]].
- **Personal Touches**: Incorporate personal items—photos, fabrics, or artwork—to give each cue a unique identity and make the zones feel owned. Related idea: [[concepts/anchor-items]].

**Benefits**
- Enhances perceived organization in compact or flexible spaces.
- Allows easy reconfiguration as needs change.
- Supports mental mapping of activities, reducing cognitive load.
