## Summary

- **Anchor items**: Bring familiar objects (blanket, lamp, photos, fabric) into the new place to create immediate emotional anchors. [[concepts/anchor-items]]
- **Scent cues**: Use a favorite candle, soap, or room spray to trigger a sense of home.
- **Micro‑routines**: Start a simple daily habit (morning tea, music, stretching) to signal that the space is yours. [[concepts/routine]]
- **Clear surfaces**: Designate a few key surfaces (kitchen counter, bedside table) to stay tidy and intentional, which adds a feeling of stability without new purchases.
- **Personal displays**: Hang photos, art, postcards, or a fabric scrap you already own to tell your story in the room.
- **Lighting**: Position a beloved lamp where you’ll use it each evening to set a warm, consistent rhythm.
- **Zone cues over walls**: Use small objects (lamps, trays, folded blankets) to signal functional zones (reading, work, wind‑down) even when they overlap. [[concepts/zoning]]
- **Clear pathways**: Keep at least one unobstructed walk‑through to create order and make the space feel larger.
- **Visual anchor for multi‑use rooms**: Choose the piece you use most (often a sofa or favorite chair) as the room’s anchor; orient it toward a pleasant view.
- **Anchor piece placement**: Place the anchor first, then tuck the workspace slightly off‑center, using lighting or angle to differentiate.
- **Guest layer**: Treat guest accommodations as a temporary overlay that can be unfolded when needed without disrupting the primary zones.
