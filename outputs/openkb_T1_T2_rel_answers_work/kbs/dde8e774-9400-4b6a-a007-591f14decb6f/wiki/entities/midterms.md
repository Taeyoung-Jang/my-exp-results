# Midterms

Midterms are a set of significant assessments that typically take place in the middle of an academic term. They serve as a checkpoint for students and instructors to gauge learning progress before the final examinations.

## Role in the Weekly Schedule

In the context of the schedule outlined in [[summaries/session-06_2022-10-14]], midterms trigger a shift in the regular [[concepts/weekly-schedule]]:
- **Increased study blocks:** Free evenings and some workout time are reallocated to additional focused study sessions.
- **Adjusted job hours:** When possible, campus‑job hours (see [[entities/campus-job]]) are moved earlier in the week to free afternoons for intensive revision.
- **Maintaining a recovery slot:** Even during midterm weeks, one lighter evening (often Friday) is preserved to avoid burnout, supporting long‑term [[concepts/habit-adherence]].

## Time‑Blocking Strategies

Effective preparation relies on [[concepts/time-blocking]]:
- Convert previously free slots (e.g., Tuesday/Thursday evenings) into dedicated review periods.
- Shorten workouts to 20‑30 minute sessions to retain physical health while prioritising study time.
- Use the weekend’s long study block for deep work on the most demanding subjects.

## Comparison with Finals

While both are high‑stakes assessment periods, **midterms** generally involve a lighter workload than [[entities/finals]], allowing for more flexibility in the schedule. The same principles of schedule adaptation and habit adherence apply, but finals may require further contraction of optional activities.
