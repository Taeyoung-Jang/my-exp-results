# Decision Tree for Commitment Evaluation

**Definition**: A quick, three‑step decision framework that helps you decide if a new meeting or social invitation fits your current priorities, energy level, and weekly breathing room.

---

## Core Questions (as presented in [[summaries/session-38_2024-10-14]])
1. **Priority Alignment** – Does the plan directly support something you care about right now (health, relationships, a work project, or genuine rest)?
2. **Energy Check** – Can you realistically attend without feeling stretched or exhausted?
3. **Weekly Breathing Room** – Will adding this commitment make your week feel too tight, reducing the downtime you need?

If any answer is *no* or feels shaky, the recommendation is to decline or postpone the plan.

---

## How to Use It
- **Before saying yes**: Run through the three questions in order. Write a brief note (e.g., "Priority: yes, Energy: low, Breathing room: ok") to make the decision explicit.
- **When you hit your cap**: Combine the decision tree with the [[concepts/commitment-cap]] (max two weeknight commitments). Once the cap is reached, the tree will almost always return *no* for additional weeknight plans.
- **Weekly Planning**: Apply the tree during your weekly review to slot the two allowed commitments into your [[concepts/weekly-schedule]] (e.g., Tuesday and Thursday).

## Benefits
- **Speed** – Takes under a minute per invitation.
- **Clarity** – Reduces mental load by focusing on three concrete criteria.
- **Energy Management** – Protects downtime by ensuring each new commitment is truly valuable.
- **Scalability** – Works for both work meetings and social plans.
