# Time Blocking

**Time blocking** is a planning technique that divides the day into dedicated blocks, each reserved for a specific activity such as studying, work, or exercise. By treating each block as an appointment, it creates clear boundaries, reduces decision fatigue, and improves focus.

## Key Elements
- **Fixed slots**: Choose consistent start/end times (e.g., 08:00‑10:00 for study).
- **Task specificity**: Assign a concrete task or subject to each slot (e.g., Class A problem set).
- **Flexibility buffers**: Include short breaks or “free” periods to absorb spill‑over.
- **Priority alignment**: Schedule the most demanding or high‑impact work during peak productivity windows (morning for many students).

## Benefits
1. **Clarity** – Knowing exactly what to do and when eliminates ambiguity.
2. **Focus** – Limited time encourages concentrated effort.
3. **Accountability** – Blocks act like calendar appointments you’re less likely to skip.
4. **Adaptability** – Slots can be shifted or resized for exam weeks without overhauling the entire schedule.

## Tips for Effective Use
- **Start with a template** – Replicate the structure from the example schedule and tweak as needed.
- **Review weekly** – Use a short Sunday review (20–30 min) to adjust blocks for upcoming deadlines.
- **Track progress** – Check off completed blocks; if a block is missed, reschedule rather than abandon.
- **Protect downtime** – Keep at least one evening free each week to recharge.

By consistently applying time blocking, students can align their academic, work, and wellness goals within a realistic, repeatable framework.
