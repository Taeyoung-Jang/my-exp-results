## Summary

This document provides a practical **weekly planning** system aimed at preventing over‑commitment and burnout. It introduces a concise **decision tree** for evaluating new meetings or social invitations, defines a **commitment cap** of two weeknight plans, and offers a reusable weekly **template** that reserves protected downtime and outlines how to swap nights when unexpected events arise.

### Core Concepts
- **[[concepts/decision-tree-commitments]]** – three quick questions (alignment with priorities, energy check, weekly breathing room) to decide whether to say “yes.”
- **[[concepts/commitment-cap]]** – limit of two weeknight commitments, with a suggested default of two low‑effort plans per week.
- **[[concepts/weekly-schedule]]** – a default weekly layout (Mon–Sun) assigning roles:  
  - **Monday**: fully open (protected open night)  
  - **Tuesday / Thursday** (or Wed/Thu variant): commitment slots  
  - **Wednesday**: chores or light rest  
  - **Friday**: flexible evening  
  - **Saturday**: protected downtime block before noon  
  - **Sunday**: gentle reset day  
- **Swap rule** – if the protected open night (Monday) is taken, swap one commitment night back to a rest night, keeping total “energy‑spend” nights at two. *(plain‑text concept)*
- **Decline script** – a short, polite phrasing to turn down invitations once the cap is reached. *(plain‑text concept)*

### Practical Outputs
1. **Decision Tree Checklist**  
   1. Does the plan support a current priority?  
   2. Do I have the energy for it?  
   3. Will it crowd out my breathing room?  
2. **Weekly Layout Template** (fillable table) – includes slots for open night, commitment nights, chores, downtime, and reset.  
3. **Decline Script** – “Thanks for inviting me! I’m at my limit for weeknight plans right now, so I need to pass this time. Please keep me in the loop for future ones.”  
4. **Variant Layout** – alternative version with commitment nights on Tue/Thu and guidance on handling pop‑ups on the open night.

### Takeaway
By applying the decision tree, respecting the two‑commitment cap, and using the structured template with its swap rule, you can maintain a balanced week that safeguards downtime while still accommodating meaningful social and work engagements.
