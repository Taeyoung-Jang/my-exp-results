# Early Arrival Planning

**Definition**: A proactive strategy of reaching the airport well before the scheduled departure time to minimize time pressure, accommodate unexpected delays, and allow calm preparation for the flight.

## Why it matters
- **Stress reduction**: Extra minutes give you breathing room to handle check‑in, security, and any last‑minute issues without feeling rushed.
- **Buffer for disruptions**: Allows a safety margin for unforeseen problems such as long security lines, traffic, or [[concepts/border_delays]].
- **Preparation time**: Gives you a chance to settle into your seat, organize your personal item, and start the flight in a relaxed state.

## Core components
1. **Set a generous arrival window** – Aim to be at the terminal at least **90 minutes** before a domestic flight and **120 minutes** before an international one. This aligns with a [[concepts/buffer_strategy]] that adds a cushion for variability.
2. **Use mobile tools** – Store boarding passes and seat selections on your phone beforehand, reducing on‑site paperwork.
3. **Pre‑pack essentials** – Have your minimal personal‑item items (see [[concepts/minimal-personal-item-packing]]) ready to go, so you don’t scramble at the gate.
4. **Plan for transportation** – Account for possible traffic or public‑transport delays; consider earlier departure times or alternative routes.

## Practical tips from [[summaries/session-36_2024-12-18]]
- Arrive **earlier than you think you need** to avoid the rush.
- Use that extra time to **organize your comfort anchors** (hoodie, headphones, snack, etc.) before reaching the gate.
- If you have a **window seat**, early arrival lets you settle in, adjust lighting, and prepare for the view without distraction.
