# Empire Builder (work)

The **Empire Builder** is Amtrak's premier long‑distance train that runs from **[[entities/chicago]]** to the Pacific Northwest, terminating in **[[entities/seattle]]** and **[[entities/portland]]**.  It is a key component of the flight‑free itineraries discussed in [[summaries/session-11_2022-12-18]], especially the NYC → Seattle/Portland routes.

## Key facts from the session
- **Route & connections**: Travelers departing from **[[entities/new-york-city]]** take the **[[entities/lake-shore-limited]]** to Chicago, then transfer to the Empire Builder for the westward leg.  The connection is a critical point for stress and delay management.
- **Travel time**: Door‑to‑door travel from NYC to Seattle/Portland is about **48–52 hours** without a Chicago overnight buffer; adding an overnight in Chicago extends it to **72–76 hours**.
- **Stress considerations**: The Empire Builder segment is generally reliable but shares the freight‑track delay risk common to Amtrak's long‑distance services.  Using a **[[concepts/buffer_strategy]]** (overnight in Chicago) reduces stress from 4/5 to 2/5.
- **Cost**: Coach‑class tickets for the Empire Builder are part of a total trip cost ranging from **$2,000–$3,300** for a 9‑day itinerary, depending on whether a Chicago buffer night is added.
- **Seasonal impact**: While the Empire Builder itself is not a border crossing, its timing can be affected by overall network congestion, especially during peak travel seasons.
