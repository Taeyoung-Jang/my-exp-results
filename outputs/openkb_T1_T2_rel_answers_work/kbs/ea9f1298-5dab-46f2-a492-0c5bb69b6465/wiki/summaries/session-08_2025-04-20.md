## Key Advice
1. **Shape first, tempo later** – Master the chord shape and smooth movement before introducing a [[entities/metronome]]. Once the transition feels reliable, re‑introduce the click at a very slow tempo and increase it incrementally. [[concepts/shape-first-tempo-later]]
2. **Accuracy → consistency → speed** – Prioritize correct finger placement, then steady timing, and finally tempo.  
3. **Whole‑shape placement** – Aim to land the entire chord in one coordinated motion; hover lightly if needed, but avoid one‑finger‑at‑a‑time as the primary method. [[concepts/whole-shape-placement]]
4. **Benchmark for readiness** – Approximately ten clean switches in a row without tension indicates the movement is ready for metronome work.
5. **Metronome spacing** – Start with a change every two beats, then progress to every beat before adding a strumming pattern.

## Tension‑Reduction Drill ([[entities/g-major-chord]] ↔ [[entities/c-major-chord]])
- **Pivot finger** – Use the third finger on the B‑string 3rd fret as an anchor (modern G fingering) to minimise hand jump.
- **Slow back‑and‑forth** – Move between G and C slowly, keeping the hand light, avoiding neck squeeze, and pausing when shoulders or wrists tense.
- **Session length** – ~15 minutes of focused, relaxed practice; stop before tension builds. [[concepts/tension-management-guitar]]
- **Silent vs. strummed** – Begin with silent chord placement to check accuracy, then add a gentle strum once the motion feels predictable.

## Practical Implementation Steps
1. Practice the chord shapes without a [[entities/metronome]] until they feel comfortable.
2. Perform the **pivot‑finger drill** for G → C, 15 min per session.
3. Re‑introduce a [[entities/metronome]] at a slow tempo, changing chords every two clicks.
4. Gradually reduce the interval (every click) and incorporate simple strumming patterns.
5. Monitor for tension; pause, shake out the hand, and resume relaxed.
