# Tension Management in Guitar Hand Movement

**Concept:** [[concepts/tension-management-guitar]]

## Overview
Effective tension management helps guitarists move between chords smoothly, avoid hand fatigue, and maintain relaxed posture. The approach combines ergonomic positioning, targeted drills, and mindful practice routines.

## Key Techniques from Session 08 (2025‑04‑20)
1. **Pivot‑finger anchor** – Use a finger that stays on the same string and fret across two chords (e.g., the third finger on the B‑string 3rd fret for G ↔ C). This reduces the distance the hand must travel and eliminates a large “jump.”
2. **Slow back‑and‑forth drill** – Move between the chords at a very low speed, focusing on light pressure, no neck squeezing, and a fluid hand lift‑travel‑land motion.
3. **Tension awareness** – Periodically check shoulders, wrist, and forearm. If you feel clenching, pause, shake out the tension, and resume relaxed.
4. **Session length** – About 15 minutes of focused, relaxed practice is optimal; longer sessions tend to re‑introduce tension.
5. **Silent vs. strummed practice** – Begin with silent chord placement to verify accuracy, then add a gentle strum once the motion feels predictable.

## Practice Flow
1. **Shape first** – Ensure the chord shapes are clean without a metronome (see [[concepts/shape-first-tempo-later]]).
2. Apply the **pivot‑finger drill** for problematic transitions.
3. After achieving ~10 clean switches in a row, re‑introduce a slow metronome and expand timing (refer to [[concepts/whole-shape-placement]] for whole‑shape landing).
4. Gradually increase tempo and incorporate simple strumming patterns while maintaining low tension.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- The tension‑reduction drill supports the principle of **whole‑shape placement** by encouraging the hand to move as a single unit.
- Managing tension aligns with the **shape‑first‑tempo‑later** workflow, ensuring a stable foundation before adding rhythmic pressure.
