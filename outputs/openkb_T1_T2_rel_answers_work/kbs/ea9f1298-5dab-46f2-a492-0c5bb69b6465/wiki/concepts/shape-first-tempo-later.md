# Shape First, Tempo Later

**Concept type:** Concept

**Description:** A practice approach that emphasizes mastering chord shapes and smooth transitions before introducing a metronome for timing control.

---

## Overview
The *Shape First, Tempo Later* method advises guitarists to focus on the accuracy of finger placement and the fluidity of movement **before** working with a metronome.  Once the chord transition can be performed cleanly and consistently, a slow metronome pulse is added to develop steady timing, and the tempo is increased incrementally.

## Key Elements
1. **Shape mastery** – Practice the chord shapes (e.g., G major, C major) without any timing pressure.  The goal is to land the whole shape in one coordinated motion.
2. **Movement consistency** – Repeatedly perform the transition until you can do it about ten times in a row without hesitation or tension.
3. **Introduce metronome** – Start with a very slow click, spacing chord changes every two beats.  Gradually tighten the interval to one beat and eventually add a simple strumming pattern.
4. **Benchmark** – Ten clean switches is a practical marker that the shape is ready for timed practice.
