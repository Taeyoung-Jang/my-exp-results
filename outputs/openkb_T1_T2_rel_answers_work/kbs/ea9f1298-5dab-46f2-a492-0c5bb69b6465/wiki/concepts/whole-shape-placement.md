# Whole‑Shape Placement for Chord Transitions

**Concept ID:** `concepts/whole-shape-placement`

## Definition
Whole‑shape placement is the practice of moving the fretting hand so that the complete target chord shape lands on the fretboard in a single, coordinated motion rather than positioning each finger separately.

## Core Principles
1. **Unified Motion** – Treat the chord as a single unit. The hand lifts, travels, and lands together, minimizing the time each finger spends moving independently.
2. **Accuracy First** – Before adding tempo, ensure the shape lands cleanly every time. This aligns with the [[concepts/shape-first-tempo-later]] approach of mastering shape before metronome use.
3. **Tension Control** – A smooth, whole‑shape movement reduces unnecessary hand tension, supporting the broader [[concepts/tension-management-guitar]] strategy.
4. **Benchmark for Readiness** – When a guitarist can execute roughly ten clean full‑shape switches in a row without conscious effort, the movement is ready for timed practice with a [[entities/metronome]].

## Practical Application (from [[summaries/session-08_2025-04-20]])
- **Focus on the whole shape:** The assistant advises “aim for placing the whole shape at once” and notes that one‑finger drills are useful only for fixing isolated issues.
- **Hovering vs. placement:** Lightly hovering can help visualise the target, but the goal remains a single‑action landing.
- **Transition example:** For the G‑to‑C change, using a pivot finger (the third finger on the B‑string) helps keep part of the shape stationary, making the whole‑shape move smaller and smoother.
- **Progression:** After achieving reliable whole‑shape placement, re‑introduce a [[entities/metronome]] at a very slow tempo, first changing every two beats, then every beat, before adding a strumming pattern.

## Benefits
- **Speed gains** – Once the movement is ingrained, increasing tempo requires less mental overhead.
- **Consistency** – Whole‑shape placement yields more predictable timing, which is essential for rhythmic playing.
- **Reduced injury risk** – Less frantic finger‑by‑finger adjustments lower the chance of strain.
