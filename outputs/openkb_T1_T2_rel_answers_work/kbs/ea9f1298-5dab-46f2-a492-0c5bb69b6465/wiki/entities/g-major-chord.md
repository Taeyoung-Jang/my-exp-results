# G Major Chord (work)

The **G major chord** is a foundational open chord on the [[entities/guitar]] that appears frequently in folk, pop, and rock music. It consists of the notes G, B, and D and is typically fingered using an open‑string shape.

---

## Mention in [[summaries/session-08_2025-04-20]]

In the conversation dated 2025‑04‑20, the G major chord is discussed as part of a transition to the C major chord. The assistant recommends using a **pivot‑finger** technique—keeping the third finger on the B‑string 3rd fret—so the hand movement between G and C is smaller and less tense.

---

## Recommended fingering for the pivot technique

* **Standard open G shape** (used for the pivot):
  * 6th string (E) – open
  * 5th string (A) – 2nd fret (index finger)
  * 4th string (D) – open
  * 3rd string (G) – open
  * 2nd string (B) – **3rd fret** (middle finger – serves as the pivot)
  * 1st string (E) – 3rd fret (ring finger)

Using this shape keeps the middle finger on the B‑string, providing continuity when moving to the C major chord.

## Practice advice from the session

* **Shape first, tempo later** – master the clean shape before introducing a [[entities/metronome]].
* **Whole‑shape placement** – aim to place the entire chord in one coordinated motion, as highlighted in [[concepts/whole-shape-placement]].
* **Tension management** – keep the hand light, avoid squeezing the neck, and pause when shoulders or wrists tense, following principles in [[concepts/tension-management-guitar]].
* **Metronome re‑introduction** – after achieving ~10 clean switches, bring back the [[entities/metronome]] with a change every two beats, then tighten as comfort grows, reflecting the [[concepts/shape-first-tempo-later]] approach.
