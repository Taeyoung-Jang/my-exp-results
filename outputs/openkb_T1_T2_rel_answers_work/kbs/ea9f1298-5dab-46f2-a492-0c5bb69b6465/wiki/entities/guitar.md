# Guitar (product)

The guitar is a fretted, six‑string instrument widely used for chord‑based playing, both in solo practice and ensemble contexts.

## Role in session‑08_2025‑04‑20
In the conversation **[[summaries/session-08_2025-04-20]]**, the guitar is the central tool for learning chord transitions such as **[[entities/g-major-chord]]** to **[[entities/c-major-chord]]**.  The advice focuses on:

- Developing clean chord **shapes** before adding tempo (see [[concepts/shape-first-tempo-later]]).
- Placing the entire chord shape in one motion, a principle described in [[concepts/whole-shape-placement]].
- Reducing hand tension using a pivot‑finger technique, captured by [[concepts/tension-management-guitar]].
- Employing a **[[entities/metronome]]** only after the movement feels reliable.

## Key Takeaways for Guitar Practice
1. **Shape mastery first** – Prioritize accurate finger placement and smooth motion on the guitar before introducing a metronome.
2. **Whole‑shape placement** – Aim to land the full chord at once rather than moving each finger separately.
3. **Pivot‑finger drill** – Use the third finger on the B‑string 3rd fret as an anchor when moving between G and C, minimizing hand jumps.
4. **Metronome re‑introduction** – Begin with slow, spaced clicks (change every two beats) and gradually increase tempo.
5. **Tension monitoring** – Keep the hand relaxed; stop practice if shoulder or wrist tension appears.

These principles are directly drawn from the guidance given in the session summary and form a concise framework for effective guitar chord‑change practice.
