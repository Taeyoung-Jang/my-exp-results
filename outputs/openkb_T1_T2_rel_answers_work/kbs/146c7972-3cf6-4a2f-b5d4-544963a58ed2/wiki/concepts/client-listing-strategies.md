# Client Listing Strategies

A set of approaches for presenting client information on a resume or LinkedIn profile while maintaining professional credibility and respecting confidentiality.

## Core Options
1. **Full client names** – Use when the client is publicly known and you have permission. This adds strong social proof and can make the candidate stand out.
2. **Anonymized descriptors** – Replace the name with a brief description such as “mid‑size healthcare company” or “Fortune 500 retail client.” Useful for sensitive projects where disclosure isn’t allowed.
3. **Industry or type grouping** – Summarize the client base by sector, e.g., “Clients included organizations in tech, healthcare, and professional services.” Provides breadth without exposing individual identities.

## Decision Factors
- **Confidentiality agreements**: If a contract includes NDAs, lean toward anonymization or grouping.
- **Client prominence**: Well‑known brands can boost credibility; only list them if you’re sure it’s acceptable.
- **Relevance to target role**: Highlight clients that align with the job you’re applying for, using the most appropriate format.

## Integration with Resume & LinkedIn Formats
When applying the strategies, align the presentation with the overall **[[concepts/resume-linkedin-formatting]]** guidelines:
- On a **resume**, a concise “Selected Clients:” line works well, using one of the three options.
- On **LinkedIn**, a brief “Clients include…” paragraph under the Experience description is common.

## Relationship to Umbrella Role
These strategies often accompany an **[[concepts/umbrella-role]]**, where a freelancer lists a continuous self‑employment entry. The client‑listing method chosen helps flesh out that umbrella entry without breaking the clean timeline.
