# Summary of session-35_2025-02-15

**Document type:** Conversation transcript – personal planning advice.

## Core Contribution
A practical weekly template for a commuter who wants to balance gym, errands, and social activities while minimizing peak‑hour stress, plus two explicit fallback rules.

## Weekly Template (Mon–Sun)
- **Mon:** Home ~18:15 → gym 19:00‑20:00
- **Tue:** Social night, depart after 19:00 to avoid rush
- **Wed:** Errands after work, leave ~19:00, back by 20:30
- **Thu:** Gym 19:00‑20:00
- **Fri:** Light evening – catch‑up slot if anything slipped, otherwise free
- **Sat:** Gym late morning 10:30‑11:30; afternoon open for social plans
- **Sun:** Backup errands late morning ~11:00; afternoon reset

## Fallback Rules (explicit)
1. **If a train delay or late workday makes a weekday gym or errand impossible,** move that activity to **Friday evening** or **Sunday morning** (whichever remains free).
2. **If two weekday activities collide,** prioritize **gym**, shift **errands** to **Sunday**, and move **social plans** to **Friday or the weekend**.

## Rules of Thumb for Peak‑Hour Stress
- Treat any train‑dependent outing as needing a **20‑minute buffer** before the required arrival/departure time.
- Aim to travel **just before peak starts** or **30 minutes after it ends** rather than in the middle of rush hour.

## Key Concepts (potential cross‑document concepts)
- weekly routine template – a repeatable schedule anchored to off‑peak windows.
- fallback rules – explicit contingency actions for disrupted plans.
- peak hour buffer – built‑in time cushion to absorb delays.
- default time slots – pre‑selected windows that naturally avoid rush hour.
- floating blocks – flexible catch‑up periods for overflow tasks.

## Takeaway
By fixing **default slots** for gym, errands, and social events, and using **clear fallback rules** plus a **buffer habit**, the commuter can maintain a stable week without constantly checking train timetables.
