# Morning Timing

**Definition**: The *morning timing* concept captures the actual span of a household’s morning—from the first person’s natural wake‑up to the last person being ready to leave. Establishing this window provides the foundation for designing efficient routines.

---

## Why It Matters
- Timing sets the boundaries for what habits are feasible.
- Knowing the true window helps identify when bottlenecks (the *crunchy moments*) occur.
- It informs decisions about layout adjustments and habit sequencing.

---

## Core Steps (from [[summaries/session-35_2024-06-15]])
1. **Observe the natural flow** for 3–5 days without making changes.
2. Record:
   - *First wake‑up time* – the moment the earliest person (adult or child) initiates the morning.
   - *Last “ready for the day” time* – when the final household member is prepared to leave.
3. Use these timestamps to calculate the *true morning window*.
4. Spot where the flow stalls by noting *crunchy moments* (e.g., bathroom traffic, simultaneous breakfast prep).

## Practical Tips
- Keep the logging light: just start‑ and end‑times plus brief notes on any crowding.
- Do not aim for precision; approximate times (e.g., 6:45 am) are sufficient.
- Review the collected data to see patterns, then prioritize the biggest timing‑related bottleneck.

---

## Outcome
By anchoring a morning routine in *morning timing*, families can align habits, layout, and scheduling to the reality of their household, reducing chaos and creating a more purposeful start to the day.
