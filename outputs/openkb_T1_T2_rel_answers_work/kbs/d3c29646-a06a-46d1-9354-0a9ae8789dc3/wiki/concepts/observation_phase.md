# Observation Phase

**Definition**: A lightweight, short‑term (typically 3–5 days) practice of simply watching how a household’s morning actually unfolds, without making any changes. The goal is to capture the *real* start‑to‑finish window and pinpoint recurring "crunchy moments" that cause stress.

## Why Use an Observation Phase?
- Provides data that grounds any later redesign in reality rather than assumptions.
- Keeps the effort minimal, avoiding overwhelm for busy families.
- Acts as a diagnostic step before tackling larger habit, layout, or timing adjustments.

## Core Steps (as described in [[summaries/session-35_2024-06-15]])
1. **Track three core timestamps** each morning:
   - *First wake‑up time*: when the first person (adult or child) initiates the day.
   - *Last “ready for the day” time*: when the final household member is actually ready to leave.
   - *Crunchy moments*: brief notes on any moment that feels crowded, delayed, or tense (e.g., bathroom traffic, multiple kids needing help simultaneously).
2. **Use a simple template** (see the session summary) to record these items quickly.
3. **Observe, don’t redesign**: Resist the urge to fix anything while recording; the focus is purely on data collection.

## Linking to Related Concepts
- The observation results feed directly into [[concepts/morning_timing]], helping families define their true morning window.
- Noted *crunchy moments* are later analyzed under [[concepts/crunchy_moments]] to surface the most disruptive bottlenecks.
- Insights from this phase often inform the high‑impact [[concepts/out_the_door_system]] tweak, as the exit routine is typically the most time‑critical.
- A successful observation can also highlight areas where a brief [[concepts/night_before_checklist]] might pre‑empt common delays.

## Expected Outcomes
- A clear picture of the morning timeline, expressed in start and end times.
- A list of recurring bottlenecks, captured succinctly.
- Confidence about which single change will have the greatest impact, based on actual flow rather than speculation.
