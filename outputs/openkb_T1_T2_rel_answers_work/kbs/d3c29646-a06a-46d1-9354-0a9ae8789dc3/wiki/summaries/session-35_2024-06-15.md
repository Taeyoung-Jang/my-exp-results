# Summary of Session 35 (2024‑06‑15)

**Main focus**: Designing a calmer, purposeful morning routine for a multi‑person household, especially with young children.

## Prioritize Timing
- Start by establishing the *true morning window* – from the first wake‑up to the last person ready to leave.
- Timing shapes which habits are realistic and where layout tweaks matter.
- [[concepts/morning_timing]]

## Observation Phase
- Conduct a 3–5 day “observe, don’t redesign” pass.
- Record only three data points each morning: first wake‑up, last ready‑time, and any *crunchy moments* (bottlenecks).
- Keeps the effort light and avoids premature redesign.
- [[concepts/observation_phase]]

## Crunchy Moments & Bottleneck Identification
- Crunchy moments are moments of crowding, delays, or tension (e.g., two kids needing the same parent, bathroom traffic).
- Use these notes to spot recurring bottlenecks. *(bottleneck identification is discussed in the text but not linked to a concept page)*
- [[concepts/crunchy_moments]]

## Simple Logging Template
```
Morning Log
Date:
First wake‑up time:
Last “ready for the day” time:
Crunchy moments (what happened and roughly when):
Anything that felt smoother than usual:
``` 
- Provides a quick, repeatable way to capture the needed data. *(no dedicated concept page)*

## Highest‑Impact Change: Out‑the‑Door System
- If only one adjustment is possible, streamline the *exit* process.
- A clear, fixed deadline makes upstream activities smoother.
- [[concepts/out_the_door_system]]

## Tiny Night‑Before Checklist (2–3 Rules)
1. All items that need to leave the house go to a single spot.
2. Shoes stay paired in that spot.
3. Kids do a quick “is my stuff here?” glance before bed.
- Checklist questions: *Are the bags packed? Are the shoes where they should be? Is anything special needed for tomorrow?*
- Works even on low‑energy evenings.
- [[concepts/night_before_checklist]]

**Takeaway**: By first defining the real timing, lightly observing for bottlenecks, and implementing a minimal out‑the‑door system with a night‑before checklist, families can gain immediate calm without large‑scale reorganization.
