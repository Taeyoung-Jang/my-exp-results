## Summary of session-36_2025-03-15

- **Crowd response variability** – People differ in how they react to large crowds based on temperament, sensory sensitivity, and past experiences. Those who find crowds energizing are often more stimulus‑seeking, while others feel overstimulation and threat.

- **Gradual exposure & supportive context** – Easing into crowded environments, having a trusted companion, and choosing a spot with some breathing room help the brain shift from threat to curiosity. [[concepts/gradual-exposure]]

- **In‑the‑moment coping techniques**  
  - **Breathing:** slow the exhale slightly longer than the inhale to promote parasympathetic activity. [[concepts/controlled-breathing-technique]]  
  - **Focused attention:** anchor awareness on a small, steady sensation (feet on the ground, texture of an object) or note three visual details without analysis.  
  - **Labeling sensations:** verbally naming the feeling (e.g., “I’m feeling jittery”) reduces its intensity.

- **Role‑based exposure** – Volunteering as an arena usher provides purpose and agency, turning the crowd from a stressor into a structured task. This combines classic exposure conditioning with the benefits of meaningful role engagement. [[concepts/role-based-exposure]]

- **Burnout vs. normal fatigue**  
  - **Burnout signs:** emotional flatness, persistent drain after rest, dread of future shifts, irritability, or a “checked‑out” feeling during work.  
  - **Normal tiredness:** temporary weariness that recovers with usual rest.

- **Recovery habits between shifts** – Quiet walks, warm showers, screen‑free downtime, gentle hydration, or simply sitting in a calm space help reset the nervous system.
