# Glare Reduction

**Definition**: Techniques and tools aimed at reducing uncomfortable brightness and reflections on screens and surfaces in a workspace.

## Core Elements

1. **Monitor Placement**  
   - Position the monitor farther from windows and angle it away from direct sunlight to cut reflections.  
   - Related concept: [[concepts/monitor_placement]].

2. **Window Treatments**  
   - Use light‑filtering roller shades with low openness (3–5 %).  
   - Prefer solar‑screen fabric for better glare and heat control while preserving daylight.  
   - Related entities: [[entities/roller_shade]], [[entities/solar_screen_fabric]].

3. **Screen Filters**  
   - Matte screen filters or diffusers can soften reflections without significantly dimming the display.  
   - Related concept: [[concepts/diffuser]].

4. **Bias Lighting**  
   - A dimmable neutral‑white light (≈ 5000–6500 K) placed behind the monitor reduces contrast between the screen and surrounding wall.  
   - Related concepts: [[concepts/bias_lighting]], [[concepts/color_temperature]], [[concepts/dimmer_switch]].  
   - Example hardware: [[entities/bias_light_strip]].

5. **Ambient Lighting Balance**  
   - Combine natural light with warm desk or floor lamps to avoid stark contrast between bright windows and darker work surfaces.  
   - Consider lumens output when selecting fixtures.  
   - Related concepts: [[concepts/fixture_type]], [[concepts/lumens]].

## Practical Guidance from Session 32 (2025‑07‑23)

- The assistant recommended **roller shades with 3–5 % openness**, recommending 5 % for most of the day and 3 % only during intense afternoon sun.
- A **solar‑screen fabric** was preferred over regular light‑filtering shades for superior glare control.
- A **bias light strip** placed around the monitor’s back edge, set to a neutral white temperature (5000–6500 K) and dimmable, helps even out screen‑to‑wall contrast.
- Monitor brightness should be matched to ambient light, and a subtle night‑light mode can reduce blue‑light strain without major color shift.
