# Bias Lighting

Bias lighting is a low‑intensity light source placed behind a display to create a neutral background glow. The purpose is to reduce the contrast between a bright screen and a darker surrounding environment, which helps minimise eye fatigue during prolonged use.

## Why It Matters
- **Glare reduction**: By evening out the overall luminance of the viewing area, bias lighting lessens the perceived glare from bright windows or overhead lights. [[concepts/glare_reduction]]
- **Color perception**: A neutral white light (≈ 5000–6500 K) avoids tinting the colors on the screen, preserving accurate colour reproduction. [[concepts/color_temperature]]
- **Comfort**: The consistent background illumination makes it easier for the eyes to adapt when shifting focus between the monitor and the surrounding space.

## Key Characteristics (from [[summaries/session-32_2025-07-23]])
- **Placement**: Install the light around the back edge of the monitor for an even halo effect, rather than using a separate light bar.
- **Dimmable**: A dimmer control lets you match the bias light intensity to ambient daylight levels, ensuring the room stays bright without being over‑lit. [[concepts/dimmer_switch]]
- **Color temperature**: Aim for a neutral white in the 5000–6500 K range to complement daylight and avoid colour shifts. [[concepts/color_temperature]]
- **Fixture type**: LED bias light strips are common because they are thin, energy‑efficient, and provide uniform illumination. [[entities/bias_light_strip]] [[entities/led_bulb]]

## Relation to Other Concepts
- **Monitor placement**: Proper monitor positioning (farther from windows, angled away) works together with bias lighting to minimise reflections. [[concepts/monitor_placement]]
- **Diffuser**: Some bias light strips include a diffuser layer to smooth the light output and prevent hotspots. [[concepts/diffuser]]
- **Lumens**: Bias lighting typically requires only a few lumens (10–30 lm) because its role is subtle illumination, not room lighting. [[concepts/lumens]]

## Practical Tips
1. Choose a bias light strip that is dimmable and can be set to a neutral white temperature.
2. Mount it directly behind the monitor, ensuring the light shines on the wall rather than into the viewer’s eyes.
3. Adjust brightness according to the time of day; lower the intensity when natural light is strong, increase it as daylight wanes.
4. Combine bias lighting with other glare‑reduction strategies, such as light‑filtering roller shades, for optimal visual comfort.
