# Color Temperature

Color temperature is a specification of the hue of a light source, expressed in kelvins (K). Lower values (e.g., 2700‑3000 K) produce a warm, yellowish light, while higher values (e.g., 5000‑6500 K) emit a cool, bluish light. In interior lighting, especially for workspaces, a **neutral white** range around **4000 K** is often recommended because it offers clear illumination without the harshness of very cool light.

## Relevance to Home Office Lighting

In the conversation summarized in [[summaries/session-01_2025-01-20]], the assistant suggests:
- Using LED bulbs in the **4000 K** range for the main overhead illumination to keep the environment clear and focused.
- Slightly increasing to **4200‑4500 K** when a space is naturally dim, providing a crisper feel without becoming overly cold.
- Pairing the neutral overhead light with a warmer task lamp for flexibility throughout the day.

These recommendations align with research that neutral white light supports productivity while minimizing eye strain, whereas overly warm or cool light can affect mood and visual comfort.

## Interaction with Other Lighting Factors

- **Lumens**: The brightness level (measured in lumens) works alongside color temperature; a well‑diffused 1500‑2500 lumens source at 4000 K can brighten a small room effectively.
- **Diffuser**: Proper diffusion (e.g., frosted domes) ensures the neutral white light is evenly spread, reducing glare.
- **Fixture Type**: Flush or semi‑flush mounts are ideal for distributing neutral white light across a workspace.
- **Dimmer Switch**: Adding a dimmer allows users to lower the intensity while maintaining the same color temperature, supporting evening comfort.
- **Bias Lighting**: A bias light placed behind a monitor can even out the contrast between the screen and surrounding walls, reducing eye strain. For this purpose, a neutral white in the **5000–6500 K** range works best because it does not tint screen colors and blends well with daylight. A dimmable bias light strip (see [[entities/bias_light_strip]]) positioned around the back edge of the monitor provides a smooth halo without shining directly into the eyes. This ties into the broader concept of [[concepts/bias_lighting]].

## Practical Tips

1. Choose **LED bulbs** (see [[entities/led_bulb]]) rated for the desired kelvin range.
2. Aim for a neutral white temperature (~4000 K) for primary task lighting.
3. Adjust upward to 4200‑4500 K only if the room feels overly gloomy.
4. Complement with warmer accent lighting for a balanced atmosphere.
5. When using bias lighting, select a neutral white strip in the 5000–6500 K range and keep it dimmable to match ambient daylight.

By understanding and applying the appropriate color temperature, users can create a lighting environment that enhances both mood and productivity.
