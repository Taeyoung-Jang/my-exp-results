## Dimmer Switch

A **dimmer switch** is a control device that varies the electrical power delivered to a light source, enabling users to fine‑tune illumination levels.  In a home workspace, a dimmer provides flexibility: bright, neutral light can be used for focused tasks, while lower, warmer light can be dialed down in the evening to reduce eye strain and create a more relaxed atmosphere.

### Why it matters in the session‑01_2025‑01‑20 lighting guide
- The assistant recommends a **neutral white LED** overhead fixture (≈4000‑4500 K) as the primary light source.
- To avoid a harsh, clinical feel after long screen time, the guide suggests adding a **dimmer switch** so the intensity can be lowered later in the day.
- Dimming complements other lighting strategies such as **task lighting**, **ambient lighting**, and **bias lighting** behind a monitor, allowing each to be balanced for mood and productivity.

### Key Benefits
1. **Glare and comfort control** – Reduces the risk of eye fatigue by allowing lower brightness when full illumination is unnecessary.
2. **Energy efficiency** – Dimming LEDs consumes less power than running them at full output.
3. **Mood adaptation** – Supports circadian lighting practices by enabling warmer, softer light in the evening.

### Practical considerations
- Choose a dimmer compatible with **LED bulbs** to avoid flicker or reduced lifespan.
- Install the dimmer on the main **overhead fixture** to affect the room’s primary light level; additional task lamps can have separate controls if desired.
- Pair with diffusers (e.g., frosted domes) to maintain even illumination while dimming.
