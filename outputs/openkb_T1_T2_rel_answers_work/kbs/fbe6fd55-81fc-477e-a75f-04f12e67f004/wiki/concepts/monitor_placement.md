# Monitor Placement

**Definition**: The practice of locating and angling a computer monitor relative to ambient light sources and the viewer to reduce glare, improve visual comfort, and maintain color accuracy.

## Key Principles

1. **Distance from bright windows**
   - Place the monitor farther from windows that receive strong daylight (e.g., a left‑hand window with afternoon sun) to lessen the contrast between the bright outside and the screen.
2. **Angling**
   - Slightly rotate the monitor so that sunlight hits the screen from the side rather than head‑on. This reduces direct reflections and the need for excessive brightness adjustments.
3. **Height and eye level**
   - The top of the screen should be at or just below eye level, allowing a natural downward gaze.
4. **Consistency with ambient lighting**
   - Align monitor brightness with the room’s overall illumination; avoid settings that are markedly brighter than the surrounding area.
5. **Complementary bias lighting**
   - Pair proper monitor placement with a dimmable bias light (e.g., a [[entities/bias_light_strip]]) behind the monitor to even out screen‑to‑wall contrast, further reducing eye strain.

## Benefits
- **Reduced glare** – By avoiding direct line‑of‑sight to bright windows, the monitor reflects less light, which ties into broader [[concepts/glare_reduction]] strategies.
- **Improved visual ergonomics** – Proper distance and angle support a relaxed posture and lessen the need for squinting.
- **Better color perception** – Keeping the monitor at a comfortable brightness level helps maintain accurate color rendering, especially when using subtle [[concepts/color_temperature]] adjustments.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/glare_reduction]] to create a comfortable workstation.
- Bias lighting ([[concepts/bias_lighting]]) can complement optimal placement by providing a balanced background illumination.
