# Summary of Session 32 (2025‑07‑23)

**Topic:** Reducing afternoon glare from a left‑hand window while keeping the room bright.

## Key Recommendations

- **Monitor placement** – Position the [[entities/monitor]] farther from the window and angle it slightly away to minimize reflections. Keep it at a comfortable viewing distance. [[concepts/monitor_placement]]
- **Screen brightness & contrast** – Match monitor brightness to the ambient light level; keep contrast near default. Use a subtle “night‑light” or low‑blue‑light mode for comfort without major color shift.
- **Light‑control window treatment** – Prefer light‑filtering **roller shades** over sheer curtains for adjustable glare reduction. Aim for a 3–5 % openness fabric; 5 % works well for most of the day, 3 % only if the sun is blinding. Use a **solar‑screen** material for superior glare and heat control while preserving daylight and view clarity. [[entities/roller_shade]] [[entities/solar_screen_fabric]] [[concepts/glare_reduction]]
- **Bias lighting** – Add a dimmable neutral‑white bias light (≈ 5000–6500 K) around the monitor’s back edge to even out screen‑to‑wall contrast. This reduces eye strain without affecting colors. [[entities/bias_light_strip]] [[concepts/bias_lighting]] [[concepts/color_temperature]] [[concepts/dimmer_switch]]

## Practical Implementation Steps

1. Install roller shades with ~5 % openness and solar‑screen fabric on the left window.
2. Slightly increase the distance between the monitor and the window; tilt the screen to avoid direct head‑on glare.
3. Adjust monitor brightness to the room’s ambient level; enable a gentle night‑light mode if needed.
4. Mount a dimmable bias‑light strip behind the monitor, set to a neutral white temperature, and tune brightness to current daylight.
