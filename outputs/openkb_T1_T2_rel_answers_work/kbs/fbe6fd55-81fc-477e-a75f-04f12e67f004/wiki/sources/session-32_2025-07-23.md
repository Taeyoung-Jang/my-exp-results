**User:** I’m trying to tweak my home office setup and could use some ideas. Ever since I started working from that spot by the big windows, I end up squinting in most of the day. What are some practical fixes for glare/brightness that won’t make the room feel dark—like monitor placement, blinds/curtains, screen filters, or lighting tips?

**Assistant:** You’ve got a few good levers to pull without turning the room into a cave. One of the easiest fixes is shifting your monitor so it isn’t facing the windows and isn’t directly backed by them. Angling it even a little—so the windows hit it from the side rather than head‑on—usually cuts a lot of glare.

Light‑control options can stay pretty soft. Sheer curtains or light‑filtering roller shades let daylight in while knocking down harsh brightness. They avoid that heavy, closed‑off feeling you get from blackout curtains. If the sun hits your windows at certain times, partially lowering a shade just during those hours can help without dimming the whole room.

A matte screen filter is another low‑effort option. It won’t make the display look dull if you get a decent one; it just diffuses reflections enough to keep you from squinting.

Finally, try balancing the room’s lighting so your eyes aren’t jumping between a bright window and a darker workspace. A warm desk lamp or floor lamp placed behind or beside your monitor can even out the contrast and reduce strain without competing with the natural light.

All of those together usually create a bright, comfortable workspace that doesn’t glare back at you.

**User:** That helps—thanks. The window is basically to my left and gets strong afternoon sun. Would you prioritize sheer curtains vs light-filtering roller shades for that, and is it better to move the monitor closer to the window or farther away? Also, any specific monitor brightness/contrast settings or “night light” type settings that tend to reduce squinting without messing up colors too much?

**Assistant:** If the sun is hitting that left window hard in the afternoon, light‑filtering roller shades usually give you more control than sheer curtains. Sheers soften the light nicely, but roller shades let you dial it down just enough without losing the brightness of the room. They’re especially good when the sun angle changes through the day.

For monitor placement, keeping it a little farther from the window usually feels better. When it’s too close, the contrast between the bright window and the screen is sharper, and you end up squinting more. A bit more distance plus a slight angle away from the window tends to reduce reflections and keep your eyes more relaxed.

For screen settings, a good starting point is lowering brightness so it matches the general light level in the room—most people keep it higher than they need. Contrast can stay around the default unless it looks washed out. The built‑in “night light” or low‑blue‑light modes can help with comfort, especially later in the afternoon, but you can keep it subtle so colors don’t shift too much. A warmer temperature that’s only slightly tinted is usually enough to take the edge off without making everything look orange.

**User:** Got it. If I go with roller shades, is there a particular openness/% or fabric type you’d recommend for cutting that harsh afternoon glare but still keeping the room bright? Also, would adding an external bias light behind the monitor actually help in my case, and if so what color temperature should it be?

**Assistant:** For strong afternoon sun, a roller shade with about 3–5% openness is usually the sweet spot. It cuts the harsh glare and direct beam of light but still keeps the room bright and lets you see outside without feeling closed in. Anything tighter than 3% starts to dim the room more than you probably want, and anything above 5% won’t tame the glare enough at that time of day.

A bias light can help, especially since the window is to your left. It evens out the contrast between your screen and the surrounding area, which reduces eye strain. A neutral white in the 5000–6500K range works best because it doesn’t tint your screen colors and blends well with daylight. You don’t need it very bright—just enough to give the wall behind the monitor a gentle glow.

**User:** If I’m doing a 3–5% openness shade, would you pick 3% or 5% given that the sun is pretty intense for a couple hours but the rest of the day is fine? Also, does it matter if the fabric is “solar screen” vs just a regular light-filtering shade? And for the bias light, should it be dimmable and placed around the back edge of the monitor, or better as a light bar behind it?

**Assistant:** Since the sun is only brutal for a couple of hours, 5% is usually the nicer everyday choice. It softens the glare enough for most people while keeping the room very bright the rest of the time. Go with 3% only if those two hours are genuinely blinding, because it will make the room feel a bit dimmer overall.

A true solar‑screen fabric is worth it if your main goal is glare control without losing daylight. It manages heat and glare better than a standard light‑filtering fabric and keeps the view clearer.

For the bias light, dimmable is ideal so you can match whatever the daylight is doing. Placement around the back edge of the monitor is usually smoother and more even than a separate light bar; it gives you that clean halo of light on the wall without shining directly toward your eyes.
