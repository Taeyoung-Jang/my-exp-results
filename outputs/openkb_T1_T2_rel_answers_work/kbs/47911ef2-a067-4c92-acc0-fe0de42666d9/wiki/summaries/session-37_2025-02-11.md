# Summary of Session 37 (2025‑02‑11)

## Core Topic
This conversation provides a practical framework for **[[concepts/post-graduation-administration]]** – securing official records, avoiding common pitfalls, and correcting name‑spelling errors such as missing accent marks.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/post-graduation-administration]]** – tasks to complete immediately after degree conferral.  
- **official documents checklist** – timeline‑based actions for transcripts, diplomas, and verification records.  
- **[[concepts/document-correction-workflow]]** – steps to fix errors on diplomas, transcripts, and related records.  
- **accented name handling** – specific advice for names with diacritics (plain text).  
- **[[concepts/employer-communication-strategy]]** – how to update applications without raising concerns.  
- **[[entities/alumni-portal]] onboarding** – transitioning from student to alumni accounts.

## Timeline Checklist
### This Week
- Request final official transcript and confirm degree conferral.  
- Download all valuable files (papers, project files, recommendation letters, financial aid records) from university portals before accounts close.  
- Verify personal email and mailing address in the university system.

### Within 30 Days
- Order sealed transcripts and degree certificates needed for jobs, visas, or further study. *(see [[concepts/documentation-requirements]])*  
- Identify specific documentation required by licensing boards or background‑check services.  
- Keep an up‑to‑date unofficial transcript for quick reference.

### Within 3–6 Months
- Confirm that external agencies can verify your degree (employers, credential evaluators).  
- Back up all documents in multiple locations (cloud, external drive, encrypted storage).  
- Join the [[entities/alumni-portal]] and set up forwarding from the closed student email.

## Handling Errors on Official Documents
1. Contact the **[[entities/university-registrar-office]]** immediately when an error (e.g., misspelled name, wrong graduation date) is discovered.  
2. Provide legal proof of the correct spelling (passport, ID, birth certificate).  
3. Request a corrected copy of every affected document (diploma, sealed transcripts).  
4. Verify that the digital student record, degree conferral record, and verification system all reflect the corrected name. *(uses [[concepts/document-correction-workflow]])*

## Post‑Correction Actions
- Review the unofficial transcript to ensure the correct accent appears.  
- Order new sealed transcripts if previous versions bear the wrong spelling.  
- Scan the corrected diploma and store it alongside the digital record.  
- Keep a high‑quality backup of the corrected documents for future uploads.

## Updating Employers or Graduate Programs
- Send a brief, factual note once the corrected documents are received, using principles from **[[concepts/email-drafting]]**:  
  > “My university recently corrected the spelling of my name (added the missing accent). Please find the updated diploma attached.”  
- Attach the corrected scan; if the portal blocks new uploads, email the admissions or HR contact directly.  
- Emphasize that the change is an administrative correction by the registrar, not a personal inconsistency.  
- Anticipate that background checks will automatically align with the corrected official record.

## Additional Recommendations
- Periodically audit your academic record for consistency across all documents.  
- Keep copies of both the original and corrected versions until you are certain all external parties have updated their files.  
- Leverage the alumni network for guidance on credential verification processes.
