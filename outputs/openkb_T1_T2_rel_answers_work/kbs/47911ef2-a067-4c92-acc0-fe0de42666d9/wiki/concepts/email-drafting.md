## Email Drafting

Email drafting is the structured creation of brief, clear, and appropriately toned messages aimed at achieving a specific communicative goal. Key elements include:

- **Brevity**: Keeping the message to a few sentences while covering all necessary points.
- **Tone**: Adjusting formality to match the audience, ranging from casual to formal.
- **Subject Line**: Providing a succinct preview of the email’s purpose.
- **Placeholders**: Using brackets (e.g., `[Student ID]`) for details that will be filled in later.
- **Clarity of Request**: Clearly stating the information needed, such as eligibility verification or documentation requirements.

The concept is illustrated in [[summaries/session-11_2024-05-12]], where the user requested two versions of an email to the [[entities/university-hr-office]] to confirm [[entities/student-health-plan]] eligibility and inquire about [[entities/student-discount-programs]]. The drafts demonstrate:

1. A concise, polite tone with a clear subject line.
2. A more formal version that includes questions on coverage start/end dates, pro‑rating, and potential gaps.
3. Use of placeholders for the student ID and expected defense date.
4. An explicit ask for required [[concepts/documentation-requirements]] (e.g., enrollment letter, advisor note).

These drafts embody the principles of effective [[concepts/email-drafting]] and highlight how email drafting intersects with other concepts such as [[concepts/student-health-plan-eligibility]] and documentation needs.
