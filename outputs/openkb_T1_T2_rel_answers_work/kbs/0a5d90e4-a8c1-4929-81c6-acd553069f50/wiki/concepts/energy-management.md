# Energy Management

**Definition:** Energy Management is the practice of using one’s personal energy and attention levels as the primary constraint when planning activities, rather than relying solely on calendar time.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-11]])
- **Energy‑centric planning:** Treat energy and attention as the limiting factor. Schedule activities that sustain or boost energy as recurring, and keep variable or creative tasks in flexible blocks.
- **Identify grounding activities:** Pinpoint a few core routines (e.g., sleep schedule, enjoyable workouts, nourishing social time) that reliably replenish energy. These become the anchors for recurring slots.
- **Support vs. pressure test:** When imagining a weekly repetition, ask whether the activity feels supportive (energy‑restorative) or pressure‑inducing (energy‑draining). Only the former should be fixed.
- **Flexible scheduling:** Allocate open time for tasks that fluctuate in interest or energy demand, such as creative work, optional hang‑outs, or hobbies. See [[concepts/flexible-scheduling]] for more detail.
- **Recurring planning:** Use recurring slots for activities that consistently support your baseline energy, aligning with [[concepts/recurring-planning]].

## Practical Application
1. **Map your weekly energy flow** – Note which days feel lighter or heavier. Avoid placing high‑focus work on days when energy naturally dips.
2. **Choose recurring social slots** – For many, late‑week (e.g., Thursday evenings) or weekend slots work well. If you keep weeknights open, decide between [[entities/saturday]] afternoon or [[entities/sunday]] midday based on which day you want to keep free for rest.
3. **Set gentle boundaries** – Anchor a recurring event between existing routines (e.g., after a protected morning routine) and define a natural endpoint (e.g., evening wind‑down) to prevent it from expanding into an all‑day commitment.
4. **Iterate and adjust** – Stick with a chosen schedule for a few weeks, then reassess whether the activity feels supportive or pressure‑inducing, and tweak accordingly.
