# Recurring Planning

**Definition**: Recurring planning is the practice of scheduling regular, repeatable activities that support core energy and attention needs, creating a predictable backbone for the week while leaving flexible space for variable tasks.

## Core Principles
- **Energy‑first focus** – Identify the few activities that consistently replenish you (e.g., sleep routine, enjoyable workouts, nourishing social time) and lock them into recurring slots. See [[concepts/energy-management]].
- **Stability vs. Flexibility** – Fixed slots provide stability; everything else should remain in open blocks to accommodate creative work, optional hang‑outs, or spontaneous opportunities. This contrast is described in [[concepts/flexible-scheduling]].
- **Iterative Tuning** – Treat the schedule as a hypothesis. Keep the recurring slot for several weeks, then assess whether it feels supportive or pressure‑inducing, and adjust accordingly.

## Application from Session 14
The conversation on 2024‑07‑11 illustrated recurring planning through practical advice:
- **Identify grounding routines** such as sleep, workouts, and a single weekly social engagement.
- **Choose the day/time** that aligns with personal energy patterns, avoiding high‑focus days (e.g., Tuesday/Wednesday) and favoring late‑week or weekend slots.
- **Weekend example**: Selecting a Saturday afternoon recurring social plan (or a Sunday midday plan) to keep the rest of the weekend open for rest. This decision involves entities like [[entities/saturday]] and [[entities/sunday]].
- **Boundary setting**: Anchor the recurring event between existing routines and limit travel to keep the activity contained.

For the full discussion, see [[summaries/session-14_2024-07-11]].

## Benefits
- Reduces decision fatigue by automating essential activities.
- Protects energy reserves, preventing over‑commitment.
- Creates predictable rhythms that improve focus and well‑being.
