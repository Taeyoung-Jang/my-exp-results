# Flexible Scheduling

**Definition**: Flexible scheduling is the practice of reserving portions of a week as unstructured, adaptable blocks that can be filled with activities whose timing or desirability may change from day to day, allowing energy and attention to remain the primary constraints.

## Core Principles
- **Energy‑first focus** – Prioritize how you feel over how many hours are available. When energy is high, you can add optional tasks; when low, you keep the block free.
- **Variable vs. fixed activities** – Keep recurring, grounding routines (sleep, workouts, nourishing social time) in fixed slots, and place creative work, optional hang‑outs, and hobbies in flexible blocks.
- **Open‑block anchoring** – Anchor flexible time between established routines (e.g., after a protected morning ritual) to create natural boundaries without rigid end‑times.

## Application in Session‑14 (2024‑07‑11)
The conversation outlines a practical workflow for weekly planning:
1. Identify core grounding activities that become recurring plans.
2. Map personal energy patterns across the week.
3. Allocate the remaining time as flexible space for creative work, optional social events, or spontaneous hobbies.
4. Use flexible blocks to test new activities without pressure; if a task repeatedly feels supportive, consider moving it to a recurring slot.

## Benefits
- Reduces over‑commitment and burnout by respecting natural energy cycles.
- Preserves spontaneity and creativity, allowing room for unexpected opportunities.
- Provides a feedback loop: flexible activities that consistently feel supportive can be promoted to recurring plans.
