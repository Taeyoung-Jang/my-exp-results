# Light Exposure Management

**Definition**: The practice of deliberately controlling light intensity and timing to regulate the body’s internal clock, especially for people whose sleep‑wake cycles differ from the natural day‑night pattern.

## Core Principles
- **Bright light after waking**: Seek strong light exposure as soon as you get up to signal alertness and reset the circadian rhythm.
- **Block morning light on the way home**: Wear sunglasses during the early‑morning commute to prevent bright light from delaying sleep onset.
- **Dim environment for pre‑sleep**: Keep lights low and avoid screens after returning home to promote melatonin production.
- **Consistent timing**: Align light exposure with the same schedule each day, even on days off, to reinforce the rhythm.

## Practical Tips from [[summaries/session-34_2025-01-10]]
- Use blackout curtains in the sleep room to create a **dark_sleep_environment** that mimics night.
- After a night shift, immediately put on sunglasses to reduce exposure to sunrise light.
- Schedule a brief period of bright light (natural or a light box) after your daytime wake‑up to boost alertness.
- Avoid bright screens at least an hour before the intended bedtime.

## Benefits
- Reduces sleep onset latency and daytime grogginess.
- Supports more stable sleep duration and quality.
- Helps align internal hormonal cycles (e.g., melatonin) with a non‑traditional schedule.
