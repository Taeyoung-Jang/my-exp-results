# Caffeine Cutoff Timing

**Definition**: The practice of ending caffeine consumption a set number of hours before the planned sleep period to minimize alertness and improve sleep onset, especially for shift workers.

---

## Core Principle
- A **six‑hour window** before the intended bedtime is recommended as a general rule. This timing aligns with the caffeine half‑life and helps reduce residual stimulation that can cause difficulty falling asleep.
- Individuals may need to adjust the window longer (e.g., 8 hours) if they are unusually sensitive.

## Application in Night‑Shift Context
- For a worker aiming to sleep at **8 a.m.**, the cutoff would be around **2 a.m.** (as discussed in [[summaries/session-34_2025-01-10]]).
- Maintaining this schedule consistently supports **[[concepts/recurring-planning]]** and stabilises the circadian rhythm.

## Relationships to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/dark_sleep_environment]]**: Reducing caffeine complements a dark, cool sleep setting by lowering physiological arousal.
- **[[concepts/light_exposure_management]]**: Pairing caffeine timing with controlled light exposure (e.g., wearing sunglasses on the way home) further signals the body that sleep time is approaching.
- **[[concepts/energy-management]]**: Proper caffeine scheduling helps balance alertness during work shifts while preserving the ability to rest effectively.
- **[[concepts/flexible-scheduling]]**: Workers with variable shift start times may need to adjust the cutoff proportionally to each sleep window.

## Practical Tips
1. **Identify your target sleep time** and count back six hours to set a caffeine curfew.
2. **Track personal response**: If you still feel wired, lengthen the cutoff incrementally.
3. **Use a reminder**: Set an alarm or note to stop coffee, tea, or other caffeine sources.
4. **Consider alternative stimulants**: Use short‑term tactics like brief exposure to bright light or a quick walk instead of caffeine late in the shift.
