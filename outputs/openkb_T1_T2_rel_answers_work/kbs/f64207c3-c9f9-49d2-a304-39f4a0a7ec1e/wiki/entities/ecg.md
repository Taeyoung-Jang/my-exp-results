# Electrocardiogram (ECG)

**Type:** product

## Overview
The electrocardiogram (ECG) is a medical device that captures the electrical signals generated by the heart, producing a trace that clinicians use to assess cardiac rhythm and detect abnormalities.

## Key Points from [[summaries/session-39_2025-07-04]]
- The conversation notes that **ECG reading is important later in paramedic training** but does not need deep study before class.
- Early learners should understand **how electricity moves through the heart** and what a **normal rhythm looks like**.
- Detailed interpretation and advanced ECG use are reserved for later stages of the program.

## Relevance to Paramedic Training
Understanding basic ECG concepts aligns with [[concepts/paramedic-training-priorities]], which emphasize foundational anatomy and physiology before tackling complex diagnostics.

## Study Recommendations
When designing a study schedule, incorporate a brief ECG overview in the early weeks, as suggested in [[concepts/study-plan-design]]. Focus on:
1. The purpose of an ECG.
2. Normal sinus rhythm characteristics.
3. Basic terminology (e.g., P wave, QRS complex, T wave).

## Related Entities
- [[entities/emt]] – EMTs typically use basic cardiac monitoring but do not interpret full ECGs.
- [[entities/paramedic]] – Paramedics perform advanced ECG interpretation in the field.

## Quick Reference
- **Device:** Portable, battery‑operated unit with leads placed on the patient.
- **Output:** Graphical trace of cardiac electrical activity.
- **Early Learning Goal:** Recognize normal rhythm and understand the physiological basis of the signal.
