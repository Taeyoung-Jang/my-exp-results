# 2008 Financial Crisis (Event)

The 2008 Financial Crisis was a worldwide banking and credit crisis that began in 2007 with the collapse of the U.S. housing market and peaked with the failure of major financial institutions in September 2008.  It led to a deep global recession, massive policy interventions, and lasting changes to financial regulation.

## Connection to the macro‑learning roadmap
The session‑09_2025-01-04 roadmap uses the 2008 crisis as a concrete illustration of how macroeconomic models and policy tools behave under stress.  It appears in the **Crises and Financial Frictions** stage, reminding readers to apply the concepts from [[concepts/financial-crises-mechanisms]] and [[concepts/short-run-macro-models]] when analyzing real‑world shocks.

## Key macroeconomic relevance
- **Expectations and inflation:** The crisis shocked inflation expectations, highlighting the importance of [[concepts/expectations-and-inflation]] in stabilising the economy.
- **Policy interaction:** Central banks, notably the U.S. Federal Reserve, deployed both rate tools and balance‑sheet measures, a classic example of the dynamics described in [[concepts/policy-interaction]].
- **Open‑economy mechanics:** The crisis propagated through international capital flows and exchange‑rate adjustments, linking to [[concepts/open-economy-mechanics]].
- **Measurement foundations:** Rapid revisions to GDP and unemployment data during the downturn illustrate the challenges covered in [[concepts/measurement-foundations]].
