# Central Bank

**Type:** Organization

The central bank is the primary monetary authority that influences the economy by setting policy interest rates, managing the money supply, and acting as a lender of last resort. In the *session-09_2025-01-04* roadmap, it is described as the “traffic controller” that adjusts rates and balance‑sheet tools to keep the macroeconomic system moving smoothly.

## Core Functions
- **Policy Rate Setting** – Determines the short‑term interest rate that guides borrowing costs across the economy. This links directly to the transmission mechanism discussed in [[concepts/policy-interaction]].
- **Money‑Supply Management** – Controls the quantity of base money, influencing inflation and liquidity.
- **Credibility & Expectations** – By communicating its goals, the central bank anchors inflation expectations, a key element of [[concepts/expectations-and-inflation]].
- **Financial Stability** – Provides liquidity during crises, a role highlighted in the discussion of [[concepts/financial-crises-mechanisms]].

## Relationship to Macro Learning Roadmap
The entity appears throughout the learning stages:
- In the **short‑run macro models** stage, the central bank’s actions shift the LM curve and affect the IS‑LM equilibrium (see [[concepts/short-run-macro-models]]).
- During the **open‑economy mechanics** stage, its policy influences exchange rates and capital flows (see [[concepts/open-economy-mechanics]]).
- It interacts with fiscal policy, shaping the overall [[concepts/policy-interaction]] landscape.
