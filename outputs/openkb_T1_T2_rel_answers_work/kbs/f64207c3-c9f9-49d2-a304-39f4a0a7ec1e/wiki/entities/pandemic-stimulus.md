# Pandemic Stimulus (Event)

**Overview**
- The *Pandemic Stimulus* refers to the unprecedented fiscal packages implemented by governments worldwide in response to the COVID‑19 pandemic beginning in 2020.  These measures included direct transfers to households, expanded unemployment benefits, payroll subsidies, and large‑scale public investment.

**Key Facts from the Session 09 (2025‑01‑04)**
- The session uses the pandemic stimulus as one of the concrete *real‑world events* (alongside the 2008 financial crisis and recent inflation) to illustrate how macroeconomic theory maps onto practice.  
- It is highlighted as a practical example when discussing the interaction of **fiscal policy** and **monetary policy**, showing how large government spending interacts with central‑bank actions.  
- The stimulus is positioned within the learning roadmap as a milestone for students to apply concepts from the [[concepts/macro-learning-roadmap]] and the [[concepts/policy-interaction]] modules.

**Relevance to Macro Concepts**
- Demonstrates the role of **fiscal policy** in stabilising output during a demand shock, a topic later covered under the *Fiscal Policy and Debt Sustainability* stage of the roadmap.
- Provides a backdrop for discussing **inflation expectations** and how large transfers can influence price dynamics, linking to [[concepts/expectations-and-inflation]].
- Serves as a case study for the **short‑run macro models** (IS‑LM, AD‑AS) when evaluating the immediate impact on aggregate demand, connecting to [[concepts/short-run-macro-models]].
- Highlights the coordination (or lack thereof) with the **central bank**, illustrating the importance of policy interaction; see [[entities/central-bank]].
- Offers a real‑world illustration of **open‑economy mechanics**, such as capital flow responses to large fiscal expansions, tying to [[concepts/open-economy-mechanics]].
