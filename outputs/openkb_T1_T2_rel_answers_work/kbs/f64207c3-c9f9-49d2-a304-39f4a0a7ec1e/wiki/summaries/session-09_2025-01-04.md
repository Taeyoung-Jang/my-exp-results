# Summary of session-09_2025-01-04

**Purpose** – Provide a structured, checklist‑style learning roadmap for macroeconomics that stays strictly within a user‑provided reading list. The guide outlines sequential study stages, mastery questions, common confusions, and criteria for “good‑enough” mastery without requiring extra material.

---

## Learning Stages & Core Concepts

| Stage | Core Concepts | Sample Mastery Questions | Common Confusions |
|-------|----------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| **GDP & Measurement** | [[concepts/measurement-foundations]] | • What does GDP include/exclude?<br>• Real vs. nominal GDP?<br>• Why do revisions happen? | Treating GDP as a welfare metric |
| **IS‑LM & AD‑AS** | [[concepts/short-run-macro-models]] | • Sketch how an interest‑rate change shifts the IS curve.<br>• Explain what moves the LM curve besides central‑bank actions.<br>• Show how AD‑AS links output with inflation in the short run.<br>• Contrast supply‑shock vs. demand‑shock effects. | Assuming the model is long‑run or “wants” to move on its own |
| **Inflation & Expectations** | [[concepts/expectations-and-inflation]] | • Differentiate cost‑push vs. demand‑pull inflation.<br>• Define “anchored expectations” and their stabilising role.<br>• Sketch how expectations feed into wage‑ and price‑setting.<br>• Explain why expectations can move inflation before actual conditions change. | Seeing expectations as pure forecasts; equating inflation solely with money growth |
| **Unemployment** | *unemployment* (plain text) | • Distinguish frictional, structural, and cyclical unemployment.<br>• Explain why unemployment exists even at “full employment.”<br>• Link unemployment movements to the business cycle.<br>• Describe how labor‑market tightness affects wages and inflation. | Believing the natural rate is a policy target |
| **Monetary Policy & Central Banks** | [[concepts/policy-interaction]] | • Outline the transmission mechanism from policy rate to spending.<br>• Contrast rate tools with balance‑sheet tools.<br>• Explain why credibility influences inflation expectations.<br>• Sketch how the central bank interacts with IS‑LM and AD‑AS frameworks. | Assuming central banks control all interest rates; treating QE as “printing money” |
| **Fiscal Policy & Debt Sustainability** | *fiscal policy* and *debt sustainability* (plain text) | • Identify when deficits are stabilising vs. destabilising.<br>• Describe conditions for sustainable debt.<br>• Explain fiscal‑monetary interaction.<br>• Draw the intuition behind the r > g debt‑dynamic rule. | Assuming high debt is automatically risky; focusing on headline deficits only |
| **Open‑Economy Macro** | [[concepts/open-economy-mechanics]] | • Explain short‑run determinants of exchange‑rate movements.<br>• Describe how interest‑rate differentials drive capital flows.<br>• Relate current‑account and capital‑account balances.<br>• Sketch how the open‑economy IS‑LM (Mundell‑Fleming) differs from the closed‑economy version. | Treating trade deficits as inherently bad; ignoring the role of the exchange‑rate regime |
| **Crises & Financial Frictions** | [[concepts/financial-crises-mechanisms]] | • Show how leverage amplifies shocks.<br>• Explain why solvent banks can still suffer runs.<br>• Distinguish credit cycles from business cycles.<br>• Sketch why standard policy tools weaken during a financial crisis. | Attributing crises to a single trigger; conflating liquidity with solvency problems |

---

## How to Use the Roadmap

1. **Read the designated material** for each stage from your curated list.  
2. **Answer the mastery questions** using only the definitions, diagrams, and examples provided in the texts.  
3. **Self‑check mastery:** you have “good enough” understanding when you can explain the concepts aloud for about a minute without looking back at the book and can sketch the key diagrams exactly as they appear.  
4. **Watch for the listed confusions**; if you notice you are slipping into them, revisit the relevant section.  
5. **Proceed to the next stage** only after meeting the mastery criteria, ensuring a solid foundation before adding complexity.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Treat macroeconomics as a narrative with households, firms, government, and central banks as the main characters.  
- Follow a **bottom‑up sequence**: measurement → short‑run models → expectations → policy → open economy → crises.  
- Use **checklists** to enforce active recall and prevent reliance on external sources.  
- Mastery is judged by **internal consistency** with the curated texts, not by breadth of outside references.
