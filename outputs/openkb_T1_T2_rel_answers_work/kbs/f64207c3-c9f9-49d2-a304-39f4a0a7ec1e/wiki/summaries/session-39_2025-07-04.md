# Summary of session‑39_2025-07-04

**Main topic**: Practical advice for a new paramedic student on how to prioritize early study material and organize packet resources.

## Key Points
- **EMT vs. Paramedic** – Highlighted the core [[concepts/differences-between-emt-and-paramedic]]: EMTs handle basic life support, while paramedics perform advanced interventions requiring longer, deeper training.
- **Early focus areas** – Emphasized building a solid foundation in **anatomy**, basic **physiology**, and **medical terminology** before diving into detailed **pharmacology** or **ECG** interpretation. (See [[entities/ecg]] for the device.)
- **Study prioritization** – Recommended a three‑step order:
  1. Core anatomy (cardiovascular, respiratory, neuro)  
  2. Overview of protocol structure (where to find sections)  
  3. Terminology and pathophysiology.  
  This aligns with the [[concepts/paramedic-training-priorities]] page.
- **Two‑week study plan** – Provided a day‑by‑day table (Day 1‑14) with 1‑hour anatomy blocks and 30‑minute protocol blocks, rotating systems and incorporating reviews. The plan follows the guidelines in [[concepts/study-plan-design]].
- **Tabbing scheme** – Suggested color‑coded tabs (Red = Cardiac, Blue = Respiratory, Green = Trauma, Yellow = Pediatrics, Orange = Assessment) and sticky‑flag markers for contraindications.
- **Quick‑index template** – A one‑page index layout to tape inside the front cover, listing tab locations and flag meanings for rapid navigation.
- **Study habits** – Stressed the importance of consistent study routines, calm‑under‑pressure practice, and verbal self‑explanation to reinforce learning.
