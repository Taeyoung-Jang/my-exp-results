# Expectations and Inflation

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** This concept explains the role of forward‑looking expectations in the formation of inflation, how they interact with monetary policy, and why anchoring expectations matters for macro stability.

---

## Core Idea
Inflation is not just a current rise in prices; it is a *behavioural* process that incorporates agents’ expectations about future price movements. When households and firms anticipate higher inflation, they adjust wage demands and price‑setting behaviour in ways that can bring about the very inflation they expect.

## Key Elements (derived from [[summaries/session-09_2025-01-04]])

1. **Cost‑push vs. Demand‑pull** – The source document distinguishes two basic sources of price pressure:
   - *Cost‑push*: Higher input costs (e.g., wages, oil) feed through to prices.
   - *Demand‑pull*: Strong aggregate demand pushes up prices.
   Both channels are amplified when agents *expect* inflation to continue.

2. **Anchored Expectations** – A central bank that convincingly commits to a low‑inflation target can keep expectations “anchored.”  Anchored expectations dampen the feedback loop: firms are less likely to pre‑emptively raise prices, and workers are less likely to demand higher wages.

3. **Expectation Feedback Loop** – The document’s mastery questions ask you to sketch how expectations feed into wage‑setting and price‑setting. In brief:
   - Expected inflation → higher wage demands → higher unit labor costs → price increases → realized inflation.
   - Expected inflation also influences price‑setting directly as firms add an expected markup to cover future cost rises.

4. **Policy Implications** – The central bank’s credibility (see [[entities/central-bank]]) is a crucial lever. Transparent communication, consistent policy actions, and a clear inflation target help shape expectations.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/policy-interaction]]** – The interaction between monetary policy and fiscal stance can alter the credibility of the inflation target, thereby affecting expectations.

- **[[concepts/open-economy-mechanics]]** – In an open economy, imported inflation and exchange‑rate expectations also feed into domestic price dynamics.
- **[[concepts/financial-crises-mechanisms]]** – During crises, expectation anchoring can break down, leading to rapid inflation or deflation spirals.
