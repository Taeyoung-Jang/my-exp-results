# Short‑Run Macro Models

**Definition**: A set of analytical frameworks that describe how an economy’s output, interest rates, and price level interact over short horizons, typically assuming some variables (e.g., technology, expectations) are fixed.

## Core Models Mentioned in the Source

- **IS‑LM** – Captures the equilibrium between the goods market (IS) and the money market (LM). It shows how changes in fiscal policy, monetary policy, and exogenous shocks move interest rates and output.
- **AD‑AS (Aggregate‑Demand / Aggregate‑Supply)** – Links the short‑run aggregate‑demand curve to a short‑run aggregate‑supply curve, allowing us to see the joint determination of output and inflation.

Both models are highlighted in the **[[summaries/session-09_2025-01-04]]** checklist as essential stepping stones before tackling expectations, policy, and open‑economy extensions.

## How the Models Fit Within the Overall Learning Roadmap

| Learning Stage | Role of Short‑Run Models |
|----------------|--------------------------|
| [[concepts/measurement-foundations]] | Provide the baseline variables (real GDP, price level) that the IS‑LM and AD‑AS frameworks manipulate.
| [[concepts/expectations-and-inflation]] | AD‑AS incorporates the short‑run Phillips‑curve intuition; expectations shape how price‑setting behavior feeds back into the aggregate‑supply curve.
| [[concepts/policy-interaction]] | IS‑LM illustrates the transmission of monetary policy; AD‑AS shows how fiscal shocks shift aggregate demand.
| [[concepts/open-economy-mechanics]] | The closed‑economy short‑run models are extended in the Mundell‑Fleming framework for open‑economy analysis.
| [[concepts/macro-learning-roadmap]] | Short‑run models are the second major block in the roadmap, bridging measurement basics and more advanced topics like financial‑crisis mechanisms.
| [[concepts/financial-crises-mechanisms]] | Understanding the baseline short‑run dynamics is prerequisite to recognizing how financial frictions can disrupt the IS‑LM/AD‑AS equilibrium during crises.

## Key Take‑aways for Mastery

1. **Identify the axes and shifts** – Be able to sketch the IS, LM, AD, and SR‑AS curves exactly as presented in your textbook.
2. **Explain shock transmission** – Articulate how a fiscal expansion, a change in the policy rate, or a supply shock moves each curve and what the new equilibrium implies for output and inflation.
3. **Connect to expectations** – Recognize that the short‑run aggregate‑supply curve depends on the expectations framework discussed elsewhere.
4. **Know the limits** – These models assume fixed price expectations, ignore financial frictions, and are strictly short‑run; they are not meant for long‑run growth analysis.
