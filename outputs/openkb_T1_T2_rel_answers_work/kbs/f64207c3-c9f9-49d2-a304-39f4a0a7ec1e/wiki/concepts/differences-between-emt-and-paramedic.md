# Differences Between EMT and Paramedic

**Definition**: The comparison of the responsibilities, training depth, and permitted medical interventions for Emergency Medical Technicians (EMTs) versus Paramedics.

---

## Scope of Practice
- **EMT**
  - Provides basic life support (BLS).
  - Core tasks: patient assessment, CPR, AED use, oxygen delivery, splinting, bleeding control, and limited medication administration.
  - Operates mainly as a stabilizer and transport facilitator.
- **Paramedic**
  - Provides advanced life support (ALS).
  - Can start IVs, administer a wider range of medications, interpret ECGs, manage airways with advanced equipment, and make complex clinical decisions.
  - Performs higher‑level interventions before a patient reaches the hospital.

---

## Training Length & Content
- **EMT**
  - Shorter certification program (typically 150‑250 hours).
  - Focuses on foundational anatomy/physiology, basic pharmacology, and essential BLS skills.
- **Paramedic**
  - Longer, more intensive program (often 1,200‑1,500+ hours) including anatomy, pharmacology, advanced assessment, clinical rotations, and specialized coursework.

---

## Decision‑Making & Responsibility
- EMTs make rapid assessments and ensure safe transport; they have limited authority to administer drugs or perform invasive procedures.
- Paramedics are expected to conduct detailed assessments, interpret diagnostic data (e.g., [[entities/ecg]]), and initiate advanced interventions, bearing greater medical liability.

---

## Relevance to Learning Path
The distinction shapes study priorities for aspiring paramedics. Early focus on **core anatomy** and **medical terminology** builds the foundation needed for the advanced curriculum described in [[concepts/paramedic-training-priorities]].
