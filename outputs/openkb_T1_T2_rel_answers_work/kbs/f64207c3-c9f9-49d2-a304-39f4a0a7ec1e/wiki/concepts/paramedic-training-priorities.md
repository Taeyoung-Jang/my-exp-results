# Paramedic Training Priorities

**Definition**: The set of foundational knowledge and study habits that a paramedic trainee should prioritize in the first weeks of a program to build a strong base for advanced clinical work.

## Core Priorities
| Priority | Why it matters | Typical early activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Anatomy & Physiology** | All later skills (assessment, interventions, medication effects) depend on an accurate mental model of the body. | Review cardiovascular, respiratory, and nervous system structures; sketch diagrams; label key vessels and nerves.
| **Protocol Structure Familiarity** | Knowing where information lives in the packet saves critical time during labs and clinicals. | Flip through the whole protocol packet, note section headings, and place color‑coded tabs.
| **Medical Terminology & Pathophysiology** | Understanding disease language makes protocol reading faster and reduces misinterpretation. | Read glossaries, create flashcards for common terms, and outline “why” a condition occurs.
| **Study Habits & Stress Management** | Consistent, focused study and the ability to stay calm under pressure are essential for high‑stakes decision making. | Set daily time blocks (e.g., 1 hr anatomy, 30 min protocol), practice verbal self‑explanations, and use brief breathing exercises before sessions.
| **Basic Pharmacology Concepts** | Early awareness of drug categories helps later medication dosing and contraindication checks. | Learn broad drug classes (e.g., analgesics, vasopressors) and their primary mechanisms.
| **ECG Fundamentals** | Foundational electrical concepts ease later rhythm interpretation. | Study normal cardiac electrical flow and identify a basic sinus rhythm.

## Recommended Study Design
The session‑39 conversation suggests a **two‑week, day‑by‑day plan** that alternates anatomy focus with light protocol review, using a color‑coded tabbing system. This aligns with the broader [[concepts/study-plan-design]] framework of breaking complex material into manageable, repetitive cycles.
