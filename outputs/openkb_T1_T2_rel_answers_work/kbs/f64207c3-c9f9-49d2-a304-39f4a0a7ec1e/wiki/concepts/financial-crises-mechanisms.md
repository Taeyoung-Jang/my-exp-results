# Financial Crises Mechanisms

**Definition**: The set of channels—leverage amplification, bank runs, credit‑cycle dynamics, and weakened policy transmission—that turn ordinary shocks into severe macroeconomic downturns.

---

## Core Mechanisms

1. **Leverage Amplification**
   - High debt levels increase the sensitivity of firms and households to changes in asset prices or income. A modest decline in asset values forces balance‑sheet adjustments, which in turn depress spending and further lower prices, creating a feedback loop.

2. **Bank Runs and Liquidity Spirals**
   - Even solvent banks can face runs when depositors doubt their ability to meet withdrawals. The panic can spread rapidly, turning a liquidity shortage into a solvency crisis.

3. **Credit‑Cycle vs. Business‑Cycle**
   - Credit cycles track the expansion and contraction of borrowing capacity. Unlike the smoother business cycle, credit cycles can generate abrupt spikes in defaults and sharp drops in lending, magnifying downturns.

4. **Policy Tool Weakening**
   - During a crisis, traditional monetary tools (e.g., policy‑rate cuts) lose effectiveness because financial institutions are unwilling or unable to transmit lower rates to borrowers. Fiscal space may also be constrained, limiting stimulus.

## Real‑World Illustrations

- The **2008 financial crisis** ([[entities/2008-financial-crisis]]) demonstrated the interaction of excessive leverage, the failure of major banks, and the limits of monetary policy.
- The **pandemic stimulus** period showed how massive fiscal outlays and unconventional monetary actions attempted to offset weakened transmission mechanisms.

---

## Takeaways

- Financial crises are not isolated events; they arise from the convergence of balance‑sheet vulnerabilities, liquidity concerns, and constrained policy transmission.
- Recognising these mechanisms allows economists to anticipate when ordinary shocks might spiral into systemic downturns and to design policies that address the specific channels of amplification.
