# Open‑Economy Mechanics

**Definition** – The study of how exchange rates, capital flows, and the balance of payments connect a country's internal macroeconomic model to the rest of the world.

---

## Core Elements (as outlined in [[summaries/session-09_2025-01-04]])

| Element | What it does | Typical questions for mastery |
|---|---|---|
| **Exchange rates** | Price of the domestic currency relative to foreign currencies; determines the cost of imports and the competitiveness of exports. | How does a change in the policy rate affect the nominal exchange rate? What forces drive a currency’s appreciation vs. depreciation? |
| **Capital flows** | Movement of financial capital across borders in response to interest‑rate differentials, risk premia, and expectations. | How do foreign‑direct investment and portfolio flows respond to a shift in the domestic interest rate? |
| **Balance of payments** | Accounting record that links the current account (trade, services, income) with the capital/financial account; ensures that all cross‑border transactions balance. | How does a persistent current‑account deficit affect the exchange rate under different exchange‑rate regimes? |

---

## Relationship to Other Macro Concepts

- The open‑economy framework extends the **[[concepts/short-run-macro-models]]** (IS‑LM, AD‑AS) by adding a third market – the foreign‑exchange market – as in the Mundell‑Fleming model.
- It interacts with **[[concepts/policy-interaction]]**: monetary policy influences exchange rates through interest‑rate channels, while fiscal policy can affect the current account.
- Understanding measurement is essential; the **[[concepts/measurement-foundations]]** guide how to calculate trade balances, net foreign assets, and real effective exchange rates.
- Expectations play a role: although not directly linked to the whitelist, the concept of anchored expectations is covered in the broader learning roadmap.
- The overall learning sequence is framed by **[[concepts/macro-learning-roadmap]]**, where open‑economy mechanics form the penultimate stage before studying **[[concepts/financial-crises-mechanisms]]**.

## Checklist for Mastery (derived from the session‑09 roadmap)

1. **Explain** how exchange rates are determined in the short run (interest‑rate parity, expectations).  
2. **Describe** the channels through which capital flows respond to policy changes.  
3. **Illustrate** the balance‑of‑payments identity and how current‑account and capital‑account movements offset each other.  
4. **Connect** the open‑economy extensions to the closed‑economy **[[concepts/short-run-macro-models]]** you have already mastered.  
5. **Identify** common confusions (e.g., treating a trade deficit as inherently negative, assuming capital inflows always signal strength).

## When You’ve Reached “Good‑Enough” Mastery

- You can **verbally walk through** a scenario such as “a central bank raises rates, what happens to the exchange rate, capital flows, and the current account?” without consulting the text.
- You can **sketch** the Mundell‑Fleming diagram (IS‑LM with the BP curve) exactly as presented in your reading.
- You can **answer** the mastery questions above using only the terminology and diagrams from the assigned macro texts.
