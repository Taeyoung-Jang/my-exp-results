# Summary of Session 15 (2023‑06‑15)

**Purpose**: Provide a concrete, repeatable process for deciding whether to stay with a legacy software platform or migrate to an alternative, focusing on export options, conversion tools, and long‑term archival considerations.

---

## Key Concepts
- **Export Options** – native ways to save projects in open or neutral formats.  
- **Conversion Tools** – third‑party utilities or plugins that translate proprietary files.  
- **Archival Strategy** – preserving projects for future access even if they remain on the old platform.  
- **Pilot Testing** – a small‑scale, weekend‑long trial using representative projects of varying complexity. [[concepts/pilot-testing]]
- **Scoring Rubric** – a 0‑2 per‑pillar metric (editability, metadata, output fidelity) that yields a 0‑6 project score. [[concepts/scoring-rubric]]
- **Hybrid Strategy** – keeping the legacy tool for difficult projects while migrating the rest.  
- **Decision Rule** – quantitative thresholds (e.g., ≥60 % of projects scoring ≥5) to declare a migration path viable.  

---

## Checklist for a Weekend Pilot
1. **Select Representative Projects** – simple, typical, complex (3‑4 total).  
2. **Document Export/Conversion Methods** – list tool version, settings, and output locations.  
3. **Run Exports & Conversions** – keep each result in a clearly labeled folder.  
4. **Log Findings** – use the template below for each project.  
5. **Re‑open in Target Software** – note success, breakage, and required fixes.  
6. **Compare Metadata & Final Outputs** – verify completeness and visual fidelity.  
7. **Time & Score** – record minutes spent and apply the scoring rubric.  
8. **Summarize Across Methods** – fill the summary sheet and apply the decision rule.

---

## Pilot Migration Plan
- **Day 1**: Gather projects, run all export options, save results.  
- **Day 2**: Import/convert into the new platform, perform the log template, and note any manual cleanup.  
- **Day 3**: Compile scores, calculate averages, and decide on migration, hybrid, or stay‑put.

## Scoring Rubric [[concepts/scoring-rubric]]
| Pillar | 2 (Full) | 1 (Partial) | 0 (Fail) |
|--------|----------|-------------|----------|
| Reopen/Editability | Opens cleanly, full structure | Opens with fixable breakage | Cannot open or unusable |
| Metadata Preservation | All critical fields intact | Some non‑critical loss | Critical metadata missing |
| Final Output Fidelity | Identical or trivially different | Small acceptable differences | Noticeable quality loss |

**Total score** = sum of three pillars (0‑6).

Interpretation:  
- **5‑6** – reliable migration path.  
- **3‑4** – borderline; may need cleanup automation.  
- **0‑2** – not viable without major tooling.

## Decision Rule
- **Viable Full Migration**: ≥ 60 % of tested projects score **5 or higher** *and* no project scores below **3**.  
- **Hybrid**: Mixed scores (some 5‑6, some 1‑2) – migrate only the high‑scoring projects and archive the rest.  
- **No Migration**: Majority score ≤ 2 or cleanup time exceeds a reasonable threshold (e.g., > 30 min per project).

---

## Minimum Number of Pilot Projects
- **Three** – bare minimum (simple, typical, complex).  
- **Four** – recommended if project types vary widely.  
- More than four yields diminishing returns for most teams.

---

## Hybrid Strategy Overview
1. **Retain Legacy Software** for projects that fail the rubric.  
2. **Export & Archive** those projects in the most stable neutral format.  
3. **Migrate** only the projects that scored 5‑6, using the documented workflow.  
4. Periodically revisit archived projects if future tooling improves.
