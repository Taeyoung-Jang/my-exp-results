## Gluten‑Free Meal Planning

Gluten‑free meal planning is a systematic approach to creating weekly menus that avoid wheat, barley, rye, and their derivatives while ensuring nutritional balance, variety, and ease of preparation. The concept emphasizes a **small core set of versatile ingredients**, batch cooking, and vigilant label checking to minimize hidden gluten sources.

### Core Staples
- Grain base: rice (or other gluten‑free grains)
- Pasta: certified gluten‑free
- Proteins: chicken thighs, ground turkey or beef, eggs
- Canned goods: beans, diced tomatoes
- Broth: gluten‑free broth (e.g., [[entities/pacific-foods]])
- Vegetables: onions, garlic, carrots, leafy greens, frozen mixed vegetables, potatoes
- Condiments: olive oil, gluten‑free tamari or coconut aminos, a trusted gluten‑free spice blend (e.g., from [[entities/simply-organic]] or [[entities/primal-palate]])
- Carbohydrate wraps: corn or cassava tortillas

These 15–20 items are sufficient to compose **seven different dinner dishes** for two adults while leaving leftovers for lunches or a bonus meal.

### Batch‑Cooking Workflow
The plan uses a **two‑session preparation model** (see [[concepts/batch-cooking-workflow]]):
1. **Sunday bulk cook** – cook rice, roast chicken, brown ground meat, simmer a tomato‑bean‑vegetable soup, and pre‑cook gluten‑free pasta. All components are portioned into containers for the week.
2. **Mid‑week top‑up** – a short 20‑30‑minute session to refresh rice, add extra chicken, or chop fresh vegetables as needed.

This workflow reduces daily cooking time, maintains food safety, and allows quick assembly of meals such as stir‑fry, tacos, fried rice, and omelets.

### Label Red Flags
When shopping for gluten‑free ingredients, watch for common hidden sources of gluten (see [[concepts/label-red-flags]]):
- Terms like **wheat**, **barley**, **malt**, **malt extract**, **yeast extract**, and **hydrolyzed wheat protein**.
- **Modified food starch** without a gluten‑free label.
- Vague ingredients such as **natural flavors** or **seasoning blends** that lack certification.
- Soy sauce (contains wheat) – swap with tamari labeled gluten‑free or coconut aminos.

### Safe Swaps
- **Soy sauce → tamari** (gluten‑free) or **coconut aminos**.
- **Broths → products explicitly marked gluten‑free**, such as those from [[entities/pacific-foods]].
- **Spice blends → certified gluten‑free** single spices or blends from [[entities/simply-organic]] or [[entities/primal-palate]].
- **Processed meats → packages that display a clear “gluten‑free” statement and omit barley malt, wheat starch, or unspecified natural flavors**.

### Practical Outcome
Following this concept enables a **consistent, low‑stress dinner rotation** that meets gluten‑free dietary requirements, minimizes waste, and simplifies grocery shopping. The detailed implementation, timeline, and ingredient list are documented in [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]].
