# Emotional Intelligence

**Definition**

Emotional Intelligence (EI) is the capacity to perceive, interpret, and regulate emotions in oneself and in others, enabling effective interpersonal interaction and decision‑making.

**Relevance to boundary communication**

In the context of [[concepts/boundary_communication]], EI helps a person convey limits with empathy, maintain rapport, and avoid conflict. By reading cues and responding calmly, a speaker can steer conversations away from sensitive topics without sounding dismissive.

**Connection to workplace confidentiality**

Applying EI supports respect for [[concepts/workplace_confidentiality]] by recognizing when information is too personal or HR‑related and choosing language that protects privacy while still offering support.

**Insights from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-10]]**

The session‑13 guidance provides multiple phrasing options (casual, warm‑but‑firm, formal) that illustrate EI in practice: each line balances clarity with a supportive tone. For example, the warm‑but‑firm phrase "I want to support you, but I shouldn’t hear personal details about others" shows self‑awareness and concern for the listener’s feelings.

**Key takeaways**
- Use self‑awareness to notice when a conversation enters a confidential area.
- Apply social awareness to gauge the speaker’s emotional state.
- Choose language that sets a boundary while preserving trust.
- Practice brief, empathetic statements that align with professional norms.
