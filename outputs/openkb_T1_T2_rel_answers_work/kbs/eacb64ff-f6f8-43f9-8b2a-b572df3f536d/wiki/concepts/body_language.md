# Body Language

**Definition**: Body language is the collection of non‑verbal signals — eye contact, facial expressions, posture, leaning, and vocal tone — that convey attitudes, intentions, and emotional states in interpersonal interactions.

---

## Key Elements Highlighted in [[summaries/session-32_2024-12-11]]

- **Eye Contact**: Maintaining eye contact for too long can feel intense and may be interpreted as overly probing. Moderate, relaxed eye contact signals openness.
- **Leaning**: Leaning in too closely can be perceived as invasive, especially when discussing surface‑level topics.
- **Vocal Tone**: A heavy or emotionally weighted tone can add unintended depth to neutral questions, making them appear more personal.
- **Posture**: An open, slightly angled posture invites conversation, while a closed or overly rigid stance may reinforce a closed‑off [[concepts/group_vibe]].

These cues were identified as subtle signals that can unintentionally communicate “too personal” even when the spoken words are benign.

## Practical Tips for Managing Body Language

1. **Eye Contact**: Aim for brief, regular eye contact (about 3‑5 seconds per glance) and look away naturally.
2. **Leaning**: Keep a comfortable distance; a slight forward lean shows interest, but avoid excessive proximity.
3. **Tone**: Use a relaxed, conversational tone; avoid dramatically lowering or raising your voice when asking casual questions.
4. **Posture**: Adopt an open stance—shoulders relaxed, torso slightly turned toward the speaker.
5. **Observe Feedback**: Watch how others mirror your cues; reciprocal openness indicates a welcoming vibe.

---

## When Body Language Signals a Closed‑Off Group

- Limited eye contact and minimal nodding.
- People forming tight, inward‑facing clusters.
- Frequent glances toward exits or phones.
- Closed‑off posture (arms crossed, bodies angled away).

In such contexts, adjusting your own non‑verbal signals can help bridge the gap, but it may also be necessary to respect the group’s natural boundaries.
