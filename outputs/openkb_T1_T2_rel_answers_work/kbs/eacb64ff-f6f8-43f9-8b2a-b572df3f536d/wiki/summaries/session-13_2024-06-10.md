# Summary of session‑13_2024‑06‑10

**Document type:** Conversational guidance  
**Date:** 2024‑06‑10  

## Context  
The user seeks practical language for setting personal boundaries at work when colleagues (including peers, managers, or seniors) share confidential or sensitive information they shouldn’t be hearing. They request short, in‑the‑moment phrases in different tones, group‑chat announcements, 1:1 templates, and upward‑compatible lines.

## In‑the‑moment boundary lines  

### Super‑casual (≈5‑7 words each)  
- “I’m gonna tap out of this part, thanks.”  
- “Probably shouldn’t know the details—let’s keep it high‑level.”  
- “That sounds a bit too deep for me, let’s zoom out.”  
- “I’m trying to keep my brain out of specifics.”  
- “I don’t want to step into anything that’s not my business.”  

### Warm‑but‑firm  
- “I want to support you, but I shouldn’t hear personal details about others.”  
- “I care about you; let’s stay away from private info.”  
- “I hear you, but let’s avoid someone else’s private info.”  
- “I respect everyone’s boundaries, so let’s keep this broader.”  
- “I’m here for you, just not the specifics about other people.”  

### Formal  
- “To stay in an appropriate lane, I’d prefer not to hear details about someone else’s situation.”  
- “I can offer general support, but I can’t discuss others’ performance or personal matters.”  
- “I need to avoid sensitive information that isn’t intended for me; let’s keep it general.”  
- “I’d like to step back from this part to maintain proper boundaries.”  
- “I’m committed to respecting confidentiality, so I can’t take in those specifics.”  

## HR / performance‑specific lines (no threat tone)  
- “I should probably stay clear of anything HR‑related so I don’t hold info I shouldn’t have, but I can talk about how you feel.”  
- “Performance or HR topics get tricky for me; let’s keep it big‑picture rather than individual.”  

## Group‑chat boundary announcements  

| Length | Draft |
|--------|-------|
| **Very short** | “Quick heads‑up: I’m steering clear of personal or performance details, so if I redirect, that’s why. All good vibes.” |
| **Medium** | “Hey all, just sharing a small boundary I’m setting for myself. I work best when I’m not pulled into sensitive details about folks who aren’t present—personal stuff, performance, HR‑ish conversations, etc. If I guide the chat away, it’s nothing personal, just keeping things clean on my end.” |
| **More explicit** | “Hi team, I wanted to mention something to keep things comfortable for me. I appreciate the confidence you place in me, but sometimes conversations drift into details I probably shouldn’t hear—like performance or personal issues. I’ll start gently steering away from those topics. This is a personal boundary, not a critique of anyone. Thanks for understanding.” |

### Revised upbeat version (still explicit)  
> “Hey team, quick note from me. I’ve noticed I work best when I’m not pulled into the more sensitive details about people’s personal or performance situations. Just so things stay easy for everyone, I might gently steer conversations away from that stuff when it pops up. No big deal—just a small boundary on my end so we can keep everything positive and comfortable.”  

## 1:1 overshare template (kind but direct)  
> “Hey, can I share something quickly? I really value our chats, but sometimes the details you share about other people feel a bit heavier than I should be hearing. I’m trying to stay in the right lane, so if I shift the topic, it’s just me keeping a boundary—not anything about you. Thanks for understanding.”  

## Upward‑friendly lines (for manager or senior)  
- “I might not be the right person for the finer details, but I’m here for the bigger picture.”  
- “I want to make sure I stay in the right lane, so I may step back from specifics, though I’m listening.”  
- “Got it. I’ll let you steer this, but I’ll stay out of the sensitive parts so I don’t hold anything I shouldn’t.”  
- “I’m happy to help however I can—just flagging that I might not need the deeper details to be useful.”  
- “Totally hear you. I’ll keep my end general so I don’t accidentally take in more than I should.”  

## Follow‑up 1:1 after repeated upward oversharing  
> “Hey, I wanted to check in about something small. Occasionally our conversations dip into sensitive details about people or situations, and I’m trying to be careful about not holding information that isn’t meant for me. I always want to be supportive, so if I steer us back to the broader picture now and then, that’s why. Just wanted to share that so it doesn’t seem abrupt. I really appreciate your understanding.”
