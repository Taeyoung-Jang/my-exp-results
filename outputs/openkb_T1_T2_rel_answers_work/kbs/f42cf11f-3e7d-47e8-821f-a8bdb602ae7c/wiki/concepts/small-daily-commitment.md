# Small Daily Commitment

**Definition:** A reading habit strategy that sets a minimal, consistent daily target (e.g., one chapter or ~10 minutes) to lower effort barriers and generate steady progress.

**Why it works:** Small goals feel manageable, reduce procrastination, and create a feedback loop of accomplishment that fuels future sessions.

**Key points from [[summaries/session-06_2025-07-17]]:**
- Target size: one chapter or ~10 minutes.
- Described as “tiny bits usually build momentum on their own.”
- Serves as a pressure‑relief mechanism when combined with [[concepts/main-book-side-reads]].

**Relationship to other concepts:**
- Works best when paired with [[concepts/curiosity-driven-selection]]—curiosity supplies the motivation, while the small commitment keeps the action easy.
- Supports the *main book + side reads* framework by ensuring the main book is approached in bite‑sized sessions, preventing it from feeling like a chore.

**Practical tips:**
1. Choose a concrete metric (pages, chapters, minutes) before you start.
2. Set a timer or use a bookmark to signal the end of the session.
3. Celebrate completion of the micro‑goal to reinforce the habit.
4. If resistance appears, switch to a side read (see [[concepts/main-book-side-reads]]) rather than abandoning the habit.

**Potential pitfalls:** Setting the commitment too large defeats the purpose; aim for a duration that feels “easy‑but‑meaningful.”
