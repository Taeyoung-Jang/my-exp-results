# Curiosity‑Driven Selection

**Definition**: A reading‑choice strategy that prioritises the book a person feels most curious about *right now* instead of searching for the "perfect" next title.

---

## Why it works
- **Longer motivation half‑life**: Curiosity tends to sustain interest longer than the short‑term pressure of finding the "right" book.
- **Reduces decision fatigue**: By removing the need for extensive comparison, the choice becomes quick and low‑stress.
- **Facilitates small daily commitments**: When the selected book is intrinsically interesting, committing to a single chapter or ten minutes feels natural. ([[concepts/small-daily-commitment]])

---

## Practical steps (from [[summaries/session-06_2025-07-17]])
1. **Ask yourself what you’re most curious about today** – let that feeling guide the pick.
2. **Set a tiny reading goal** – one chapter or ~10 minutes.
3. **Allow guilt‑free quitting** – if curiosity fades and the book feels like a chore, use the "annoyed vs relieved" test to decide whether to move on.

---

## Relationship to other concepts
- **Main book + side reads**: Curiosity‑driven selection usually determines the *main* book, while side reads can satisfy secondary curiosities without disrupting the primary focus. ([[concepts/main-book-side-reads]])
- **Small daily commitment**: Selecting by curiosity pairs well with committing to small reading blocks, reinforcing habit formation. ([[concepts/small-daily-commitment]])

---

## Benefits
- Keeps reading enjoyable rather than feeling like a chore.
- Builds momentum through consistent, interest‑driven micro‑sessions.
- Provides a clear mental cue for when to switch to a side read or quit.
