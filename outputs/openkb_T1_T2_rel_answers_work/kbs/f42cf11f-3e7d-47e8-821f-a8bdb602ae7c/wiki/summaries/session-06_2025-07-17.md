# Summary of session-06_2025-07-17

- **Curiosity‑driven selection** – Choose the book you’re most curious about *today* rather than trying to find the “perfect” next read. This lowers pressure and leverages a longer‑lasting motivation. [[concepts/curiosity-driven-selection]]

- **Small daily commitment** – Aim for a very modest target (e.g., one chapter or ~10 minutes). Tiny doses build momentum without feeling like work. [[concepts/small-daily-commitment]]

- **When it’s okay to quit** – Apply the *annoyed vs. relieved* test: imagine the book being taken away; if you’d feel relieved, it’s fine to stop. This helps distinguish a temporary lull from a lack of interest.

- **Main book + side reads** – Designate one “main” book as the default. Keep side reads (up to two) as optional palate cleansers for moments of resistance. This reduces decision fatigue and keeps the primary focus clear. [[concepts/main-book-side-reads]]

- **Implementation rhythm** – No rigid schedule is needed. When you sit down and feel ready, read the main book. If you notice internal resistance (mind drifting, dread), switch to a side read briefly, then return.

- **Side‑read limits** – One side read is ideal; two is manageable. More than two tends to dilute attention and undermine the main book’s momentum.

- **Overall benefit** – The combination of curiosity‑based choice, tiny commitments, a clear quit test, and a primary‑plus‑side‑read structure makes reading enjoyable, sustainable, and less like a chore. See the full summary page: [[summaries/session-06_2025-07-17]]
