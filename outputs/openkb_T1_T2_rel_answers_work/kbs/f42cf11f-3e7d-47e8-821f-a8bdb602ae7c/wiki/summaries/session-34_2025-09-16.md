# Summary of session-34_2025-09-16

**Main focus**: Practical space‑saving furniture ideas and concrete layout options for a small 10 × 11 ft room that includes a window, a closet door on the same wall, and an entry door on the adjacent wall. The user has installed a wall‑mounted Murphy bed, so the assistant provides designs that keep a clear path to the closet and a functional desk area, plus sanity‑checks for required clearances.

## Key Concepts
- **Space‑saving furniture** – wall‑mounted fold‑down desks, Murphy beds, beds with built‑in drawers, slim vertical storage units. [[concepts/space-saving-furniture]]
- **Layout planning** – anchoring major pieces to perimeter walls, keeping circulation paths clear, using floating or diagonal desk placements. [[concepts/layout-planning]]
- **Murphy bed** – a fold‑down bed that disappears when not in use, allowing the room to serve as a workspace most of the day. [[concepts/murphy-bed]]
- **Clearance guidelines** – minimum open floor in front of a lowered Murphy bed (≈ 7 ft) and required walkway width between entry and closet (≈ 36 in, minimum 30 in). *(plain text)*
- **Walkway width** – recommended 3 ft (≈ 36 in) for comfortable movement; narrower paths feel cramped. *(plain text)*

## Findings & Recommendations
1. **General space‑saving tips**
   - Use a wall‑mounted fold‑down desk that tucks away when not needed.
   - Choose a bed with built‑in drawers or a lofted platform to free floor space.
   - Keep a single tall, narrow storage unit to reduce visual clutter.
   - Sketch a floor plan first; align major pieces along the same wall when possible.

2. **Three concrete layout options with a Murphy bed**
   - **Option A**: Murphy bed on the wall opposite the window, folds down toward the entry door. Desk sits under the window, shallow enough for the closet door to swing fully. Walkway from entry to closet remains clear.
   - **Option B**: Murphy bed mounted on the wall that shares the entry door. When up, the wall is a clean vertical surface; desk runs along the opposite wall, keeping the closet side free and providing a direct path between doors.
   - **Option C**: Murphy bed placed on the window‑adjacent portion of the window‑and‑closet wall, leaving clearance for the bed to fold down without blocking the closet. Desk positioned in the diagonal corner opposite the closet, creating a cozy work zone while preserving an uninterrupted path.

3. **Clearance sanity‑check**
   - **Murphy bed clearance**: Aim for ~7 ft of open floor from the mounting wall to the opposite side to allow full deployment without hitting furniture.
   - **Walkway width**: Target 36 in (3 ft) for a natural feel; 30 in is the absolute minimum if the path stays free of obstacles.
   - Ensure the desk depth and chair footprint stay within the walkway limits, especially when the bed is down.

## Practical Takeaways
- Prioritize a **clear 3‑ft corridor** from the entry door to the closet; all furniture (desk, chair, bed foot) must stay outside this zone.
- When the Murphy bed is up, the room becomes an open **workspace zone**; when down, maintain at least **7 ft of clearance** in front of the bed for safe lowering and movement.
- Use **vertical storage** and **multi‑function pieces** to keep floor area available for both sleeping and working.
