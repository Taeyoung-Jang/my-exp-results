# Summary of session-34_2025-01-15

**Primary focus**: Practical steps for professionals in high‑stakes jobs to assess, monitor, and maintain sleep quality, including when to self‑manage versus seek medical help, and concrete CPAP monitoring rules.

## Key Concepts
- [[concepts/sleep-diary]]: Use a short sleep diary to capture bedtime, wake time, awakenings, and subjective restfulness.  
- [[concepts/sleep-hygiene]]: Consistent schedule, wind‑down routine, limited caffeine/screen use.  
- Professional evaluation: Seek a specialist if sleep problems persist despite habit changes or if symptoms like loud snoring, apneas, or excessive daytime sleepiness appear.  
- Sleep stability log: Simple daily/weekly table to record sleep metrics, daytime alertness, and any unusual factors.  
- Trigger thresholds: Tiered response model (self‑correction, non‑urgent check‑in, urgent evaluation) based on duration and frequency of deviations.  
- [[concepts/cpap-metrics]]: Usage hours, Apnea‑Hypopnea Index (AHI), and leak rates guide when to adjust mask fit/humidity versus contacting a sleep clinic.

## Practical Recommendations
1. **Initial self‑assessment**  
   - Track sleep patterns for 1–2 weeks using the diary.  
   - Implement basic [[concepts/sleep-hygiene]] tweaks.  
2. **When to involve a clinician**  
   - Persistent issues > 7 days or unexplained patterns ≥ 3 times/week.  
   - Return of original treated symptoms (e.g., apneas, loud snoring).  
3. **Urgent action**  
   - Sudden, severe daytime sleepiness compromising safety.  
   - Any medical red‑flag (e.g., acute breathing pauses).

## CPAP “If X Then Y” Rules
| Metric | Trigger (If X) | First‑line self‑fix | When to call clinic (Then Y) |
|--------|----------------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| **Usage hours** | < recommended hrs for ≥ 2‑3 nights in a row | Check mask seal, adjust headgear, raise humidity, address congestion | Use remains low for a full week despite fixes |
| **AHI** | Elevated above clearance level for ≥ 2 nights | Tighten mask fit, clear nasal passages, try different cushion, adjust humidity | Elevated for 5‑7 consecutive nights after fixes |
| **Leak** | Persistent high leak spikes for several days | Re‑position mask, replace cushion, clean seal, ensure proper headgear tension | Leak stays high despite adjustments, causing fragmented therapy |

## Minimal Documentation Approach
- Record **only** total CPAP hours, a brief “normal/off” note for AHI and leak, and any adjustment made.  
- End‑of‑week one‑sentence summary (e.g., “Metrics stable; no deviations.”) is sufficient for follow‑up with the sleep clinician.

## Take‑away
Maintain a lightweight yet systematic log, apply tiered response thresholds, and follow clear CPAP metrics rules to preserve sleep health and certification eligibility without excessive paperwork.
