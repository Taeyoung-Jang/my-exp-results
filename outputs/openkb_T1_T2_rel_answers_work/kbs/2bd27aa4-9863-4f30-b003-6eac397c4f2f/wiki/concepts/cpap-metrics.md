# CPAP Metrics

**Definition**: The objective parameters tracked from a CPAP device—primarily nightly usage hours, Apnea‑Hypopnea Index (AHI), and leak rate—that indicate therapy effectiveness and signal when to adjust habits or seek professional help.

---

## Core Metrics

| Metric | What it measures | Typical therapeutic target | When to act |
|--------|------------------|----------------------------|------------|
| **Usage hours** | Total time the device is worn each night. | ≥ 4‑5 hrs/night (as prescribed by the clinician). | If usage falls below the prescribed minimum for **more than 2‑3 consecutive nights**, first try self‑fixes (mask fit, humidity, congestion). If low usage persists for a full week, contact the sleep clinic.
| **Apnea‑Hypopnea Index (AHI)** | Number of apnea or hypopnea events per hour of sleep. | Below the clearance threshold established by the specialist (often < 5 events/hr). | A single night of a higher AHI is usually tolerable. If AHI remains elevated for **5‑7 consecutive nights** despite mask‑fit adjustments, seek clinician review.
| **Leak rate** | Volume of air escaping the mask seal, expressed as L/min. | Typically < 24 L/min (device‑specific). | Occasional spikes are fine. Persistent high leak for **several days** after re‑seating the mask or swapping cushions warrants a clinic call.

---

## Immediate Self‑Care Steps (First‑Line Fixes)
1. **Mask fit** – Re‑position, tighten straps, replace worn cushions.
2. **Humidity** – Increase humidifier setting to reduce dryness and nasal congestion.
3. **Nasal congestion** – Use saline rinses or a decongestant if appropriate.
4. **Device cleaning** – Ensure tubing and mask are free of debris.

If these adjustments do not restore metrics within the timeframes above, move to the next escalation tier.

---

## Escalation Tiers
- **Self‑correction**: Minor metric deviations that resolve with the steps above.
- **Non‑urgent check‑in**: Persistent metric drift (usage, AHI, or leak) beyond the thresholds but without safety‑critical symptoms. Schedule a routine call or email to the sleep clinic.
- **Urgent stop‑and‑evaluate**: Sudden, severe drops in alertness, return of original apnea symptoms, or CPAP data indicating therapy failure (e.g., AHI spikes > 10 events/hr with high leak). Stop safety‑sensitive duties and seek immediate medical evaluation.

These tiers align with the [[concepts/hard-stop-rule]] for safety‑critical professions such as aviation, where the [[entities/faa]] mandates prompt action on any health issue that could impair performance.

## Integration with Daily Routine
Tracking CPAP metrics fits naturally into a broader [[concepts/sleep-diary]] and a [[concepts/flexible-daily-routine]]. By logging nightly values alongside bedtime, wake time, and daytime alertness, the user creates a **minimum‑viable‑day** record that is lightweight yet sufficient for clinician review.
