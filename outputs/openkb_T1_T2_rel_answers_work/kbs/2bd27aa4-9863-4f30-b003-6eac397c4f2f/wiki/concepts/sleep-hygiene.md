# Sleep Hygiene

**Definition**: A set of behavioral and environmental practices that support consistent, restorative sleep, especially important for safety‑critical professions.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-34_2025-01-15]])
- **Consistent schedule**: Go to bed and wake at the same times each day, even on weekends. This stabilises the circadian rhythm and reduces the need for drastic adjustments when duty schedules shift.
- **Wind‑down routine**: Allocate a quiet, screen‑free period (15‑30 min) before bedtime to signal the body it’s time to sleep.
- **Limit stimulants**: Reduce caffeine and nicotine intake after mid‑day and avoid alcohol close to bedtime, as both can fragment sleep.
- **Environment optimisation**: Keep the bedroom dark, cool, and quiet; use comfortable bedding and consider white‑noise if needed.
- **Physical activity**: Regular exercise supports sleep quality, but avoid vigorous activity within a few hours of bedtime.

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- Use a [[concepts/sleep-diary]] to track adherence to hygiene practices and identify patterns that may need adjustment.
- Pair hygiene with a [[concepts/flexible-daily-routine]] to accommodate shift work while preserving core sleep windows.
- When duty schedules differ between weekdays and weekends, apply a [[concepts/weekday-weekend-mode]] that maintains a minimum overlap in sleep timing.
- For pilots and other FAA‑regulated professionals, hygiene is a baseline requirement before any medical evaluation; see [[entities/faa]].
- If sleep quality drops despite hygiene, refer to the [[concepts/hard-stop-rule]] to trigger a self‑correction or professional check‑in.
- The [[concepts/minimum-viable-day]] concept helps define the least amount of sleep needed to stay functional on high‑stress days.
- Scheduling naps or alertness‑boosting activities can be guided by [[concepts/time-since-waking-scheduling]].

## Practical Tips for High‑Stakes Jobs
1. **Pre‑flight/shift checklist**: Include a brief review of sleep‑hygiene compliance (bedtime, caffeine, screen use).
2. **Log deviations**: Note any travel, jet lag, or schedule changes that force a temporary shift in routine.
3. **Adjust gradually**: When a schedule change is unavoidable, shift bedtime/wake time by ≤ 15 minutes per day to minimise circadian disruption.
4. **Monitor with CPAP**: If using a CPAP device, ensure hygiene practices do not interfere with mask fit or humidity settings (see [[concepts/cpap-metrics]] for related guidance).

## When Hygiene Is Not Enough
- Persistent daytime sleepiness, loud snoring, or breathing pauses despite strict hygiene warrant a professional evaluation (see [[concepts/hard-stop-rule]] and the detailed guidance in [[summaries/session-34_2025-01-15]]).
