## Time‑Since‑Waking Scheduling

**Definition**: A method of planning daily activities using offsets measured from the moment you wake up (e.g., +0:00‑+1:00, +1:00‑+4:00) rather than fixed clock times. This approach keeps the sequence of routines stable even when sleep patterns fluctuate.

**Key Elements**
- **Startup period (+0:00‑+1:00)** – hydrate, small bite, brief movement.
- **Focus block (+1:00‑+4:00)** – deep or primary work while mental clarity is highest.
- **Flexible inserts** – workout, meals, chores can be placed at defined offsets (e.g., workout at +4:00‑+5:00) without breaking the overall order.
- **Wind‑down (~+14:00)** – a cue to begin bedtime preparation, anchored to waking time rather than a set hour.

**Why It Works**
- Aligns energy peaks with tasks regardless of when you actually rise.
- Prevents the entire day from drifting later when sleep is irregular.
- Provides a clear, repeatable sequence that can be expressed as a fill‑in‑the‑blank checklist.

**Practical Applications from [[summaries/session-15_2024-06-15]]**
- A universal template that uses `W` (wake time) and offsets (e.g., `W+0:10` for startup, `5:00‑6:00 pm` for a mini focus block) works for any wake time after 10 am.
- Worked examples (6:30, 9:15, 11:20) illustrate how offset‑based blocks map to concrete clock times.
- The “minimum viable day” checklist compresses the schedule while preserving the offset structure.

**Implementation Tips**
1. **Identify your typical wake‑window** (e.g., 6 am–11 am) and note the offset ranges you need.
2. **Create a fill‑in‑the‑blank checklist** using `W` and the offsets.
3. **Anchor wind‑down** to a consistent offset (≈ +14 hours) to avoid sleep drift.
4. **Adjust on the fly** by sliding all blocks forward or backward while keeping their order unchanged.

By basing a daily routine on elapsed time since waking, you retain structure, respect natural energy cycles, and remain resilient to irregular sleep patterns.
