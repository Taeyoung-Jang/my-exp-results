# Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) Device

A CPAP device provides a continuous stream of pressurized air through a mask to prevent airway collapse while a person sleeps. It is commonly prescribed for obstructive sleep apnea and is essential for maintaining sleep quality and daytime alertness, especially for individuals in safety‑sensitive occupations.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-34_2025-01-15]]
- **Usage Hours**: The device should be used for the full night, matching the clinician‑recommended minimum nightly hours. A drop below this target for more than two to three consecutive nights triggers a self‑check (mask fit, humidity, congestion) and, if unresolved for a week, a non‑urgent check‑in with the sleep clinic.
- **AHI (Apnea‑Hypopnea Index)**: Elevated AHI for two nights prompts immediate mask‑fit adjustments and nasal clearing. Persistent elevation for five to seven nights despite these fixes requires contacting the clinician.
- **Leak Rate**: Occasional leak spikes are addressed by re‑positioning the mask or replacing cushions. Continuous high leak over several days after adjustments signals the need for a clinic call.
- **Self‑Help Steps**: Adjust mask fit, increase humidification, treat nasal congestion with saline or decongestants, and ensure the headgear is snug but comfortable.
- **Documentation**: Users are advised to keep a minimal log (see [[concepts/sleep-diary]]) noting daily CPAP hours, a brief note on whether AHI and leak appear “normal” or “off,” and any adjustments made. A weekly one‑sentence summary suffices for follow‑up.

## Operational Context
For pilots and other FAA‑regulated professionals, consistent CPAP use is part of maintaining medical certification. Deviations that affect alertness can jeopardize clearance, so the device’s performance must be regularly reviewed.
