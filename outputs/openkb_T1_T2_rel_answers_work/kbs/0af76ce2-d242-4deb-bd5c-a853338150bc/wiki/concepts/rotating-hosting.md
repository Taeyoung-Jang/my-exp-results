# Rotating Hosting

Rotating hosting is a **shared routine** where friends or acquaintances alternate the responsibility of planning and hosting social activities. Rather than one person consistently providing the venue, supplies, or coordination, the role is **traded off** among participants. This approach helps:

- **Distribute effort** so no single individual feels burdened with always being the host.
- **Encourage engagement**, as each person contributes ideas and resources.
- **Maintain low‑key social dynamics**, fitting well with [[concepts/low-key-social-activities]] that prioritize comfort over spectacle.

### Key Practices from [[summaries/session-03_2022-05-12]]

- **Explicit framing**: Pose invitations as a shared routine, e.g., “Let’s trade off hosting movie nights.”
- **Flexible scheduling**: Suggest activities like walks, coffee meet‑ups, or at‑home hangouts where the next host can simply pick the location or activity.
- **Clear cues**: After hosting once, follow up with a line such as “Next time we can do it at your place,” signalling openness for others to step up.

### Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/low-key-social-activities]]**: Rotating hosting complements low‑key activities by keeping gatherings informal and manageable.
- **[[concepts/social-routines]]**: By embedding hosting duties into a regular rhythm, rotating hosting becomes a stable part of a broader social routine, fostering consistent, meaningful contact.

### Benefits

1. **Reduces social fatigue** for the primary host.
2. **Promotes equity**, ensuring everyone feels valued and invested.
3. **Facilitates variety**, as different hosts bring unique preferences and spaces.

Implementing rotating hosting can transform occasional meet‑ups into a sustainable, balanced social practice.
