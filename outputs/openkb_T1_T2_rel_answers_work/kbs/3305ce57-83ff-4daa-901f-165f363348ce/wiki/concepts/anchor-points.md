# Anchor Points

**Definition**: Fixed moments in a day that provide a skeletal structure for activities while leaving the surrounding time flexible.

## Overview
Anchor Points act as reference markers—such as the start of work, a lunch break, a wrap‑up time, or an evening wind‑down. Rather than dictating each minute, they create a predictable framework that helps reduce decision fatigue and anxiety about timing.

## How They Were Applied in [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-15]]
- **Morning anchor**: "Ready to walk out the door" set at **9:20 am**.
- **Mid‑day anchor**: Lunch around **12:00 pm**.
- **Afternoon anchor**: Wrap‑up around **4:30 pm**.
- **Evening anchor**: Wind‑down around **7:30 pm**.

Each anchor is paired with a modest [[concepts/buffer-time]] to absorb variability, while the intervals between anchors remain open for floating tasks or spontaneous work.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/buffer-time]]** provides the safety margin around each anchor, typically 10‑30 % extra time, preventing the feeling of being rushed.
- **[[concepts/flexible-scheduling]]** describes how the gaps between anchors can host low‑priority or optional tasks, keeping the overall day adaptable.

## Benefits
- Reduces the perception of a day being dictated by the clock.
- Offers predictability for anxiety‑prone moments (e.g., morning preparation, travel).
- Enables a balance of structure and flexibility, improving productivity and well‑being.

## Practical Tips
1. Identify 3‑4 natural chunks in your typical day.
2. Assign a concrete time to each chunk (the anchor).
3. Add a small buffer before/after each anchor.
4. Use the open intervals for flexible tasks or brief breaks.
