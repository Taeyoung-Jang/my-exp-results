# Attention Shifting

**Definition**: A mental technique that deliberately moves one’s focus from the perception of waiting or delay to a more engaging, soothing, or productive stimulus, thereby lowering stress and the urge to check the clock.

## How it appears in [[summaries/session-39_2025-06-15]]
The session proposes several practical applications of attention shifting:
- Listening to music, a podcast, or an audiobook instead of staring at the clock.
- Reading a short article or a few pages of a book.
- Performing a brief breath exercise to calm jittery feelings.
- Engaging in a low‑effort phone task (e.g., clearing notifications) when screens are acceptable.

These tactics are presented as quick, discreet ways to make idle time feel purposeful.

## Key Elements
| Element | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Sensory Grounding** | Noting what you see, hear, or feel to anchor attention without formal mindfulness. |
| **Micro‑Countdown** | Setting a personal short timer (5–15 min) to give structure to the waiting period. |
| **Mini‑Session Ritual** | Starting a brief activity (e.g., a song) that mimics a scheduled slot, providing rhythm. |
| **Reframing** | Viewing the wait as “bonus recovery time” to reduce negative appraisal. |

## Practical Takeaways
1. **Pick a low‑effort stimulus** (audio, reading, light phone use) as soon as you notice a wait.
2. **Use a micro‑countdown** to give the activity a clear, short endpoint.
3. **Ground yourself** by briefly noting sensory details before moving back to the chosen stimulus.
4. **Reframe the wait** as a purposeful break, reinforcing calm.
