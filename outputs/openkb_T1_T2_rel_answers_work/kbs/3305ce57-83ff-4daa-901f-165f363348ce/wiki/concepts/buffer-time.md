# Buffer Time

**Definition**: Buffer time is a modest amount of extra time deliberately added to planned activities so that unexpected delays or overruns do not jeopardize the overall schedule.

---

## Why Buffer Time Matters

In the conversation documented in [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-15]], buffer time is presented as a core element of a low‑stress scheduling method. The approach contrasts with rigid minute‑by‑minute planning by:

- **Reducing panic**: Providing a safety margin where anxiety about being late is common.
- **Maintaining flexibility**: Allowing the rest of the day to stay open for floating tasks.
- **Improving predictability**: Creating a repeatable cushion (e.g., a fixed 10‑minute travel buffer) that users can rely on.

---

## Typical Buffer Sizes

| Context | Recommended Buffer |
|---|---|
| General tasks | Add **25‑30 %** of the estimated duration (e.g., plan 1.5 h for a 1 h task). |
| Morning preparation | Include a small **comfort buffer** (e.g., 5 min) before the “ready‑to‑leave” anchor. |
| Travel to appointments | Use a fixed **10‑minute** cushion on top of the normal travel time. |

These percentages and fixed minutes are suggestions; users should adjust them after a few days of observation.

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/anchor-points]]** provide the fixed moments in a day (start work, lunch, wrap‑up). Buffer time surrounds these anchors, ensuring they are met without stress.
- **[[concepts/flexible-scheduling]]** uses buffer time to create gaps between anchors where “floating” tasks can be placed if spare time remains.

---

## Practical Example (from the source)

1. **Morning anchor** – Ready to leave at 9:20 am.
2. **Preparation window** – Starts at 8:30 am, comprising a 45‑minute routine plus a 5‑minute buffer.
3. **Travel** – Normal 20 minutes + 10 minute buffer = 30 minutes total.
4. **Outcome** – Arrival around 9:40 am, with a pleasant 15‑minute pocket for reading or mindfulness if early.

---

## Implementing Buffer Time

1. **Estimate task duration** based on past experience.
2. **Calculate buffer** using the 25‑30 % rule or a fixed minute value for travel/commutes.
3. **Insert buffer** immediately before or after the relevant anchor point.
4. **Review** at the end of each day and adjust buffers as needed.

---

*Buffer time is a simple yet powerful tool for turning a schedule into a supportive framework rather than a restrictive timeline.*
