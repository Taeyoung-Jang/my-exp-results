# Blackout Curtains

**Type:** Product

**Overview**
Blackout curtains are specially designed window treatments that block or severely reduce external light, creating a consistently dark environment ideal for daytime sleeping or shift‑work schedules.

**Key Points from [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]]**
- Recommended as part of a **light‑exposure** management strategy for night‑shift workers.
- Used in conjunction with sunglasses on the commute to prevent bright light signals that delay sleep onset.
- Helps maintain the effectiveness of a **gradual‑sleep‑shift** by reinforcing a “night” environment immediately upon arriving home.
- Often paired with a **sleep‑mask** for additional light blockage, especially when total darkness is needed.

**Related Concepts**
- [[concepts/light-exposure]] – Managing light to align circadian rhythms.
- [[concepts/gradual-sleep-shift]] – Incremental adjustments to sleep timing.

**Related Entities**
- [[entities/sunglasses]] – Used on the commute to limit daylight exposure before reaching the darkened bedroom.
- [[entities/sleep-mask]] – Provides supplemental eye coverage when curtains alone are insufficient.

**Practical Tips**
1. Install blackout curtains in the bedroom to eliminate daylight intrusion.
2. Combine with a sleep mask for total darkness if light leaks around the edges.
3. Pair with a consistent wind‑down routine and limited caffeine intake (see [[concepts/caffeine-cutoff]]) for optimal sleep quality.
