# Sunglasses (product)

Sunglasses are a type of **product** designed to block or filter visible light, reducing glare and protecting eyes from bright conditions. In the context of night‑shift adjustment (see [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]]), they are recommended as a simple, low‑cost tool for managing **light exposure** during the post‑work commute.

## Key points from session‑37
- **Purpose:** Wear sunglasses for the entire walk home after a night shift to prevent bright daylight from signaling the brain that it’s still daytime, which can delay the onset of sleep.
- **Combined with other items:** Pair with [[entities/blackout-curtains]] and a [[entities/sleep-mask]] at home to create a dark environment conducive to sleep.
- **Effect on circadian rhythm:** By minimizing bright light, sunglasses support a smoother transition to the “night mode” required for a flipped schedule, complementing strategies like a gradual sleep shift ([[concepts/gradual-sleep-shift]]) and controlled light exposure ([[concepts/light-exposure]]).

## Practical usage tips
1. **Choose dark lenses** that block a high percentage of visible light; polarized lenses are optional but not required.
2. **Keep them on** for the whole commute, avoiding direct sun exposure even on brief stops.
3. **Combine with dim lighting** at home immediately after arrival to reinforce the night cue.
4. **Avoid bright screens** during the first hour in the dark environment; this further reduces stimulus that could counteract the benefits of sunglasses.
