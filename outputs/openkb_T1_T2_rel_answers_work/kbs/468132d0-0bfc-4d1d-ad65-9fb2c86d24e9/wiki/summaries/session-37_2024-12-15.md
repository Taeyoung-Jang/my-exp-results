# Summary of session‑37_2024-12-15

**Main focus:** Practical strategies for adjusting to a flipped work schedule (night shift) while minimizing fatigue.

## Key Recommendations

- **Gradual shift** – Change sleep/wake times by ~1 hour per day rather than an abrupt flip. [[concepts/gradual-sleep-shift]]
- **Consistent wind‑down routine** – Dim lights, limit screens, and engage in calming activities before the new bedtime.  
- **Light management**
  - Get bright light during the “day” portion of the shift to boost alertness. Use a 10,000‑lux [[entities/bright-light-box]] for 20‑30 min in the first 2‑3 h of work. Stop use at least 6 h before intended sleep. [[concepts/light-exposure]]
  - On the commute home, wear [[entities/sunglasses]] and avoid direct sunlight; use [[entities/blackout-curtains]] or a [[entities/sleep-mask]] at home.  
- **Caffeine timing** – Cut off caffeine 6–8 h before the target bedtime (e.g., stop by 3 a.m. for a 9 a.m. sleep). [[concepts/caffeine-cutoff]]
- **Screen use** – Minimize stimulating screen activity during the first hour at home; keep lights low to aid winding down.  
- **Flexible experimentation** – Once the schedule stabilises, you may trial brief morning light exposure on the commute or adjust blackout levels.

## Practical Example
- Target sleep time: **9 a.m.**
- Light‑box window: **midnight‑1 a.m.** (stop by **3 a.m.**) → provides a “morning” cue without interfering with sleep.
- Caffeine last cup: **around 3 a.m.**
- Immediate post‑commute: sunglasses throughout walk, dim apartment upon arrival.

These practices together help re‑entrain the circadian rhythm, reduce zombie‑like fatigue, and promote more restorative sleep during unconventional hours.
