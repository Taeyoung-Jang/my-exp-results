# Light Exposure for Shift Work

**Description:** Managing light cues to align circadian rhythms for night‑shift workers.

## Overview
Light is the strongest external cue that influences the human circadian system. For people whose work hours are flipped (e.g., night‑shift workers), intentional manipulation of light exposure can help:

1. **Promote alertness during the work "day"** – exposure to bright light early in the shift signals wakefulness.
2. **Facilitate sleep during the daytime** – reducing light exposure after the shift helps the body prepare for sleep.

Both strategies are discussed in [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-15]].

## Practical Guidelines

### Bright Light Exposure During the Shift
- Use a **bright‑light box** (approximately 10,000 lux) for 20–30 minutes, positioned at arm’s length.
- Schedule the light box use in the **first 2–3 hours of the shift**; this acts as a surrogate “morning” cue.
- Stop bright‑light exposure **at least six hours before the intended bedtime** to avoid delaying sleep onset.
- Example: For a target sleep time of 9 a.m., use the light box around **midnight–1 a.m.** and stop by **3 a.m.**.

### Reducing Light After the Shift
- **Wear sunglasses** on the commute home to block strong daylight that can push the internal clock later.
- Upon arriving home, keep the environment **dim**; use **blackout curtains** or a **sleep mask** to create a night‑like setting.
- Avoid bright screens and stimulating activities during the first hour at home; dim lighting supports the wind‑down routine.
