## Workspace Boundary Cues

**Definition**: Strategies and signals that help a person mentally shift into and out of work mode by creating clear, repeatable cues in their environment.

### Why they matter
- Prevents blending of work and home life, especially when working long hours.
- Supports focus by giving the brain a recognizable pattern that marks "work time."
- Enables quick transitions back to personal life once the cues are turned off.

### Types of cues highlighted in [[summaries/session-06_2024-06-15]]
1. **Visual cues**
   - Dedicated lamp, rug, or specific desk arrangement that only appears during work hours.
   - Using a particular chair placement or pulling a laptop stand out as a visual trigger.
   - Small physical dividers like a plant, shelf, or folding screen to demarcate a workspace.
2. **Auditory cues**
   - A consistent playlist or background sound that plays only while working.
   - Putting on headphones, even without listening to anything, creates an "invisible door."
3. **Olfactory cues**
   - A mild, consistent scent (e.g., a diffuser) that is present only during work periods.
4. **Ritual cues**
   - Turning on a specific light, closing the laptop, or tidying the desk for a minute to signal the start or end of the workday.
   - Consistent start‑and‑stop actions reinforce the mental boundary.

### Practical implementation tips
- Choose one cue from each category (visual, auditory, scent, ritual) and keep it consistent.
- Pair cues with ergonomic practices from [[concepts/ergonomics]] for a holistic, comfortable workspace.
- Use clutter‑control methods from [[concepts/clutter-management]] to keep the visual cue area tidy and effective.
- In shared rooms, leverage lightweight dividers (plants, shelves) and headphones to maintain privacy without permanent walls.
