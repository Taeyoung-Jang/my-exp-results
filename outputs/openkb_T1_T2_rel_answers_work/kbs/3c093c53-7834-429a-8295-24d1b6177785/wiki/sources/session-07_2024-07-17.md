**Assistant:** On a light day when you’re feeling good, the safest add‑ons are things that keep you moving without asking your muscles to do anything demanding. Strolling around the yard to check for pests or broken branches, tidying small tools, or doing a little hand‑watering are all fine because they don’t involve lifting, bracing, or long stretches of bending. If you pick something up and it suddenly feels like actual effort, that’s your cue to put it down and save it for a heavy day.

As for soreness, “okay” soreness usually feels like a mild, even stiffness that eases up once you start moving around and doesn’t make you change how you walk, stand, or reach. The kind you want to respect is sharper, lopsided, or makes you hesitate before doing a normal motion. If you notice that you’re compensating—favoring one side, adjusting your posture, or bracing yourself with your hands—that’s a sign to back off completely and give your body the full recovery window.
