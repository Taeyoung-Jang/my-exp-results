## Summary

- Introduces a **48‑hour recovery window** as the core principle for planning yard work, ensuring at least two days between any heavy effort. [[concepts/recovery_window]]
- Recommends a **heavy‑day / light‑day rhythm** using Monday and Thursday as the main heavy days, with the intervening days dedicated to low‑strain activities. This scheduling approach aligns with overall task‑scheduling strategies for physical recovery. [[concepts/task_scheduling_for_physical_recovery]]
- Provides a **two‑week concrete schedule** for a 12 × 6 ft flower‑bed project (clearing, edging, soil amendment, planting, mulching) with **~90‑minute limits** for each heavy session and a clear task breakdown. The plan follows a work‑intensity pattern that balances effort and rest. [[concepts/work_intensity_pattern]]
- Details **task grouping** so similar effort types (digging, hauling, edging) are done together, reducing repeated strain.
- Enumerates **low‑strain light tasks** (tool tidy‑up, brief watering, planning, spot checks) and flags **sneaky heavy tasks** (hauling bags, prolonged kneeling, detailed hand‑weeding) that should be saved for heavy days.
- Suggests **safe add‑on activities** for days when you feel good (yard scouting, light pruning, equipment organization) that won’t compromise the next heavy day.
- Explains how to **differentiate soreness**: mild, even stiffness that eases with movement is okay, whereas sharp, asymmetric pain or compensatory movements signal the need to back off and respect the recovery window.

### Key Takeaways
1. **48‑hour buffer** is non‑negotiable for sustained energy. [[concepts/recovery_window]]
2. **Heavy sessions** capped at ~1.5 hours with a real break.
3. **Light days** focus on truly low‑strain chores; avoid any lifting or extended kneeling.
4. **Monitor soreness** – mild stiffness is fine; sharp or limiting pain means rest.
5. **Adjustable schedule** – shift days as needed but keep the recovery interval intact. [[concepts/task_scheduling_for_physical_recovery]]
