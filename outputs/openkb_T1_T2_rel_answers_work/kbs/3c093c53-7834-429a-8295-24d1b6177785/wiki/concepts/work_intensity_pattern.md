# Work Intensity Pattern (Heavy vs Light Days)

**Definition**: A work‑intensity pattern that alternates **heavy** (high‑effort) days with **light** (low‑effort) days, ensuring at least a 48‑hour recovery period between any two heavy sessions.

## Core Principles
1. **Recovery window** – Respect a [[concepts/recovery_window]] of roughly two days before repeating heavy effort.
2. **Heavy days** – Concentrate demanding tasks (digging, hauling, edging, soil mixing, planting) into a single, time‑capped session (≈ 1.5 h) with a real break.
3. **Light days** – Limit activities to truly low‑strain actions such as tool tidy‑up, brief watering, planning, or visual checks. Avoid any lifting, prolonged kneeling, or detailed hand‑weeding.
4. **Task grouping** – Cluster tasks that require similar muscle groups on the same heavy day to reduce repeated strain.

## Implementation from [[summaries/session-07_2024-07-17]]
- **Monday & Thursday** were chosen as the heavy‑day anchors, providing a full 48‑hour buffer.
- Each heavy session was limited to about **90 minutes**, allowing the user to stop before fatigue sets in.
- Light days included activities like **tool organization, short spot‑checks, and planning**; sneaky heavy actions (hauling bags, extended kneeling) were explicitly flagged to stay on heavy days.
- The pattern was applied over a two‑week plan for a 12 × 6 ft flower‑bed, sequencing clearing → edging → soil amendment → planting → mulching across alternating heavy days.

## Typical Heavy‑Day Tasks
- Clearing vegetation and debris
- Edge shaping
- Digging and mixing compost
- Planting
- Spreading mulch (when scheduled as a heavy task)

## Typical Light‑Day Tasks
- Light watering
- Tool cleanup
- Reviewing plant selections
- Short scouting walks for pests or damage
- Minor, non‑lifting adjustments

## Benefits
- Maintains consistent progress without cumulative exhaustion.
- Reduces risk of overuse injuries by spacing strenuous motions.
- Provides flexibility: days can be shifted as long as the 48‑hour buffer remains intact.
