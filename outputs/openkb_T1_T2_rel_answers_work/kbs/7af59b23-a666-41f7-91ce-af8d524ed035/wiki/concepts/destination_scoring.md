# Destination Scoring Method

**What it is** – A lightweight, conversational tool for couples (or any two travelers) to evaluate and rank potential weekend get‑aways using a small set of decisive criteria.

---

## Core Idea
The method boils down the decision to a **simple three‑point scale** for each trait:
- **1 = weak** – the location barely meets the need.
- **2 = decent** – it satisfies the requirement adequately.
- **3 = great** – it strongly matches the desire.

By applying the same scale across a handful of traits, you can quickly see which destination scores highest overall without drowning in spreadsheets.

---

## Typical Traits (6‑point checklist)
| Trait | What to assess |
|-------|----------------|
| **Vibe match** | How closely the overall feel (relaxed, outdoorsy, foodie, artsy, etc.) aligns with both travelers’ preferences. [[concepts/travel_vibe]] |
| **Shared activities** | Availability of activities each person enjoys (hiking, coffee shops, museums, etc.). |
| **Estimated total cost** | Rough budget fit based on lodging, food, transport, and activities. [[concepts/budget_split]] |
| **Travel time & convenience** | Driving distance or flight length plus any hassle (e.g., airport transfers). |
| **Expected crowds** | Likelihood of the spot being busy during the intended weekend. |
| **Special factor** | Unique element that makes the trip feel memorable (e.g., a scenic overlook, local festival). |

## How to Use It
1. **Select 2‑4 candidate destinations** that are feasible within your geographic range or flight budget.
2. **Fill out the checklist** for each location, assigning a 1‑3 score for every trait.
3. **Summarize** the scores (either by adding them or by noting the strongest categories) to spot the most balanced option.
4. **Cross‑reference** the outcome with your overall budget split and booking timeline to ensure feasibility.

---

## Example (excerpt from [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]])
> "You can compare destinations by picking four or five traits that matter most to both of you… then talk through each destination one trait at a time and give it a simple gut‑level score, like ‘strong,’ ‘fine,’ or ‘weak.’"

The example illustrates the same three‑point intuition (strong = 3, fine = 2, weak = 1) in a conversational format.

---

## Benefits
- **Speed:** Decision can be made in a single sitting.
- **Transparency:** Both partners see the same criteria and scoring.
- **Flexibility:** Traits can be tweaked to match specific trips (e.g., replace “crowds” with “weather”).
- **Integration:** Works hand‑in‑hand with the budget split approach and the recommended booking timeline.
