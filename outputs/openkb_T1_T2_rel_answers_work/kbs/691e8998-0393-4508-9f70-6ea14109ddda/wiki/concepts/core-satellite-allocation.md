# Core / Satellite Allocation

**Definition**: A portfolio design approach that divides assets into a stable, broadly diversified *core* (typically low‑cost equity or bond funds) and a more aggressive *satellite* segment (individual stocks, thematic funds, crypto, etc.) that seeks excess returns.

---

## Why use a core/satellite structure?
- Provides a solid, market‑baseline performance foundation while allowing the investor to express higher conviction ideas.
- Reduces overall volatility compared to an all‑satellite portfolio because the core absorbs market swings.
- Enables systematic risk management through explicit allocation targets and position‑size caps.

---

## Typical allocation example (from [[summaries/session-40_2024-06-15]])
| Segment | Target share of total portfolio | Example composition |
|---------|-------------------------------|--------------------|
| **Core (ETF)** | **~65 %** | Broad‑market, growth‑tilted equity ETFs (e.g., large‑cap U.S. and international index funds). |
| **Satellite** | **~25 %** | Individual high‑growth stocks, sector/theme ETFs, other high‑volatility ideas. |
| **Crypto sleeve** | **~10 %** | Bitcoin, Ethereum, or other liquid cryptos, kept small relative to the whole portfolio. |

The core is intended to dominate the long‑term return driver, while satellites capture upside from selective bets.

---

## Position‑sizing rules within the framework
- No single ETF should exceed **30 %** of total assets.
- Individual stocks or thematic positions capped at **6 %** each.
- Any single crypto asset limited to **3 %**.
- The entire satellite bucket (including crypto) generally kept below **40 %** of the portfolio.

These caps are linked to the broader [[concepts/position-sizing]] practice, ensuring that no single idea can dominate the portfolio.

---

## Rebalancing considerations
- **Drift‑band rebalancing**: intervene when any segment moves more than **5 percentage points** from its target (see [[concepts/drift-band-rebalancing]]).
- **Contribution‑driven rebalancing**: allocate new monthly contributions (e.g., $2 k) first to under‑weighted segments before selling existing holdings (see [[concepts/contribution-driven-rebalancing]]).
- An annual full‑portfolio review to reset allocations to the defined targets.

---

## Decision checklist (related to [[concepts/decision-checklist]])
1. **Time commitment** – How much effort can you devote to research and monitoring?
2. **Risk tolerance** – Are you comfortable with the volatility of a larger satellite allocation?
3. **Capital availability** – Do you have regular contributions to use for rebalancing?
4. **Diversification goal** – Does the core provide sufficient broad market exposure?
5. **Cap adherence** – Will you enforce the per‑position limits consistently?

---

## Benefits
- **Stability**: The core reduces portfolio drawdowns during market stress.
- **Flexibility**: Satellites let you pursue higher returns without jeopardizing the whole portfolio.
- **Simplicity**: Clear allocation targets and caps make ongoing management straightforward.

---

## Limitations
- May under‑perform pure active strategies in very strong bull markets if the satellite portion is too small.
- Requires discipline to stick to caps and rebalance regularly.
