# Position Sizing

**Definition**

Position sizing is the practice of setting explicit limits on how large any single investment can become within a portfolio. By capping the weight of each holding, investors protect themselves from outsized losses if a concentrated bet underperforms.

**Why It Matters**

- Controls concentration risk and aligns with a broader **risk‑assessment** framework.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with a **core‑satellite‑allocation** strategy, ensuring the core remains diversified while satellites stay bounded.
- Provides clear rules for when to rebalance, especially when combined with a **drift‑band‑rebalancing** or **contribution‑driven‑rebalancing** approach.

**Typical Caps from Session 40**

| Asset Type | Maximum Weight per Holding | Overall Sleeve Limit |
|------------|----------------------------|----------------------|
| Broad equity **ETF** | ≤ 30 % of total portfolio | Core can occupy 60‑75 % of portfolio |
| Individual **stock** | ≤ 6 % of total portfolio (up to 8 % for high conviction) | Satellite sleeve ≤ 40 % of portfolio |
| **Crypto** (individual) | ≤ 3 % of total portfolio | Crypto sleeve ≤ 10 % of portfolio |

If a position breaches its cap, the recommended response is to **redirect new contributions** toward under‑weighted holdings until the oversized position drifts back below the limit. Only if the breach persists after several months should a trim be executed.

**How It Fits Into a Portfolio Ruleset**

1. **Set target caps** (as above) as part of the overall **decision‑checklist** for portfolio construction.
2. **Monitor drift**: Use a 5‑percentage‑point drift band to trigger a review of position sizes.
3. **Rebalance**: Apply the drift‑band rule annually, and use monthly $2 k contributions to bring positions back into line, minimizing the need to sell.
