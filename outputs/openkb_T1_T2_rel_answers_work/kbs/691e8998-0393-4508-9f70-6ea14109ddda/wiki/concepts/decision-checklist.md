# Decision Checklist

A **Decision Checklist** is a concise, step‑by‑step framework that helps a person evaluate whether a safe (certain) choice truly aligns with their values or whether it is being chosen out of avoidance. It originated from the discussion in [[summaries/session-05_2022-06-15]] and is designed for quick use—roughly two minutes—via a series of yes/no questions that can be scored.

---

## Core Structure

The checklist consists of five questions, each addressing a distinct dimension of the decision:

1. **Protection of Core Values** – Does the safe option protect something genuinely important (e.g., income stability, health, routine)?
2. **Real Threat Assessment** – Would the risky option realistically threaten that important thing in a meaningful way?
3. **Fears vs. Reality** – Are the fears about the risky option based on realistic probabilities rather than worst‑case storytelling?
4. **Value‑Aligned Upside** – Would the upside of the risky option noticeably improve something you truly value?
5. **Emotional Signal** – Does choosing the safe option feel like calm relief (aligned) or avoidance (potential fear)?

Each “yes” answer adds one point, except question 4, where a “yes” (meaning the upside matters) subtracts a point. The total score guides interpretation:

- **Score ≥ 2** – The safe choice is likely aligned with personal values.
- **Score ≤ 1** – Pause and double‑check for avoidance or hidden fear.

---

## Origins & Context

The checklist builds on several psychological ideas:

- **[[concepts/risk-assessment]]** – Evaluating the probability and impact of negative outcomes.
- **[[concepts/loss-aversion]]** – The tendency to weigh potential losses more heavily than equivalent gains, which influences how we view risk.
- **Future‑Self Perspective** – Imagining how you will feel about the decision a year later, a technique mentioned in the source session.

In the source conversation, the author sought a *2‑minute* version that could be stored in a notes app. The resulting format satisfies that need while keeping the process systematic and repeatable.

---

## How to Use

1. **Copy the five questions** into your preferred notes or task app.
2. **Answer “yes” or “no”** for each question as honestly as possible.
3. **Calculate the score** using the rule above.
4. **Interpret the result** – if the score is low, spend a few minutes revisiting the underlying fears or gather more information about the risky option.

This checklist can be applied to a wide range of decisions, from career moves (e.g., stable job vs. startup) to financial choices (e.g., guaranteed income vs. speculative investment) and everyday personal dilemmas.
