# Contribution‑Driven Rebalancing

**Definition**: A rebalancing approach that prioritises directing fresh contributions toward under‑weight asset classes or positions, using cash inflows to restore target allocations before executing any sell trades.

---

## Core Idea

In a long‑term growth portfolio, the investor receives regular cash (e.g., $2,000 per month). Instead of frequently selling assets to keep the portfolio on target, the investor first checks which segments are below their prescribed weightings and allocates the new money there. Only if the overweight position remains above its cap after several months of contribution‑driven adjustments does the investor trim the excess.

---

## Why It Matters

- **Minimises transaction costs** – fewer sell orders mean lower commissions and tax impact.
- **Preserves upside** – keeping winning positions longer can capture more gains.
- **Behavioural guardrail** – it reduces the temptation to constantly "tweak" the portfolio based on short‑term market noise.

---

## Steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-40_2024-06-15]])

1. **Identify target allocation** – e.g., 65 % ETF core, 25 % satellite, 10 % crypto sleeve.
2. **Check drift** – determine which sleeve or asset class is below its target weight.
3. **Direct contributions** – allocate the monthly $2 k contribution first to those under‑weight areas, respecting individual caps (e.g., ≤30 % for any ETF, ≤6 % for a stock, ≤3 % for a crypto asset).
4. **Monitor caps** – if an overweight position still exceeds its cap after a few months, trim only enough to bring it back just under the limit.
5. **Annual review** – once a year perform a full rebalance to bring everything back to target, but the bulk of the alignment during the year is handled by contributions.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Works with a **drift‑band rebalancing** trigger (e.g., act when a sleeve drifts >5 % from target) [[concepts/drift-band-rebalancing]].
- Relies on **position sizing** caps to define acceptable maximum weights [[concepts/position-sizing]].
- Forms part of an overall **core‑satellite allocation** framework [[concepts/core-satellite-allocation]].

---

## Practical Example

- Portfolio value: $120k (including cash).
- Target: 65 % ETFs, 25 % satellites, 10 % crypto.
- Current weights: ETFs 60 %, satellites 30 %, crypto 10 %.
- Monthly $2k contribution: allocate $1k to ETFs (bring them to 65 %), $800 to satellites (reduce drift), $200 to crypto (maintain sleeve size).

By the end of the year the portfolio stays close to target without selling any holdings, illustrating the essence of contribution‑driven rebalancing.

---

## Benefits Summary

- Lower turnover and tax drag.
- Simpler execution – only buy orders most of the time.
- Aligns cash flow with strategic allocation, reinforcing discipline.
