# Loss Aversion

**Definition**: Loss aversion is the cognitive bias where individuals feel the pain of losing something more strongly than the pleasure of gaining something of equal value.

## Relationship to Decision‑Making
In the context of choosing between a safe option and a riskier one, loss aversion often drives people to favor the guaranteed outcome. The session‑05_2022-06-15 discussion highlights how:
- Some people naturally focus on potential gains, while others are more attuned to what they could lose.
- This bias can make the *real downside* of a risky choice feel larger than its actual probability, leading to an over‑estimation of danger.
- Recognizing loss aversion helps separate genuine value‑based preferences from fear‑driven avoidance.

## How It Informs the Checklist
The quick decision framework presented in [[summaries/session-05_2022-06-15]] includes a specific question to counteract loss aversion:
> **Are your fears about the risky option based on real probabilities rather than worst‑case storytelling?**

By explicitly checking the realism of perceived losses, the checklist mitigates the pull of loss aversion and encourages a more balanced evaluation.
