# Summary of session-05_2022-06-15

**Main contribution**: A practical framework for weighing safe (certain) versus risky (potentially higher‑payoff) options, culminating in a 2‑minute yes/no checklist that scores alignment with personal values versus avoidance.

## Key Concepts
- **Risk vs. Certainty** – The tension between a guaranteed outcome and a variable one with higher upside. [[concepts/risk-assessment]]
- **Loss Aversion & Gain Focus** – People differ in weighting potential losses versus gains. [[concepts/loss-aversion]]
- **Safety Net Evaluation** – Checking whether you could survive the downside.
- **Motivation Check** – Distinguishing fear‑driven avoidance from genuine preference.
- **Future‑Self Perspective** – Asking which choice you’ll be proud of later.
- **Decision Checklist** – Structured, step‑by‑step questions to clarify values and risks. [[concepts/decision-checklist]]

## Findings & Ideas
1. **Underlying Drivers** – Past experience, mood, and personal buffers shape the safe‑vs‑risky balance.
2. **Grounding Questions** – Identify real downside, verify safety net, clarify meaningful upside, assess motive, and project future satisfaction.
3. **Adapted Checklist for Certainty‑Seekers** – Focuses on what the safe option protects, whether risk truly threatens those protections, over‑estimation of danger, value‑aligned payoff, and emotional signal (relief vs. avoidance).
4. **2‑Minute Scoring Tool** – Five yes/no questions with a simple point system:
   - Protects important value? (+1)
   - Risk threatens that value? (+1)
   - Fears are realistic? (+1)
   - Upside meaningfully improves valued goal? (‑1)
   - Feeling of relief vs. avoidance? (+1)
   - **Interpretation**: Score ≥2 → safe choice aligned; ≤1 → double‑check for avoidance.

## Practical Example
- **Stable job vs. higher‑paying startup** – Apply the checklist to see if stability protects core values, whether the startup realistically endangers them, if fears are proportionate, whether extra pay advances key goals, and what emotional response arises.

## Takeaway
The provided checklist and scoring rule give a quick, repeatable method to confirm that a safe decision truly matches personal priorities, while flagging decisions that may be driven by hidden fear.
