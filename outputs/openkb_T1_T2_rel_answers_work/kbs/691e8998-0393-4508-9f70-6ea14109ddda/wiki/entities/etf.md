# ETF (Exchange‑Traded Fund)

An **ETF** is a pooled investment vehicle that holds a basket of assets (stocks, bonds, commodities, etc.) and trades on an exchange like a single stock. ETFs provide instant diversification, low expense ratios, and high liquidity, making them a common choice for the *core* portion of a long‑term portfolio.

## Key points from [[summaries/session-40_2024-06-15]]
- **Core‑satellite role**: The document recommends a **core** allocation of roughly **65 %** of the portfolio in broad equity ETFs, forming the stable foundation for a growth‑tilted strategy. ([[concepts/core-satellite-allocation]])
- **Position‑size caps**: No single ETF should exceed **30 %** of the total portfolio value. This cap helps maintain diversification while allowing a broad index fund to hold a larger slice if desired. ([[concepts/position-sizing]])
- **Rebalancing interaction**: ETFs are part of the annual *full* rebalance and the **drift‑band** check that triggers adjustments when any sleeve drifts more than **5 percentage points** from its target. ([[concepts/drift-band-rebalancing]])
- **Contribution‑driven rebalancing**: New monthly contributions ($2 k) are first directed to under‑weighted ETFs, using inflows to keep the core at target before selling any holdings. ([[concepts/contribution-driven-rebalancing]])

## Typical characteristics
- **Liquidity**: Can be bought or sold throughout the trading day at market prices.
- **Expense ratios**: Generally lower than actively managed mutual funds.
- **Transparency**: Holdings are disclosed daily, allowing investors to see exactly what assets are owned.
