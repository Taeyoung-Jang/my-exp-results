# Cryptocurrency (product)

Cryptocurrency refers to a class of digital assets that use cryptography for security and operate on decentralized networks. In the investment policy outlined in [[summaries/session-40_2024-06-15]], crypto is treated as a **satellite sleeve** within a growth‑oriented portfolio.

## Role in the Portfolio
- **Target allocation**: 10 % of total portfolio value, forming part of the satellite/crypto sleeve.
- **Individual asset cap**: No single cryptocurrency should exceed roughly **3 %** of the overall portfolio (or a few percent of the crypto sleeve) to avoid excessive concentration.
- **Tolerance range**: Investors comfortable with high volatility may allocate between **1 %–10 %** of the portfolio to crypto, depending on personal risk tolerance.

## Guardrails and Rules
- **Position‑sizing**: The crypto cap aligns with the broader [[concepts/position-sizing]] framework that limits any one position’s weight.
- **Rebalancing**: Crypto exposure is monitored using a **drift‑band** approach; if the crypto sleeve deviates more than **5 percentage points** from its 10 % target, a rebalance is triggered (see [[concepts/drift-band-rebalancing]]).
- **Contribution‑driven rebalancing**: Monthly contributions (e.g., $2 k) are first directed toward under‑weighted allocations, including crypto, before selling existing positions. This follows the [[concepts/contribution-driven-rebalancing]] principle.

## Interaction with Other Assets
- Crypto is positioned alongside **broad equity ETFs** (the core) and individual high‑volatility stocks (the satellite). The ETF core typically occupies 60‑75 % of the portfolio, providing stability while crypto adds a high‑risk, high‑potential return component.
- The ETF entity is referenced as [[entities/etf]].

## Summary
Cryptocurrency, as presented in the session‑40 policy, is a small, high‑volatility component of a diversified, long‑term growth portfolio, governed by strict caps and rebalancing rules to manage risk while allowing for upside potential.
