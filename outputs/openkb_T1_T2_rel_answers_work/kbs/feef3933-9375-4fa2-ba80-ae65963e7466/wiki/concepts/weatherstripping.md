## Weatherstripping

**Definition**: Weatherstripping refers to flexible strips—often foam or rubber—applied to the moving parts of a window or door to block air infiltration.

### Purpose and Benefits
- Reduces drafts by sealing gaps around sashes, tracks, and hinges.
- Improves thermal comfort and lowers heating costs.
- Helps control interior moisture by limiting cold‑air infiltration that can cause condensation.

### Typical Materials
- **Medium‑density adhesive foam weatherstripping** – cut‑to‑size, self‑adhesive strips suitable for windows and doors. [[entities/medium_density_adhesive_foam_weatherstripping]]
- Rubber or vinyl profiles for heavier‑duty applications.

### Application Process (as described in [[summaries/session-05_2022-10-30]])
1. **Identify draft locations** – run a hand or tissue around the window when closed to locate moving‑part leaks.
2. **Prepare the surface** – clean the sash edges and tracks to ensure adhesion.
3. **Cut and apply** – measure the length needed, cut the foam, peel the backing, and press firmly onto the sash or frame where drafts were felt.
4. **Test operation** – open and close the window to verify smooth movement; re‑adjust if needed.

### How Weatherstripping Works with Other Insulation Methods
- Works alongside **caulking** ([[concepts/caulking]]) which seals static seams between the frame and wall.
- Complements removable window‑insulation film and thermal curtains for a layered approach to [[concepts/window_insulation]].

### Quick DIY Weekend Plan (from the source)
- **Saturday**: Focus on caulking static gaps.
- **Sunday**: Apply weatherstripping to the sash after caulk has cured, targeting the vertical sides and the horizontal meeting rail of a double‑hung window. [[entities/double_hung_window]]

### Maintenance Tips
- Inspect strips annually; replace if compressed or cracked.
- Use compatible adhesive when re‑applying to avoid residue that interferes with window operation.
