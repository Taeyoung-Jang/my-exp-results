# Drip Irrigation

## Definition
Drip irrigation is a precise, low‑flow watering technique that delivers water directly to the root zone of plants through emitters. It is especially valuable in [[concepts/desert-landscaping]] where water conservation and deep rooting are priorities.

## Core Principles
- **Match emitter flow to plant needs** – choose 1 GPH or 2 GPH emitters based on the desired volume per watering.
- **Deep, infrequent watering** – aligns with [[concepts/establishment-phase-watering]] by encouraging roots to penetrate soil rather than remaining superficial.
- **Placement strategy** – use one emitter near the base for smaller plants, or multiple emitters spaced around larger plants (e.g., [[entities/agave]]) to broaden the wet zone and minimize crown rot.

## Practical Guidance from Session 37 (2024‑10‑30)
- **Volume targets**:
  - Agave: 3–5 gal per session.
  - Ocotillo: 4–6 gal per session.
- **Emitter configurations**:
  - 1 GPH emitter → run 3–5 hr for agave, 4–6 hr for ocotillo.
  - 2 GPH emitter → run 1.5–2.5 hr for agave, 2–3 hr for ocotillo.
- **Scheduling**:
  - First 4 weeks: weekly runs (≈1 hr for two 2 GPH ocotillo emitters; ≈2 hr total for two 1 GPH agave emitters).
  - Weeks 5‑8: extend to every 10–14 days, adjusting for soil moisture depth.
- **Rain offset** – skip a drip cycle only when rain wets the soil a few inches deep; light sprinkles do not count.
- **Soil considerations** – sandy soil with decomposed granite drains quickly, so deep watering and full dry‑down between cycles are crucial.

## Benefits
- **Water savings** – delivers only the water plants need, reducing runoff and evaporation.
- **Root development** – deep watering promotes stronger, drought‑resistant root systems.
- **Flexibility** – emitter flow rates and run times can be easily adjusted as plants mature.
