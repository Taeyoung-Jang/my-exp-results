# Caulking

**What it is**: Caulking involves applying a flexible sealant to fixed seams around a window—typically where the frame meets the wall or trim—to prevent air infiltration and moisture penetration.

**Why it matters**: In the context of home comfort, caulk addresses the largest, coldest drafts that come from static gaps. It also helps stop condensation on window frames by creating a watertight barrier.

## Key Characteristics
- **Ideal product**: Paintable exterior‑grade silicone or latex‑silicone caulk (see [[entities/exterior_grade_silicone_caulk]]). This type adheres well outdoors, remains flexible, and can be painted to match surrounding surfaces.
- **Application sites**: Rigid seams around the perimeter of the window—trim‑to‑wall joints, frame corners, and any cracked or missing caulk.
- **Complementary measures**: Works best when paired with [[concepts/weatherstripping]] on moving parts and with overall [[concepts/window_insulation]] strategies.

## Recommended Application Process (from [[summaries/session-05_2022-10-30]])
1. **Remove old material** – Use a razor scraper or putty knife to scrape away flaky or cracked caulk.
2. **Clean the surface** – Wipe the seam with a rag and mild cleaner to ensure adhesion.
3. **Apply a fresh bead** – Load a caulk gun with the chosen caulk and run a continuous bead along the gap.
4. **Shape the seal** – Smooth the bead with a damp finger or tooling knife for a uniform finish.
5. **Cure time** – Allow the caulk to set for a few hours to become dry‑to‑touch; full cure may take overnight, depending on product specifications.

## Sequencing with Other Repairs
- **First step**: Perform caulking on Saturday (or the first day of a weekend project) because it needs curing time before other work.
- **Next step**: After the caulk has cured, install weatherstripping on the sash and tracks on Sunday. This order ensures that the biggest airtight gaps are sealed before fine‑tuning moving parts.

## Quick Draft Diagnosis
- **Trim/frame leak**: Feel for airflow along the outer edge where the window meets the wall. Persistent drafts here indicate the need for additional caulk.
- **Sash leak**: Feel for airflow around the moving parts; weatherstripping is the appropriate remedy.

## Maintenance Tips
- Periodically inspect caulk joints for cracks, especially after extreme temperature changes.
- Touch up any new gaps with a small bead of caulk and allow proper curing before re‑checking for drafts.
