# Exterior‑Grade Silicone Caulk

**Category:** Sealant (product)  
**Type:** `product`

## Overview
Exterior‑grade silicone caulk is a paintable, weather‑resistant sealant designed for sealing the rigid seams around windows, doors, and trim. In the context of [[summaries/session-05_2022-10-30]], it is recommended as the primary material for closing trim‑to‑wall gaps that cause the largest drafts.

## Key Characteristics
- **Paintable:** Accepts exterior paint for seamless appearance.
- **Weather‑Resistant:** Handles exposure to wind, rain, and coastal humidity.
- **Curing Time:** Typically dry to the touch within a few hours; full cure may take overnight, allowing other tasks (e.g., [[concepts/weatherstripping]]) to be completed the same weekend.
- **Flexibility:** Remains flexible after cure, preventing cracks from thermal expansion.

## Recommended Use in Window Insulation
1. **Preparation:** Scrape away old, flaky caulk with a putty knife, clean the seam with a rag and mild cleaner.
2. **Application:** Load the caulk into a caulk gun and apply a continuous bead along the frame‑to‑wall or frame‑trim joints.
3. **Smoothing:** Smooth the bead with a damp finger or caulk‑smoothing tool.
4. **Curing:** Allow the caulk to cure for the time specified on the tube (usually a few hours to overnight) before proceeding to other fixes.
5. **Follow‑Up:** After curing, test for remaining drafts. If any persist, touch up specific spots with extra caulk.

## Relationship to Other Materials
- Works in tandem with [[concepts/weatherstripping]] (foam or rubber strips) to seal moving parts of a [[entities/double_hung_window]].
- Complementary to [[entities/medium_density_adhesive_foam_weatherstripping]] for sash‑level leaks.
- Often paired with [[entities/thermal_curtains]] and a small [[entities/dehumidifier]] to manage indoor moisture after sealing.
