## Drip Emitter

A **drip emitter** is a small irrigation device that releases water at a controlled low flow rate, typically measured in gallons per hour (GPH). It is commonly used in **desert‑style landscaping** to provide deep, infrequent watering that encourages root growth while minimizing evaporation.

### Key Facts from Session 37 (2024‑10‑30)
- **Flow options:** The document references 1 GPH and 2 GPH emitters.
- **Water volume targets:** For establishing **[[entities/agave]]**, 3–5 gal per watering; for **[[entities/ocotillo]]**, 4–6 gal per watering.
- **Duration calculations:** 
  - 1 GPH → run 3–5 hr for agave, 4–6 hr for ocotillo.
  - 2 GPH → run 1.5–2.5 hr for agave, 2–3 hr for ocotillo.
- **Placement recommendations:**
  - Agave: two 1 GPH emitters spaced around the base to spread moisture and avoid crown rot.
  - Ocotillo: a single emitter is sufficient; two 2 GPH emitters can be used for a 1‑hour weekly run.
- **Scheduling:** During the first 4 weeks, run emitters weekly; extend to every 10‑14 days after the plants are established, adjusting for rain events.

### Typical Applications
- **[[concepts/drip-irrigation]]**: Provides precise water delivery to individual plants, reducing waste.
- **[[concepts/establishment-phase-watering]]**: Supplies deep, infrequent water pulses to promote root penetration during the early growth stage.
- **[[concepts/desert-landscaping]]**: Supports low‑maintenance, water‑efficient yards with drought‑tolerant species.
