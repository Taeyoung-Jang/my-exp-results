## Double‑Hung Window

A **double‑hung window** is a type of residential window that features two movable sashes—an upper and a lower—sliding vertically within the same frame. This design allows both sashes to be opened for ventilation and makes cleaning easier.

### Role in [[summaries/session-05_2022-10-30]]
The conversation focuses on improving the thermal performance of a double‑hung window by:
- Applying **[[concepts/caulking]]** to seal the trim‑to‑wall gaps, which are often the largest sources of drafts.
- Adding **[[concepts/weatherstripping]]** to the moving parts of the sashes (vertical sides and the meeting rail) to stop air infiltration when the window is closed.
- Using **[[concepts/window_insulation]]** film and **[[entities/thermal_curtains]]** as additional layers to retain heat.
- Managing any residual moisture with a **[[entities/dehumidifier]]** or by briefly venting the window after sealing.

### Recommended Materials & Tools
- **[[entities/medium_density_adhesive_foam_weatherstripping]]** – applied to the sash edges and tracks.
- **[[entities/exterior_grade_silicone_caulk]]** – used to seal rigid seams around the frame.
- Caulk gun, scraper, rag, and mild cleaner (mentioned in the checklist).
- Optional: a small dehumidifier for post‑seal moisture control.

### Placement Guidelines for Weatherstripping
For a typical double‑hung window, place weatherstripping:
1. Along the vertical sides of each sash where they meet the tracks.
2. On the horizontal meeting rail where the upper and lower sashes close.
3. On the top edge of the upper sash if it is loose.
> **Note:** Avoid the bottom edge that needs to drain water.
