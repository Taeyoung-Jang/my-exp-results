# Ocotillo (plant)

Ocotillo (*Fouquieria splendens*) is a hardy desert shrub prized for its striking, tall red‑flowered stalks and its suitability for **desert‑style landscaping** that emphasizes water efficiency.

## Key Characteristics
- Native to the southwestern United States and northern Mexico.
- Deep‑rooted and highly drought‑tolerant once established.
- Can thrive on sandy soils with a surface layer of decomposed granite or gravel.

## Watering Guidance from [[summaries/session-37_2024-10-30]]
- **Establishment Phase:** Target ~4 gallons per watering for the first 8 weeks.
- **Emitter Options:** Two 2‑GPH emitters running ~1 hour weekly (or every 10‑14 days after the first month) provide the needed volume.
- **Soil Check:** Verify moisture 2–3 in. deep after each run; skip a cycle only if a real rain soak occurs.
- **Placement:** A single emitter is generally sufficient, but the document recommends two 2‑GPH emitters to ensure even distribution.

## Irrigation Concepts Applied
- The plant’s watering plan follows principles of [[concepts/drip-irrigation]] and [[concepts/establishment-phase-watering]].
- It fits within broader [[concepts/desert-landscaping]] strategies that prioritize low water use, simple plant palettes, and clean, gravel‑covered ground.

## Related Entities
- Often paired with other drought‑tolerant plants such as [[entities/agave]].
- Uses [[entities/drip-emitter]]s for precise water delivery.
