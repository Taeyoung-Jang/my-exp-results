## Thermal Curtains

**What they are**: Heavy or densely‑woven fabric panels that hang over windows to add a layer of thermal resistance.  They trap warm air inside a room and help keep the glass surface cooler, which reduces condensation.

**Key points from [[summaries/session-05_2022-10-30]]**:
- Recommended as an inexpensive, quick win to make a room feel warmer and limit moisture around windows.
- Useful in conjunction with other insulation methods such as [[concepts/window_insulation]] film and [[concepts/weatherstripping]].
- Should be placed so they do not block necessary airflow; pulling them back slightly can improve ventilation if condensation appears after sealing.

**Related concepts**:
- [[concepts/window_insulation]] – complementary technique that blocks drafts before the glass.
- [[concepts/weatherstripping]] – seals moving parts of the window; thermal curtains address the remaining heat loss.

**Related entities**:
- [[entities/dehumidifier]] – can be used alongside thermal curtains to manage lingering moisture.

**Practical tips**:
1. Choose curtains with a thermal lining or blackout backing for maximum insulation.
2. Hang them as close to the window frame as possible while still allowing the window to open.
3. Combine with proper ventilation (e.g., briefly cracking the window) to prevent excess humidity.
