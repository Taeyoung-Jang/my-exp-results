# Garmin (Organization)

Garmin is a global technology company best known for its GPS navigation products and a broad portfolio of wearable fitness devices. In the context of the **session-04_2024-06-15** discussion, Garmin is highlighted as the primary recommendation for users seeking a smartwatch or fitness watch that balances long battery life (7‑10 days), reliable health monitoring, and adequate smart features such as notifications.

## Key Points from the Session
- **Long‑Battery Wearables:** The assistant recommends Garmin’s **Forerunner 255** [[entities/garmin-forerunner-255]], **Forerunner 165** (non‑AMOLED) [[entities/garmin-forerunner-165]], and **Vivoactive** models with LCD displays [[entities/garmin-vivoactive]] as the top choices for a week‑plus battery while still providing solid [[concepts/health-tracking-accuracy]] and notification support.
- **Battery‑Life Optimization:** Garmin devices achieve extended runtime by using efficient LCD screens, limiting always‑on displays, and offering configurable settings that reduce power‑hungry features like continuous [[entities/gps]] usage or all‑night SpO2 monitoring. These strategies map directly to the broader concept of [[concepts/battery-life-optimization]].
- **Trade‑offs:** The primary compromises compared to a full‑featured smartwatch include the absence of an [[entities/amoled]] display, a smaller app ecosystem, and a more utilitarian user interface.

## Related Entities and Concepts
- **Competitors Mentioned:** Fitbit [[entities/fitbit]], Fitbit Charge [[entities/fitbit-charge]], and Withings hybrid watches [[entities/withings]] / [[entities/withings-hybrid-watch]] are discussed as alternatives with longer battery life but fewer smart capabilities.
- **Core Technologies:** Garmin’s wearables rely heavily on **GPS** [[entities/gps]] for outdoor activity tracking, and they integrate sensors for heart‑rate, sleep, and (optionally) SpO2.
