# AMOLED

## Overview
AMOLED (Active‑Matrix Organic Light‑Emitting Diode) is a display technology prized for high contrast, vivid colors, and the ability to keep the screen always on. These advantages come with a trade‑off: significantly higher power consumption compared to LCD or e‑ink displays.

## Relevance in [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-15]]
The session discusses how an always‑on [[entities/amoled]] screen is the biggest battery drain on smartwatches. The user is willing to sacrifice AMOLED to achieve a 7‑10 day battery life, and the assistant recommends non‑AMOLED Garmin models as a solution.

## Battery‑life impact
- **Always‑on display** and high brightness are primary culprits that prevent devices with AMOLED from meeting the desired battery targets. See [[concepts/battery-life-optimization]] for strategies to mitigate this.
- Selecting a lower‑power LCD display, as found on the [[entities/garmin-forerunner-255]] and [[entities/garmin-forerunner-165]], can extend daily runtime to a week or more.

## Comparison with other wearables
- **Fitbit** devices (e.g., [[entities/fitbit]]) and **Withings** hybrid watches ([[entities/withings-hybrid-watch]]) typically use LCD or e‑ink screens, helping them achieve longer standby periods.
- Within the [[entities/garmin]] ecosystem, opting for non‑AMOLED variants of the VIVOACTIVE or Forerunner lines balances smart features with battery longevity.

## Key takeaways
- AMOLED offers superior visual experience but is a major battery‑life limiter.
- Users prioritizing multi‑day battery should consider devices that employ LCD or e‑ink displays, especially the Garmin models highlighted in the discussion.
