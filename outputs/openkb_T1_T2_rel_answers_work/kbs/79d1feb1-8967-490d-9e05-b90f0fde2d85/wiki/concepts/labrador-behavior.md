## Labrador Behavior

Labrador retrievers often exhibit a distinctive **"happy mask"** – an open mouth, wagging tail, and generally upbeat facial expression that can hide underlying stress. This breed‑specific behavior influences how owners interpret body language, especially on walks or during social encounters.

### Key Characteristics
- **Masking Stress:** Labs may continue to smile and wag while showing subtle signs of discomfort such as a stiffened torso, narrowed wag, or quick shake‑off after an interaction.
- **Enthusiastic Greeting:** Even when uncertain, Labradors are likely to approach humans actively, which can be mistaken for confidence.
- **Power‑through Tendency:** Labs often persist in interactions they think are expected of them, ignoring early stress signals like premature panting or avoidance glances.
- **Panting Nuances:** Excited panting is accompanied by a loose, bouncy body, whereas stressed panting appears with a tighter mouth, narrower tongue, and overall stiffness.

### Practical Tips for Owners
1. **Watch the Whole Body:** Look beyond the smiling face; a straightened back or reduced body looseness indicates tension.
2. **Detect Early Panting:** If panting starts earlier than usual on a walk and persists without exertion, consider it a stress cue.
3. **Notice Shake‑offs:** A dry shake right after a hug or loud noise signals a need for a reset.
4. **Give Space:** When you see these subtle signs, step back, soften your posture, and let the dog approach on its own.
