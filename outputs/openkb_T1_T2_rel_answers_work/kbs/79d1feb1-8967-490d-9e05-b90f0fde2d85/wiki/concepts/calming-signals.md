# Calming Signals

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Dog calming signals are subtle behaviors that indicate stress or a desire to diffuse tension.

---

## Overview
Calming signals are low‑key cues dogs use to communicate discomfort, uncertainty, or a wish to de‑escalate a social situation. They often appear **out of context**—for example, when a dog licks its lips, yawns, sniffs the ground, shakes off, or pants without a physical reason. Recognizing these signals helps humans respond appropriately and avoid escalating stress.

## Key Signals Identified in [[summaries/session-02_2024-07-10]]
| Signal | Typical (neutral) use | Stress‑related context |
|--------|-----------------------|------------------------|
| **Lip licking** | After eating or drinking | Quick, tiny licks with no food; indicates unease |
| **Yawning** | When tired or bored | Exaggerated yawning during an interaction, especially with strangers or other dogs |
| **Sniffing the ground** | Exploring interesting scents | Sniffing nothing when tension rises; a form of avoidance |
| **Shaking off** | After getting wet or a physical disturbance | Dry shake right after a hug, loud noise, or any stressful event |
| **Panting** | When hot or after exercise | Panting without heat or exertion, paired with a stiff body, wide eyes, or a tight mouth |

## How to Respond
1. **Give space** – Step back or turn sideways to reduce perceived pressure.
2. **Soften your own body language** – Avoid direct, looming eye contact; adopt a relaxed posture.
3. **Allow the dog to approach** – Do not reach over the head or force contact; let the dog come to you on its own terms.
4. **Monitor the overall picture** – Look for additional signs (tail, ears, posture) to confirm stress before intervening.
