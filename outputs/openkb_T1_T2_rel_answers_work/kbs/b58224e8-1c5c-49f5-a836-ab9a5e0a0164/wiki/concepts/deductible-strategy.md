# Deductible Strategy

**Definition**: A method for choosing an auto‑insurance deductible that balances premium cost savings against the amount the policyholder can comfortably pay out‑of‑pocket after a loss.

---

## Core Principles
1. **Financial Comfort Threshold**
   - Identify the maximum amount you could pay without financial strain. In the example from [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-15]], a $1,000 deductible matched the user’s comfort level, while $2,500 would be burdensome.
2. **Premium Impact**
   - Higher deductibles lower the regular premium; lower deductibles raise it. The trade‑off should be quantified based on the user’s budget.
3. **Risk Tolerance**
   - Align the deductible with how much risk you are willing to accept. This aligns with the broader [[concepts/insurance-coverage-decision]] framework of paying for coverage that protects against expenses you cannot reasonably absorb.
4. **Vehicle & Coverage Context**
   - When you already carry comprehensive and collision coverage, the deductible becomes the primary lever for cost control.

---

## Practical Steps (Illustrated by the Session)
1. **Assess Available Savings** – Determine a comfortable out‑of‑pocket amount (e.g., $1,000).
2. **Compare Policy Options** – Review quotes at different deductible levels.
3. **Select the Deductible** – Choose the level that keeps premiums low while staying within the comfort threshold.
4. **Re‑evaluate Periodically** – As the loan balance, vehicle value, or personal finances change, adjust the deductible accordingly.

---

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- The deductible decision is part of an overall [[concepts/insurance-coverage-decision]] process, which also weighs optional add‑ons such as gap insurance or roadside assistance.
- When evaluating whether to add **gap insurance**, the deductible choice may influence the perceived need for additional protection, linking to [[concepts/gap-insurance-evaluation]].

---

## Example Scenario
In the referenced conversation, the driver could comfortably cover a $1,000 surprise expense but not $2,500. The recommendation was to set the deductible at **$1,000**, achieving a balance between lower premiums and manageable out‑of‑pocket exposure.
