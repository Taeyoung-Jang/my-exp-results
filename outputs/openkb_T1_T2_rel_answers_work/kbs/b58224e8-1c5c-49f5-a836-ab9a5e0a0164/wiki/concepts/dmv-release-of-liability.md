# DMV Release of Liability

## Overview
The **DMV Release of Liability** is the official form or electronic submission you file with your local Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) to declare that you are no longer the registered owner of a vehicle. Submitting this release protects you from future tickets, tolls, or violations that may be incurred by the new owner.

## Why It Matters
- **Liability protection** – Without a filed release, the DMV may still list you as the owner, leaving you financially responsible for citations or toll charges.
- **Clear title transfer** – The release is a required step in the broader [[concepts/vehicle-ownership-transfer]] process and must be completed before you cancel insurance or surrender plates.

## Key Steps (as described in [[summaries/session-34_2025-02-15]])
1. **Complete the transfer‑of‑ownership form** provided by the [[entities/dmv]].
2. **Submit the Release‑of‑Liability** either in person, by mail, or online.
3. **Obtain a receipt or confirmation number** – this serves as proof that the agency has received your filing.
4. **Return license plates** (if required by your state) after filing the release.

## Proof and Retention
- Keep the **DMV receipt for plate surrender** and the **release‑of‑liability confirmation** together with the signed title or bill of sale.
- Recommended retention period: at least one year, or until the next registration cycle, aligning with [[concepts/document-retention-periods]].

## Verifying Completion
- Check the vehicle’s record or title status online using the VIN or plate number. The record should show:
  - Your name removed as the registered owner.
  - The release‑of‑liability listed as filed.
- If the DMV still shows you as the owner, contact them with the receipt to correct the record.

## Interaction with Insurance
- Cancel or transfer insurance **after** you have confirmation that the DMV has processed the release. Cancelling too early can trigger a “no insurance on file” notice, which is resolved by providing the release receipt.
