# Summary of session‑39 2025‑04‑16  

**Document type:** conversation – personal‑finance advice (light‑weight, low‑maintenance system)

### Core recommendation: “hub checking + autopay”  
1. **Single checking‑account hub** – route all income in, pay all expenses out.  
2. **Autopay fixed bills** – ideally via a credit‑card that itself autopays to the hub, creating one predictable outflow.  
3. **Autopay credit‑card balances** – set to **statement‑balance** (not full balance) to keep amounts predictable. [[concepts/statement-balance-autopay]]  
4. **Buffer / safety‑net** – keep a minimum floor (e.g., a few hundred dollars) in the checking account to avoid overdrafts. [[concepts/buffer-floor-checking]]  
5. **Monthly/weekly “money reset”** – 10‑15 min review to confirm buffer, spot anomalies, and adjust autopay dates if needed.

### Managing 0 % intro‑rate cards  
* **Autopay the minimum** to guarantee on‑time payment.  
* Make any **extra payoff** manually, allowing flexibility.  
* Align credit‑card due dates to the same week each month for predictability.

### Calculating extra payments for promo payoff  
1. **Basic target** = remaining balance ÷ months left in promo (e.g., $4,800 ÷ 9 ≈ $533 /mo).  
2. Treat this as an *ideal* average; in strong months pay more, in tight months pay less, then rebalance later.  
3. **Monthly check‑in** – compare cumulative payments to *target so far* (months × $533). If behind, increase next good‑month payment; if ahead, you can relax.

### Buffer strategy for irregular income  
* **Passive buffer** – leave the chosen floor in checking as a non‑touched “floor” amount. [[concepts/buffer-floor-checking]]  
* Optional **“fake‑bill”**: treat the buffer as a recurring savings transfer if that mental model helps you build it faster, but the simple version—keep it in checking and don’t touch it—is usually enough.

### Practical example (numbers)  
* Balance: $4,800, promo ends in 9 months, minimum ≈ $45.  
* Target payoff: $533 /mo.  
* Scenario: $600 extra for 3 months, $100 extra for a tight month, then adjust subsequent months to stay on average $533.  
* Monthly check‑in formula: `cumulative_paid – (months_elapsed × $533)` → positive = ahead, negative = behind.
