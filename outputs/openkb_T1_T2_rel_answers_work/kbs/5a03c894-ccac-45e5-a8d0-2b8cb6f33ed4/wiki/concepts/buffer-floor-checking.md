# Buffer Floor in Checking

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A safety‑net amount deliberately left untouched in a checking account so that routine automated payments never cause an overdraft.

---

## Why it matters
In a low‑maintenance personal‑finance system the checking account acts as the central hub for all income and expenses. Since most outflows are automated—fixed‑bill autopay, credit‑card statement‑balance autopay, and minimum‑payment autopay—a sudden large charge or a timing mismatch can push the balance below zero. The *buffer floor* provides a cushion that absorbs unexpected withdrawals and protects against costly overdraft fees.

## How to set it up
1. **Choose a floor amount** – Typically a few hundred dollars, enough to cover one or two typical billing cycles plus a small safety margin.
2. **Treat it as a non‑touchable floor** – Do not schedule any regular debits that would dip below this level. You can think of it as an immutable “minimum balance”.
3. **Monitor periodically** – During the monthly “money reset” (a brief 10‑15 min review) verify that the balance stays at or above the floor. Adjust upcoming inflows/outflows if it is trending downward.
4. **Adjust for income variability** – In high‑income months let the floor grow; in lean months keep spending within the buffer limits. Some people formalise the floor as a recurring "fake bill" that transfers money into a savings account, but the simplest method is to leave the money idle in checking.

## Interaction with other concepts
- Works hand‑in‑hand with the **[[concepts/hub-checking-autopay]]** model, where all automatic payments flow through the same account.
- Complements **[[concepts/statement-balance-autopay]]**, which limits the variability of credit‑card autopay amounts.
- Provides protection against the consequences of **[[concepts/penalty-apr]]** if a credit‑card payment is missed.
