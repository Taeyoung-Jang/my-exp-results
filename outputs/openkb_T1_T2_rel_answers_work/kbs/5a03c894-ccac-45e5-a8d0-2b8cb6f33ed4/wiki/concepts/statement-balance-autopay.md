# Statement‑Balance Autopay

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Autopaying only the statement balance to keep payments predictable and avoid overdrafts.

---

## Overview

Statement‑balance autopay is a payment strategy where a credit‑card holder authorises an automatic transfer that covers **only the amount shown on the monthly statement** (the *statement balance*), rather than the full current balance.  This approach locks in a fixed, known payment each month, reducing the risk that a larger, unexpected charge will drain the checking account and cause an overdraft.

## Why Use It?

- **Predictability:** The payment amount is set when the statement closes, so it does not change due to transactions that occur after the cut‑off date.
- **Overdraft protection:** By limiting the autopay to a known figure, the user can keep a small buffer in the checking account (see [[concepts/buffer-floor-checking]]) without worrying that a surprise charge will trigger a shortfall.
- **Automation with control:** The user still benefits from the convenience of autopay while retaining the ability to make *extra* manual payments toward the balance if desired.

## How It Fits Into a Low‑Stress Money System

In the “hub checking + autopay” model described in [[summaries/session-39_2025-04-16]], the checking account serves as the central hub for all income and expense flows.  Bill payments and credit‑card balances are routed automatically from this hub.  Setting autopay to the *statement balance* aligns with this model by:

1. Keeping the monthly outflow predictable, which makes budgeting around the hub easier.
2. Allowing the user to keep a **buffer floor** in the checking account for safety (see [[concepts/buffer-floor-checking]]).
3. Providing a reliable safety net for the 0 % introductory‑rate cards discussed in the same session: autopay the minimum to avoid late fees, then manually add extra payments as needed.

## Implementation Steps

1. **Identify the statement date** for each credit card (the day the monthly cycle ends).
2. **Set up autopay** in the card’s online portal or through the checking‑account’s bill‑pay feature, selecting the *statement balance* as the payment amount.
3. **Confirm the autopay schedule** aligns with the hub‑checking cash‑flow calendar, ideally within the same week each month.
4. **Maintain a buffer** in the checking account – a few hundred dollars is typical – to cover any incidental fees or unexpected expenses.
5. **Monthly check‑in:** Review the actual payment taken versus the target buffer level.  If the buffer dips below the floor, transfer funds from savings or postpone a discretionary expense.
