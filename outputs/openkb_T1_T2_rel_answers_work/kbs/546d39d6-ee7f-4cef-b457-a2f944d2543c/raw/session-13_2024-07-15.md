**User:** Can you help me put together a simple skincare/sun-protection routine that won’t feel greasy? I’m exposed to pretty intense sunlight on a daily basis since the sky here stays clear most of the time, and I’m starting to notice my skin getting more dry and a bit uneven. I’d love recommendations for sunscreen type (mineral vs chemical), SPF level, how often to reapply if I’m in and out of buildings, and any lightweight moisturizer/after-sun stuff that actually helps.

**Assistant:** Sure, I can put something simple and non‑greasy together.

If your skin is drying out and getting uneven from strong sun, a lightweight moisturizer plus a reliable sunscreen is usually enough. Most people who dislike a greasy feel do better with modern chemical sunscreens because they sink in quickly and don’t leave a cast, but if your skin is on the sensitive side, a mineral option can be gentler. Either way, an SPF 30 or 50 is the safest zone for daily strong‑sun exposure.

Since you’re moving in and out of buildings, reapplying every two to three hours while you’re outdoors is usually enough. If you’re mostly inside with short sun bursts, you can stretch it a bit unless you’re sweating a lot.

For a moisturizer, look for something labeled “gel cream” or “oil‑free” so it feels light. Ingredients like glycerin, hyaluronic acid, and ceramides are helpful without heaviness. For after-sun comfort, an aloe or panthenol gel does a nice job calming dryness without adding grease.

If you want, you can tell me your skin type and I can tailor it even more.

**User:** My skin is pretty normal leaning dry, and I’m prone to a bit of stinging if products have fragrance or alcohol. I also have a medium/tan skin tone, so I’d like to avoid anything that leaves a white cast. Can you suggest a few specific sunscreen textures/formats that stay matte (or at least not shiny), plus a simple AM/PM routine with product-type suggestions? Also, how much sunscreen should I actually be using on my face/neck?

**Assistant:** Given your normal‑to‑dry skin and sensitivity to fragrance and alcohol, you’ll probably be happiest with a lightweight, alcohol‑free chemical sunscreen in a gel‑cream or fluid texture. These tend to dry down closer to a natural or soft‑matte finish and won’t leave a cast on medium/tan skin. Mineral formulas that avoid a white cast are improving, but they’re still hit‑or‑miss, so a gentle chemical option is usually easier.

For an easy morning routine, think of it as a quick hydrate‑and‑protect setup. A simple, fragrance‑free gel‑cream moisturizer with hyaluronic acid or ceramides should give you enough hydration without feeling heavy. Once that sinks in, follow with your sunscreen of choice in a fluid or gel‑cream texture. If you want a more matte finish, look for sunscreens labeled as “matte,” “oil‑free,” or “dry touch.”

At night, keep it equally simple. Cleanse with a mild, low‑foam cleanser, then use a slightly richer but still fragrance‑free moisturizer so your skin can make up for the daytime sun exposure. If you want an extra boost, a gentle after‑sun soothing gel with aloe or panthenol can be layered before your moisturizer when your skin feels warm or tight.

For the amount of sunscreen, using about two strips along your index and middle finger is a good benchmark for the face and neck. Spread it generously rather than trying to stretch a thin layer. And if you’re outdoors on and off, reapplying every couple of hours while you’re exposed is usually enough.

Many people carry a small travel tube of their main sunscreen and use it when they’ll be in the sun for more than a few minutes; sticks are more of an “in a pinch” option.

If you want, I can narrow the choices further based on whether you prefer a completely matte look or more of a natural finish.
