## Tide Monitoring

**Definition**: Tide monitoring is the practice of checking current and forecasted tide heights to determine whether a waterfront boardwalk is safe for use or likely to be submerged.

### Why It Matters
- On high tide, the boardwalk can become flooded or slippery, creating hazards for both the walker and the dog.
- Accurate tide information allows walkers to plan routes, set thresholds, and decide when to switch to a backup inland path.

### Key Elements from [[summaries/session-32_2025-03-16]]
1. **Threshold Setting**
   - Identify the tide height at which the boardwalk becomes unsafe (e.g., `___ ft`).
   - A suggested starting point is the local high‑tide mark minus 1 ft, often around 4–6 ft depending on location.
2. **Timing Checks**
   - Consult tide charts before leaving and note whether the tide is at or above the threshold.
   - If the tide is rising and within about an hour of peak, treat conditions as marginal and opt for the backup route.
3. **Decision Flow**
   - *First* check the tide. If it exceeds the threshold, go straight to the inland route.
   - If tide is safe, then evaluate visibility and wind before deciding to use the boardwalk.

### Practical Tips for Walkers
- **Use reliable sources**: local marine forecasts, tide tables, or dedicated apps.
- **Record observations**: note the actual waterline on the boardwalk over several days to refine your personal threshold.
- **Combine with other factors**: high tide alone isn’t always a no‑go; consider visibility and wind as well.

### Quick Reference Checklist
- **Before leaving**: Check tide height → Compare to personal threshold → Mark OK/No‑go.
- **At the entrance**: Observe boardwalk surface (wet, shiny, sounds of water) → Confirm tide condition.
- **During walk**: Re‑assess if tide appears to rise unexpectedly; switch to backup route if needed.

Implementing systematic tide monitoring helps ensure a safe, stress‑free waterfront walk for you and your dog.
