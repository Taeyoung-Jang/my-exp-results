# Visibility Estimation

**Definition**
Visibility estimation is the practice of quickly gauging how far you can see in low‑visibility conditions—especially fog—without specialized instruments. It helps decide whether it is safe to use a waterfront boardwalk or if a backup inland route is required.

**Why it matters**
Accurate, on‑the‑spot visibility checks are a core part of the [[concepts/dog-walk-safety]] routine described in [[summaries/session-32_2025-03-16]]. When visibility drops below a user‑defined threshold, the checklist advises switching to a safer, land‑based path.

**Key techniques from the source**
1. **Landmark test** – Choose a fixed object (tree, sign, house) that lies roughly at your minimum safe distance. If you can see it clearly, visibility meets the threshold; if it disappears, visibility is insufficient.
2. **Car‑length count** – Approximate distance by counting parked cars: each car length ≈ 5 yards. Stack the count until you reach the desired yardage.
3. **Residential lot width** – Many lots span 15–20 yards. Visualizing one or two lot widths provides a quick yard‑estimate.

**Suggested starting thresholds**
- Minimum comfortable visibility: **80–100 yards** (about one long city block). Users can fill in a custom value on their checklist.
- If the farthest landmark is invisible, treat visibility as *poor* and trigger the backup route.

**Integration with other concepts**
- Visibility estimation works alongside [[concepts/tide-monitoring]] to form a complete safety decision tree for waterfront walks.
- It is also relevant to broader outdoor safety planning, where quick visual assessments guide route selection.

**Practical checklist excerpt**
```
Visibility Threshold ___ yards (default 80‑100)
Quick Test:
- Spot known landmark at threshold distance?  Yes / No
- Count parked‑car lengths to estimate distance
- Visualize lot widths for yard estimate
```

By using these low‑tech methods, walkers can make reliable go/no‑go decisions even in dense fog, keeping both themselves and their dogs safe.
