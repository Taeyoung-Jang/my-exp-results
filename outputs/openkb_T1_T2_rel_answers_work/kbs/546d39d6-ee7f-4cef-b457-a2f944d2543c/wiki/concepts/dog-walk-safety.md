# Dog Walk Safety

Dog Walk Safety is a set of practical guidelines and a printable checklist designed to keep both dog and handler safe when walking along waterfront boardwalks where tide, fog, wind, and slippery surfaces can create hazards. The concept draws primarily from the recommendations in [[summaries/session-32_2025-03-16]].

## Core Elements

1. **Tide Monitoring** – Determine whether the boardwalk will be submerged or excessively wet. Use local tide charts and treat the posted high‑tide height as a threshold; if the water level is at or above this point, consider the boardwalk a no‑go. See [[concepts/tide-monitoring]] for common methods.

2. **Visibility Estimation** – Assess fog density without special tools by checking if a known landmark (e.g., a tree, house, sign) within a set distance remains visible. Typical starting thresholds are 80–100 yards (about one long city block). For more detail, refer to [[concepts/visibility-estimation]].

3. **Wind Assessment** – Feel for steady, on‑shore gusts and listen for water slapping the boardwalk. When wind exceeds around 15 mph (caution) or 20 mph (no‑go), abort the waterfront route.

4. **Gear Recommendations** – In low‑visibility conditions, equip the dog with a small clip‑on light, wear a low‑beam headlamp, and both wear reflective items. Waterproof clothing and shoes with good traction are essential on slick planks.

5. **Backup Route Strategy** – Have a predefined inland loop that never depends on tide or visibility. Switch to this route automatically if any safety threshold is crossed or if the boardwalk appears wet, shiny, or you hear water beneath it.

## Checklist Workflow

- **Before you leave**: check tide, visibility, wind; attach gear.
- **At the entrance**: inspect boardwalk surface; verify visibility to the chosen landmark.
- **During the walk**: monitor fog and wind; retreat to the backup route if conditions deteriorate.

The checklist is intentionally printable and customizable, with blanks for user‑specific tide heights, visibility distances, and wind limits.
