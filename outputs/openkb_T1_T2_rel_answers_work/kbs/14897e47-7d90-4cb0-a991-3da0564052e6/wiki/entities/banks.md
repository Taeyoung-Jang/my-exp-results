# Banks

**Type:** organization

Banks are financial institutions that must comply with a range of regulatory regimes, notably [[concepts/anti_money_laundering]], [[concepts/know_your_customer]] and international [[concepts/tax_reporting]] obligations such as FATCA and CRS.  In the context of **session‑38 (2024‑12‑13)**, banks are described as:

- Requiring strict identity verification for customers with foreign ties, often demanding an unexpired foreign passport.
- Accepting alternative government‑issued IDs (national ID card, residence permit, driver’s licence) when a passport is unavailable, provided they satisfy the same regulatory standards.
- Requesting additional documentation such as proof of address, tax identification numbers, source‑of‑funds evidence, and specific tax forms like the [[entities/w_8ben]] for non‑U.S. persons.
- Placing accounts in a “pending documentation” status when required documents are missing, which typically leaves deposits intact but restricts outgoing transfers, wire payments, debit‑card use, and online access.
- Allowing customers to request a written list of acceptable documents, the exact compliance deadline, and the regulatory basis for the requirement.  Escalation paths include speaking with the branch manager and, if needed, the bank’s compliance department.

These practices illustrate how banks operationalise their legal duties under frameworks administered by entities such as [[entities/fatca]] and [[entities/crs]], and why they may enforce stringent document policies for foreign‑linked customers.
