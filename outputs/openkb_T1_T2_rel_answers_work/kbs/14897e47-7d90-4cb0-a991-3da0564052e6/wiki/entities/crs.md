# CRS (Common Reporting Standard)

**Type:** other

The **Common Reporting Standard (CRS)** is a global standard for the automatic exchange of financial account information between tax authorities. It was developed by the Organisation for Economic Co‑operation and Development (OECD) to combat tax evasion and improve [[concepts/tax_reporting]] compliance worldwide.

## Role in Banking
- Banks are required to collect and report information on account holders who are tax residents of participating jurisdictions.
- Compliance with CRS often overlaps with [[concepts/anti_money_laundering]] and [[concepts/know_your_customer]] obligations, as banks must verify identity, residency, and source‑of‑funds details.
- In the context of [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-13]], banks referenced CRS when explaining why they need foreign passports, tax identification numbers, and forms such as the [[entities/w_8ben]] for non‑U.S. persons.

## Interaction with Other Regulations
- CRS works alongside the U.S. **Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA)**, which imposes similar reporting requirements for U.S. taxpayers. Both frameworks increase the documentation burden on banks, especially for customers with cross‑border ties.
- While FATCA is U.S.‑specific, CRS is a multilateral agreement adopted by many countries, including the [[entities/united-states]] (which participates via FATCA) and numerous other jurisdictions.

## Practical Implications for Customers
- Customers may be asked for a **foreign passport**, national ID, or other government‑issued ID to establish tax residency under CRS.
- Failure to provide the required documentation can lead to account restrictions, such as limited transaction capabilities, until compliance is achieved.

## Related Entities and Concepts
- [[entities/banks]] implement CRS reporting as part of their compliance programs.
- The standard is closely linked to [[concepts/anti_money_laundering]] and [[concepts/know_your_customer]] frameworks, which together form the backbone of financial regulatory compliance.
