# Know Your Customer (KYC)

**Definition**: A set of procedures banks and financial institutions follow to confirm the identity of their clients, understand the nature of their activities, and mitigate risks related to money laundering, terrorist financing, and tax evasion.

---

## Why KYC Matters
- **Regulatory compliance**: Required by laws and regulations such as [[entities/fatca]] and the [[entities/crs]] framework.
- **Risk management**: Helps institutions detect and prevent illicit activity, tying directly into [[concepts/anti_money_laundering]].
- **Tax reporting**: Enables accurate reporting of foreign income and assets, supporting obligations under [[concepts/tax_reporting]].

---

## Core Elements of KYC
1. **Customer Identification Program (CIP)**
   - Collection of government‑issued ID (passport, national ID, driver’s licence).
   - Verification of authenticity and expiration status.
2. **Customer Due Diligence (CDD)**
   - Assessment of the customer’s source of funds, transaction patterns, and risk profile.
   - May require additional documentation such as tax identification numbers and forms like [[entities/w_8ben]].
3. **Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)**
   - Applied to high‑risk customers, e.g., those with foreign citizenship, multiple tax residencies, or large cross‑border transactions.
   - Involves deeper checks, possibly requesting proof of foreign passport renewal or detailed income statements.

---

## Practical Example from [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-13]]
- A customer with foreign ties was asked for an unexpired foreign passport, reflecting the bank’s KYC obligation to verify identity against international standards.
- The bank also requested tax‑related documents (e.g., a signed W‑8BEN) to satisfy both KYC and [[concepts/tax_reporting]] requirements.
- When the passport was unavailable, the bank suggested alternative IDs (national ID, residence permit) and a temporary “pending documentation” status, illustrating how KYC processes can be flexible yet must adhere to regulatory deadlines.

---

## Interaction with Other Concepts & Entities
- **[[concepts/anti_money_laundering]]**: KYC is a foundational component of AML programs, providing the first line of defense against illicit funds.
- **[[entities/banks]]**: Implement KYC policies to remain compliant with domestic and international regulations.
- **[[entities/fatca]]** and **[[entities/crs]]**: Drive specific KYC data‑collection requirements for U.S. tax residents and global tax exchange participants, respectively.
- **[[concepts/tax_reporting]]**: KYC data feeds the reporting obligations to tax authorities worldwide.

---

## Typical Customer Experience
- Initial account opening: submission of primary ID and proof of address.
- Ongoing monitoring: periodic updates when the customer’s circumstances change (e.g., new citizenship, passport renewal).
- Escalation: if required documents are missing, the bank may place the account in a restricted state and request a written list of acceptable documents and deadlines, as noted in the session‑38 case.

---

**Key Takeaway**: KYC balances regulatory compliance, risk mitigation, and customer service by requiring clear, verifiable identification and ongoing diligence, especially for customers with international links.
