# Work Authorization Phrasing

This concept captures the recommended wording to communicate that a candidate is legally permitted to work in the United States on resumes, cover letters, and application forms.

## Core Recommendations
- Use a concise, neutral line such as **“Authorized to work in the United States”** or **“U.S. work authorization**”.
- For U.S. citizens, **“U.S. citizen**” is acceptable, but the neutral phrasing is often preferred for broader applicability.
- Avoid legalistic language like “eligible” or overly detailed statements about sponsorship unless the job posting explicitly requests it.

## Placement Guidance
- Place the phrase as a **single line directly beneath contact details** in the resume header (see [[concepts/resume_header_placement]]).
- In a cover letter, embed it in a brief sentence, e.g., “I am fully authorized to work in the United States and do not require sponsorship.”

## Sponsorship Mention
- Do **not** include a “no sponsorship required” clause if the authorization statement is already present. Add it only when the posting specifically asks about sponsorship (see [[concepts/sponsorship_statement]]).

## Relation to Citizenship Mention
- Mentioning **“U.S. citizen”** is a valid alternative but is more personal; it signals eligibility implicitly. Refer to [[concepts/citizenship_mention]] for when this approach is appropriate.
