# Anti‑Money Laundering

Anti‑Money Laundering (AML) refers to the set of laws, regulations, and procedures designed to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate revenue. Financial institutions must implement AML controls to detect, report, and deter suspicious activity that could facilitate money‑laundering, terrorist financing, or other financial crimes.

## Core Elements
- **Customer Due Diligence (CDD):** Verifying the identity of customers and understanding the nature of their business relationships.
- **Transaction Monitoring:** Ongoing analysis of account activity to spot patterns that may indicate illicit behavior.
- **Reporting Obligations:** Filing suspicious activity reports (SARs) and other regulatory filings when required.
- **Risk Assessment:** Evaluating the risk profile of customers, especially those with foreign ties, to apply appropriate levels of scrutiny.

- **Citizenship considerations** – While not a standalone concept in this wiki, the need to verify foreign citizenship aligns with AML risk management.

## Practical Implications for Customers
- Be prepared to provide multiple forms of identification (passport, national ID, residence permit) and proof of address.
- Maintain up‑to‑date tax documentation, such as the W‑8BEN form, to satisfy both AML and tax‑reporting requirements.
- Understand that banks may place accounts in a "pending documentation" status, limiting certain functionalities until AML compliance is confirmed.
