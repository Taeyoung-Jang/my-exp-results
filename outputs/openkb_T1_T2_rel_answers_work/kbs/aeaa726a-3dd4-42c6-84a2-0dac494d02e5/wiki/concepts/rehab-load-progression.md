# Rehab Load Progression

**Definition**: A structured approach to incrementally increase rehabilitation exercise intensity and weight‑bearing based on how the injured leg responds, aiming to avoid overloading while promoting healing.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-15_2024-04-12]])
1. **During‑exercise pain monitoring**
   - Pain should stay at or below a mild level (≤ 3/10). Higher pain suggests the load is too high and signals a need to back off.
   - No compensatory bracing, breath‑holding, or altered movement patterns.
2. **Next‑day evaluation**
   - Use the [[concepts/next-day-check]]: mild, fading soreness is acceptable; increased pain, stiffness, or reduced confidence indicates overloading.
3. **Recognizing overloading**
   - Watch for [[concepts/overloading-signs]] such as sharp pain spikes, swelling, guarding, or a loss of trust in the joint.
4. **Pain differentiation**
   - Distinguish normal post‑exercise soreness (dull, transient) from irritation pain (sharp, localized, persistent) as described in [[concepts/pain-cues]].
5. **Partial weight‑bearing progression**
   - When full weight‑bearing isn’t possible, apply the [[concepts/partial-weight-bearing]] guidelines: start with light pressure, ensure no sharp pain at the fracture site, and increase load in small, controlled steps.

## Practical Rule‑of‑Thumb Workflow
| Step | Action | Decision Criterion |
|------|--------|--------------------|
| 1️⃣ | Perform rehab exercise | Pain ≤ 3/10, no altered mechanics |
| 2️⃣ | End of session | Record any immediate discomfort |
| 3️⃣️⃣ | Next‑day check [[concepts/next-day-check]] | If pain unchanged or improved → maintain or slightly increase load; if pain worsens or confidence drops → reduce load |
| 4️⃣ | Adjust load | Small increments (e.g., add 5‑10% more reps or a slight increase in weight) only if both checks are clear |
| 5️⃣ | Repeat cycle | Continue monitoring for [[concepts/overloading-signs]] |

## When to Pause or Regress
- Sharp or localized pain at the injury site during or after loading.
- Noticeable increase in pain or stiffness the following day.
- Feeling of the leg being “untrustworthy” or a tendency to guard.
- Any swelling or new tenderness.

## Safety Note
All progression should be aligned with clinician clearance. The guidelines above complement professional advice and are meant for self‑monitoring between appointments.
