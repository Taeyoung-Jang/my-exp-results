# Overloading Signs

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Observable cues that suggest a rehabilitation exercise or weight‑bearing increase is too aggressive for the healing leg.

---

## Core Indicators

1. **Pain spikes during activity** – Sharp or localized pain that rises sharply when loading the injured area, rather than staying at a mild, steady level.
2. **Increased swelling or tenderness** – New or worsening swelling, or tenderness to light pressure around the fracture or sprain site.
3. **Guarding or altered mechanics** – Unconscious bracing, limp, or avoidance of certain angles that changes the normal movement pattern.
4. **Next‑day symptom regression** – Pain that is clearly worse the following day, or a reduced willingness to bear weight compared with the previous day.
5. **Loss of confidence in the limb** – Feeling that the joint or bone might “give way,” leading to hesitation in loading the leg.

These signs map directly onto the practical checks described in [[summaries/session-15_2024-04-12]], where the assistant recommends monitoring both **during‑exercise** pain levels and a **next‑day check**.

## Practical Use

When planning or reviewing a rehab session, ask:

- **During the exercise:** Is the pain staying ≤ 3/10 and not sharp?
- **After the session (next‑day):** Are symptoms the same or improved, or have they worsened?
- **If any overloading sign appears:** Reduce the load, rest, and reassess before progressing.
