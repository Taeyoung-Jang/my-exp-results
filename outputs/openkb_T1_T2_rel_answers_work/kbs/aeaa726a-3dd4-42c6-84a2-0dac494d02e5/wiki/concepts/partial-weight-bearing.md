# Partial Weight-Bearing

**Definition**: A rehabilitative strategy that gradually reintroduces load onto a recovering leg—often with crutches, a boot, or other assistive devices—by monitoring pain, confidence, and functional response.

---

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-15_2024-04-12]])
- **Light Pressure Tolerance**: The fracture or injury site should be painless under gentle pressure before adding weight.
- **Controlled Load Increments**: Add a small amount of weight (e.g., a few pounds or a short step) and assess how the leg reacts. The sensation should be *controlled* rather than jarring.
- **Pain Monitoring**: During the load, discomfort must stay mild (≤ 3/10) and should not become sharp or localized at the injury site.
- **Next‑Day Evaluation**: Apply the [[concepts/next-day-check]] after each change. If pain is the same or improves, the progression is acceptable; if pain worsens or confidence drops, reduce the load.
- **Overloading Detection**: Watch for [[concepts/overloading-signs]] such as sharp pain at the fracture, a “angry” feeling after loading, increased swelling, or a loss of confidence in the leg.
- **Pain Differentiation**: Distinguish normal muscle soreness from irritation pain using [[concepts/pain-cues]]—sharp, persistent pain signals a need to back off.

---

## Practical Rules of Thumb
1. **Baseline Check**: Ensure the leg is calm at rest and the injured area is not tender.
2. **Increment Test**: Apply a minimal load (e.g., shift weight onto crutches with a slight foot press) and note the pain level.
3. **Immediate Feedback**: Pain should stay mild and not change movement patterns.
4. **24‑Hour Follow‑Up**: Use the [[concepts/next-day-check]] to confirm there is no increase in pain or stiffness.
5. **Adjust Accordingly**:
   - *Progress* if the next‑day check shows same or reduced symptoms.
   - *Hold steady* if symptoms are unchanged but not worsening.
   - *Back off* if any [[concepts/overloading-signs]] appear or pain exceeds the mild threshold.

---

## Relation to Overall Rehab Load Progression
Partial weight‑bearing is a specific subset of [[concepts/rehab-load-progression]]. It focuses on the mechanical load aspect, complementing other elements such as exercise intensity and range‑of‑motion work. Successful weight‑bearing progression relies on the same feedback loops: monitoring pain cues, respecting overloading signs, and applying the next‑day check.

---

## Summary
Partial weight‑bearing provides a safe, stepwise pathway to return functional loading to a healing leg. By adhering to light‑pressure tolerance, mild pain thresholds, and systematic next‑day evaluation, patients can confidently increase weight‑bearing without risking re‑injury.
