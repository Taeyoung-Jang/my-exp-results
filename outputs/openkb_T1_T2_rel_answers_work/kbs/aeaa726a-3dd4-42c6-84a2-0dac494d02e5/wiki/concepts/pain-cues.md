# Pain Cues

**Definition**: Indicators used during rehabilitation to distinguish harmless muscle soreness from pain that suggests the underlying injury is being irritated or re‑injured.

**Key characteristics (from [[summaries/session-15_2024-04-12]] )**
- **Normal post‑exercise soreness**  
  - Dull, tired ache in the muscles.  
  - Appears a few hours after activity, fades within a day.  
  - Does not alter movement pattern; can be warmed up out of it.
- **Irritation pain**  
  - Sharper, more localized to the injury site.  
  - Often arises during the exercise, persists or worsens the next day.  
  - May cause limp, guarding, hesitancy to bear weight, or a change in movement mechanics.

**How pain cues inform progression**
- During a set, pain should stay in the mild range (≤ 3/10). Exceeding this suggests **[[concepts/overloading-signs]]**.  
- The **[[concepts/next-day-check]]** looks for reduced or stable discomfort; worsening pain signals a need to back off.  
- When increasing **[[concepts/partial-weight-bearing]]** or overall **[[concepts/rehab-load-progression]]**, pain cues are the primary feedback mechanism.

**Practical rule of thumb**
> If the discomfort is a mild, diffuse ache that disappears within 24 hours, continue or modestly increase load.  
> If the pain is sharp, localized, or remains elevated the next day, reduce load and re‑evaluate.

**Clinical relevance**
Proper interpretation of pain cues helps prevent **[[concepts/overloading-signs]]**, supports safe load progression, and aligns with clinician‑guided milestones.
