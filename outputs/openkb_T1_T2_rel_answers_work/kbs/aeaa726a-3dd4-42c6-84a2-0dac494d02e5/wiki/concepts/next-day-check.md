# Next-Day Check

**Definition**: A short‑term follow‑up assessment performed on the day after a rehab session to determine whether pain, swelling, or functional confidence have improved, stayed the same, or worsened.

## Why It Matters
The "next‑day check" acts as a safety net in [[concepts/rehab-load-progression]]. It helps differentiate between normal post‑exercise soreness and irritation pain that indicates over‑loading. A positive check (same or better than before) supports maintaining or advancing the load, while a negative check (increased pain, stiffness, loss of confidence) signals the need to back off.

## Key Indicators
- **Pain level**: Should be equal to or less than the prior day's pain, and typically ≤ 3/10 if it is still present.
- **Soreness**: Mild, dull, and fading within 24 hours.
- **Functional confidence**: Ability to bear weight or perform the same movement without guardedness.
- **Swelling or stiffness**: Minimal or unchanged; any increase is a red flag.

## Decision Guidance
| Next‑Day Check Outcome | Recommended Action |
|------------------------|--------------------|
| Pain ≤ previous day, soreness fading, confidence stable or rising | Keep load the same or progress slightly (see [[concepts/rehab-load-progression]]) |
| Pain increased, new sharp pain, or reduced confidence | Reduce load, rest, and reassess before progressing |

## Practical Application
1. **During rehab**: Keep pain ≤ 3/10 and note any compensations.
2. **After the session**: Log pain level, soreness quality, and any functional changes.
3. **Next morning**: Review the log:
   - If the pain score is unchanged or lower and you can move without guarding, you pass the check.
   - If pain spikes, you feel hesitant to weight‑bear, or swelling appears, you fail the check.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- Tied to [[concepts/pain-cues]] for distinguishing normal soreness from irritation.
- Informs decisions about [[concepts/partial-weight-bearing]] progression.
- Helps identify [[concepts/overloading-signs]] early.
