# Tapering Practice

**Definition**: A strategic short‑term decrease in workout volume and/or intensity in the days leading up to a performance or workshop, aimed at preserving technical sharpness, timing, and stamina while preventing fatigue or injury.

## Core Principles
1. **Gradual volume reduction** – Sessions get shorter each day, with the most significant cut in the final 24–48 hours.
2. **Shift from “pushing” to “polishing”** – Maintain focus on precision, timing, and musicality rather than maximal effort.
3. **Maintain a light conditioning component** – Include brief core or mobility work to keep the body primed without taxing the legs.
4. **Monitor overload signs** – Watch for excessive soreness or pain cues that indicate insufficient recovery.

## Application in Session 35 (2024‑10‑10)
The 7‑day plan presented in [[summaries/session-35_2024-10-10]] exemplifies tapering practice for a dancer preparing for a weekend workshop:
- **Monday (≈1 h)** – Relaxed run‑throughs, emphasis on timing and transitions.
- **Tuesday (≈75 min)** – 80 % effort runs, music‑focused drills.
- **Wednesday (≈90 min)** – Peak intensity day with a full‑power run, followed by detail polishing.
- **Thursday (≈1 h)** – Light marking, timing drills, gentle conditioning.
- **Friday (≈45 min)** – Rhythm drills and small‑section work, no full runs.
- **Saturday (≈30 min)** – Very light walk‑through, stretching, mental review.
- **Sunday** – Minimal warm‑up, conserving energy for the workshop.

The schedule demonstrates a clear taper: session length drops from 90 min to 30 min, intensity shifts from high effort to low effort, and the focus moves from full run‑throughs to timing drills and mental rehearsal.

## Practical Tips
- **Plan the taper ahead** – Map out session lengths and focus areas at least a week before the event.
- **Track fatigue** – Use a simple rating of perceived exertion (RPE) to ensure you’re not over‑reaching.
- **Prioritize recovery** – Include adequate sleep, hydration, and light mobility work.
- **Mental rehearsal** – Use the reduced sessions to visualize choreography and reinforce musicality.
