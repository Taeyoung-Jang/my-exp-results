# Summary of session-15_2024-04-12

**Topic:** How to determine safe progression during leg injury rehabilitation, especially for a fibula fracture with limited weight‑bearing.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/rehab-load-progression]]** – Incremental increase of exercise intensity and weight‑bearing based on how the leg responds.
- **[[concepts/pain-cues]]** – Distinguishing normal post‑exercise soreness (dull, fades within a day) from irritation pain (sharp, localized, persists or worsens).
- **[[concepts/next-day-check]]** – Evaluating pain and functionality the day after a session; worsening pain or reduced confidence signals a need to back off.
- **[[concepts/partial-weight-bearing]]** – Gradual reintroduction of load using crutches, boot, or assistive devices, guided by tolerance and symptom trends.
- **[[concepts/overloading-signs]]** – Pain spikes during activity, swelling, guarding, loss of confidence, or next‑day setbacks.

## Practical Rules of Thumb
1. **During Exercise:**
   - Pain should stay ≤ 3/10 on a ten‑point scale.
   - No compensatory bracing, breath‑holding, or altered movement patterns.
2. **Next‑Day Evaluation:**
   - Acceptable: mild, fading soreness; same or improved confidence.
   - Red flag: increased pain, reluctance to bear weight, new stiffness.
3. **Partial Weight‑Bearing Progression:**
   - Light pressure on the fracture site should be painless.
   - Small increments of load should feel controlled, not jarring.
   - Monitor for sharp pain at the fracture site or heightened sensitivity the following day.

## Decision Flow
- **If both checks are clear →** maintain or slightly increase load.
- **If irritation pain or next‑day worsening →** reduce load, rest, and reassess.
- **If uncertain →** consult the clinician before advancing.

## Clinical Caveats
- Follow clinician‑approved milestones; these guidelines complement, not replace, professional advice.
- Healing timelines vary; small, monitored changes over 1‑2 days are safer than large jumps.
