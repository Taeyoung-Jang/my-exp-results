# Early Shift Energy Management

**Definition**: A set of practical strategies that help individuals adapt to a work schedule that begins at or before 4 am, preserving daytime alertness, supporting good nighttime sleep, and maintaining a modest evening social life.

---

## Overview
The concept draws on the recommendations from [[summaries/session-36_2024-10-21]], focusing on how to align physiological rhythms with an early shift. It combines several sub‑strategies that together form a coherent routine.

---

## Core Practices
1. **Gradual Schedule Adjustment** – Shift bedtime earlier in 15‑20 minute steps every few nights so the body can adapt without a sharp disruption.  
2. **Light Exposure** – Seek bright natural or artificial light immediately after waking to cue the brain for daytime activity. Dim lights and limit screens in the hour before sleep. See [[concepts/light-exposure]].
3. **Wind‑down Routine** – Establish a consistent 10‑minute quiet period before bed (e.g., reading, gentle stretching) to signal sleep onset. See [[concepts/wind-down-routine]].
4. **Caffeine Timing** – Restrict caffeine to the first half of the shift and set a cut‑off around 1 pm for a target bedtime of 7:30–8:00 pm. This aligns with the [[concepts/caffeine-cutoff]] principle.
5. **Coffee‑Nap** – If a short nap is planned during a lunch break, a modest coffee taken just before the nap can boost post‑nap alertness. See [[concepts/coffee-nap]].
6. **Nap Strategy** – Keep naps to 15‑20 minutes and schedule them in the late morning or early afternoon (e.g., around an 11:30 am lunch break). Longer or later naps interfere with nighttime sleep. See [[concepts/nap-strategy]].
7. **Mid‑Afternoon Slump Management** – Counter low energy with movement (walk, stretch), bright light exposure, or a protein‑rich snack rather than additional caffeine. See [[concepts/mid-afternoon-slump-management]].
8. **Micro‑Evenings** – Accept that long nights out are limited; instead create a brief, enjoyable evening routine (simple dinner, a show, a chat) and protect 1–2 evenings per week for social activities. See [[concepts/micro-evenings]].

| 3:30 pm | Brief walk or stretch to beat slump |
| 5:30 pm | End shift, begin wind‑down (dim lights) |
| 7:30 pm | Aim to fall asleep |

## Why It Matters
Implementing these coordinated tactics helps early‑shift workers:
- Maintain consistent daytime performance without excessive caffeine dependence.
- Preserve nighttime sleep quality, preventing chronic fatigue.
- Retain a sense of normalcy in their social and personal life, reducing burnout risk.
