# Coffee Nap

A **coffee nap** is a technique where a modest amount of coffee is consumed immediately before a short (15‑20 minute) nap. The caffeine begins to take effect as the nap ends, amplifying alertness when you wake.

## How It Works
- Caffeine takes about 20 minutes to reach peak blood‑level concentration. By timing a nap so you wake up as the caffeine kicks in, you get the combined restorative effect of the nap and the stimulating effect of caffeine.
- The nap should be brief (≈15‑20 minutes); longer naps can lead to sleep inertia and interfere with nighttime sleep.

## Practical Guidance from [[summaries/session-36_2024-10-21]]
- Ideal for early‑shift workers who have a lunch break around **11:30 am**. Drink a small coffee **right before** the 15‑20‑minute nap.
- Keep the coffee modest (e.g., a single shot or a weak brew) to avoid excess caffeine later in the day.
- Pair with a **[[concepts/caffeine-cutoff]]** strategy: ensure the last caffeine of the day is no later than ~1 pm to protect bedtime.
- Use as part of an overall **[[concepts/early-shift-energy-management]]** plan, alongside light exposure, nap timing, and evening wind‑down.

## Benefits
- Boosts post‑nap alertness more than a nap alone.
- Reduces the need for additional caffeine later in the shift, helping maintain the **[[concepts/caffeine-cutoff]]**.
- Supports steady energy without compromising the **[[concepts/wind-down-routine]]** needed for a consistent **[[concepts/sleep-schedule-adjustment]]**.

## When to Use
- During a scheduled lunch break when a short nap is feasible.
- When you need a quick reset but must still fall asleep around **7:30‑8:00 pm**.
- As an alternative to a second cup of coffee later in the day.
