## Caffeine Cutoff

**Definition**: The latest time of day a person should consume caffeine to minimize its impact on nighttime sleep quality.

### Why it matters
- Caffeine is a stimulant that can remain in the body for 5‑7 hours, potentially delaying the onset of sleep.
- Setting a personal cutoff helps maintain consistent sleep hygiene while still allowing daytime energy boosts.

### Typical practice highlighted in [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-23]] and [[summaries/session-36_2024-10-21]]
- In the earlier schedule (session‑14), the user aimed to **avoid coffee after noon**, with the final cup by **11:30 am**.
- In the newer early‑shift context (session‑36), the recommendation shifts to a **latest caffeine intake around 1 pm** to support a target bedtime of **7:30–8:00 pm**. This later cutoff aligns with the longer wake‑up time and still respects the 6‑hour buffer before sleep.

### Strategies to support a caffeine cutoff
1. **Morning‑focused coffee routine** – Have the first cup with breakfast and a second cup before the cutoff (e.g., 10:30‑11:30 am for a noon cutoff, or 12:30‑1:00 pm for a 1 pm cutoff).
2. **Coffee‑nap technique** – If a short nap is taken around the lunch break (e.g., 11:30 am), a modest coffee *just before* the nap can boost alertness as the nap ends, while still keeping the overall caffeine window early enough.
3. **Non‑caffeinated alternatives** – Switch to water, herbal tea, or sparkling water after the cutoff.
4. **Backup energy boosts** – Use protein‑rich “crash snacks” (Greek yogurt, apple + nut butter, hummus + crackers) or posture‑reset movements instead of caffeine.
5. **Mid‑afternoon slump management** – Incorporate brief light exposure, a quick walk, or a protein snack rather than another caffeine dose.

### Interaction with other concepts
- Part of a broader **daily routine design** that balances work, meals, exercise, and sleep.
- Influences the timing of [[concepts/wind-down-routine]] because avoiding late‑day stimulants helps the body wind down more naturally.
- Supports [[concepts/early-shift-energy-management]] by aligning stimulant intake with the demands of an early wake‑up.
- Connects to [[concepts/mid-afternoon-slump-management]] as an alternative strategy for sustaining alertness.

### Practical tips
- Mark the cutoff time on your calendar or set a reminder (e.g., 1 pm for an early‑shift schedule).
- Keep a stash of low‑sugar, high‑protein snacks handy for afternoon slumps.
- Pair the cutoff with short desk‑friendly movement breaks to sustain alertness without stimulants.
- If you use a coffee‑nap, keep the pre‑nap coffee modest and ensure the nap stays within 15‑20 minutes.
