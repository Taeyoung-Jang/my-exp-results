# Good Neighbor Etiquette

**Definition**: A set of low‑commitment, respectful practices for welcoming and interacting with new neighbors, especially in apartment buildings, that foster friendliness without creating obligatory expectations.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-14]])

1. **Brief, warm greetings** – A smile, "hello," and an optional "Welcome to the building" when you cross paths in the hallway. This establishes a friendly tone while keeping the interaction short. [[concepts/simple_hallway_greetings]]
2. **Offer low‑commitment help** – If a natural moment arises, you can say something like, "If you ever need help finding the mailroom, just let me know." The focus is on practical, occasional assistance rather than personal favors. [[concepts/low_commitment_communication]]
3. **Avoid unnecessary gifts or contact‑info exchange** – No need to give presents or exchange phone numbers. Keep the relationship to hallway hellos unless both parties explicitly choose otherwise.
4. **Set clear, polite boundaries** – When asked for a number or to borrow something, use brief, friendly declinations that reference any prior agreement to keep things simple. This maintains respect and prevents expectations from forming. [[concepts/boundary_setting]]

## Example One‑Liners

- **Number request (hallway)**: "Oh, I actually don’t share my number, but I’ll see you around."
- **Number request (chat)**: "I tend to keep my phone private, but I’m always happy to say hi when we cross paths."
- **Borrow request (hallway)**: "Sorry, I have a no‑lending rule, but good luck with the move."
- **Borrow request (chat)**: "I’d love to help, but I stick to a no‑lending rule. I hope that’s okay."
- **Reinforcing a prior agreement (hallway)**: "Oh right—let’s stick with what we talked about."
- **Reinforcing a prior agreement (chat)**: "I remember we agreed to keep things simple with just hallway hellos, so I’m going to stick with that. Hope that’s okay."
