# Boundary Setting

**Boundary Setting** is the practice of communicating clear, consistent limits in interpersonal interactions to preserve mutual respect and personal autonomy. In the context of neighbor relationships, it helps maintain trust while preventing an “on‑call” dynamic.

## Why It Matters

- Supports **[[concepts/neighborhood_trust]]** by showing reliability without over‑extension.
- Enables **[[concepts/low_drama_rapport]]** through calm, predictable behavior.
- Reduces friction and misunderstandings, keeping interactions friendly and low‑key.

## Key Strategies from [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]]

1. **Consistent Polite Messaging** – Use brief, neutral phrases when declining or limiting availability (e.g., “I’m tied up right now, but hope your day goes well”).
2. **Selective Acceptance of Favors** – Say yes only when the request is genuinely easy; otherwise offer a short, warm no.
3. **Framing Requests as Housekeeping** – When clarifying a spare‑key arrangement, present the limits as practical guidelines rather than a loss of trust.
4. **Scripted Communication** – Prepare casual and formal versions of boundary‑setting language for different contexts (doorstep chat vs. text message).
5. **Predictable Patterns** – Maintain a steady tone; over time neighbors adapt to the rhythm you set.

## Example Phrases

- “I’d love to help, but I’m tied up right now.”
- “I won’t be able to swing that this week, but I hope it works out.”
- “Not a good timing day for me, unfortunately.”
- “Can’t do that one, but thanks for checking.”

These “polite no” lines keep the interaction kind while establishing clear limits.
