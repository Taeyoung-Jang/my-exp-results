# Low-Drama Rapport

Low-Drama Rapport describes a style of neighborly interaction that emphasizes **small, consistent acts of friendliness** (e.g., saying hello, remembering names, brief check‑ins after a storm) while **keeping expectations clear and low‑key**. The goal is to foster trust without creating obligations or drama.

## Core Elements

1. **Micro‑gestures** – Simple actions that signal approachability:
   - Greeting neighbors when you cross paths.
   - Remembering and using a neighbor’s name.
   - Offering a quick “How are you?” after a shared event (e.g., a heavy rain).
2. **Predictable boundaries** – Pair friendliness with steady, polite limits. When a request feels beyond a casual favor, respond with a short, neutral decline (e.g., “I’m tied up right now”).
3. **Consistent tone** – Maintain the same calm, matter‑of‑fact communication style so neighbors learn the rhythm you prefer.
4. **Scripted clarity** – For recurring arrangements (like a spare key), use a brief script that frames the agreement as **housekeeping**, not a shift in trust.

## Practical Tips from [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]]

- **Casual script for a spare key**: “Hey, about that spare key – it’s just for lockouts or if I ask first. No pressure otherwise.”
- **Polite “no” lines**: “I’d love to help, but I’m tied up right now.”; “Can’t do that one, but thanks for checking.”
- **Resetting an “on‑call” vibe**: Respond with a friendly but brief “I’m busy right now, hope your day goes well,” and limit future availability.
