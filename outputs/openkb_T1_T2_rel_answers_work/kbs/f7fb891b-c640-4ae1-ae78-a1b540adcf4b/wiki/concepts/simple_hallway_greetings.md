# Simple Hallway Greetings

**Definition**: A low‑effort, courteous way of acknowledging new neighbors in shared building spaces, typically limited to a smile, hello, and a brief welcome phrase.

## Core Elements
- **Briefness**: Keep the exchange to a single sentence or a quick friendly remark. This prevents the interaction from turning into a request for deeper contact or favors.
- **Warmth**: Use a smile and a pleasant tone to convey friendliness without implying ongoing obligations.
- **Consistency**: Repeating short greetings over time helps establish a comfortable, low‑drama rapport while maintaining personal boundaries.

## Guidance from [[summaries/session-31_2025-03-14]]
- When you see a new neighbor in the hallway, say something like “Welcome to the building” or simply “Hi, good to see you.”
- Offer a single practical line if the moment feels natural, e.g., “If you need help finding the mailroom, just let me know,” but avoid extending the conversation.
- Avoid gifts, number exchanges, or promises to lend items; these can create expectations that conflict with the goal of simple greetings.

## Practical One‑Liners
- **Hallway pass**: “Hi, welcome to the building!”
- **Slightly longer chat**: “If you need help finding the mailroom, just let me know.”
- **Reinforcing an agreement**: “Oh right—let’s stick with what we talked about.”

These lines illustrate how simple hallway greetings can be both friendly and boundary‑respecting, fostering a pleasant living environment without unnecessary expectations.
