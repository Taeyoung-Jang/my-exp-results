# Neighborhood Trust

**Definition**: A sense of reliable, low‑drama rapport with nearby residents that balances friendliness with clear personal boundaries.

## Core Elements

1. **Consistent Small Gestures**
   - Simple actions like saying hello, remembering a neighbor’s name, or checking in after a storm create a foundation of trust without creating obligations.
2. **Boundary Setting**
   - Use polite, predictable replies to limit unsolicited visits or frequent favor requests, reinforcing a steady rhythm of interaction.
3. **Clear Communication**
   - Frame expectations (e.g., spare‑key use) as practical housekeeping rather than a change in trust, keeping the tone friendly and low‑drama.
4. **Emergency vs. Routine**
   - Define when a neighbor may act (true lockouts or emergencies) and when they should hold back, preventing the relationship from feeling "on‑call."

## Practices from [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]]
- **Low‑Drama Rapport**: Regular, brief greetings and occasional check‑ins after notable events.
- **Polite Refusals**: Short, neutral "no" statements (e.g., "I’m tied up right now") maintain kindness while setting limits.
- **Spare‑Key Script**: A concise script clarifying that the spare key is for lockouts or pre‑arranged situations, not for routine entry.
