# Summary of Session 31 (2025‑03‑14)

**Topic:** How to welcome new neighbors in an apartment building while keeping interactions light‑touch and avoiding unintended obligations.

## Core Recommendations
- **Initial greeting**: A brief smile, hello, and optional “Welcome to the building” establishes warmth without implying ongoing duties. [[concepts/good_neighbor_etiquette]]
- **Low‑commitment help**: Offer a single practical line (e.g., “If you need help finding the mailroom, just let me know.”) rather than personal aid. [[concepts/low_commitment_communication]]
- **Avoiding gifts or number exchange**: No need to give presents or swap phone numbers; keep interactions limited to hallway hellos. [[concepts/simple_hallway_greetings]]

## Polite Decline Strategies
- **Number request**:  
  - Hallway pass: “Oh, I actually don’t share my number, but I’ll see you around.”  
  - Longer chat: “I tend to keep my phone private, but I’m always happy to say hi when we cross paths.”
- **Borrowing request**:  
  - Hallway pass: “Sorry, I have a no‑lending rule, but good luck with the move.”  
  - Longer chat: “I’d love to help, but I stick to a no‑lending rule. I hope that’s okay.”

These lines illustrate **boundary setting** done with friendliness and confidence. [[concepts/boundary_setting]]

## Reinforcing a Prior Agreement
When an explicit agreement to keep things simple was made:
- **Number request (brief)**: “Oh right—let’s stick with what we talked about.”
- **Number request (warm)**: “I remember we agreed to keep things simple with just hallway hellos, so I’m going to stick with that. Hope that’s okay.”
- **Borrow request (brief)**: “Ah, we said no favors, remember? But good luck with whatever you’re tackling.”
- **Borrow request (warm)**: “We decided to skip borrowing and lending, so I’m going to stick with that, but I hope the move’s going smoothly.”
