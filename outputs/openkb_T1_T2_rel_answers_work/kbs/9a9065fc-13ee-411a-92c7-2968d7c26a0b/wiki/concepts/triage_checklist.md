# Triage Checklist

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A brief, structured routine that lets you scan communications, physical items, and pending tasks, then sort them using a three‑question filter so you can rebuild a realistic short‑term plan in 10–15 minutes.

---

## Overview
The *Triage Checklist* originates from the conversation in [[summaries/session-37_2025-04-26]] where the assistant offered a concrete, copy‑ready checklist for people who have fallen behind after an unexpected disruption (e.g., jury duty).  It is designed to be **time‑boxed**, ensuring the reset process itself does not become another source of overwhelm.

---

## Step‑by‑Step Procedure
| Time | Action | Goal |
|------|--------|------|
| **3 min** | Scan voicemail for anything that sounds like a deadline, problem, or request that might have moved on. | Capture hidden urgent items without responding. |
| **3 min** | Scan texts, missed calls, and email with the same filter. | Identify digital fires. |
| **2 min** | Scan physical items (receipts, tickets, notes). Flag anything that hints at reimbursements, appointments, or tasks you meant to do. | Surface non‑digital obligations. |
| **2 min** | Write a rough list of everything you just noticed. Do **not** organize yet—just get it out of your head. | Create an external dump. |
| **3‑5 min** | Apply the **three‑question filter** (see [[concepts/three_question_filter]]):
- *Will anything bad happen if this waits another day?* → **Do Soon**
- *Will doing this noticeably reduce my stress?* → **Do Later**
- *Is it already resolved or irrelevant?* → **Drop** |
| **1 min** | Choose one consequence‑based task and one stress‑relief task for tomorrow, then loosely distribute the remaining items across the next 5‑7 days. | Build a lightweight, realistic plan. |

---

## Core Elements
1. **Time boxing** – Each sub‑step has an explicit minute allocation to prevent the reset from expanding into a full‑blown planning session.
2. **Three‑question filter** – The decision matrix that classifies items into three buckets (Do Soon, Do Later, Drop).  See [[concepts/three_question_filter]] for details.
3. **Receipt pile** – A dedicated scan of physical receipts or paper items that often hide hidden obligations (e.g., reimbursements, calendar clues). This is the unique addition for backlogs like the one described in the source session.
4. **Outcome‑focused planning** – Rather than a exhaustive weekly schedule, the checklist ends by selecting a *small win* and a *stress‑relief* task for the next day, allowing momentum to rebuild organically.

## Usage Scenarios
- **Post‑crisis catch‑up** – After an event that removes you from communication channels (court, vacation, illness).
- **Inbox overload** – When email, messages, and physical paperwork have accumulated beyond a manageable level.
- **Project reset** – When a single task has hijacked a schedule and you need to re‑establish a realistic roadmap.

---

## Benefits
- **Rapid mental declutter** – Externalizing tasks reduces cognitive load.
- **Prioritization based on real impact** – The three‑question filter shifts decisions from feeling‑based to consequence‑based.
- **Low planning overhead** – The entire process fits in a short, focused work block.
- **Actionable follow‑up** – The final step creates an immediate, doable plan for the next day.
