## Overview
The document provides a practical guide for clinicians starting their first week on a hospital floor. It outlines common initial shocks, habits for staying calm, a flexible “first‑week” checklist, a printable pocket‑note template, a rapid triage decision tree, and a set of ready‑made one‑liners for communicating with a preceptor.

## Key Concepts
- **Hospital onboarding** – the transition experience for new staff [[concepts/hospital_onboarding]]  
- **Task management** – using a single running list and visual cues to track duties [[concepts/task_management]]  
- **Stress management** – brief breathing pauses and routine building to stay calm [[concepts/stress_management]]  
- **First‑week checklist** – a conversational flow rather than a rigid list (plain text)  
- **Pocket‑note template** – a printable plain‑text form for patient‑level information (plain text)  
- **Triage decision tree** – a three‑step mental script to prioritize when pulled in multiple directions (plain text)  
- **Preceptor communication** – specific phrasing to ask for guidance without appearing lost (plain text)

## Pocket‑note Template (plain text)
```
Room: ________    Name: __________  
Main reason here: __________________  
Key watch‑fors: _____________________  
Most recent vitals (if relevant): __________________  
Meds/labs/procedures due soon: __________________  
Today’s tasks: __________________  
Follow‑ups or “circle back later”: __________________  
```

## Rapid Triage Decision Tree
1. **Safety first** – Is anyone potentially unsafe if not addressed now? If yes, act immediately.  
2. **Workflow impact** – Does the task affect another team member’s schedule or a scheduled event? Prioritize next.  
3. **All‑others** – Tasks that can wait a few minutes go to the bottom of the list.

## Preceptor One‑liners
- “Can you confirm I’m prioritizing the right thing before I move on?”  
- “I’m not sure if this is urgent—can you help me gauge the timeline?”  
- “I know what needs to happen, but I’m not sure about the next step. What would you do first?”  
- “I think I’m missing where this supply is kept. Can you point me in the right direction?”  
- “I’m falling a bit behind—what would you like me to tackle next so I don’t miss anything important?”  
- “Can you talk me through your thought process here so I understand how you’re weighing things?”  
- “Before I act, can I check that I’m interpreting this correctly?”  
- “I’m not confident I’m seeing the whole picture—can you tell me what you’d focus on in this situation?”  
- “If you have a second, can you show me the fastest way you handle this task?”  
- “I want to make sure I’m learning your workflow—does this approach look reasonable to you?”

## Recommendations
- Spend 2–3 minutes at shift start to fill the pocket‑note template.  
- Keep a single running “to‑do” list and cross out completed items.  
- Use the three‑step triage script whenever you are pulled in multiple directions.  
- Memorize a few preceptor one‑liners and keep them on the pocket note for quick reference.

These practices together help new clinicians transition smoothly, maintain patient safety, and build confidence during the critical first week.
