# Summary of session-10_2024-06-15

## Overview
This conversation explores what makes visual design feel “right” versus merely decorative. It identifies core factors that separate strong visual communication from style‑only work and provides two concrete, bullet‑point checklists—one for posters and one for app screens—complete with quick tests and red‑flag examples.

## Key Factors for Strong Visual Communication
- **Intention & Clarity** – The design must convey its main message instantly. [[concepts/clarity]]
- **Visual Hierarchy** – Eye‑movement should follow a deliberate path, guiding the viewer from primary to secondary information. [[concepts/visual-hierarchy]]
- **Tone Alignment** – Visual tone (color, typography, imagery) should match the content’s personality. [[concepts/visual-tone]]
- **Spatial Rhythm & Consistency** – Consistent spacing, scale, and rhythm create an internal logic. [[concepts/spatial-rhythm]]
- **Purposeful Elements** – Every element should earn its place; decorative items that don’t add meaning dilute communication.

## Poster Review Checklist
- Identify the main message in one glance.
- Ensure the headline dominates supporting details.
- Verify eye flow: headline → date → location → call‑to‑action.
- Match typography and color tone to the event’s mood.
- Confirm imagery supports, not competes with, the message.
- Maintain consistent spacing for intentional rhythm.
- Remove non‑essential decorative elements.
- **Quick test:** *5‑second glance* – can you restate the poster’s purpose and details? [[concepts/quick-test]]
- **Red‑flag examples:**
  - Artwork louder than the title.
  - Date rendered too small or overly stylized, causing it to be missed.

## App Screen Review Checklist
- Make the primary action or purpose instantly obvious.
- Align visual hierarchy with typical mobile scanning patterns. [[concepts/visual-hierarchy]]
- Design interactive elements to look tappable and distinct from graphics.
- Use spacing to group related items and avoid accidental separation. [[concepts/spatial-rhythm]]
- Align visual tone with functional intent, not just trends. [[concepts/visual-tone]]
- Eliminate visual noise that doesn’t aid comprehension.
- Ensure text remains legible at normal phone‑holding distance.
- **Quick test:** *Squint test* – blur the screen; the main action and key info should still stand out. [[concepts/quick-test]]
- **Red‑flag examples:**
  - Buttons that resemble decorative illustrations.
  - Aesthetic spacing that makes linked items feel unrelated.

## Takeaways
- Strong visual design is purpose‑driven; clarity, hierarchy, tone, and rhythm are the pillars.
- The provided checklists give designers a rapid, repeatable method to assess whether a layout communicates effectively or merely looks good.
- Quick tests (5‑second glance, squint) and concrete red‑flag examples help catch common pitfalls early.
