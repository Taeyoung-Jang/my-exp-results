# Quick Test

**Definition**

A *quick test* is a rapid, low‑effort evaluation technique used by designers to confirm that a visual layout communicates its core message and hierarchy at a glance. It focuses on immediate perception rather than detailed analysis.

**Common Quick Tests from Session‑10**

- **5‑second glance** – View the design for five seconds and ask yourself: *What is the main message and key details?* If you cannot answer, the hierarchy or clarity is likely weak. [[concepts/visual-hierarchy]] [[concepts/clarity]]
- **Squint test** – Slightly blur or squint your eyes while looking at the design. The most important elements (primary action, headline, CTA) should still stand out. This reveals contrast and tonal alignment issues. [[concepts/visual-tone]]

**When to Use**

- During fast‑paced design reviews for **posters** and **app screens**.
- As a first‑pass check before deeper usability testing.
- When iterating on visual style to ensure functional intent remains clear.

**Relation to Other Concepts**

- Supports **[[concepts/clarity]]** by confirming instant understandability.
- Reinforces **[[concepts/visual-hierarchy]]** by testing the visual flow.
- Helps maintain **[[concepts/visual-tone]]** consistency, ensuring tone matches purpose.
- Can expose problems in **[[concepts/spatial-rhythm]]** when spacing causes elements to blend together.
