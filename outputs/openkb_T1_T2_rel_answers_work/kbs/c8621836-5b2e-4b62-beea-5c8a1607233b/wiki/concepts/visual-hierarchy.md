## Visual Hierarchy

Visual hierarchy is the purposeful arrangement of design elements that directs a viewer’s attention in a logical, efficient order. In strong visual communication, hierarchy ensures that the most important information is perceived first, with secondary details following in a natural flow.

### Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]])
- **Immediate clarity** – The primary message should be recognizable within the first second of viewing. This ties closely to [[concepts/clarity]].
- **Eye‑movement path** – Layouts should lead the eye from the headline or main call‑to‑action to supporting details (date, location, secondary text). In the poster checklist, this is described as moving *"from headline to date, location, and call‑to‑action."* For app screens, the hierarchy should match typical mobile scanning patterns.
- **Consistent rhythm** – Spacing, scale, and contrast must be coherent, creating an internal logic that supports the hierarchy. See also [[concepts/spatial-rhythm]].
- **Tone alignment** – Visual tone (color, typography, imagery) must reinforce the hierarchy rather than compete with it, linking to [[concepts/visual-tone]].

### Practical Application
1. **Identify the focal point** – Determine which element conveys the main message.
2. **Establish contrast** – Use size, weight, color, or placement to make the focal point stand out.
3. **Create a flow** – Arrange secondary elements so they are visually linked to the focal point, using proximity and alignment.
4. **Validate with a quick test** – Apply a *5‑second glance* or *squint test* (see [[concepts/quick-test]]) to ensure the hierarchy holds under brief or blurred inspection.

### Red‑Flag Examples
- Artwork that overpowers the headline, causing the primary message to be lost.
- Buttons that look like decorative graphics, breaking the functional hierarchy on an app screen.
