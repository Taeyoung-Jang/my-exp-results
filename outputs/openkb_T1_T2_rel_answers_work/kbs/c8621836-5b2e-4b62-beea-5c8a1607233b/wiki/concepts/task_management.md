# Task Management

**Definition**: A personal system for capturing, prioritizing, and completing work items using a single running list and visual cues to reduce mental load and maintain patient safety.

## Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-13]])
1. **Single running list** – Write every new task the moment it appears and cross it out when finished. This prevents forgetting and frees mental bandwidth.
2. **Prioritization by urgency** – Use the three‑step triage decision tree: safety first, workflow impact second, everything else third.
3. **Visual hierarchy** – Keep the list in a dedicated pocket‑note template, with sections (e.g., "time‑sensitive", "follow‑ups") that stand out visually.
4. **Brief review loops** – At shift start and during brief lulls, scan the list for hidden items and update priorities.

## Practical Tools
- **Pocket‑note template** (plain‑text) that separates patient info, watch‑fors, meds/labs due, tasks, and follow‑ups.
- **Cross‑off method** – Crossing completed items provides immediate feedback and reduces anxiety.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/stress_management]] – A calm routine and brief breathing pauses support effective task handling.
- [[concepts/hospital_onboarding]] – Early clinicians benefit from structured task management to navigate the fast‑paced environment.
- [[concepts/visual-hierarchy]] – Organizing information with clear headings and sections improves list readability.

## Benefits
- **Reduced cognitive overload** – Externalizing tasks frees mental space for clinical reasoning.
- **Improved patient safety** – Prioritizing unsafe situations first ensures critical needs are met.
- **Efficient communication** – A clear list makes it easier to hand off tasks to teammates or preceptors.
