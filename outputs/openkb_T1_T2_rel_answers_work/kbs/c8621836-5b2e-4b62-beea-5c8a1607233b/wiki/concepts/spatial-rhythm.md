# Spatial Rhythm

**Definition**: The purposeful arrangement of spacing, scale, and repetition in a visual layout that creates a sense of flow and internal logic.

**Why it matters**: Spatial rhythm helps the eye move predictably through a design, reinforcing [[concepts/visual-hierarchy]] and supporting [[concepts/clarity]]. When rhythm is inconsistent, elements feel accidental, causing confusion and reducing communicative power.

**Key points from [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]]**:
- Consistent spacing creates an intentional rhythm that makes a layout feel cohesive.
- Rhythm works together with hierarchy: regular gaps guide the eye from primary to secondary information.
- Matching rhythm to the visual tone ensures the design feels purposeful rather than merely decorative.
- Quick sanity checks (e.g., the 5‑second glance or squint test) can reveal rhythm problems when key information gets lost in uneven gaps.

**How to evaluate spatial rhythm**:
1. Scan the layout for uniform margins, padding, and line spacing.
2. Look for repeated patterns (grid columns, vertical/horizontal spacing) that guide the viewer.
3. Check that related groups of elements share consistent spacing; unrelated groups should have clear separation.
4. Use the [[concepts/quick-test]] (e.g., a brief glance) to see if the rhythm supports instant comprehension.

**Design tips**:
- Establish a baseline grid and stick to it.
- Use multiples of a base unit (e.g., 8 px) for margins and padding.
- Align text and imagery to the same rhythm to avoid visual “jank.”
- Adjust rhythm to match the intended tone: tighter rhythm for energetic content, looser rhythm for calm, reflective pieces (see [[concepts/visual-tone]]).
