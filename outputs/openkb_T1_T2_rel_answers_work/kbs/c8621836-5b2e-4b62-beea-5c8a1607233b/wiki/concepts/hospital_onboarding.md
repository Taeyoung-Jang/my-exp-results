# Hospital Onboarding

**Hospital onboarding** is the short‑term process that helps newly‑assigned clinicians adjust to the fast‑paced, noisy, and communication‑heavy environment of a hospital or clinical unit. The goal is to move a newcomer from feeling overwhelmed to operating safely and confidently within the first few weeks.

---

## Core Challenges
- **Sensory overload** – loud alarms, constant movement, and overlapping conversations can be shocking.
- **Communication intensity** – coordinating with nurses, techs, and other staff is a dominant part of the work.
- **Task juggling** – rapid switches between patient care, charting, and supply management.

These challenges are echoed in [[summaries/session-38_2025-02-13]], where a trainee describes the “blur of vitals, charting, and supply‑closet confusion” on the first day.

---

## Key Practices for Successful Onboarding
### 1. Establish a Personal Routine
- Spend 2–3 minutes at shift start to scan assignments and note time‑sensitive items.
- Use a **pocket‑note template** (plain‑text form) to capture patient name, room, main issue, watch‑fors, vitals, and pending tasks.

### 2. Centralised Task Management
- Keep a single running “to‑do” list; cross out items as they are completed.
- Separate items into *time‑sensitive*, *workflow‑impacting*, and *low‑priority* groups.
- This mirrors the principles of [[concepts/task_management]].

### 3. Rapid Triage Decision Tree
1. **Safety first** – Is any patient potentially unsafe if not addressed now?
2. **Workflow impact** – Does the task affect another team member’s schedule or a scheduled event?
3. **Everything else** – Defer until the higher‑priority items are resolved.

### 4. Stress Management Techniques
- Take a slow, deep breath before moving to the next task.
- Keep the routine simple; avoid over‑complicating with excessive checklists.
- Supports the broader framework of [[concepts/stress_management]].

### 5. Structured Preceptor Communication
Prepare a handful of concise questions (e.g., “Can you confirm I’m prioritizing the right thing before I move on?”). These one‑liners help surface reasoning without appearing lost and reinforce learning.

---

## Practical Artifacts
- **Pocket‑note template** (plain text) – ready to copy and print.
- **Preceptor one‑liners** – a list of 8‑10 ready phrases for on‑the‑fly use.
- **Triage script** – a three‑step mental checklist for moments when you’re pulled in multiple directions.
