# Summary of Session 13 (2023‑06‑15)

**Main focus:** How to choose between a travel‑rewards credit card and a flat‑rate cash‑back card, especially for a user with a strong credit score, $2,500‑$3,500 monthly spend, and 2‑3 annual trips. (See the full conversation in [[summaries/session-13_2023-06-15]].)

## Key Comparison Factors
- **Spending alignment** – match the card’s reward categories to your actual spend (groceries, dining, gas, online purchases). [[concepts/reward-alignment]]
- **Annual fee math** – calculate net value: `(spending × reward rate) – annual fee`. [[concepts/annual-fee-math]]
- **Ease of redemption** – cash‑back is usually automatic; travel cards require tracking point values and portal redemption rates. [[concepts/redemption-complexity]]
- **Long‑term value vs sign‑up bonus** – avoid over‑valuing a large intro bonus if the ongoing rate is weak. (plain text)

## Common Mistakes
1. Over‑estimating travel frequency and thus the value of travel perks. (plain text)
2. Selecting a card for a big sign‑up bonus without checking post‑first‑year value. (plain text)
3. Ignoring annual‑fee payback and assuming all spend earns the advertised rate. (plain text)
4. Missing caps, excluded merchant categories, or redemption minimums on cash‑back cards. (plain text)

## Simple Quick‑Math Method
1. Estimate yearly spend (e.g., $30k‑$42k).
2. Multiply by the card’s headline reward rate.
3. Subtract any annual fee (or add travel credit if applicable).
4. For travel cards, assume a **portal redemption rate** of 1.0¢‑1.25¢ per point; 1.25¢ is typical only for mid‑tier cards with modest fees. (plain text)

### Example
- 2% cash‑back → $30k × 0.02 = $600 net (no fee).
- Travel card: 2 pts/$ × 1.25¢ = 2.5% effective value; subtract $95 fee, add $50 travel credit → net ≈ $650 if you earn points on most spend.

## When Travel Cards Can Beat Cash‑Back
- **Fixed‑value portal redemption** (≥1.25¢/pt) on most spend.
- **Automatic travel credits** that offset the fee.
- **Strong travel protections** (primary rental‑car insurance, trip‑delay/cancellation coverage) that you would otherwise pay for. (plain text)
- **Low‑fee cards** (≈$95) where the credit and protections exceed the fee for 2‑3 trips a year.

## Perks Worth Verifying for Mid‑Tier Travel Cards
- Annual travel credit (auto‑applied). (plain text)
- Primary rental‑car insurance. (plain text)
- Trip‑delay, trip‑cancellation, and baggage‑delay coverage. (plain text)
- Lounge access (only valuable if you value the experience). (plain text)
- Point transfer options (optional, not required for a simple setup). (plain text)

## Cash‑Back Card Gotchas to Watch
- Category caps or spend limits. (plain text)
- Exclusions (e.g., certain online merchants, cash‑like transactions). (plain text)
- Redemption minimums or delayed cash‑back posting. (plain text)
- Whether cash‑back is issued as statement credit, direct deposit, or gift cards (affects flexibility). (plain text)

## Bottom‑Line Recommendation
- For the described spend and travel frequency, a **flat‑rate 2% cash‑back card** is the default choice: simple, predictable, and high net value.
- A **low‑/mid‑fee travel card** can edge out cash‑back only if it offers a reliable 1.25¢/pt portal rate **plus** an automatic travel credit and solid travel insurance that you will actually use.
- If those conditions aren’t met, stick with cash‑back for ease and consistent returns.
