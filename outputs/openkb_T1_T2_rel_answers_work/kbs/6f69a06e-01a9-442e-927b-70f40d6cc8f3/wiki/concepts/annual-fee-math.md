# Annual Fee Math

**Definition**: Annual Fee Math is the quantitative process of comparing a credit‑card’s reward earnings to its recurring annual fee, producing a net‑value figure that indicates whether the card delivers positive returns over time.

---

## Core Steps (as discussed in [[summaries/session-13_2023-06-15]])
1. **Estimate yearly spend** – total expected spend across all categories (e.g., $30,000–$42,000 for a $2,500‑$3,500/month user).
2. **Apply the headline reward rate** – multiply spend by the card’s primary earn rate (e.g., 2 % cash‑back or 2 points per dollar for a travel card).
3. **Convert points to cash value** – for travel cards, assume a portal redemption rate (commonly 1.0 ¢ / point; occasionally 1.25 ¢ / point for mid‑tier cards). This step links directly to the **redemption complexity** of travel rewards and the need for a simple conversion.
4. **Subtract the annual fee** – reduce the gross reward value by the card’s fee (or add any automatic travel credits before subtracting).
5. **Result = Net Annual Value** – a positive number signals that the card pays for itself; a negative number indicates the opposite.

---

## Why It Matters
- **Simplifies decision‑making**: By boiling down the comparison to a single dollar amount, users can quickly see which card offers better long‑term value without building complex spreadsheets.
- **Highlights hidden costs**: Reveals when a flashy sign‑up bonus is outweighed by a high fee after the first year.
- **Connects to reward alignment**: The accuracy of the calculation depends on how well the card’s reward categories match the user’s actual spend patterns, tying into [[concepts/reward-alignment]].

---

## Practical Example (from the session)
- **Cash‑back card**: 2 % on $30,000 spend → $600 reward, $0 fee → **$600 net**.
- **Travel card (mid‑tier)**: 2 points/$ on $30,000 spend = 60,000 points. Assuming 1.25 ¢/point = $750 value. Fee $95, plus $50 automatic travel credit → $750 – $95 + $50 = **$705 net**.

This illustrates how a modest fee can be offset when the redemption rate is favorable and the card provides automatic credits.

---

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/redemption-complexity]]** – Determines how easy it is to apply the assumed point‑value conversion; higher complexity may reduce the effective net value.
- **[[concepts/reward-alignment]]** – Influences the reliability of the reward rate used in the calculation; mis‑aligned spend can lower actual earnings.

---

## When to Apply
- Evaluating new credit‑card applications where the annual fee is non‑zero.
- Comparing a flat‑rate cash‑back offering against a travel‑rewards card.
- Deciding whether a sign‑up bonus justifies a higher fee after the first year.
