# Reward Alignment

**Definition**: Reward Alignment is the practice of selecting a credit‑card whose reward structure (categories, rates, and bonuses) closely matches the cardholder’s real‑world spending habits, ensuring maximum value with minimal effort.

## Why It Matters
- Aligning rewards to spend prevents wasted potential where high‑rate categories are rarely used.
- It simplifies the decision process, reducing the need for micromanagement of points or cash‑back categories.
- Proper alignment directly influences the net value calculated in [[concepts/annual-fee-math]].

## Key Elements from Session 13 (2023‑06‑15)
- The user spends $2,500–$3,500 per month on groceries, dining, gas, and occasional large online purchases. A card that offers flat‑rate rewards across these everyday categories (e.g., 2% cash‑back) aligns well with this spend profile.
- Travel‑focused cards only add value if the user’s travel frequency and related expenses (flights, hotels, rentals) match the card’s bonus categories and perks. Otherwise, the mis‑alignment reduces net benefit.
- The conversation highlighted the need to compare reward rates against actual spend, a core step of reward alignment.

## Practical Steps
1. **List Primary Spend Categories** – Identify where most dollars go (e.g., groceries, dining, gas).
2. **Map Card Rewards** – Check each card’s reward rates and category caps.
3. **Calculate Expected Return** – Multiply projected spend by the applicable rate; this feeds into [[concepts/annual-fee-math]].
4. **Assess Redemption Simplicity** – Even with good alignment, a complex redemption process (see [[concepts/redemption-complexity]]) can erode value.

## When Alignment Favors Travel Cards
- The user has consistent travel spend that matches the card’s bonus categories (e.g., travel purchases, airline‑specific spend).
- The card provides automatic travel credits or perks that directly offset the annual fee, making the effective reward rate higher.

## When Alignment Favors Cash‑Back Cards
- Spend is dominated by everyday categories with no significant travel spend.
- A flat‑rate cash‑back card offers a uniform rate across all purchases, eliminating the need to track categories.
