# Summary

This document presents two **explicit 60‑minute wind‑down routines**—one for an **anxious night** and one for a **bored night**—that combine **screen‑time management** with **offline bridge activities** to protect sleep and focus.

### Core Elements
- **Low‑stimulation mode**: At the start of the hour, the iPhone is set to Do Not Disturb (or Sleep focus), Night Shift (warm‑tone filter), reduced brightness, and **grayscale** via Settings → Accessibility → Color Filters. This creates a calming visual environment. [[concepts/low-stimulation-mode]]
- **Content selection rules**:
  - *Anxious night*: Calm, predictable media (comfort show, familiar audiobook). **Rule:** no new content after 00:47; stop opening new apps after 00:35.
  - *Bored night*: Lightly engaging media (short reading, slow‑pace video essay, simple puzzle game). **Rule:** no new sessions after 00:45; avoid app hopping after 00:35.
- **Mid‑hour shift**: Transition from video/interactive media to softer audio or ambient content (podcast, lo‑fi playlist) to gradually lower cognitive load.
- **Screen wrap‑up (00:25‑00:20)**: Pause, lock the phone, and place it on a charger away from the bedside.
- **Offline bridge**: A brief non‑screen activity—stretching, washing the face, a warm shower, or deep breaths—to signal the brain that bedtime is approaching. [[concepts/offline-bridge-activity]]

### iPhone‑Specific Steps
1. **Enable DND/Sleep Focus** via the lock‑screen focus button. [[entities/iphone]]
2. **Activate Night Shift** in Control Center (press‑hold brightness slider → Night Shift). [[entities/iphone]]
3. **Turn on Grayscale**: Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Color Filters → Grayscale. [[entities/iphone]]
4. **Lower brightness** to the lowest comfortable level. [[entities/iphone]]
5. **Maintain these settings** until morning to prevent accidental re‑stimulation. [[entities/iphone]]

- [[concepts/offline-bridge-activity]] – short physical or mindfulness tasks transitioning to sleep.

### Outcome
By following the prescribed timelines, users can **maintain a relaxing wind‑down hour** that respects an hour‑long screen allowance while **preventing late‑night overstimulation**, ultimately supporting better sleep quality and daytime focus.
