## Session 31 – Evening Intentionality (2025-02-11)

**Main goal:** Offer practical, flexible strategies to prevent evenings from “disappearing” while still allowing enjoyable screen time.

### Key ideas
- **Evening anchors** – a short, repeatable ritual after dinner (e.g., tea, quick tidy, stepping outside) that signals a transition. [[concepts/evening_anchor]]
- **Flexible menu‑style routine** – choose one or two activities rather than a fixed checklist, keeping the evening relaxed yet structured. [[concepts/flexible_routine]]
- **Screen block** – a bounded period (60‑90 min) for videos and messages with clear start/end cues. [[concepts/screen_block]]
- **Screen‑block templates**  
  1. **60‑minute**: 10 min message sweep → single chosen video → transition (light, water, move).  
  2. **90‑minute**: message sweep → pre‑selected viewing “feature” (2 episodes or saved videos) → phone‑away stretch → offline wind‑down.  
- **Message inbox sweep** – treat the messages segment as a quick review, replying only to time‑sensitive items; use mental framing (“reviewing, not chatting”) to avoid conversational spirals.  
- **One‑liner replies** – short acknowledgments that close the loop without inviting further chat (e.g., “Got this, thanks! Will reply tomorrow.”).

### Practical transition techniques
- Use a physical cue after the screen block (turn on a lamp, make tea, stretch, move to a different spot) to signal the shift to an offline wind‑down. [[concepts/offline-bridge-activity]]  
- Place the phone out of reach, plug it in, and engage a brief offline activity (reading, light stretching, journaling). [[concepts/wind-down-routine]]

### Take‑away
Implement a **menu‑style evening**: pick an anchor, decide on a screen‑block length, follow a concise message‑sweep, use a transition cue, and finish with a calming offline practice. This balances relaxation with intentionality without feeling rigid.
