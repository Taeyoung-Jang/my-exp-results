# Low‑Stimulation Mode

**Type:** Concept

Low‑Stimulation Mode is a set of coordinated device settings and environmental adjustments designed to minimize sensory and cognitive input during the evening wind‑down period. By dimming visual stimuli, limiting notifications, and simplifying color contrast, the mode helps the brain transition toward sleep without the abrupt disengagement that can feel punitive.

## Core Components (as described in [[summaries/session-11_2024-10-12]])

1. **Do Not Disturb / Sleep Focus** – Activates a quiet period where calls, alerts, and most notifications are silenced. On an iPhone this is toggled via the lock‑screen Focus button.
2. **Night Shift (or equivalent warm‑tone filter)** – Shifts the screen’s color temperature toward warmer hues, reducing blue‑light exposure that can suppress melatonin.
3. **Reduced Brightness** – Lowers screen luminance to the lowest comfortable level, decreasing visual intensity.
4. **Grayscale (Color Filters)** – Removes chromatic saturation, making the UI less eye‑catching and lowering the urge for app‑hopping.

These steps are typically applied at the very start of a planned hour‑long wind‑down routine, establishing a calmer visual container for any remaining screen use.

## Why It Matters

- **Sleep hygiene**: Warm tones and low brightness mitigate the impact of evening screen exposure on circadian rhythms.
- **Focus reduction**: Grayscale and DND curb the dopamine spikes associated with bright, colorful notifications and app icons.
- **Ease of transition**: A consistent, low‑stimulation environment signals the brain that it is time to unwind, making the subsequent offline bridge activity smoother.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- Part of a broader [[concepts/wind-down-routine]] that blends screen time with offline activities.
- Provides the sensory foundation for the [[concepts/offline-bridge-activity]] that follows the screen window.
