# Flexible Routine

**Definition**: A menu‑style, adaptable approach to evening planning that provides a few optional activities rather than a rigid checklist, allowing the night to feel relaxed yet purposeful.

## Core Principles
1. **Choice over Checklist** – Pick one or two activities from a short list (e.g., tea, quick tidy, stretch) instead of committing to a long to‑do list. This keeps the routine flexible while still giving the evening shape.
2. **Anchors** – Use a consistent, low‑effort ritual after dinner to signal the transition into the night (see [[concepts/evening_anchor]]).
3. **Bounded Screen Time** – Pair the flexible routine with a defined "screen block" (60–90 min) that includes a brief message sweep and a pre‑selected viewing segment (see [[concepts/screen_block]]).
4. **Transition Cue** – End the screen block with a physical cue (lighting, tea, a stretch) that bridges to an offline activity (see [[concepts/offline-bridge-activity]]).
5. **Low‑Stimulation Mode** – After the transition, shift to calmer, low‑stimulus activities to support a smooth wind‑down (see [[concepts/low-stimulation-mode]]).

## Application in Session 31
The concept is illustrated in [[summaries/session-31_2025-02-11]] where the assistant recommends:
- Selecting a single "anchor" after dinner (tea, tidy, stepping outside).
- Choosing a 60‑ or 90‑minute screen block with a clear start/end.
- Using a brief physical transition (turn on a lamp, make tea) before moving into a calming offline wind‑down routine (see [[concepts/wind-down-routine]]).

These steps embody a flexible routine by providing a menu of options, clear boundaries, and gentle hand‑offs between digital and offline phases.

## Benefits
- **Reduces decision fatigue** – Fewer choices and a repeatable structure.
- **Prevents evening drift** – The anchor and bounded screen block give the night a recognizable shape.
- **Maintains relaxation** – Choice keeps the routine feeling optional, not obligatory.
- **Facilitates transition** – Physical cues help the brain shift from high‑stimulus screen time to low‑stimulation evening activities.
