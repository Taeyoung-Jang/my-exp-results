# Evening Anchor

An **Evening Anchor** is a short, repeatable activity performed right after dinner that signals to the brain that the night phase is beginning. It creates a mental cue that helps prevent the evening from “disappearing” into unstructured time.

## Core Characteristics
- **Simplicity:** Takes only a minute or two; examples include a cup of tea, a quick kitchen tidy, or stepping outside for a breath of fresh air.
- **Consistency:** Performed each night to build a reliable habit loop.
- **Transition Signal:** Marks the shift from daytime tasks to the evening’s intentional activities.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/flexible_routine]] by providing a stable starting point while the rest of the night remains menu‑style.
- Sets the stage for a bounded [[concepts/screen_block]] (e.g., 60‑90 min of video and messages) by giving a clear pre‑screen cue.
- Bridges into the [[concepts/offline-bridge-activity]] and overall [[concepts/wind-down-routine]] that follow the screen block.

## Practical Examples from [[summaries/session-31_2025-02-11]]
- **Tea ritual:** Brew a calming tea and sip while reflecting on the day.
- **Quick tidy:** Put dishes in the dishwasher and wipe the counter, creating a tidy environment for relaxation.
- **Outdoor breath:** Open a window or step onto a balcony for a minute of fresh air.

## Benefits
1. **Mental framing:** Signals the brain to switch modes, reducing the likelihood of endless scrolling.
2. **Structure without rigidity:** Provides a gentle anchor while keeping the rest of the evening flexible.
3. **Improved transition:** Makes moving from screen time to offline activities smoother and more intentional.

## Implementation Tips
- Choose an activity you enjoy and can complete in under two minutes.
- Pair the anchor with a physical cue (e.g., turning on a lamp) to reinforce the transition.
- Keep the anchor consistent; over time it becomes an automatic trigger for the evening flow.
