# Non‑Negotiable Tasks

**Definition**: A non‑negotiable task is an activity that, if missed or postponed for a week, creates stress, friction, or derails progress toward important goals. These tasks form the anchor points of a flexible weekly outline.

## Core Characteristics
- **Impact‑Focused**: Skipping the task leads to immediate negative consequences (e.g., increased workload later, loss of momentum).
- **Priority Indicator**: Serves as a decision‑making shortcut when prioritising a crowded schedule.
- **Anchor Role**: Provides structural stability in a plan that otherwise emphasizes flexibility and slack.

## How the Concept Appears in Session 40
In the conversation from [[summaries/session-40_2024-05-23]], the assistant advises the user to:
1. Identify tasks that, when imagined a week later, would cause stress or derail progress.  
2. Treat those tasks as **non‑negotiables** and place them in dedicated *anchor windows*.
3. Contrast them with *floating* tasks, which can shift without noticeable impact.

This approach aligns non‑negotiables with the broader strategy of [[concepts/flexible-planning]] by giving the flexible system reliable reference points.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/flexible-planning]]**: Uses non‑negotiables as the stable backbone around which flexible windows and slack are built.
- **[[concepts/slack-time]]**: The presence of non‑negotiables justifies the need for intentional slack, ensuring that unexpected changes do not compromise critical tasks.
- **[[concepts/block-scheduling]]**: Non‑negotiables can be blocked into recurring windows, but the blocks remain loose to preserve flexibility.
- **[[concepts/midweek-change-rule]]**: When mid‑week changes occur, non‑negotiables remain fixed while other tasks may float.

## Practical Steps to Identify Non‑Negotiables
1. **Stress Test**: Ask yourself, *"If I postpone this task for a week, will I feel stressed or will it impede a larger goal?"*  
2. **Outcome Mapping**: Outline the downstream effects of delaying the task.
3. **Anchor Placement**: Assign the task to a consistent weekly window (e.g., morning errands, evening project time).
4. **Review Weekly**: At the end of each day, confirm that non‑negotiables were addressed; if not, adjust the upcoming schedule accordingly.

## Benefits
- Reduces decision fatigue by clarifying what must happen.
- Increases motivation, as the plan feels supportive rather than restrictive.
- Enhances resilience; slack time can absorb disruptions without jeopardising critical outcomes.
