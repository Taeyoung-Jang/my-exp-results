# Block Scheduling

Block scheduling is a planning method that divides a week into broader **blocks** rather than fixed‑time slots.  The approach emphasizes:

- **Non‑negotiable anchors** such as work hours, appointments, or essential errands.
- **Flexible blocks** for deep work, life‑admin tasks, and downtime that can shift within the day.
- A **float block** (or “catch‑up” block) used to absorb unexpected tasks without reshuffling the entire calendar.

The system aims to provide enough structure to stay grounded while allowing enough looseness to avoid the feeling of constantly rearranging a puzzle.

## Key Elements (from [[summaries/session-10_2022-05-23]])

1. **Deep‑work blocks** – 2‑3 hour periods, 2–3 per week, placed where energy peaks, but not tied to an exact clock time.
2. **Life‑admin / errand blocks** – Scheduled mid‑week or weekend when energy naturally dips.
3. **Downtime blocks** – Reserved evenings and a “nothing planned” slot (e.g., Sunday afternoon) for true rest.
4. **Float block** – One or two weekly pockets that act as overflow for shifting tasks.
5. **Spousal coordination** – The weekly plan is reviewed in a short **Sunday check‑in** to align shared responsibilities while keeping personal blocks flexible.

## Benefits
- Reduces the cognitive load of minute‑by‑minute scheduling.
- Increases resilience to last‑minute changes.
- Encourages clear communication of shared commitments.
