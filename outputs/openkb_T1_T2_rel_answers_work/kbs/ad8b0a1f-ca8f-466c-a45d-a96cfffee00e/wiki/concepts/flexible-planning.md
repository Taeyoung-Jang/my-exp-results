# Flexible Planning

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A planning approach that uses adaptable windows and slack to balance structure and flexibility.

---

## Overview
Flexible planning is a method of organizing your week (or any time period) that emphasizes **flexible windows** instead of fixed time slots.  The goal is to create a structure that supports you without feeling rigid, allowing you to adapt to changes, energy fluctuations, and unexpected events.

Key elements of flexible planning include:

- **Anchor windows** – 3‑4 recurring blocks (e.g., errands, household flow, personal projects) that act as the skeleton of the week.
- **Non‑negotiable tasks** – Core activities that must happen each week; they become the anchors that keep the rhythm stable.
- **Slack time** – Intentional open periods that provide breathing room for surprises, rest, or spontaneous activities.
- **Supportive intent** – Framing each window with a positive, motivating purpose rather than a duty.
- **Low‑effort social connections** – Tiny actions (a quick check‑in text, a podcast, a café visit) that add a sense of connection without formal scheduling.

These components help ensure that a plan feels motivating rather than oppressive, while still moving important goals forward.

---

## How It Works (Based on [[summaries/session-40_2024-05-23]])

1. **Identify non‑negotiables** – Ask yourself whether postponing a task for a week would cause stress or derail progress.  If the answer is yes, that task becomes a non‑negotiable and anchors a weekly window.
2. **Create anchor windows** – Choose 3–4 recurring periods (e.g., late Monday mornings for errands, mid‑week afternoons for household tasks, early evenings for personal projects).  These windows are flexible; you decide the exact time each day.
3. **Leave slack** – Keep whole days or large blocks unscheduled so you can absorb unpredictability and maintain energy.
4. **Add supportive framing** – Re‑label each window with a warm purpose (e.g., “get out into the world” for errands) to reduce feelings of duty.
5. **Inject social micro‑connections** – During windows, add tiny social actions like a short text to a friend or listening to a podcast, which increase connection without heavy planning.

## Benefits
- **Motivation:** By focusing on a few key windows and building in slack, you avoid feeling overwhelmed.
- **Adaptability:** The plan can shift with changes in energy, mood, or external circumstances.
- **Connection:** Tiny social actions make the week feel less isolated.
- **Efficiency:** Non‑negotiables keep important tasks from slipping through the cracks.

---

## Implementation Tips
- Start with **two anchor windows** and gradually add a third if needed.
- Review each day: note what changed, adjust tomorrow’s windows, and keep the process guilt‑free.
- Choose **one enjoyable activity** per week to look forward to; embed it within a window.
- Keep slack **visible** (e.g., a blank calendar block) so you remember it’s a resource, not wasted time.
