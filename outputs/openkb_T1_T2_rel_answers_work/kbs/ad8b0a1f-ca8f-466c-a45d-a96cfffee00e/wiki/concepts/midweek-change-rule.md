## Midweek Change Rule

The **Midweek Change Rule** is a concise protocol for handling schedule adjustments that occur during the week and impact shared responsibilities, especially between partners. It originates from the guidance in [[summaries/session-10_2022-05-23]] and is designed to keep communication lightweight while preserving the stability of a block‑based weekly plan.

### Core Elements
1. **Trigger Condition**: Notify your partner **only** when a schedule change affects a shared commitment (e.g., a joint errand, a time the other person was expecting you, or a mutually‑important appointment).
2. **Notice Timing**: Send the notification as soon as the change is known.
3. **Information to Include**:
   - What specifically changed.
   - What support or adjustment you need from your partner.
   - Your proposed solution or new time slot.
4. **Communication Channel**:
   - Use a **text message** for straightforward updates that include the above information.
   - Escalate to a **quick call** when the change creates a conflict that requires joint decision‑making (e.g., overlapping commitments that need re‑allocation).
5. **Documentation**: Log major midweek changes in a shared notes document (see the template in the [[concepts/spouse-checkin-workflow]] page) to keep a brief record for reference.

### Relationship to Other Concepts
- The rule supports a **block‑scheduling** approach by allowing flexible blocks to absorb surprises while ensuring shared blocks remain coordinated [[concepts/block-scheduling]].
- It is an integral sub‑process of the broader **spouse check‑in workflow**, which establishes a weekly review routine for aligning schedules [[concepts/spouse-checkin-workflow]].

### Practical Example
> *“Hey, I need to move our grocery trip from Thursday morning to Friday evening because a client meeting ran late. I can handle the list prep on Thursday night. Does Friday work for you?”*

This message follows the rule: it states the change, the impact, and a proposed solution, using a text rather than a call because the suggestion resolves the conflict without further discussion.
