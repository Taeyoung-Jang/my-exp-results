# Micro Learning Habit

**Definition**: A micro learning habit is a deliberately small, repeatable study action that can be performed even on the most hectic days, typically within a short, fixed time block (e.g., a 20‑minute lunch break).

## Core Principles
- **Simplicity** – Choose an action that feels almost too easy (e.g., read a short paragraph, watch a brief video segment, or complete one skill drill).
- **Consistency** – Perform the action daily, treating it as a “bubble” that requires minimal decision‑making.
- **Automaticity** – By keeping the effort minimal, the habit becomes automatic rather than a negotiation with yourself.
- **Scalability** – Start with the lowest effort level; once the habit feels stable, gently increase difficulty or duration as bandwidth allows.

## Practical Structure (from [[summaries/session-32_2025-03-14]])
1. **1 minute** – Cue: remind yourself of the current focus and open the material.
2. **15 minutes** – Focused study: execute a single, well‑defined micro‑task.
3. **2 minutes** – Quick note: jot a one‑sentence summary or next step.
4. **2 minutes** – Wrap‑up: close the material and transition back to work.

## Tracking
Use an ultra‑simple method that takes only seconds, such as a single line in a phone notes app, an X on a paper calendar, or checking off a recurring reminder. This aligns with the [[concepts/simple_tracking]] approach.

## Choosing What to Focus On
Select a skill or topic that will provide the biggest immediate benefit in the next 1‑3 months. The choice should be driven by personal relevance rather than external expectations, reinforcing motivation and habit stability.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The **Course Vetting** checklist ([[concepts/course_vetting]]) helps decide whether a course can be consumed within a micro learning habit.
- **Simple Tracking** ([[concepts/simple_tracking]]) ensures the habit remains frictionless and sustainable.

## Benefits
- Builds steady progress without overwhelming time commitments.
- Adapts to variable daily schedules (e.g., 10, 15, or 20‑minute windows).
- Reduces guilt on missed days by treating each session as independent.
