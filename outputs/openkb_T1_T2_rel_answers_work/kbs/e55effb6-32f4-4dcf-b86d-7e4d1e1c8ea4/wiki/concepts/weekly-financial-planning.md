# Weekly Financial Planning Worksheet

**Definition**: A repeatable, fill‑in‑the‑blank worksheet that translates a low‑income monthly budget into actionable weekly steps, helping people with irregular earnings allocate money consistently.

## Purpose
- Provides a concrete structure for each paycheck while keeping the overall monthly budget intact.
- Ensures essential bills are covered first, then builds a safety net and funds irregular expenses.
- Supports the broader practice of [[concepts/budgeting-unstable-income]] by anchoring spending to the lowest reliable income.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with a financial buffer strategy described in [[concepts/financial-buffering]].

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-36_2023-09-15]])
1. **Paycheck amount** – total earnings for the week.
2. **Immediate bills** – rent share, utilities, phone, insurance, minimum debt, etc., due within the next 7 days.
3. **Weekly necessities** – groceries, gas/transit, medication/essentials.
4. **Extra** – whatever remains after the above items.

## Percentage Rule for the Extra
| Portion | Percentage | Destination |
|---------|------------|-------------|
| No‑call buffer | 40 % | Savings to cover weeks with no work (the core of [[concepts/financial-buffering]]). |
| Sinking funds | 30 % | Irregular costs such as car repairs, clothing, school supplies. |
| Discretionary | 20 % | Small personal wants or optional savings. |
| Flexible overflow | 10 % | Can be shifted to any category that needs extra cash that week. |

## Worksheet Layout (plain‑text table for easy copy‑paste)
```
Weekly Budget Worksheet
--------------------------------------
Paycheck this week: __________

Bills due in next 7 days: __________
Groceries (this week): __________
Gas or transit (this week): __________
Medication/essentials: __________

Extra remaining after the lines above: __________

Apply percentage rule to the extra:
- No‑call buffer (40%): __________
- Sinking funds (30%): __________
- Discretionary (20%): __________
- Flexible overflow (10%): __________
--------------------------------------
```

## How It Connects to the Monthly Bare‑Bones Budget
- The weekly worksheet mirrors the monthly categories (rent, utilities, groceries, transportation, debt, emergency cushion) outlined in the session‑36 budget template.
- At month‑end, users compare the sum of weekly allocations against the monthly percentages to verify they stayed within the low‑income baseline.

## Benefits
- **Consistency**: Even when hours swing, the rule‑based split removes emotional decision‑making.
- **Buffer Growth**: Regular 40 % contributions quickly build a buffer that can cover 1–2 weeks of bare‑bones expenses.
- **Flexibility**: The 10 % overflow lets users adapt to unexpected needs without breaking the system.
