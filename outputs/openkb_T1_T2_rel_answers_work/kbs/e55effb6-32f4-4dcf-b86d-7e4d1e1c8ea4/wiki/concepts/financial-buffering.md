# Financial Buffering (Time & Money)

**Definition**: Financial buffering is the practice of maintaining both monetary and temporal reserves that protect you against periods of unpredictable or zero income, especially common in jobs with unstable schedules such as substitute teaching.

## Core Elements

1. **Monetary Buffer**
   - Aim for a safety net of **2 weeks to 1 month** of your *bare‑bones* monthly expenses (housing, utilities, groceries, transportation, minimum debt, essentials).  
   - Start by building a **two‑week** buffer; once achieved, expand toward a full month.
   - The buffer is funded primarily from the *extra* portion of any higher‑earning weeks using a fixed rule (see [[concepts/weekly-financial-planning]]).

2. **Temporal Buffer**
   - Reserve regular blocks of unassigned time in your calendar (e.g., 30‑minute and 1‑hour slots) that can absorb surprise meetings, last‑minute assignments, or extra grading work.
   - Anchor predictable routines (meal prep, errands, grading) at consistent times to create a stable backbone for the week.

## How It Connects to Unstable Income Budgeting

Financial buffering is a key sub‑strategy within [[concepts/budgeting-unstable-income]]. While the unstable‑income budget defines the *must‑cover* baseline based on the lowest reliable monthly earnings, buffering adds a **protective layer** that cushions the inevitable swings.

## Weekly Implementation

The weekly worksheet described in [[summaries/session-36_2023-09-15]] operationalizes buffering:

- After covering immediate bills, **40 %** of the remaining “extra” goes to the **no‑call buffer** (monetary).
- The same worksheet encourages a **flexible time buffer** by keeping a floating list of tasks that can be slotted into any free time slots.

These percentages ensure that each good week directly strengthens both money and time reserves.

## Benefits

- Reduces anxiety during *no‑call* weeks.
- Prevents debt accumulation when income drops.
- Provides mental room to handle last‑minute work without sacrificing personal well‑being.
- Enables a smoother transition between high‑earning and low‑earning periods.
