**Key Concepts**  
- [[concepts/commute-options]]: comparison of public transit, driving, and biking in terms of cost, reliability, and stress.  
- [[concepts/morning-chaos-reduction]]: strategies to make mornings calmer by choosing the right mode of transport.  
- Light‑rail smoothing tips (timing buffers, real‑time apps, disruption handling).  
- [[concepts/backup-plan-threshold]]: recommended delay cutoff (10‑15 minutes) to trigger a mode switch.  
- Criteria for choosing between bus, rideshare, or bike‑share as a backup.  
- No‑call window (8:00‑8:45 am) with no time‑sensitive commitments.

**Findings & Recommendations**  
1. **Cost & Stress**: Public transit is usually cheapest and least stressful; driving is most expensive and stressful; biking is cheapest and consistent for short‑to‑medium trips but weather‑dependent.  
2. **Light‑Rail Tips**:  
   - Add a 5‑minute buffer to the schedule.  
   - Check real‑time service apps before leaving.  
   - Have a simple default backup (nearest parallel bus, bike‑share, or rideshare).  
3. **Backup Planning**:  
   - Use a 10‑15 minute delay cutoff to decide when to switch.  
   - Choose the backup mode based on speed, weather, and route complexity:  
     * **Bus** – if a parallel route is available soon.  
     * **Rideshare** – if bus headways are long or you’re already behind.  
     * **Bike‑share** – if weather is good and the route is safe.  
4. **Scheduling**: Treat the 8:00‑8:45 am window as a quiet period for non‑urgent matters.

**Actionable Steps**  
- Implement a daily 5‑minute buffer for your light‑rail commute.  
- Install and regularly check the transit agency’s real‑time app.  
- Define your personal backup cutoff (e.g., 12 minutes) and pre‑select a preferred backup mode.  
- Mark 8:00‑8:45 am on your calendar as a quiet window for non‑urgent tasks.  

These practices aim to reduce [[concepts/morning-chaos-reduction]], improve reliability, and keep your commute stress‑free.
