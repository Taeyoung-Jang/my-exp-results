# Verbal Cue for Location Memory

A **verbal cue** is a simple spoken statement that names a visual reference (e.g., a cross‑street, house number, or landmark) right after you park or leave an item. The act of saying the cue out loud creates a brief auditory memory trace that reinforces the visual cue, making it easier to recall later without relying on apps.

## How it works (as described in [[summaries/session-32_2024-12-13]])
1. **Spot the reference** – When you park, identify a nearby permanent feature (street sign, house number, shop front).
2. **State it aloud** – Say the reference clearly (e.g., “Parked at 5th & Maple”).
3. **Connect mentally** – Briefly visualize the feature while you speak, linking the verbal label to the location.

## Benefits
- **Low‑tech** – No need for phone cameras or GPS apps.
- **Quick** – Takes only a second or two.
- **Memory‑friendly** – Auditory rehearsal strengthens recall, especially under time pressure.

## Practical tips
- Use a consistent phrasing pattern ("Parked at …", "Left my bag by …").
- Pair the cue with a brief glance at the reference to cement the visual‑verbal link.
- For items inside the car, apply the same method (e.g., “Scraper in the driver’s door pocket”).
