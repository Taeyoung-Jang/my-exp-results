# Commute Options

**Definition**: [[concepts/commute-options]] refers to the set of transportation modes—public transit, driving a personal vehicle, and biking—evaluated for their cost, reliability, and associated stress levels.

## Key Dimensions

1. **Cost**
   - *Public transit*: Typically the cheapest option, requiring only fare or pass fees.
   - *Driving*: Involves fuel, maintenance, insurance, parking, and depreciation, making it the most expensive.
   - *Biking*: Minimal ongoing costs (bike purchase, occasional maintenance) and no fare.

2. **Reliability & Predictability**
   - *Public transit*: Predictable schedules once known, though subject to occasional delays or service disruptions.
   - *Driving*: Provides control over timing, but traffic congestion and parking availability can introduce uncertainty.
   - *Biking*: Not affected by traffic jams but can be impacted by weather and road conditions.

3. **Stress Factors**
   - *Public transit*: Lower stress if service is reliable; stress spikes during delays or overcrowding.
   - *Driving*: Highest stress due to traffic, navigation, and parking hassles.
   - *Biking*: Generally low stress for short routes, but weather, safety concerns, and secure bike storage add occasional anxiety.

## Practical Implications for Morning Routines

- Choosing **public transit** or **biking** can reduce morning chaos by offering more predictable or calmer experiences compared to driving. This aligns with the [[concepts/morning-chaos-reduction]] goal of a smoother start to the day.
- When transit delays exceed a reasonable buffer (see [[concepts/backup-plan-threshold]]), a backup mode—bus, rideshare, or bike‑share—should be considered.
