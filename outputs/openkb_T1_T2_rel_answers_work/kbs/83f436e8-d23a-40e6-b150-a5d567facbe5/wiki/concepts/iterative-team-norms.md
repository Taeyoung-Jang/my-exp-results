# Iterative Team Norms

**Definition** – Iterative Team Norms are a set of shared agreements that start as a simple draft and are continuously refined through team feedback, real‑world signals, and short‑cycle adjustments. The intent is to give the team clear expectations while keeping the process lightweight and collaborative.

---

## Why Iterate?
- Turns norms into a **living document** rather than a static rule set.
- Encourages **ownership** because team members co‑create and regularly revisit the norms.
- Allows rapid response to changing contexts (new projects, hybrid work setups, evolving responsibilities).

---

## Core Practices (as described in [[summaries/session-34_2024-07-20]])
1. **Draft as Shared Agreement**
   - Begin with a short list of expectations (communication cadence, decision‑making flow, ownership language).
   - Frame it as a *first draft* to be edited, not a mandate. See [[concepts/shared-agreements]].
2. **Co‑creation in One‑on‑Ones**
   - Use early 1:1 conversations to surface personal work preferences and clarify existing project ownership.
   - Incorporate the insights into the draft before presenting it to the whole group.
3. **Team Review Session**
   - In the first team meeting, walk through the draft, ask “Does this match how you see the work?” and capture real‑time edits.
   - Keep the tone collaborative; the goal is alignment, not role imposition.
4. **Lightweight Routine Integration**
   - Tie each norm to a concrete routine (e.g., Monday async update prompts – see [[concepts/async-update-prompts]]; mid‑week check‑in).
   - Ensure routines stay brief and purpose‑driven, reinforcing the norms without adding busywork. Related concept: [[concepts/lightweight-routines]].
5. **Measure & Adjust**
   - Track simple signals such as reduced blocker frequency, clearer status updates, and higher commitment follow‑through.
   - Use these metrics to decide which parts of the norm need tweaking.
6. **Document Decisions Lightly**
   - Log any changes to the norms in a one‑line decision log (date, decision, owner, follow‑up location) – see [[concepts/decision-log-lite]].
7. **Hybrid Team Visibility**
   - Apply the same norm‑driven routines across remote and in‑office members to ensure equal visibility and participation. See [[concepts/hybrid-team-coordination]].
8. **Ownership Clarity**
   - Pair norms with a simple RACI‑lite ownership map for each project (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) – see [[concepts/racilite-ownership]].

---

## Example Norm Draft (First Iteration)
- **Communication:** Respond to async messages within 24 hours; use Monday/Friday prompts to share priorities and blockers.
- **Ownership:** Each project has a single *Responsible* owner; the *Accountable* lead signs off on deliverables.
- **Decision‑Making:** Individual decisions are taken by the responsible owner; team‑level decisions are discussed in the Wednesday check‑in.
- **Improvement Cycle:** Review norms every month; adjust based on measurable signals and team feedback.

---

## Benefits
- **Clarity without rigidity** – expectations are explicit but remain adaptable.
- **Increased psychological safety** – team members see norms as co‑created, reducing fear of top‑down control.
- **Better alignment** – regular, purpose‑driven routines keep everyone on the same page, crucial for hybrid teams.

---

## Getting Started
1. Schedule short 1:1s with each team member (≈30 min) to gather preferences.
2. Draft the initial set of norms and share them in a team channel.
3. Hold a brief team meeting to co‑edit the draft.
4. Publish the first version and note the decision in the decision log.
5. Track the defined measurable signals for the next two weeks and iterate.

---

*Iterative Team Norms* are most effective when they are treated as a collaborative experiment, continuously refined based on real‑world observations and team input.
