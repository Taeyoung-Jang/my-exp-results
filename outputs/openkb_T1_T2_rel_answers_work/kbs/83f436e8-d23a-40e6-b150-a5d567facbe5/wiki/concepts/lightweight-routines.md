# Lightweight, Purpose‑Driven Routines

**Definition**: Simple, recurring activities that exist to solve a specific coordination problem, keeping the team aligned while remaining minimal in effort and time.

## Core Principles
- **Purpose first**: Each routine is introduced only when it addresses a clear need (e.g., quick status sharing, early decision surfacing).  
- **Brevity**: Activities are kept short (5‑15 minutes) and have a single, concrete outcome.
- **Flexibility**: Routines are treated as drafts that can be adjusted after a few cycles based on feedback.
- **Shared ownership**: The team co‑creates the rhythm, turning expectations into [[concepts/shared-agreements]].

## Typical Elements (from [[summaries/session-34_2024-07-20]])
| Routine | When | Typical Prompt | Goal |
|---------|------|----------------|------|
| **Monday async update** | Start of week | "Top 1‑2 priorities, potential blockers, support needed" | Align focus and surface early help needs. |
| **Mid‑week team check‑in** | Wednesday | Quick sync (15 min) on progress and upcoming decisions | Ensure visibility for both remote and in‑office members; supports [[concepts/hybrid-team-coordination]]. |
| **Friday async wrap‑up** | End of week | "What moved, what’s still in motion, support needed next week" | Capture outcomes and plan next steps. |
| **30‑minute 1:1** | Weekly or bi‑weekly | Structured agenda covering personal check‑in, ownership, blockers, wins, next steps | Build trust and keep individual work aligned with team goals. |

## Why It Works
- **Reduces busywork**: By limiting frequency and length, routines stay low‑friction.
- **Improves signals**: Regular async prompts generate measurable signals such as faster blocker resolution and clearer ownership.
- **Supports hybrid teams**: Consistent async channels give remote members the same visibility as those in the office.
- **Feeds into continuous improvement**: Routines are reviewed and refined as part of [[concepts/iterative-team-norms]].

## Connecting Tools
- **Decision logging**: A lightweight [[concepts/decision-log-lite]] can be attached to any routine to capture outcomes without extra steps.
- **Ownership clarity**: Use a simple [[concepts/racilite-ownership]] table alongside routines to make responsibility explicit.

## Getting Started
1. Identify the most pressing coordination gap (e.g., unclear weekly priorities).  
2. Draft a one‑sentence purpose for a routine that would close that gap.  
3. Introduce the routine with a brief invitation to co‑create the format.  
4. After two cycles, ask the team for quick feedback and adjust duration, frequency, or prompts.
