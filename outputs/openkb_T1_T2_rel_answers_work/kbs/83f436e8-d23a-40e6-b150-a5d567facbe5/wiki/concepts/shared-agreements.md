# Shared Agreements

**Definition**: Shared Agreements are co‑created expectations that a team defines together, framing norms as mutual commitments instead of imposed rules.

## Core Principles
- **Co‑creation**: Team members discuss and agree on what will help them work smoothly, turning expectations into shared ownership.
- **Purpose‑driven**: Each agreement is tied to a concrete coordination problem (e.g., reducing blockers, clarifying communication channels).
- **Iterative refinement**: Agreements start as a first draft and are revisited regularly to adapt to evolving needs. See [[concepts/iterative-team-norms]].
- **Lightweight implementation**: The agreements are expressed in concise language and supported by brief routines, avoiding heavyweight processes. Related idea: [[concepts/lightweight-routines]].

## How It Was Applied in Session 34
In the conversation "[[summaries/session-34_2024-07-20]]", the new team lead was advised to:
1. **Invite conversation** about preferred ways of working during one‑on‑ones, letting the team shape expectations.
2. **Summarize themes** back to the group and propose a simple set of norms (communication cadence, decision‑surfacing, ownership) as a shared draft.
3. **Position norms as experiments** – explicitly stating they can be adjusted together, which reinforces the collaborative nature of the agreements.
4. **Model the behavior** by being clear about personal availability and response expectations, signaling that clarity is a two‑way street.

## Supporting Practices
- **Async update prompts** help surface what the team needs and keep agreements visible without meetings. See [[concepts/async-update-prompts]].
- **Decision log lite** records who owns what and what was decided, reinforcing the agreed ownership structure. See [[concepts/decision-log-lite]].
- For hybrid teams, shared agreements ensure remote and in‑office members have equal visibility and input. See [[concepts/hybrid-team-coordination]].
- A simple **RACI‑lite ownership map** can be used to clarify responsibilities within the agreements. See [[concepts/racilite-ownership]].

## Benefits
- Reduces perception of micromanagement.
- Increases team autonomy and psychological safety.
- Improves alignment on priorities, communication, and decision‑making.
- Provides a clear, adaptable framework that scales with team growth.
