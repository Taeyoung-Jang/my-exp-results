# Weekly Themes

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Broad, recurring categories that guide task allocation across a flexible week.

---

## Overview
Weekly Themes are high‑level, loosely defined topics or activity types that are assigned to different portions of the week (e.g., early‑week, mid‑week, late‑week). They act as a mental cue rather than a fixed schedule, helping self‑employed or flexible workers decide which kinds of tasks fit best in a given time block without prescribing exact start times or durations.

## Key Elements
1. **Broad Categorization** – Themes should be general enough to encompass many possible tasks (e.g., "creative/strategy," "client‑facing," "admin/maintenance").
2. **Temporal Placement** – Typically aligned with natural energy cycles: early week for high‑energy creative work, mid‑week for collaborative or client work, late week for wrap‑up and administrative duties.
3. **Flexibility** – Themes are suggestions, not obligations. When a week’s demands shift (e.g., heavy client calls), the same theme can be applied to different days or adjusted in intensity.
4. **Integration with Anchors** – Themes complement [[concepts/anchor-blocks]] by providing a contextual backdrop for each anchor’s purpose.

## Application from Session 14
In the conversation dated 2024‑06‑15, the assistant proposes a simple three‑theme model:
- **Early week (Monday‑Tuesday):** Creative or strategic work.
- **Mid‑week (Wednesday‑Thursday):** Outward‑facing or client‑related activities.
- **Late week (Friday):** Administrative tasks, wrap‑up, and review.

These themes guide the placement of the recommended anchor blocks (e.g., a Monday planning session, a Wednesday focus block, a Friday wrap‑up) while allowing the user to shift tasks around as needed.

## Benefits
- **Reduces decision fatigue** – Provides a quick mental filter for task selection.
- **Supports sustainable routines** – Aligns work type with typical energy levels, making the schedule easier to maintain.
- **Enables adaptability** – Weeks with unusual demands can still fit within the same thematic framework.
