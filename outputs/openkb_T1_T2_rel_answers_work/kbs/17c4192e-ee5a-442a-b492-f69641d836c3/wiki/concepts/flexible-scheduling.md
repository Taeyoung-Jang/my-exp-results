# Flexible Scheduling

**Definition**: Flexible scheduling is the practice of adjusting core time blocks and thematic focus periods to accommodate variable workloads while preserving a minimal structural backbone.

---

## Core Elements

1. **Anchor Blocks**
   - Fixed, purpose‑driven slots (e.g., a Monday planning session or a Friday wrap‑up) that remain constant across weeks.  
   - These provide stability without dictating every minute of the day.  
   - See [[concepts/anchor-blocks]] for details on how to choose and place anchors.

2. **Weekly Themes**
   - Broad categories (creative/strategy, client‑facing, admin) assigned to parts of the week to guide task selection.  
   - Themes are intentionally vague, allowing tasks to flow into the appropriate slot.
   - See weekly‑themes for a deeper dive.

---

## How It Works in Practice

In the conversation **[[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]]**, flexible scheduling is illustrated through:

- **Shifting focus blocks** when a week is heavy on client calls (moving them to early mornings or late afternoons) while keeping the anchor slots intact.
- **Scaling anchors** up or down: start with two anchors (Monday planning, Friday wrap‑up) and add a third or fourth only if the week feels too loose.
- **Adjusting theme emphasis**: during a client‑intensive week, the “outward‑facing” theme expands; during a solo‑work week, the “deep‑work” theme dominates mid‑week.

These tactics ensure the schedule remains both **structured enough** to provide direction and **elastic enough** to accommodate the inevitable fluctuations of self‑employment.

---

## Benefits

- **Resilience**: The week retains coherence even when unexpected meetings arise.
- **Energy Alignment**: By matching themes to typical energy levels (e.g., creative tasks early in the week), productivity is optimized.
- **Reduced Over‑Planning**: Only essential anchors are set, preventing the feeling of being locked into a rigid grid.

---

## Implementing Flexible Scheduling

1. Identify 2–4 anchor blocks that are non‑negotiable.
2. Define three simple weekly themes.
3. Review each week’s demands and **adjust the timing** of focus blocks, not the anchors.
4. Use short daily or weekly reviews to fine‑tune the arrangement.
