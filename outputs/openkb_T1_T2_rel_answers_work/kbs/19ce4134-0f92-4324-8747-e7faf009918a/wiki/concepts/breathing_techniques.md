# Breathing Techniques

Breathing techniques are structured patterns of inhalation and exhalation used to regulate the autonomic nervous system, reduce anxiety, and sharpen mental focus. In the context of short morning routines, they serve as a quick reset that can be performed in a seated or standing position without any equipment.

## Core Patterns from [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-15]]

1. **Slow nasal breathing** – Inhale for 4 seconds, exhale for 6 seconds. The longer exhale signals the body to shift toward parasympathetic dominance, helping to lower heart rate.
2. **Box breathing** – Inhale 4 seconds, hold 2 seconds, exhale 6 seconds, hold 2 seconds. The added holds extend the pause between breaths, further calming the nervous system.
3. **Exhale counting** – After a basic breathing phase, count each exhale up to a set number (e.g., 20) and then restart. This provides a simple, repetitive mental anchor that prevents wandering thoughts.

These patterns are deliberately timed (e.g., 1 minute of breathing) to fit within a five‑minute micro‑routine, allowing seamless transition to the next activity.

## How It Works

- **Physiological impact**: Extended exhalations stimulate the vagus nerve, promoting relaxation and reducing cortisol levels.
- **Cognitive impact**: The rhythmic focus on breath or count creates a narrow attentional window, limiting the space for anxiety‑driven rumination.
- **Behavioral cueing**: By pairing a breathing phase with a clear time cue (e.g., a timer or internal count), the mind receives a predictable structure, making the routine feel automatic.

## Integration with Other Morning Routine Elements

Breathing techniques often precede or accompany other micro‑habits:
- Followed by gentle **[[concepts/stretching_sequences]]** to release muscular tension.
- Paired with **[[concepts/mindful_grounding]]** practices such as hand‑on‑chest awareness or sensory scanning.
- Can be aligned with a personal **core rhythm** of the day, establishing a consistent start‑of‑day cadence.

## Practical Tips

- **Posture**: Sit upright with feet flat on the floor for maximum diaphragmatic movement; standing is fine once calm is achieved.
- **Micro‑movement**: Begin with a tiny physical action (e.g., loosening the jaw) to break the immediate anxiety loop before breathing.
- **Consistency**: Use the same timing (e.g., 1 minute) each morning to build a habit loop that cues the brain into a calm state.
