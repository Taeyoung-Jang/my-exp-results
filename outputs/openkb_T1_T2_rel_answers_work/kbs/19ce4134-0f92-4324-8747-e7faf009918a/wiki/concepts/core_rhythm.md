# Core Rhythm

**Core Rhythm** is the practice of keeping a consistent order of key morning activities—meditation, movement, breakfast, and getting ready—while allowing the duration of each block to expand or contract based on the day’s demands. This approach creates a reliable backbone for the morning, reducing decision fatigue and preserving essential habits even when time is limited.

## How It Appears in the Source Document
The concept is illustrated in [[summaries/session-08_2024-06-15]], which provides three schedule variants (slow day, busy day, and a contingency plan). Each schedule follows the same sequence:
1. **Meditation** (always retained, 20 min on regular days, trimmed to 10 min on a rushed day)
2. **Movement** (a light workout, ranging from 1 min to 15 min)
3. **Breakfast** (quick‑assembly options)
4. **Getting ready** (including buffer time before exiting)

By keeping this order constant, the user can shift the length of each block without feeling like the routine is broken.

## Benefits
- **Predictability:** Knowing exactly which habit follows the previous one reduces mental load.
- **Flexibility:** Only the *time* allocated to each block changes, not the *presence* of the block.
- **Resilience:** Even on a rushed morning, the core rhythm ensures the most valuable habits (meditation, movement, nutrition) are still performed in a compressed form.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The intentional slack between activities is captured by [[concepts/buffer_time]], which helps prevent a sense of rushing.
- Pairing the meditation with an existing habit (e.g., after brushing teeth) is an example of [[concepts/habit_stacking]], reinforcing the rhythm.

## Practical Tips to Implement a Core Rhythm
1. **Define the Fixed Order:** Write down the exact sequence you want to follow.
2. **Set Minimum Block Lengths:** Decide the shortest acceptable duration for each habit (e.g., 10 min meditation, 1 min micro‑workout).
3. **Plan Buffers:** Allocate a few minutes of buffer after the main blocks to absorb small delays.
4. **Night‑Before Prep:** Lay out anything needed for each block to streamline transitions.

By adopting a Core Rhythm, you create a sturdy yet adaptable morning framework that supports consistency and reduces stress.
