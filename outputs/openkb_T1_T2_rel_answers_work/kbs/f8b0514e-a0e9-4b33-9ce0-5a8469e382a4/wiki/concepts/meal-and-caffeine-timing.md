# Meal and Caffeine Timing

**Description**: Strategies for aligning food and stimulant consumption with a consistent early‑morning sleep‑wake cycle.

---

## Core Idea

Properly timing meals and caffeine can reinforce a desired sleep schedule, especially for those who need to rise before dawn (e.g., bakery or coffee‑shop workers).  The goal is to create strong cues for alertness when needed and promote sleep readiness at night.

---

## Key Practices (from [[summaries/session-36_2025-03-14]])

1. **First Meal Soon After Wake‑up**
   - Eat the first substantial meal within an hour of getting up to signal the start of the day's active phase.
   - This helps boost metabolism and reinforces the wake signal from bright‑light exposure.

2. **Caffeine Window**
   - Consume caffeine early in the shift, ideally within the first 3–4 hours after waking.
   - Avoid caffeine later in the day (e.g., after mid‑afternoon) to prevent interference with the wind‑down ritual and bedtime.

3. **Consistent Timing on Days Off**
   - Keep meal and caffeine timing close to the work‑day pattern even on weekends; a deviation of up to an hour is generally tolerable.
   - Larger shifts can desynchronize the internal clock, making early morning wake‑ups harder.

## Practical Tips

| Time of Day | Action |
|-------------|--------|
| **Morning (0‑2 h after waking)** | Eat a balanced breakfast; have coffee/tea if needed. |
| **Mid‑Morning (2‑4 h after waking)** | If more caffeine is required, keep the dose modest. |
| **Afternoon (after ~4 h)** | Limit caffeine; consider decaf or non‑caffeinated alternatives. |
| **Evening (2‑3 h before bedtime)** | Finish any remaining food; avoid heavy meals that could delay sleep onset. |

---

## Why It Matters

- **Circadian Alignment**: Food intake and caffeine are strong zeitgebers (time‑givers) that can shift the internal clock when mistimed.
- **Sleep Quality**: Late caffeine can increase sleep latency and reduce deep‑sleep proportion.
- **Performance**: Proper timing sustains alertness during early shifts without compromising recovery sleep.
