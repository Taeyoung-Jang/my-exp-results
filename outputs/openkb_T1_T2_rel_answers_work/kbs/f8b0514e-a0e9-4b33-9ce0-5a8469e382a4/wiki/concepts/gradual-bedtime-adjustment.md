# Gradual Bedtime Adjustment

**Definition**: A step‑by‑step method of moving bedtime earlier in small increments (typically ~30 minutes every one to two nights) to help the body adapt to a new, earlier wake‑up time without experiencing a sudden shock to the sleep system.

---

## Why It Matters
People who start early‑morning jobs—such as bakers, baristas, or shift workers—often struggle with the abrupt change in their daily rhythm. Jumping directly to a much earlier bedtime can lead to difficulty falling asleep, fragmented sleep, and feeling like a "zombie" during the first days of the new schedule.

Gradual Bedtime Adjustment reduces these negative effects by:
- Allowing the circadian rhythm to **slowly reset**.
- Minimizing sleep debt and daytime sleepiness.
- Supporting a smoother transition to consistent early wake‑times.

---

## How to Implement
1. **Determine the target bedtime** (e.g., 9:00 PM for a pre‑dawn wake‑up). 
2. **Shift earlier** by about **30 minutes** every **1‑2 nights** until the target is reached.
3. **Maintain a consistent wake‑time** even on days off, anchoring the rhythm (see [[concepts/sleep_consistency]]).
4. Pair the shift with a **wind‑down ritual** (dim lights, limit screens; see [[concepts/wind-down-ritual]]).
5. Use **light exposure** in the morning to reinforce the new schedule (see [[concepts/light-exposure-for-circadian-entrainment]]).

## Practical Tips
- Track bedtime and wake‑time in a simple log.
- Use a bedtime alarm as a reminder to start winding down.
- Avoid caffeine after mid‑afternoon to prevent interference with earlier sleep.
- If a slip occurs, resume the incremental steps rather than trying to catch up instantly.
