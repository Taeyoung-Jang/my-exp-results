# Wind‑down Ritual

A **wind‑down ritual** is a short, consistent bedtime routine that signals to the brain that it is time to prepare for sleep. By creating predictable cues—such as dimming lights, reducing screen exposure, and engaging in a calming activity—individuals can more easily transition from wakefulness to sleep, especially when adjusting to early‑start work schedules.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-36_2025-03-14]])
- **Lighting**: Dim the lights in the hour before bed to reduce blue‑light exposure.
- **Screen Management**: Turn off or limit screens (phones, computers, TV) to avoid stimulating content.
- **Calming Activity**: Perform a repeatable, soothing activity (e.g., reading a physical book, light stretching, meditation) each night.
- **Consistency**: Start the ritual at the same time each evening to reinforce the cue.

## How It Supports Other Sleep Strategies
- Works hand‑in‑hand with **[[concepts/gradual-bedtime-adjustment]]** by providing a stable nightly anchor while bedtime is being shifted.
- Complements **[[concepts/light-exposure-for-circadian-entrainment]]** by lowering light levels at night, contrasting with bright light exposure in the morning.
- Enhances **[[concepts/sleep_consistency]]** by making the bedtime schedule more reliable.
- Helps manage **[[concepts/meal-and-caffeine-timing]]** by creating a clear demarcation between evening meals/caffeine and sleep.
- Reduces the need for late‑day naps that could interfere with **[[concepts/nap-timing-and-sleep-pressure]]**.

## Practical Tips
1. **Set a Fixed Start Time** – Begin the ritual at the same clock time each night.
2. **Dim the Environment** – Use lamps or low‑watt bulbs; consider blackout curtains.
3. **Screen Curfew** – Power down screens at least 30‑60 minutes before the ritual.
4. **Choose a Calming Activity** – Keep it short (5‑15 minutes) and repeatable.
5. **Track Success** – Note how quickly you fall asleep after the ritual; adjust elements if needed.

By integrating a wind‑down ritual, sleepers can smooth the transition to rest, maintain circadian alignment, and improve overall sleep quality.
