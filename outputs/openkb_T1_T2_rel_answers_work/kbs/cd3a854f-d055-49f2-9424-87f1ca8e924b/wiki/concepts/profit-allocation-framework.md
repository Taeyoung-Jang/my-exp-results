# Profit Allocation Framework

**Definition**: A practical, repeatable checklist that guides small‑business owners on how to split each dollar of profit among four priority buckets—operational stability, high‑cost debt reduction, proven growth investments, and a resilience buffer.

## Core Steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-15_2023-06-15]])
1. **Stability** – Ensure essential operating expenses are covered and maintain a modest safety margin.
2. **High‑cost Obligations** – Pay down debt or obligations with the highest interest or risk first (e.g., a business loan).
3. **Proven Growth** – Invest in revenue‑generating activities with a clear, predictable return (effective marketing, capacity‑expanding equipment, etc.).
4. **Future/Experimental Growth** – Allocate a smaller portion to longer‑term or experimental initiatives that may not yield immediate returns.
5. **Resilience Buffer** – Continuously grow a cash cushion to handle unexpected shocks.

The framework is intended as a mental checklist rather than a rigid formula, allowing owners to revisit and adjust allocations each month based on actual cash flow.

## Monthly Allocation Example
- **Revenue:** $12,000
- **Essential expenses:** $9,000
- **Net profit:** $3,000
- **Current loan:** $18,000 @ 9 %
- **Cash reserves:** $4,000

A suggested split:
- **Buffer:** $1,000 → builds toward a two‑month expense reserve.
- **Extra loan principal:** $1,500 → would retire the loan in roughly 12 months.
- **Flex/Other:** $500 → covers minor maintenance, ad‑hoc marketing, or rolls into the buffer if unused.

## Comfort Floor & Strong‑Month Adjustments
- **Comfort floor:** Aim for roughly two months of essential expenses (≈ $18k) as a resilient safety net. One month is survivable but leaves the business vulnerable.
- **Strong month (e.g., $5k profit):** Keep the baseline allocation and split any surplus—often 50 % to the buffer and 50 % to the loan—until the buffer reaches the comfort floor. Once the buffer is sufficient, direct all additional profit to debt repayment.
