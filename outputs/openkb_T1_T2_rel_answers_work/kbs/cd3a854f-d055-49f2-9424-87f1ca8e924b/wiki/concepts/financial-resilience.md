# Financial Resilience

Financial resilience is the capacity of a business to absorb unexpected cash‑flow disruptions without jeopardizing operations or strategic goals. It is built through disciplined profit allocation, a solid cash buffer, and clear priorities for debt repayment and growth.

## Core Elements

1. **Cash Buffer** – A reserve that covers essential expenses for a set period (commonly two months). In the example from [[summaries/session-15_2023-06-15]], a "comfort floor" of about $18k (two months of $9k expenses) is recommended.
2. **Debt Management** – Prioritizing high‑interest obligations while still feeding the buffer ensures that debt reduction does not create new cash‑flow risk.
3. **Growth Allocation** – After stability and debt are addressed, surplus funds can be directed toward proven revenue‑generating investments.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/cash-flow-management]]** provides the broader framework for tracking inflows, outflows, and net profit, which feeds directly into building resilience.
- The **[[concepts/profit-allocation-framework]]** outlines the order of operations (stability → obligations → growth → resilience) that underpins a resilient financial posture.

## Practical Guidance from Session 15

- **Set a buffer target**: Aim for at least two months of essential expenses before accelerating loan paydown.
- **Allocate monthly**: In a typical month with $3k profit, the example splits $1k to the buffer, $1.5k to extra loan principal, and $0.5k to flexible needs.
- **Adjust in strong months**: When profit spikes (e.g., $5k), split the excess between the buffer and loan until the buffer reaches the comfort floor, then direct surplus to debt reduction.

By consistently applying these practices, a small business can maintain operational continuity, reduce financial stress, and position itself for sustainable growth.
