### Core ideas
- **Must‑pay essentials** (e.g., rent, car lease, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments) are funded first each pay period. [[concepts/must-pay-essentials]]
- Distinguish **fixed expenses** from **flexible spending**; fixed costs stay roughly the same each month while flexible items (groceries, gas, discretionary purchases) can vary. [[concepts/fixed-vs-flexible-expenses]]
- Use a **four‑section template** in priority order: 1) Must‑pay essentials, 2) True expenses / sinking funds, 3) Savings, 4) Flexible spending. [[concepts/budgeting-template]]
- For biweekly pay, split each monthly amount in half so that funds are set aside each paycheck, keeping you ahead of due dates. *(biweekly budgeting)*
- **Sinking funds** smooth out non‑monthly costs (e.g., car maintenance, gifts, annual memberships). *(sinking funds)*
- Allocate a modest, consistent amount to an **emergency fund** each period.
- **Rule of thumb**: flexible money should be roughly 15‑25 % of take‑home pay after fixed bills are covered.

### Example calculation (biweekly $2,400 take‑home)
| Category | Monthly amount | Per‑paycheck amount |
|----------|----------------|---------------------|
| Fixed bills (rent, lease, insurance, utilities, phone, subscriptions, credit‑card min.) | $2,605 | $1,302.50 |
| Sinking funds (maintenance, gifts, membership) | $1,340 / yr | $51.50 |
| Emergency‑fund starter | — | $100 |
| **Remaining flexible pool** | — | **≈ $946** |

The example shows how to **bucket** money: either keep separate accounts/buckets inside a budgeting app or simply label the amounts within one checking account. *(budgeting buckets)*

### Practical tips
- Move the fixed‑bill amount to a dedicated “Bills” account each payday if that helps visualise the allocation. [[concepts/cash-flow-management]]
- Keep groceries and gas inside the flexible pool, or treat them as sub‑categories that still draw from the same pool.
- Review and adjust percentages as income or expenses change. [[concepts/financial-resilience]]
