# Summary of Session 15 (2023‑06‑15)

**Main contribution:** A practical framework for small‑business owners to decide how each dollar of profit should be allocated, balancing cash‑flow stability, debt reduction, and growth.

## Core Framework
1. **Stability first** – Cover essential operating costs and maintain a safety buffer.  
2. **High‑cost obligations** – Prioritize paying down high‑interest debt (e.g., business loan).  
3. **Proven growth investments** – Allocate to revenue‑driving activities with clear ROI.  
4. **Future/experimental growth** – Seed longer‑term opportunities after the above are satisfied.  
5. **Resilience buffer** – Continuously grow a cash cushion for unexpected shocks.

> This sequence can be treated as a mental checklist rather than a rigid formula. [[concepts/cash-flow-management]] [[concepts/profit-allocation-framework]]

## Monthly Allocation Example
- **Revenue:** $12,000  
- **Essential expenses:** $9,000  
- **Net profit:** $3,000  
- **Loan balance:** $18,000 @ 9 %  
- **Current cash reserve:** $4,000  

**Proposed split:**
- **Buffer:** $1,000 → builds reserve toward a two‑month expense floor.  
- **Extra loan principal:** $1,500 → clears the loan in ~12 months.  
- **Flex/other:** $500 → minor maintenance or marketing; excess rolls into the buffer.

This illustrates the order of operations and a simple percentage split for a typical month. (plain‑text references to buffer, debt reduction, and monthly allocation)

## Comfort Floor for Cash Reserves
- Recommended comfort floor: ~2 months of essential expenses (~$18k for this case).  
- One month is survivable but leaves the business vulnerable.  

## Handling Strong Months
- If profit rises to $5k, keep the baseline allocation and split the extra $2k (e.g., 50 % to buffer, 50 % to loan) until the buffer reaches the comfort floor.  
- Once the buffer is sufficient, direct all surplus to debt repayment.
