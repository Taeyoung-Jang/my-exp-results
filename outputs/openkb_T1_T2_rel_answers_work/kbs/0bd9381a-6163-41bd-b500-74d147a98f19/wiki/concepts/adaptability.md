# Adaptability in Friendships

**Definition**: The capacity of friends to adjust their expectations, habits, and interactions as personal circumstances, priorities, and environments shift over time.

## Why Adaptability Matters
- Prevents relationships from becoming *brittle* when life events (e.g., moving, new jobs, family changes) occur.
- Allows the friendship to retain its core emotional bond while the *shape* of the interaction evolves.
- Supports long‑term satisfaction by acknowledging that the ways friends connect will naturally transform.

## Key Elements from [[summaries/session-05_2022-07-10]]
1. **Flexible expectations** – Friends should not cling to a static version of the relationship frozen at a past age.
2. **Mutual willingness to change** – Both parties notice and accept shifts, such as reduced frequency of meet‑ups or new interests.
3. **Balancing effort** – When one person steps back, adaptability involves observing whether the other reciprocates, rather than forcing the prior pattern.

## Related Concepts
- [[concepts/consistent-presence]]: Consistent presence provides stability, but adaptability determines whether that presence remains meaningful as contexts evolve.
- [[concepts/honest-communication]]: Open dialogue about changing needs enables adaptability; honest communication surfaces the adjustments needed.

## Practical Tips to Foster Adaptability
- **Check‑in on life changes**: Regularly ask how each other's circumstances are evolving.
- **Adjust interaction formats**: If in‑person hang‑ups become hard, shift to brief messages or virtual meet‑ups.
- **Embrace new interests**: When a friend develops a new hobby, show curiosity and integrate it when possible.
- **Allow space**: Recognize that stepping back temporarily can be a healthy adaptation rather than neglect.

Adaptability is not about abandoning a friendship, but about *growing together* in ways that respect each person’s current life stage while preserving the underlying bond.
