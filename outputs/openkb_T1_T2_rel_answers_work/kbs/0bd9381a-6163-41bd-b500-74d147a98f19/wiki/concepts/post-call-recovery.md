# Post‑Call Recovery

**Definition**: A set of practical habits and timing strategies that help interns recover from 24‑+ hour calls, reset their sleep homeostasis, and maintain functional performance.

---

## Core Principles
- **Treat sleep as a protected appointment** – set a clear caffeine cutoff (no caffeine after the latter half of the shift) so the stimulant wears off before the intended nap.
- **Short, bounded naps** – aim for 60‑90 minutes when you first get home; longer naps push bedtime and create a jet‑lag loop.
- **Immediate light exposure** – open blinds or step outside for ~10 minutes after waking to signal daytime to the circadian system.
- **Simple recovery meal** – a quick protein‑carb snack stabilizes blood sugar and aids alertness.
- **Gradual return to normal bedtime** – target your usual sleep window that night; a full reset usually occurs the next morning.

## Handling Common Variations
- **Late arrival (1‑2 pm)**: Keep the nap short (≈1 h) despite a later start; if too wired, lie still in a dark, quiet room for 20‑30 minutes to give the brain a partial reset.
- **Oversleep (≥4 h)**: Upon waking, get sunlight immediately, eat, stay awake until a reasonable bedtime (e.g., midnight) and let the schedule drift back over the next day.
- **Inability to nap**: Use a “quiet‑reset” – lie down, close eyes, practice deep breathing for 20‑30 minutes; this still reduces cortisol and prepares you for later sleep.

---

## Sample Post‑Call Day (adjustable for arrival time)
| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| Arrival (11 am‑2 pm) | Hydrate, quick shower if helpful, drop belongings |
| +30 min | Short nap (60‑90 min) |
| Wake | Light exposure (10 min), simple meal |
| Afternoon | Light activities (laundry, walk, low‑stakes TV) |
| Evening (9‑10 pm) | Begin wind‑down routine, aim for regular bedtime |
| Next morning | Resume normal schedule |
