# Call Bag Checklist

**Definition**: A small, pre‑assembled collection of essential items that an intern keeps packed at all times to streamline on‑call preparation, minimise last‑minute scrambling, and support basic self‑care during long shifts.

---

## Core Components
| Category | Items (examples) |
|----------|------------------|
| **Clothing** | Scrubs, a warm layer (jacket or sweater) |
| **Personal hygiene** | Deodorant, lip balm, small toiletries kit, spare toothbrush (if needed) |
| **Electronics** | Phone charger, spare socks, pens (2–3) |
| **Sleep aids** | Foam earplugs (for on‑call room naps) |
| **Nutrition** | Non‑perishable snacks: nuts, jerky, granola bars, instant oatmeal packets |
| **Miscellaneous** | Badge, ID, any required paperwork |

The goal is to have a bag that *never* needs to be assembled before a shift; it simply gets refilled when something is used or goes missing.

---

## Why It Matters
- **Decision‑fatigue reduction** – By having everything ready, interns avoid the mental load of figuring out what to bring during a chaotic on‑call schedule.
- **Self‑care support** – Includes basic hygiene and quick‑energy foods to maintain physical well‑being during 24‑28‑hour calls.
- **Sleep hygiene** – Earplugs enable short restorative naps in noisy call rooms, complementing the broader [[concepts/post-call-recovery]] strategy.
- **Automation synergy** – The checklist works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/automation-for-interns]]: both aim to establish repeatable routines that survive unpredictable rotations.

---

## Implementation Tips
1. **Designate a single bag** (backpack, duffel, or sturdy tote) and keep it in a consistent location – e.g., a locker or near the hospital entrance.
2. **Weekly audit**: spend a few minutes each weekend checking the bag and restocking any missing items.
3. **Standardise inventory**: use a printable checklist (paper or phone note) that mirrors the table above; tick off each item after a shift.
4. **Personalise**: add any specialty items (e.g., a compact stethoscope, allergy medication) that you personally need.
