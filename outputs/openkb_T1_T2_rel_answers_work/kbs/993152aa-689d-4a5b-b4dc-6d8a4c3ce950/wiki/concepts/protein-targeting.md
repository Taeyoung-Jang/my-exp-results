# Protein Targeting

**Definition**: Estimating an individual's daily protein requirement based on body weight, activity level, and goals, then distributing that amount across meals and snacks.

## Core Elements
1. **Determine baseline need**
   - Use body weight (kg) as a starting point. A common range is 1.2–1.8 g protein per kilogram for active adults.
   - Adjust for activity: higher for regular resistance training, lower for primarily sedentary lifestyles.
2. **Set a daily target**
   - For the user in [[summaries/session-07_2025-06-15]] (77 kg, moderate‑high activity), the suggested range is 110–130 g protein per day.
3. **Allocate per meal**
   - With two main meals, aim for ~40–50 g protein each, leaving room for 10–20 g from optional snacks.
4. **Translate to food portions**
   - Provide concrete portion examples (e.g., 5–6 oz cooked chicken ≈ 40–45 g protein, 10–12 oz tofu ≈ 40–45 g protein, 4 eggs ≈ 25 g protein, 1–1.5 cup Greek yogurt ≈ 15–25 g protein, 1 can tuna ≈ 25–30 g protein).

## Practical Application
- **Meal planning**: Use the per‑meal protein goal to craft simple templates that combine a high‑protein item with vegetables and a carbohydrate source.
- **Snack integration**: Add 10–20 g protein via quick options like Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tuna packets, or jerky when the main meals fall short.
- **Portion awareness**: Visual cues such as a hand‑size piece of chicken or a block‑size portion of tofu help meet targets without precise weighing.
