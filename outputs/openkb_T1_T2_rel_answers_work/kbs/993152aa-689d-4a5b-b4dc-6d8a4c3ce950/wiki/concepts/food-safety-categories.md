# Food Safety Categories for Temporary Crown Wearers

**Purpose** – Provide a clear framework for deciding which foods are *avoid*, *use caution*, or *generally safe* when wearing temporary crowns. The categories are derived from the meal‑planning guidance in [[summaries/session-37_2025-09-15]].

---

## Overview

Temporary crowns create tight margins that can trap small particles or be pulled by sticky textures. Sorting foods into three risk levels helps prevent accidental dislodgement, irritation, or the need for emergency dental care.

---

## 1. Avoid

| Food | Why it’s risky | Safer alternative |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Rice / quinoa** | Grains easily slip into crown margins and are hard to rinse out. | Blend grains into a smooth soup or puree before eating. |
| **Tortillas (even soft)** | Requires a bite that stretches the crown; the dough can pull at the temporary material. | Cut into very small pieces and soak in broth, or replace with a soft, blended flat‑bread puree. |
| **Thick nut butters** | Sticky consistency creates suction and can pry the crown loose. | Thin heavily with milk or blend into a smoothie; use only a small spoonful mixed into a liquid. |
| **Corn kernels** | Small kernels can become lodged in the crown‑tooth interface. | Remove kernels or blend corn into a velvety soup. |

---

## 2. Use Caution

| Food | Concern | Mitigation |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Pasta** | Even when cooked, it still requires chewing. | Over‑cook until very soft, cut into bite‑size pieces, or blend into a creamy sauce. |
| **Beans (whole or chunky)** | Can wedge between crown and tooth. | Mash or fully blend into a smooth hummus‑style dip. |
| **Eggs** | Firm or rubbery textures may tug at the crown. | Prepare as soft‑scrambled eggs or blend into a custard‑like base. |
| **Cheese (semi‑hard)** | Chewy varieties can pull on the crown. | Choose soft cheeses such as [[entities/cottage-cheese]] or [[entities/ricotta]]; avoid mozzarella sticks or aged cheddar. |

---

## 3. Generally Safe

These foods are low‑risk when prepared to a fully smooth, spoon‑able consistency.

- **Oats** – Cook until very soft and stir extra milk for a creamy texture. (see [[concepts/batch-prep-quantities]])
- **Hummus** – Thin with broth or water to remove any gritty texture. [[entities/hummus]]
- **Smoothies & blended soups** – No chewing needed; fully blended ingredients eliminate particle trapping.
- **Mashed potatoes**, **polenta**, **silken tofu**, **avocado**, **white bean puree** – All become smooth, non‑adhesive bases. ([[entities/mashed-potatoes]], [[entities/polenta]], [[entities/silken-tofu]], [[entities/avocado]], [[entities/white-bean]])
- **Greek yogurt**, **cottage‑cheese**, **ricotta** – Naturally soft, high‑protein options.

---

## Practical Tips

1. **Blend & Strain** – Any solid ingredient (vegetables, grains, beans) should be processed in a high‑speed blender and then strained through a fine mesh to ensure a completely smooth texture.
2. **Thin Liquids** – Use broth, milk, or water to adjust viscosity without creating sticky residues.
3. **Portion Control** – Prepare in batches sized for a 5‑day workweek (see [[concepts/batch-prep-quantities]]). Small, individual containers reduce the temptation to bite large pieces.
4. **Observe Texture** – If a food feels gritty or requires any chewing, treat it as *caution* or *avoid*.
