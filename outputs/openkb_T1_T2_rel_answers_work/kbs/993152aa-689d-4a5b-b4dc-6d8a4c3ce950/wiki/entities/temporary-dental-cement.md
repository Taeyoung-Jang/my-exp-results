## Temporary Dental Cement

Temporary dental cement is a short‑term, over‑the‑counter adhesive used to hold a **temporary crown** in place until a permanent restoration can be placed.  It is typically a water‑based or resin‑based paste that hardens quickly, providing a provisional seal that can be removed by a dentist without damaging the tooth.

### Key Points from Session 37 (2025‑09‑15)
- The user was advised that temporary dental cement may be used **only if explicitly approved by the dentist**; otherwise it can interfere with crown fit or trap debris.
- It is **not a substitute for professional dental care** and should not be used as a permanent fix.
- When a crown feels loose, the recommended steps are to stop chewing, rinse with warm water, and **avoid using any adhesive** unless the dentist has previously instructed that a specific pharmacy‑grade cement is safe for that case.
- If the crown **falls off**, keep it clean and dry, store it in a small container, and contact the dentist promptly.

### Practical Guidance
1. **Check dentist instructions** before applying any over‑the‑counter cement.
2. **Apply sparingly**: a thin layer on the crown interior, press firmly, and hold for the recommended time.
3. **Do not use super‑glue or non‑dental adhesives**; they can damage the tooth surface.
4. **Monitor**: if pain, increased mobility, or inability to bite occurs, remove the cement if possible and contact the dentist immediately.
