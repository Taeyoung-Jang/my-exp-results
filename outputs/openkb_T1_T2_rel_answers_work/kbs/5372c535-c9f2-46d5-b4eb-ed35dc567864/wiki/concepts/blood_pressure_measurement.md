# Blood‑Pressure Measurement Protocol

A **blood‑pressure measurement protocol** is a repeatable set of actions performed immediately after waking to obtain an accurate and comparable reading each day.  The goal is to minimise physiological and environmental influences that could skew the systolic and diastolic values.

---

## Core Steps (30‑Minute Morning Window)
1. **Sit calmly for 1‑2 minutes** – after getting out of bed, remain seated, breathe slowly, and avoid any movement or conversation.
2. **Take the reading** – use a validated cuff while seated with back support, legs uncrossed, arm at heart level, and in a quiet environment.
3. **Hydrate** – drink a glass of water right after the measurement; hydration helps normalise vascular tone without affecting the reading.
4. **Gentle stretch or brief walk** – light movement for a few minutes to fully awaken the body, but avoid anything that raises heart rate substantially.
5. **Simple breakfast** – consume a low‑sugar, protein‑rich meal (e.g., yogurt with berries, toast with nut butter, quick oatmeal).
6. **Delay caffeine** – postpone coffee until the latter half of the 30‑minute window, after the reading and breakfast, to prevent the stimulant from spiking the measurement.

---

## Why These Steps Matter
- **Consistency**: Repeating the same order each day reduces day‑to‑day variability, making trends more meaningful.
- **Stress avoidance**: Skipping phone checks, email, or social media eliminates mental arousal that can elevate blood pressure.
- **Physical calm**: Minimal movement before the cuff is placed prevents transient rises in blood pressure caused by muscular activity.
- **Caffeine timing**: Aligns with findings in [[concepts/caffeine_timing]] that caffeine on an empty stomach can cause jitteriness and temporary spikes.
