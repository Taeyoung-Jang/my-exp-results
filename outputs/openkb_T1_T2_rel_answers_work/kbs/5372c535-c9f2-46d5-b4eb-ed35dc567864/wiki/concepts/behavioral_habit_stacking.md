# Behavioral Habit Stacking

**Definition**: Behavioral habit stacking is the practice of attaching a small, easy‑to‑perform action to an existing anchor habit, thereby creating a chain of behaviors that reinforce each other and increase the likelihood of the routine sticking.

## How it works
1. **Identify an anchor habit** – a behavior you already do consistently (e.g., turning off the alarm and standing up).  
2. **Choose a tiny cue** – a micro‑action that takes less than a minute (e.g., splashing water on your face, a quick stretch).  
3. **Attach the cue** – perform the micro‑action immediately after the anchor habit.  
4. **Repeat** – consistency turns the pair into an automatic sequence.

## Example from [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]]
The morning routine suggested for the user uses habit stacking in several places:
- **Anchor**: Silencing the alarm placed across the room forces the user to stand up.  
- **Stacked action**: While drinking the first cup of coffee, the user performs a quick wake‑up move (face splash, stretch, or stepping outside). This tiny step reinforces the awakening habit and makes the coffee‑drinking moment a trigger for alertness.
- **Further stack**: Preparing the second cup while dressing creates a smooth transition from “getting ready” to “leaving the house.”

By keeping each stacked action under two minutes, the routine stays simple and low‑friction, which is crucial for habit formation.

## Relation to other concepts
- This technique is a core component of [[concepts/morning_routine_design]], where the goal is to build repeatable start‑of‑day sequences.
- It often leverages devices enabled by [[concepts/smart_home_automation]] (e.g., smart plugs that start coffee brewing) to create reliable cues.

## Practical tips
- **Start small** – Choose actions that take ≤30 seconds.
- **Make it specific** – “splash water on face for 10 seconds” works better than a vague “wake up.”
- **Keep the chain linear** – One cue leads to the next; avoid branching that can create decision fatigue.
- **Pair with a reward** – The aroma of fresh coffee provides an intrinsic reward that strengthens the stack.

## When to adjust
If a stacked action feels cumbersome, reduce its duration or replace it with an even simpler cue. Conversely, once a stack is solid, you can add another micro‑action to deepen the routine.
