# Time Blocking

**Definition**: Scheduling fixed blocks of time for particular activities (e.g., focused work, meals, exercise) to create structure, protect deep work, and reduce decision fatigue.

## Core Elements
- **Anchor points**: Start and end of the day with routine activities that frame the schedule.
- **Focused work blocks**: Dedicated periods for high‑energy tasks, as seen in the weekday schedule from [[summaries/session-13_2024-06-15]].
- **Buffer periods**: Short gaps (10‑15 min) between blocks to handle transitions and unexpected tasks, linking to [[concepts/buffer-time]].
- **Energy alignment**: Matching task difficulty to natural energy peaks throughout the day. The schedule from [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-13]] demonstrates aligning sleep, meals, and workouts to a late‑shift energy curve, using consistent wake‑up and bedtime times to support circadian rhythm.

## Benefits
- Improves productivity by protecting deep‑work windows.
- Reduces the mental load of deciding *what* to do next.
- Provides clear structure while still allowing flexibility via buffers.
- Supports health goals by synchronizing meal and exercise blocks with work shifts, helping to prevent late‑night snacking.

## Application in Session‑13 Example
The schedule proposed in the session‑13 conversation uses time‑blocking to:
1. Separate **morning routine**, **breakfast**, and **wind‑down** as fixed points.
2. Allocate two major **focused work blocks** before lunch and a longer block after lunch.
3. Insert **buffer** periods (e.g., 10‑15 min) to aid transitions and accommodate surprises.
4. Include dedicated slots for **exercise**, **dinner**, **downtime**, and a **daily review** at the end of the day (see [[concepts/daily-review]]).

## Application in Session‑37 Example
The late‑shift routine in session‑37 extends time‑blocking to a non‑traditional schedule:
1. **Consistent anchor points**: Wake at 7:45 am and bedtime by 11:15 pm each day, even on weekends, to keep a stable circadian rhythm.
2. **Meal blocks**: Fixed times for breakfast (8:15 am), early lunch (11:15 am), main shift meal (3:30–4:00 pm), optional work snack (6:00 pm), and light evening snack (9:00 pm) align with the [[concepts/shift-meal-timing]] principle.
3. **Workout block**: Morning exercise (8:45–9:15 am) after breakfast, prioritizing energy for the workday and avoiding post‑work fatigue.
4. **Buffer periods**: Small gaps between activities (e.g., commute, transition to work) act as natural buffers, reinforcing the [[concepts/buffer-time]] concept.
5. **Weekend adaptation**: Same wake/sleep anchors with adjusted activity blocks, preserving the rhythm while allowing lighter movement or rest.
