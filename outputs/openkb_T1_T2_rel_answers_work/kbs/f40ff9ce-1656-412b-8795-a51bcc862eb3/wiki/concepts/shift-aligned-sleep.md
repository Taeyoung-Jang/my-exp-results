# Shift‑Aligned Sleep

**Definition**: A sleep schedule that keeps wake‑up and bedtime consistent while aligning core sleep hours to support a late‑shift work pattern.

## Core Elements
- **Fixed wake time** (e.g., 7:45 am) and **fixed bedtime** (around 11:15 pm) even on days off, preserving circadian rhythm.
- **Sleep window** of roughly 7–8 hours that fits before the early‑morning portion of a day that is otherwise a "prime time" for a noon‑to‑8 pm shift.
- **Buffer time** before bed (wind‑down routine starting ~10:45 pm) to reduce stimulation and aid sleep onset.

## Why It Matters
- Prevents the internal clock drift that often accompanies rotating or late shifts, reducing fatigue and improving alertness during work hours.
- Supports metabolic stability, helping to regulate appetite and avoid late‑night snacking.
- Enables a predictable routine that pairs well with **[[concepts/time-blocking]]** for meals, workouts, and commute.

## Practical Application (from [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-13]])
- **Wake at 7:45 am** → immediate breakfast → morning workout.
- **Bedtime routine** starts at 10:45 pm, aiming for sleep by 11:15 pm.
- Weekend days retain the same schedule, only activity blocks shift (light cardio or rest), keeping the sleep anchor stable.
