# Buffer Time

**Definition**: Buffer Time refers to short, deliberately scheduled periods (typically 10‑15 minutes) placed between activities to absorb overruns, handle unexpected tasks, and give mental reset before moving on.

## Why Buffer Time matters
- **Reduces friction** when switching from one activity to another, preventing the feeling of a continuous sprint.
- **Accommodates variability** in task length, especially in work blocks that may run over or finish early.
- **Creates mental space** for a quick check‑in, allowing you to note what’s working and what needs adjustment.

## How Buffer Time is used in practice
The source document *session-13_2024-06-15* demonstrates Buffer Time in a daily routine:
- After each focused work block (8:00‑10:00, 10:15‑12:00, 13:00‑15:30) a 10‑15 minute buffer is scheduled.
- Buffers also appear before and after exercise, and between lunch and the afternoon work block.
- These buffers are labeled explicitly in the schedule, e.g., `10:00–10:15 Buffer`.

## Relationship to other concepts
- Buffer Time is a key component of [[concepts/time-blocking]], providing the flexibility needed for realistic schedules.
- It supports the [[concepts/daily-review]] habit by giving a moment to reflect on the preceding block before moving forward.

## Practical tips for implementing Buffer Time
1. **Keep it short** – 5‑15 minutes is usually enough to transition without eating into core work.
2. **Label it** – Clearly mark buffer slots in your calendar so you treat them as intentional.
3. **Use it purposefully** – Review notes, stretch, hydrate, or handle minor tasks that arise.
4. **Adjust as needed** – Track how often buffers are needed and tweak their length over time.

## Example from the source
```text
8:00–10:00 Focused work block
10:00–10:15 Buffer
10:15–12:00 Focused work block
12:00–12:45 Lunch
12:45–13:00 Buffer
```
This pattern repeats throughout the day, ensuring a smooth flow while keeping the overall schedule realistic.
