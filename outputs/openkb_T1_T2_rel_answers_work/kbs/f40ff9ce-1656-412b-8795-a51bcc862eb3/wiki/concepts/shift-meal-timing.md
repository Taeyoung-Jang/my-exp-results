## Shift Meal Timing

**Definition** – A structured approach to placing meals at specific clock times that complement a non‑traditional work schedule, especially a noon‑to‑8 p.m. shift. The goal is to stabilize energy levels, prevent late‑night grazing, and support recovery from workouts.

**Core Principles**
- **Front‑loading calories**: Eat a solid breakfast soon after waking and a substantial early‑lunch before the shift starts.
- **Mid‑shift fueling**: Schedule a main meal around 3:30–4:00 pm to avoid hunger during work hours.
- **Evening buffer**: Limit post‑work eating to a light snack (e.g., [[entities/greek-yogurt]] or a protein shake) around 9 pm, keeping portions small to protect sleep quality.
- **Consistent timing**: Keep wake‑up and bedtime consistent (see [[concepts/shift-aligned-sleep]]) to anchor the meal windows.

**Typical Daily Schedule** (derived from [[summaries/session-37_2024-12-13]])
| Time | Meal | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| 8:15 am | Breakfast | Eggs, toast, oatmeal, or a protein smoothie with [[entities/protein-powder]]. |
| 11:15 am | Early Lunch | Protein (e.g., [[entities/chicken-breast]]) with grains and veggies – a pre‑shift fuel. |
| 3:30‑4:00 pm | Main Shift Meal | Grain‑protein bowl or hearty sandwich; aims to sustain energy through the rest of the shift. |
| 6:00 pm (optional) | Light Work Snack | Small, portable items – banana, nuts, or a single‑serve yogurt. |
| 9:00 pm | Evening Snack | Half‑to‑one cup Greek yogurt with berries, or a single‑scoop protein shake. |

**Benefits**
- Reduces impulsive late‑night snacking.
- Aligns nutrient intake with circadian rhythms, improving sleep (see [[concepts/shift-aligned-sleep]]).
- Supports workout recovery by delivering protein soon after exercise.

**Implementation Tips**
1. **Plan ahead**: Use batch‑meal‑prep strategies to have ready‑to‑eat portions for the 11:15 am and 3:30 pm meals.
2. **Set reminders**: Treat each meal as a time‑block appointment.
3. **Track intake**: Review daily logs (see [[concepts/daily-review]]) to adjust portion sizes or timing.
4. **Flexibility**: On off‑days, keep the same wake‑sleep window but shift meals earlier or later as needed, maintaining the same intervals.

By stabilizing when you eat, shift workers can maintain steady energy, improve recovery, and protect sleep quality.
