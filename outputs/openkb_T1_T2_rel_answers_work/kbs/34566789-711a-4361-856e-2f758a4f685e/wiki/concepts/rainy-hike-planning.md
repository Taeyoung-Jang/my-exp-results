# Rainy Hike Planning

**Definition**: Strategies and considerations for selecting and completing easy, short hikes when the weather is wet or drizzly, focusing on comfort, safety, and minimal crowd impact.

## Key Elements

1. **Trail Selection**
   - Choose trails with good natural drainage and a clear, well‑defined path.  The **[[entities/coal-creek-falls]]** trail in **[[entities/bellevue]]** (accessed via the **[[entities/lakemont-boulevard]]** trailhead) is highlighted as a suitable option because it stays relatively dry and offers a short 60–90 minute out‑and‑back loop.

2. **Duration & Difficulty**
   - Aim for hikes lasting 1–1.5 hours to keep exposure limited.  A gentle elevation profile and minimal navigation challenges are ideal for drizzly days.

3. **Footwear**
   - Waterproof shoes provide extra protection, but sturdy sneakers with good tread are acceptable if you don’t mind occasional damp spots.  The trail’s drainage reduces the need for heavy boots.

4. **Parking & Access**
   - Early arrival improves chances of finding the small lot or street parking near the trailhead.  In the case of **[[entities/coal-creek-falls]]**, parking is usually available on **[[entities/lakemont-boulevard]]** if you get there before the midday rush.

5. **Timing**
   - Mid‑morning (around 9‑11 am) often offers drier footing and fewer hikers, aligning with the broader theme of **[[concepts/low-key-activities]]**.

6. **Safety Precautions**
   - Carry a light rain jacket, extra layers, and a small pack of emergency supplies.  Check recent trail reports for any washouts before heading out.
