# Gym Tour Red Flags

A **gym tour red flag** is any observable cue during a site visit that suggests future dissatisfaction, hidden costs, or a poor fit for your training routine. Recognizing these signals early helps you avoid contracts that feel restrictive or environments that hinder consistency.

---

## Core Red Flags Identified in [[summaries/session-40_2024-06-15]]

1. **Overcrowded weight area during your target hours**
   - When two‑thirds or more of the racks are occupied between 5‑7 pm, expect long wait times and rushed sets.
   - Look for people hovering around equipment, stacking plates, or using the floor as a waiting area.

2. **Staff evading clear answers**
   - If sales reps dodge questions about monthly costs, cancellation steps, or fee structures, the contract likely contains hidden penalties.
   - A lack of written documentation or vague “we’ll discuss later” responses is a strong warning.

3. **Insufficient free‑weight infrastructure**
   - Few squat racks, limited deadlift platforms, or a cramped layout that forces you to share space can limit your barbell work.
   - Observe the condition of bars, plates, and overall flooring; rusted or poorly maintained gear can signal neglect.

4. **Poor cleanliness or maintenance**
   - Dirty benches, sticky floors, or a bad chalk policy may indicate overall low upkeep, affecting safety and motivation.

5. **No‑show amenities that don’t add value**
   - Extravagant locker rooms, pools, or saunas that are rarely used can distract from the core resources you need and may inflate membership price.

---

## How to Spot Red Flags Quickly (10‑minute rubric)

- **Count racks**: Total stations vs. those in use. > 66 % occupancy → red flag.
- **Watch wait times**: If a rack stays occupied for the entire 10‑minute window, expect regular delays.
- **Observe overflow behavior**: People standing with belts on, waiting by racks, or using unconventional spaces for lifts.
- **Ask direct questions**: Use a pressure‑proof script (see [[concepts/strength-training-gym-checklist]]) and note if staff deflect or provide written answers.

## Action Steps

1. **Document observations** during the 5–7 pm window.
2. **Score each red‑flag category** (e.g., 1 = none, 5 = severe).
3. **Compare across gyms** and prioritize the location with the fewest high‑severity flags.
4. **Follow up** by requesting written contract terms for any lingering concerns.

By systematically noting these red flags, you can filter out gyms that look good on paper but falter in real‑world usage.
