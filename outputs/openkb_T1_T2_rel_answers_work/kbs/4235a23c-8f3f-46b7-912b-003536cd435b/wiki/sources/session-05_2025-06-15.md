# Conversation session 5

Date: 2025-06-15 14:30

**User:** I’m trying to figure out why a couple apps on my phone keep nagging me to enable location even though I keep my location services turned off for most apps by default. Can you help me think through what settings I should check (iPhone vs Android tips are fine), and what permissions are actually worth turning on without giving up too much privacy?

**Assistant:** A good first step is to check whether the apps are asking for location because they’re genuinely blocked from doing something, or simply because they *want* extra data. On both iPhone and Android you can usually stop the nagging without giving the apps constant access.

On an iPhone, open the app’s entry in Settings and look at its location permission. Switching it to “Ask Next Time or When I Share” or “While Using the App” is usually enough. If an app doesn’t truly need your location to function, it will still work but might show a one‑time pop‑up you can dismiss. It also helps to check whether “Precise Location” is turned on; leaving it off gives the app only a rough idea of where you are.

On Android, open the app info screen, tap Permissions, and set Location to “Allow only while using the app” or “Deny.” You can also turn off “Use precise location” there. Some manufacturers add extra privacy settings like permission auto‑reset, which automatically revokes access if you haven’t used the app in a while.

In general, the permissions worth allowing are the ones that directly affect the app’s core function. Maps, ride‑hailing, weather, and check‑in apps usually work best with “While Using the App.” Anything like shopping, social media, or casual games usually works fine with location denied entirely. If an app keeps nagging even though it doesn’t really need the data, that’s usually a sign you can safely keep it blocked.

**User:** On iPhone, where exactly is the “Precise Location” toggle, and is there a way to stop repeated prompts without fully granting location? Also, on Android, is “Allow only while using” still safe if the app runs in the background (like with background refresh), or do I need to change another setting too?

**Assistant:** On an iPhone, you’ll find the Precise Location toggle by opening Settings, scrolling down to the specific app, tapping Location, and looking just under the permission options. It sits right below the “Never / Ask Next Time / While Using / Always” choices. Turning it off keeps the app from knowing your exact spot. To reduce repeated prompts, choosing “Ask Next Time or When I Share” usually helps, but some apps still nag. If that happens, “While Using the App” is the least‑invasive option that stops most pop‑ups without giving the app constant access.

On Android, “Allow only while using the app” means the app can’t get location while running in the background. Background refresh or background activity doesn’t override that. If the app truly needs background location, it has to request the special “Allow all the time” permission, and you would have to manually approve it. As long as you don’t grant that, background access stays blocked.

**User:** Got it. One more thing: if I set an iPhone app to “While Using,” can it still grab location in the background via widgets, Live Activities, or Background App Refresh? And on Android, where do I check whether an app has “Allow all the time” location already enabled (and how do I revoke it)?

**Assistant:** On an iPhone, “While Using” is pretty strict. An app can only access location when it’s visibly active on screen or when it’s running something that counts as user‑initiated foreground activity. Widgets, Live Activities, and Background App Refresh don’t count as full foreground use, so the app can’t silently grab your location from those. The only exception is if the app also has the “Always” permission, but since you wouldn’t give it that, you’re safe.

On Android, you can check for “Allow all the time” by going to Settings, then Privacy or Security & Privacy (the wording varies), then Location, then App Location Permissions. You’ll see the apps grouped by permission level, including any that have “Allow all the time.” Tap an app to change it, and switch it to either “Allow only while using” or “Deny.” That instantly revokes background access.

**User:** Thanks—last question: are there any iPhone system services (like “Significant Locations,” “Find My,” “Location-Based Alerts,” etc.) that can make it *seem* like an app is using location even when the app’s permission is set to “While Using” or “Never”? I want to keep the useful stuff on but cut down on anything that’s basically tracking.

**Assistant:** iPhone system services can trigger the location indicator even when no app you’ve installed is allowed to access your location, but they **don’t** hand that location over to apps that are set to “While Using” or “Never.” The system is simply using location for its own features.

The ones most likely to make it look like “something” is using your location are things like Location‑Based Alerts, Location‑Based Suggestions, System Customization, Significant Locations, and sometimes Networking & Wireless. These let iOS itself use your location to power things like time‑sensitive reminders, Siri suggestions, optimized Wi‑Fi/Bluetooth behavior, or traffic routing. Again, apps don’t get access unless you granted it.

If you want to keep the useful features without unnecessary tracking, the biggest privacy‑saver is turning off Significant Locations. It’s mainly for system learning and isn’t required for day‑to‑day functions. Location‑Based Alerts and Find My are generally harmless from a privacy‑to‑third‑party‑apps perspective, and they won’t leak location to apps you’ve denied.

So the short version is: system services can make the location arrow appear, but they don’t override your app permissions. The only thing to watch for is whether you’re comfortable with iOS itself logging some movement patterns; turning off Significant Locations is the main way to cut that down.
