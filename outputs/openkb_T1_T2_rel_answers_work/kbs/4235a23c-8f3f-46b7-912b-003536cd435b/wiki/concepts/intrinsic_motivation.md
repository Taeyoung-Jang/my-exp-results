# Intrinsic Motivation

**Intrinsic motivation** refers to the drive to engage in an activity because it is inherently enjoyable, satisfying, or aligned with personal values, rather than for external rewards or pressure.

## Key Characteristics
- **Internal satisfaction**: The activity itself feels rewarding.
- **Self‑determination**: Individuals feel autonomous and in control of their actions.
- **Sustained engagement**: Motivation persists even when external incentives are absent.

## Relevance to Sustainable Running
In [[summaries/session-35_2025-09-15]], the advice emphasizes focusing on how a run feels—energy levels, mood, and enjoyment—over raw numbers like pace or distance. This shift supports intrinsic motivation by:
1. Encouraging runners to notice personal **feel‑good cues** rather than performance metrics.
2. Reducing **performance anxiety** that can arise from constant live stats.
3. Aligning the running habit with broader **habit formation** principles ([[concepts/habit_formation]]), making the activity a natural part of daily life.

## Practical Tips from the Session
- **Turn off live metrics**: Hide pace/distance during the run to keep attention on bodily sensations.
- **Selective tracking**: Record only a few representative runs, allowing most outings to remain free of data pressure.
- **Focus on consistency**: Regular, enjoyable runs build a sense of competence and autonomy.

## Connections to Other Concepts
- **Sustainable Running**: Intrinsic motivation underpins a long‑term, burnout‑free running routine ([[concepts/sustainable_running]]).
- **Habit Formation**: When motivation is internal, habits are more likely to stick ([[concepts/habit_formation]]).

## Related Entities
- **Running App**: Configurable to hide live stats, supporting intrinsic focus.
- **Smartwatch**: Can be set to minimal display mode for the same purpose.

By prioritizing internal rewards, runners can maintain a **motivating, low‑pressure environment** that encourages regular activity and long‑term adherence.
