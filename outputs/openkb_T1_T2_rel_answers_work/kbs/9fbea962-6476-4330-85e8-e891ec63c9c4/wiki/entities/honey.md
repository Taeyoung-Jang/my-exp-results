## Honey

Honey is a viscous natural sweetener produced by bees from floral nectar. In cooking it is prized for its ability to **round out harsh heat** and add a subtle depth of flavor without making a dish overtly sweet.

### Uses highlighted in [[summaries/session-03_2024-09-05]]
- Recommended as a **finishing addition** for spicy dishes such as chili, curry, or stir‑fries to temper heat and create a more complex palate.
- Paired with a **tiny squeeze of acid** (e.g., [[entities/lemon]] or [[entities/lime]]) to balance sweetness and prevent a cloying finish.
- Can be combined with flavored oils like [[entities/chili-oil]] or [[entities/sesame-oil]] for an added layer of richness.

### Relationship to seasoning concepts
- Fits within the **[[concepts/seasoning-layering]]** approach: added at the final stage to smooth and integrate earlier flavor layers.
- Works alongside **[[concepts/acidity-timing]]** by counteracting the sharpness of late‑stage acids.
- While not a spice, honey complements toasted spices discussed in **[[concepts/spice-toasting]]**, helping to bind and mellow their intensity.

### Related pantry staples
- Often used together with other universal ingredients such as [[entities/salt]], [[entities/garlic]], [[entities/onion]], [[entities/paprika]], and [[entities/cumin]] to build balanced, layered flavors.
