# Paprika

Paprika is a ground spice made from dried red peppers. It is valued for its vibrant color, mild heat, and slightly sweet, earthy flavor. In the **seasoning-layering** approach described in [[summaries/session-03_2024-09-05]], paprika is listed as a core pantry staple to use early in the cooking process to build depth.

## Usage in cooking
- **Early stage**: Add paprika right after aromatics like [[entities/garlic]] and [[entities/onion]] to allow the spice to bloom in oil, following the principles of [[concepts/spice-toasting]].
- **Mid stage**: Combine with other warm spices such as [[entities/cumin]] and a pinch of [[entities/salt]] to create a rounded heat profile.
- **Finishing touches**: While paprika is typically not a finishing spice, a small sprinkle can reinforce color and flavor before serving.

## Relation to other concepts
- Part of the **three‑wave heat plan** that moves from deep early heat to bright late heat, as outlined in [[concepts/seasoning-layering]].
- Works well with acidic finishes (e.g., [[entities/lemon]] or [[entities/lime]]) to balance its earthiness, linking to [[concepts/acidity-timing]].

## Practical tip
To avoid a dusty flavor, toast paprika briefly in oil after the onions and garlic have softened; this releases its aromatic oils without burning.
