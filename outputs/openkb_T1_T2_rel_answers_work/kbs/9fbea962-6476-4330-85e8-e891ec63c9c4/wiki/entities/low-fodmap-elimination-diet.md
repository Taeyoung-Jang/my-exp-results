## Low‑FODMAP Elimination Diet

The **Low‑FODMAP Elimination Diet** is a medically‑guided eating protocol designed to reduce gastrointestinal symptoms, especially for people with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS). During the elimination phase, all high‑FODMAP foods—including onions, garlic, certain fruits, dairy, and legumes—are avoided. The diet focuses on a limited set of safe foods that are low in fermentable carbohydrates.

### Key Facts from [[summaries/session-33_2025-05-07]]
- **Core base foods**: plain rice, baked chicken, and a simple low‑FODMAP broth prepared in bulk on Sundays.
- **Weekly structure**: a 7‑day dinner checklist that reuses the base foods while varying texture, herbs, spices, and safe add‑ins (e.g., carrots, zucchini, spinach, cucumber).
- **Portion guidance**: specific amounts such as ½ cup cooked carrots, 3–4 oz chicken, and 1 cup rice per meal to stay within safe limits.
- **Flavor kits**: three pre‑made condiment jars (lemon‑herb, ginger‑sesame, golden) that add variety without extra prep.
- **Safe rotation list**: herbs and spices like thyme, oregano, rosemary, basil, parsley, chives, scallion greens, turmeric, black pepper, and ginger; vegetables such as carrots, zucchini, spinach, cucumber, and green beans in standard portions.
- **Hidden risks**: common pantry items (store‑bought broths, seasoning blends, sauces) may contain onion or garlic powder; see [[concepts/hidden-onion-garlic-sources]] for details.

### Practical Use
The diet’s strict framework enables predictable meal preparation while the flavor kits and safe‑rotation list keep meals interesting. By following the checklist and monitoring hidden onion/garlic sources, individuals can maintain symptom control without feeling stuck in repetitive meals.
