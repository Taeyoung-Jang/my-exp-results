# Energy Signals

**Definition**: Energy Signals are observable cues—behavioural, emotional, and physiological—that indicate whether a person is genuinely energized by a type of work or role. They help individuals validate their true motivation rather than mistaking novelty or prestige for lasting drive.

---

## Core Elements (as described in [[summaries/session-39_2025-03-14]])

1. **Sustained Focus** – Time seems to pass quickly when engaged in the task, even if progress is not immediately visible.
2. **Voluntary Re‑engagement** – The person returns to the problem on their own after periods of ambiguity or low‑feedback.
3. **Internal Pride** – A sense of satisfaction arises without external validation (e.g., a “quiet pride” when completing a strategic analysis).
4. **Energy Level During the "Murky Middle"** – Consistent enthusiasm when the work is messy, slow‑feedback, or unclear, as opposed to feeling drained.
5. **Proactive Influence** – A natural inclination to align stakeholders or shape discussion, even without formal authority.

These signals are contrasted with **drain signals** (e.g., feeling exhausted, avoidance of the task, or reliance on immediate praise) which suggest a mismatch between the role and intrinsic motivation.

---

## How Energy Signals Relate to Other Concepts

- **[[concepts/motivation_type]]** – Energy Signals help differentiate between short‑term (quick wins) and long‑term (strategic) motivation types.
- **[[concepts/slow_feedback]]** – Many Energy Signals surface during slow‑feedback cycles, making them essential for evaluating strategic roles.
- **[[concepts/validation_methods]]** – Tracking Energy Signals is a practical validation method for confirming true motivation.
- **[[concepts/career_decision_factors]]** – Energy Signals are a key factor when weighing career moves, especially between operational and strategic tracks.
- **[[concepts/strategic_vs_operational_roles]]** – The presence of Energy Signals for strategic tasks indicates suitability for strategic versus operational positions.

---

## Practical Use

1. **Journal Daily** – Note moments of high focus, voluntary re‑engagement, and internal pride.
2. **Signal Rating** – Assign a simple 1‑5 rating for each signal after a work episode.
3. **Pattern Review** – After 2‑3 weeks, review the pattern: consistent high scores suggest a good fit; mixed or low scores signal a possible misalignment.
4. **Feedback Loop** – Combine self‑observations with external feedback (e.g., comments on clarity or influence) to triangulate the signal.
