# Motivation Type

**Definition**: Motivation Type refers to the underlying driver that energizes a person’s work behavior, typically falling on a spectrum from a preference for quick, visible wins (short‑term, operational focus) to a preference for slower, strategic influence that may take months or years to materialize.

---

## Overview

In the context of career growth and personal fulfillment, understanding one’s Motivation Type helps individuals choose roles, projects, and experiments that align with their natural energy sources. The concept is discussed in depth in [[summaries/session-39_2025-03-14]], where a dialogue explores how to surface and validate this preference.

---

## Two Primary Poles

| Pole | Typical Characteristics | Related Concepts |
|------|--------------------------|------------------|
| **Short‑term / Operational** | Thrives on immediate feedback, rapid results, and tangible outcomes. Enjoys execution‑heavy tasks and feels drained by prolonged ambiguity. | [[concepts/strategic_vs_operational_roles]], [[concepts/energy_signals]] |
| **Long‑term / Strategic** | Draws energy from shaping direction, influencing without authority, and working on problems with slow‑feedback loops. Comfortable with ambiguity and enjoys thinking about systems and trade‑offs. | [[concepts/slow_feedback]], [[concepts/validation_methods]], [[concepts/energy_signals]] |

---

## Identifying Your Motivation Type

1. **Signature Response** – Recall moments when you felt genuinely energized. Note whether the work involved quick wins or strategic, ambiguous challenges. (See [[concepts/energy_signals]] for a deeper look.)
2. **Feedback Loop Preference** – Observe how you react when progress is delayed. Persistent enthusiasm suggests a strategic orientation; frustration points to an operational bias.
3. **Drain vs. Drive** – Tasks that feel limiting rather than empowering indicate a mismatch with your Motivation Type.

---

## Validation Methods

To confirm a hypothesized Motivation Type, apply small‑scale experiments (see [[concepts/validation_methods]]) such as:
- Drafting a cross‑team problem statement.
- Writing a lightweight strategy memo.
- Facilitating alignment conversations.
Track energy levels, outcomes, and qualitative feedback to see which pole resonates over time.

---

## Career Decision Factors

Motivation Type intersects with broader career decision frameworks. When considering a new role or project, weigh how well the opportunity aligns with your preferred pole. This alignment influences long‑term satisfaction and performance (see [[concepts/career_decision_factors]]).

---

## Practical Implications

- **For managers**: Offer opportunities that match team members’ Motivation Types to boost engagement.
- **For individuals**: Seek experiments that expose you to both poles; the sustained energy signal will guide your career trajectory.
