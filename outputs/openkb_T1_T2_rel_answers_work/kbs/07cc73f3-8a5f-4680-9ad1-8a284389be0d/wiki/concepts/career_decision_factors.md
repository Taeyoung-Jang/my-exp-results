# Career Decision Factors

**Definition**: The set of personal and professional considerations used to evaluate whether a strategic (broader, cross‑team, higher‑level) or operational (hands‑on, execution‑focused) role aligns with an individual’s long‑term goals and well‑being.

## Core Elements

1. **Energy Source**
   - Identify which type of work energizes you day‑to‑day. Does influencing direction and coordination excite you, or do you thrive on shipping, fixing, and seeing quick results? Observe the **energy signals** that indicate genuine engagement.
2. **Skill Evolution**
   - Strategic roles tend to develop influence, communication, and big‑picture thinking, while operational roles deepen technical mastery and domain expertise.
3. **Lifestyle & Stress**
   - Broader roles often involve ambiguity, political navigation, and less predictable schedules. Operational roles are usually more predictable but can be intense in the moment.
4. **Motivation Authenticity**
   - Ask whether you’re drawn to a role because it genuinely excites you or simply because it sounds impressive. Relate this to your underlying **motivation type** to ensure the drive is authentic.
5. **Feedback Horizon**
   - Consider the speed of feedback you prefer. Strategic work often operates on **slow‑feedback** cycles, where outcomes become visible only after weeks or months. Operational work usually provides rapid, tangible results.
6. **Validation Through Experiments**
   - Test hypotheses about your fit by running short‑term experiments (e.g., shaping a problem statement, drafting a strategy memo, facilitating alignment meetings). Use the **validation methods** outlined in related guidance and track energy, outcomes, and feedback over 60–90 days.
