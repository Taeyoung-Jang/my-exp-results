# Validation Methods for Career Choices

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-15]]

## Overview
Validation methods are practical approaches used to test assumptions about a potential career move before making a final decision. They help you gather concrete evidence about day‑to‑day responsibilities, cultural fit, and personal impact, reducing reliance on impressions or surface‑level information. Recent insights from [[summaries/session-39_2025-03-14]] add a focus on measuring personal energy signals and long‑term motivation during these tests.

## Core Methods Highlighted in the Source Document

1. **Informational interviews** – Speak with people already in the target role or adjacent positions. Ask how they spend their time, which tasks feel most meaningful, and what challenges they face. Framing these conversations around “maximizing effectiveness” positions you as thoughtful rather than hesitant.
2. **Small‑scale experiments** – Take on a short‑term cross‑team project, lead a temporary initiative, or shadow a colleague for a few weeks. This hands‑on exposure provides first‑hand signals about the role’s realities and demonstrates a willingness to stretch. The recent session suggests designing 60–90‑day experiments that specifically test a preference for strategic, slow‑feedback work, tracking energy, outcomes, and feedback as validation data.
3. **Shadowing** – Spend a day or two observing the workflow of the role you’re considering. Direct observation clarifies the balance between strategic coordination and operational execution.
4. **Energy‑signal tracking** – While conducting experiments or interviews, note moments when you feel absorbed, proud without external validation, or drained. Linking these observations to [[concepts/energy_signals]] helps confirm whether the role aligns with your intrinsic motivation type ([[concepts/motivation_type]]).
5. **Reflection on feedback loops** – Pay attention to how quickly you receive meaningful feedback. Roles with **slow feedback** cycles (see [[concepts/slow_feedback]]) may be a better fit if you remain energized during ambiguous periods.

These methods align with the broader decision‑making framework described in [[concepts/career_decision_factors]] and help differentiate between [[concepts/strategic_vs_operational_roles]].

## Why Validation Matters
- **Reduces uncertainty** – Real data replaces speculation, making the eventual choice more evidence‑based.
- **Preserves relationships** – Asking thoughtful questions signals ambition and curiosity, not avoidance of growth.
- **Supports impact‑focused narratives** – Insights gathered can be used to articulate why a role does or does not match your strengths, as seen in the recommended communication style.
- **Highlights true motivation** – Tracking energy signals and comfort with slow‑feedback loops reveals whether you are driven by quick wins or long‑term strategic impact.

## Applying the Methods
1. Identify 2‑3 individuals in the target role.
2. Prepare concise, impact‑oriented questions.
3. Schedule a brief experiment or shadowing slot (1‑2 weeks max) that includes a strategic, ambiguous component.
4. During the experiment, log:
   - Energy levels (e.g., sustained focus, voluntary engagement)
   - Outcome relevance (e.g., influence on decisions, clarity created)
   - Feedback received (qualitative notes on clarity, thought partnership)
5. Reflect on the experience against your personal energy sources and skill‑growth goals, using the lenses of [[concepts/motivation_type]] and [[concepts/energy_signals]].

By systematically applying these validation methods—and explicitly measuring how you respond to slow‑feedback, strategic work—you can make a more confident, data‑driven career decision.
