# Summary of session-39_2025-03-14

**Main focus**: Helping the reader discover whether they are driven by quick, visible wins or by slower, strategic impact, and providing concrete short‑term experiments to validate that motivation.

## Key Concepts
- **Motivation type** – distinction between short‑term, tangible outcomes and long‑term, strategic impact [[concepts/motivation_type]].
- **Signature response** – personal pattern of energy and pride that reveals true drivers (plain text).
- **Slow‑feedback loops** – environments where results are delayed, common in strategy work [[concepts/slow_feedback]].
- **Strategic influence without authority** – shaping direction, aligning stakeholders, and driving decisions without formal ownership (plain text).
- **Energy signals** – observable cues (e.g., sustained focus, voluntary engagement, pride without external validation) that indicate fit [[concepts/energy_signals]].

## Findings & Insights
1. **Self‑assessment**: Reflect on moments of genuine energy; note the nature of the work, feedback speed, and internal satisfaction.
2. **Validation**: Observe reactions after the novelty fades. Consistent enthusiasm for ambiguous, long‑term problems signals a true strategic drive [[concepts/validation_methods]].
3. **Drain vs. Drive**: Execution‑heavy tasks that feel limiting suggest a need for broader influence.

## Practical Experiments (60‑90 days)
| # | Experiment | Success Indicator | Energy‑Tracking Method |
|---|------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Draft a problem statement for a cross‑team initiative and get stakeholders to adopt it. | Team uses your framing in discussions. | Note enjoyment of messy interviews and synthesis after excitement wanes. |
| 2 | Write a lightweight strategy memo on a topic of interest to your manager. | Memo sparks useful discussion or informs a decision. | Record whether writing feels absorbing or forced. |
| 3 | Facilitate a small alignment conversation among two‑three stakeholders with conflicting views. | Group leaves with clearer direction. | Assess whether influencing feels energizing or draining. |
| 4 | Own a multi‑week analysis of a trade‑off or hypothesis for the next roadmap. | Manager cites your analysis in planning. | Track sustained motivation through slow feedback periods. |
| 5 | Pilot a low‑risk, cross‑functional process improvement. | Others adopt or request continuation. | Observe satisfaction with behind‑the‑scenes persuasion work. |

## How to Measure Fit
- **Energy**: Journal daily feelings during ambiguous phases; look for sustained focus and voluntary revisiting of the problem.
- **Outcomes**: Count instances where your contributions shape conversation, decisions, or alignment.
- **Feedback**: Seek qualitative comments on clarity, thought partnership, or influence rather than execution.

## Suggested Next Steps
1. Choose 2–3 experiments that align with current workload.
2. Set up a simple tracking sheet (energy rating, outcome notes, feedback captured).
3. Review after each experiment; adjust focus toward activities that consistently energize.
