# Tone Variation

**Concept type:** Concept

**Definition**: Tone variation is the deliberate adjustment of language style—ranging from formal to professional‑casual to casual—to match the desired audience, purpose, and voice of a piece of communication.

## Overview
In the context of the *member spotlight* drafts created in [[summaries/session-11_2023-05-24]], tone variation was explored through three distinct registers:
1. **Formal** – elevated diction and structured phrasing.
2. **Professional‑casual** – friendly yet polished, suitable for a club newsletter.
3. **Casual** – relaxed and conversational.

The user ultimately selected the professional‑casual register, illustrating how tone influences the perceived intimacy and authority of the text.

## Key Elements
- **Audience awareness**: Selecting a tone that aligns with readers’ expectations (e.g., club members). 
- **Consistency**: Maintaining the chosen tone across all sentences (the final versions are all exactly three sentences long). 
- **Subtlety of references**: Adjusting how prominently a personal detail (the subject’s Buddhist practice) is mentioned to fit the tone; the professional‑casual versions keep the reference understated.
- **Placeholder integration**: Use of `[Name]`, `[Role]`, `[Contribution]` tokens to allow easy customization without breaking the tone. See [[concepts/placeholder-usage]] for more on that technique.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- The practice of tone variation directly shapes the effectiveness of a **[[concepts/member-spotlight]]** piece, determining whether it feels welcoming, authoritative, or relaxed.
- It works hand‑in‑hand with **[[concepts/placeholder-usage]]**, ensuring that the placeholders inherit the chosen voice.

## Example from Source Document
> *Professional‑casual*: "This month’s member spotlight features [Name], our [Role], whose recent work on [Contribution] has made a meaningful impact. Their background as a practicing Buddhist quietly informs the thoughtful, intentional way they participate in club activities. We’re glad to highlight the positive presence they bring to our community."

The above illustrates how tone variation guides word choice (e.g., "quietly informs" vs. more formal phrasing) while keeping the structure and placeholders intact.
