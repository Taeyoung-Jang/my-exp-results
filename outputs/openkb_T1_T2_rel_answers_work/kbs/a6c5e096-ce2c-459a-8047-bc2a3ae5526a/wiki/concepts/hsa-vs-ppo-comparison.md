# HSA vs PPO Comparison

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-39_2025-03-13]]

## Overview
This concept explains how to evaluate the trade‑offs between a high‑deductible health plan that is eligible for a **Health Savings Account** (HSA) and a traditional **Preferred Provider Organization** (PPO) plan. It builds on the [[concepts/benefit-selection-framework]] and provides a practical decision‑making tool that can be plugged into a [[concepts/cost-comparison-checklist]].

## Core Comparison Dimensions
| Dimension | HSA‑eligible High‑Deductible Plan | Traditional PPO |
|-----------|----------------------------------|-----------------|
| **Premiums** | Usually lower; saves monthly cash flow. | Higher monthly cost but more predictable. |
| **Deductible** | High (often $1,400‑$2,500 individual). | Lower (often $200‑$500). |
| **Out‑of‑Pocket Max** | Caps exposure, but may be higher than PPO. | Typically lower caps. |
| **Copays / Coinsurance** | Coinsurance after deductible (e.g., 20%). | Fixed copays for office visits, ER, specialists. |
| **Prescription Tiers** | Same tier structure, but you pay after deductible unless covered by coinsurance. | Copays often apply from first fill. |
| **Network** | Must stay in‑network to retain HSA eligibility; many PPO networks are compatible. | Broad network; out‑of‑network coverage possible but at higher cost. |
| **Tax Advantage** | Contributions are pre‑tax, grow tax‑free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax‑free. | No intrinsic tax benefit. |
| **Employer Contribution** | Often a direct cash contribution to the HSA (e.g., $500‑$1,000). | May offer a premium subsidy or match on contributions to a **Flexible Spending Account**. |

## Decision Logic for Generally Healthy Individuals
1. **Calculate Premium Savings** – Subtract the PPO premium from the HSA plan premium.
2. **Add Tax Savings** – Multiply expected HSA contributions (including employer match) by the employee’s marginal tax rate (federal + state). This amount is a direct cost reduction.
3. **Compare to Deductible Gap** – If the combined premium + tax savings cover most of the higher deductible, the HSA plan is financially advantageous.
4. **Assess Risk Tolerance** – Ensure the individual can afford the deductible and out‑of‑pocket max in a worst‑case year.
5. **Network Fit** – Verify that preferred doctors are in‑network; if not, the PPO may be necessary despite higher premiums.

### Quick Rule of Thumb
> When **(premium savings + tax savings + employer HSA contribution) ≥ 70 % of the deductible difference**, the HSA plan usually wins for a healthy employee.

## Practical Checklist Integration
The comparison can be embedded into a [[concepts/cost-comparison-checklist]] that includes:
- Premium per paycheck
- Deductible amount
- Out‑of‑pocket maximum
- Copay/coinsurance tables
- Prescription tier costs
- Network status of preferred providers
- HSA vs. **Flexible Spending Account** eligibility and rules
- Employer contributions (match or subsidy)

## Related Entities

- [[entities/flexible-spending-account]] – Often paired with PPOs; funds must be used within the plan year.
- [[entities/preferred-provider-organization]] – The network model typically used by PPOs.

## When to Revisit the Comparison
- **Open enrollment** each year or after a major life event (e.g., marriage, birth).
- Change in employer contribution levels.
- Significant shift in health usage patterns (e.g., chronic condition diagnosis).
