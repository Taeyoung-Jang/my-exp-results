# Benefit Selection Framework

**Definition**: A systematic method for assessing personal health usage, financial comfort, provider preferences, and retirement goals to choose the most suitable workplace benefit package.

## Core Elements
1. **Personal Health Utilization**
   - Frequency of medical visits, regular medications, and expected dental/vision needs.
2. **Financial Comfort Level**
   - Ability to absorb deductible and out‑of‑pocket costs, and interest in tax‑advantaged accounts such as an HSA or FSA.
3. **Provider Network Alignment**
   - Whether preferred doctors, dentists, or specialists are in‑network.
4. **Retirement Considerations**
   - Employer match, contribution limits, and long‑term savings goals.
5. **Tax‑Advantaged Account Eligibility**
   - HSA eligibility (high‑deductible plans) vs. FSA‑only options.

These elements form a decision matrix that guides users to focus on the benefits that matter most to them before diving into plan specifics.

## How It Was Applied
In [[summaries/session-39_2025-03-13]], the framework was used to:
- Identify key questions about health care usage and financial risk tolerance.
- Prioritize dental and vision choices after securing the retirement match.
- Guide a focused comparison of medical plans, emphasizing premiums, deductibles, out‑of‑pocket maximums, copays/coinsurance, prescription tiers, network coverage, and HSA/FSA rules.

## Practical Use
1. **Answer the framework questions** – Gather personal data on health usage, finances, and provider preferences.
2. **Map answers to benefit categories** – Determine which benefits (medical, dental, vision, retirement) need deeper analysis.
3. **Apply the cost‑comparison checklist** – Populate a side‑by‑side table of plan metrics.
4. **Run scenario analysis** – Use the HSA vs. PPO comparison to factor tax savings and worst‑case costs.
5. **Make an informed choice** – Select the plan that aligns with both day‑to‑day comfort and worst‑case protection.
