# Cost Comparison Checklist

**Type:** Concept

## Definition
A systematic list of quantitative and qualitative attributes used to evaluate and contrast alternative benefit plans—especially medical, dental, vision, and retirement options—so individuals can make informed enrollment decisions.

## Core Elements (derived from [[summaries/session-39_2025-03-13]])
| Category | What to Capture | Why it Matters |
|----------|----------------|----------------|
| **Premium** | Cost per paycheck (or per month) | Baseline expense regardless of utilization |
| **Deductible** | Amount you pay before the plan shares costs | Determines upfront cash needed for care |
| **Out‑of‑Pocket (OOP) Max** | Maximum total you would owe in a year | Caps worst‑case financial exposure |
| **Copays / Coinsurance** | Fixed fees or percentage shares for office visits, ER, urgent care, specialists | Affects cost of routine and occasional care |
| **Prescription Tier Costs** | Price levels for generic, brand‑name, specialty meds | Medication expenses can dominate yearly spend |
| **Network Coverage** | Whether preferred doctors, hospitals, or specialists are in‑network | In‑network care dramatically lowers actual costs |
| **HSA / FSA Eligibility** | Whether the plan supports a Health Savings Account or Flexible Spending Account, and any employer contributions | Enables tax‑advantaged savings; influences overall value |
| **Employer Contributions / Match** | Retirement match amount, HSA contributions, or other subsidies | Directly reduces out‑of‑pocket cost |
| **Utilization Assumptions** | Expected frequency of doctor visits, prescriptions, dental/vision services | Aligns the checklist with personal health patterns |
| **Risk Tolerance** | Comfort with high deductible & lower premium vs. higher premium & predictable costs | Guides choice between HSA‑eligible high‑deductible plans and traditional PPOs |

## How to Use the Checklist
1. **Gather Data** – Pull the numeric values for each category from the plan documents.
2. **Populate a Table** – Create a side‑by‑side matrix comparing each plan.
3. **Apply Personal Filters** – Use your utilization assumptions and risk tolerance to weight each row.
4. **Calculate Net Cost** – Add premium cost, expected out‑of‑pocket spending (based on usage), and subtract tax savings from HSA contributions (e.g., `contribution × marginal tax rate`).
5. **Decision Rule** – If the net cost of the HSA‑eligible plan is lower *and* you can afford the deductible in a worst‑case scenario, prefer it; otherwise choose the lower‑deductible PPO.
