# Preferred Provider Organization (PPO)

**Type:** Product (health insurance)

## Overview
A Preferred Provider Organization (PPO) is a health insurance plan that contracts with a network of doctors, hospitals, and specialists. Members can receive services at lower out‑of‑pocket costs when they use in‑network providers, but they retain the flexibility to see out‑of‑network providers at a higher cost.

## Key Characteristics Mentioned in the Session
- **Lower deductible and more predictable monthly expenses** compared to high‑deductible HSA‑eligible plans.
- **Copay/coinsurance structure** that often provides fixed payments for office visits, urgent care, and specialist appointments.
- **Network focus** – checking that preferred doctors are in‑network is a primary step in the benefit‑selection process.
- **Out‑of‑pocket maximum** that caps the worst‑case financial exposure for the year.

## Role in Benefit Selection
When evaluating workplace benefits, a PPO is considered alongside HSA‑eligible high‑deductible plans. The decision framework involves:
1. Assessing personal health usage and financial comfort (see [[concepts/benefit-selection-framework]]).
2. Comparing premiums, deductibles, and OOP maxes using a side‑by‑side list (see [[concepts/cost-comparison-checklist]]).
3. Weighing the tax advantage of a Health Savings Account versus the predictability of a PPO (see [[concepts/hsa-vs-ppo-comparison]]).
