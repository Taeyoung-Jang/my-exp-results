# Flexible Spending Account (FSA)

A **Flexible Spending Account (FSA)** is a pre‑tax benefit that lets employees set aside a portion of their salary to pay for eligible medical, dental, and vision expenses.  Funds are deducted from each paycheck before taxes, reducing taxable income, and must be used within the plan year (or a limited grace period).

## Key facts from **[[summaries/session-39_2025-03-13]]**
- The assistant contrasts FSAs with **[[entities/health-savings-account]]** (HSAs), noting that FSAs are “use‑it‑or‑lose‑it” accounts while HSAs can roll over year‑to‑year.
- FSAs are useful when you have **predictable, recurring expenses** (e.g., regular dental cleanings, vision exams, or prescription costs) and want to capture tax savings on those outlays.
- When evaluating benefit options, the conversation recommends adding the FSA’s tax advantage to the **[[concepts/cost-comparison-checklist]]** to compare total yearly cost of medical plans.
- The discussion also places FSAs within the broader **[[concepts/benefit-selection-framework]]**, helping users decide whether an FSA‑eligible plan aligns with their health usage patterns and financial comfort.

## Comparison with Health Savings Account
- **Eligibility:** FSAs are available with most employer plans; HSAs require a high‑deductible health plan.
- **Rollover:** FSAs generally do not roll over (except limited carry‑over options); HSAs allow unlimited rollover.
- **Tax Benefits:** Both provide pre‑tax contributions, but HSAs also offer tax‑free growth and withdrawals for qualified expenses.
- **Strategic Use:** The conversation suggests using an FSA when you expect regular, known expenses, whereas an HSA is better for saving long‑term and covering high‑deductible plan costs.  See **[[concepts/hsa-vs-ppo-comparison]]** for a deeper dive.

## Practical Tips
- Estimate your annual qualified expenses and set the FSA contribution accordingly to avoid forfeiture.
- Combine the FSA contribution with the **[[concepts/cost-comparison-checklist]]** to calculate net cost after tax savings.
- Review your employer’s FSA rules (grace period, rollover limits) during the open enrollment period.
