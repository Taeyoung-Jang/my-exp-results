# Summary of session‑36 (2024‑06‑15)

**Overview** – The document provides a practical, four‑week progressive hiking program for newcomers to high‑altitude environments, emphasizing gradual distance/elevation gains, pacing, hydration, and clear criteria for when to turn back.

## 4‑Week Progressive Plan
- **Week 1:** 3–3.5 mi, 700–900 ft gain, effort ≈ 4/10 RPE; focus on steady breathing and conversation.  
- **Week 2:** 4 mi, 1,000–1,200 ft gain, effort ≈ 5/10.  
- **Week 3:** 5 mi, 1,300–1,600 ft gain, effort ≈ 6/10.  
- **Week 4:** 6 mi, 1,600–1,900 ft gain, effort ≈ 6–7/10.  

These targets can be extended to the user’s ultimate goal of 6–7 mi and 1,800–2,200 ft.

## Personalized Adjustments
- Baseline: 3 mi, ~700 ft gain; mild headache after pushing.  
- Adjusted weekly increments keep distance and gain increases modest (≤ ~20 %).  
- Emphasis on *slow start* (first 15 min) and regular short rests.

## Altitude Discomfort vs. Emergency Signals
| Normal | Alarm |
|---|---|
| Mild shortness of breath, light headache that eases with slower pace, water, rest. | Rapidly worsening headache, dizziness, nausea, chest tightness, confusion – stop immediately. |

## On‑Trail Symptom Checklist (Decision Tree)
1. **Stop** – rest 5 min, sit, breathe through nose, exhale via pursed lips.  
2. Sip small water amounts, eat a quick snack.  
3. **Reassess** after 5 min:  
   - Headache easing → continue at slower pace.  
   - No change → rest another 5 min.  
4. **Reassess** after 10 min total:  
   - Improvement → proceed cautiously, monitor.  
   - No improvement or new symptoms (dizziness, nausea, chest tightness) → **turn around now**.  

## Technique Recommendations
- **Trekking Pole Technique** – pole opposite foot, angled slightly behind, acts as a metronome for rhythm.  
- **Breathing Technique** – inhale for 2 steps, exhale for 2‑3 steps; keep breaths even, avoid shallow gasps.  
- **Uphill Cadence** – shorter, quicker steps keep heart rate lower and reduce wind‑edness.

## Exertion Targets & Heart‑Rate Guidance
- Desired effort levels: 4/10 (Week 1) → 6–7/10 (Week 4).  
- Approximate HR zone: **60–75 % of max HR** on climbs; altitude may push this higher, so use HR as trend, not absolute.  
- Prefer **RPE** and **talk test** over strict HR when acclimating.
