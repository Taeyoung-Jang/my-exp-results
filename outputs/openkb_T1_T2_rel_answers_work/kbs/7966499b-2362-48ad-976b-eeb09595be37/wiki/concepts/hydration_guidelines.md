# Hydration Guidelines

**Overview**
Hydration guidelines provide practical advice on how much fluid and electrolytes a runner should consume during training, especially under warm and humid conditions at sea level. These recommendations help balance performance, safety, and recovery.

---

## Core Recommendations

1. **Fluid Intake**
   - Aim for **300–500 ml per hour** in warm, humid weather (approximately one 500 ml bottle per hour).
   - Adjust based on personal thirst cues; sip every 10–15 minutes rather than large gulps.
   - Monitor for **under‑hydration** signs (rising heart rate at constant effort, fatigue, dry mouth) and **over‑hydration** signs (stomach sloshing, nausea, bloating).

2. **Electrolyte (Sodium) Intake**
   - Add **300–500 mg sodium per hour** if the run exceeds **90 minutes**, you are a salty sweater, or you notice salt residues on clothing.
   - For runs under 90 minutes, supplemental sodium is often unnecessary unless you have a high sweat‑salt rate.

3. **Weight‑Based Fine‑Tuning**
   - Weigh yourself before and after a run for a few sessions.
   - Aim to lose **≤ 1–2 % of body weight**; adjust fluid intake upward if loss exceeds this range, or reduce if you gain weight.

---

## Practical Application

- **Bottle Strategy**: Use a standard 500 ml handheld bottle; finish it within the hour for easy tracking.
- **Sip Frequency**: Roughly 3–5 sips every 10 minutes keeps fluid intake steady.
- **Electrolyte Mixing**: For hot, humid interval workouts, a single pre‑mixed electrolyte bottle is simplest. If mixes upset your stomach, carry plain water and an electrolyte capsule to take midway.
