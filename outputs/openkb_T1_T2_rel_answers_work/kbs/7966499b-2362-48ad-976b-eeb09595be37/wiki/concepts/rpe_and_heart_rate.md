# RPE and Heart‑Rate Guidance

**What it is** – A practical framework for monitoring exertion while hiking at altitude, combining the subjective **Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE)** scale with objective **heart‑rate (HR) zones**. The goal is to keep effort in a safe, sustainable range that supports gradual conditioning without triggering altitude‑related symptoms.

## Core Principles

1. **RPE as the primary cue**
   - Use the 1‑10 RPE scale (10 = maximal effort). For the progressive plan in [[summaries/session-36_2024-06-15]] the target ranges are:
     - Week 1: ~4/10 (easy, conversational)
     - Week 2: ~5/10
     - Week 3: ~6/10
     - Week 4: 6‑7/10 (challenging but controlled)
   - The RPE should feel like a steady rhythm; if you need to gasp for air, you are above the intended zone.

2. **Heart‑rate zone as a secondary guide**
   - Aim for **60‑75 % of maximum HR** on uphill sections. At altitude HR tends to run higher, so treat this as a trend rather than a strict limit.
   - If HR spikes into the low 80 % range but RPE remains low and breathing is even, the elevation effect is likely responsible and the effort can still be acceptable.

3. **Talk test**
   - Complement RPE/HR with the talk test: you should be able to speak in short sentences without pausing for breath. Failure of the talk test signals a need to reduce pace, regardless of HR numbers.

## Applying the Guidance in the 4‑Week Plan

- **Week 1 (3‑3.5 mi, 700‑900 ft gain)** – Keep HR around 60 % of max and RPE at 4. Use the talk test to confirm you’re below the headache threshold.
- **Week 2 (4 mi, 1,000‑1,200 ft gain)** – HR may rise to ~65 %, RPE 5. Adjust cadence if breathing becomes shallow.
- **Week 3 (5 mi, 1,300‑1,600 ft gain)** – HR 70‑75 %, RPE 6. Short, frequent rests keep the effort steady.
- **Week 4 (6 mi, 1,600‑1,900 ft gain)** – HR can approach the upper end of the range, RPE 6‑7. Monitor closely; if symptoms appear, back off.

## Why RPE Trumps HR at Altitude

Altitude reduces oxygen availability, causing HR to increase for a given workload. RPE directly reflects how the body feels, incorporating oxygen stress, hydration, and fatigue. Therefore, **RPE and the talk test are more reliable** for day‑to‑day pacing, while HR helps spot trends over multiple hikes.
