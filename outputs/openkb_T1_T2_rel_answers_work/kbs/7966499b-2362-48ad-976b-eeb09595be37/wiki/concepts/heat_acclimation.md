# Heat Acclimation

**Definition**: Heat acclimation is the physiological adaptation that occurs after repeated exposure to hot environments, improving the body’s ability to regulate temperature, maintain performance, and reduce strain.

---

## Core Mechanisms
- **Cardiovascular adjustments** – increased plasma volume and improved cardiac output.
- **Thermoregulatory changes** – earlier onset of sweating, higher sweat rate, and more dilute sweat.
- **Metabolic efficiency** – reduced core temperature rise during sub‑maximal effort.
- **Perceptual adaptations** – lower perceived exertion at a given workload in the heat.

---

## Practical Guidance (from [[summaries/session-11_2022-06-15]])
- **Sea‑level context** – Heat acclimation is independent of altitude; sea‑level runners can follow the same principles without the added hypoxic stress present at elevation.
- **Gradual exposure** – Begin with short sessions (15‑20 min) at modest intensity and increase duration by ~10 % each day.
- **Typical timeline** – Noticeable improvements appear after 7‑10 days of consistent exposure; full adaptation may take 2‑3 weeks.
- **Intensity recommendation** – Keep the effort modest (easy, conversational pace) during early sessions; for interval work, aim toward the higher end of fluid intake guidelines to offset increased heat production.
- **Monitoring** – Track heart rate, perceived exertion, and hydration status; avoid excessive strain that could lead to heat illness.
