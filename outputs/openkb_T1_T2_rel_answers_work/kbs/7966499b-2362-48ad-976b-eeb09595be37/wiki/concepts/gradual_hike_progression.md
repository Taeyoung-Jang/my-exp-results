# Gradual Hiking Progression

**Definition**: A stepwise increase in hike distance and elevation gain over successive weeks, designed to improve aerobic capacity and altitude tolerance while minimizing risk of overexertion.

## Core Principles
- **Small Incremental Gains**: Typically 10‑20 % increase in mileage or elevation per week to allow physiological adaptation.
- **Controlled Effort**: Target a perceived exertion of 4‑7 /10 (see [[concepts/rpe_and_heart_rate]]) and stay within a moderate heart‑rate zone (≈60‑75 % of max HR).
- **Recovery Emphasis**: Include rest days or short “recovery” walks between longer outings.
- **Altitude Acclimatization**: Progression must respect the reduced oxygen environment; slower starts and frequent hydration help (see [[concepts/altitude_acclimatization]] and [[concepts/hydration_guidelines]]).
- **Feedback Loop**: Use on‑trail symptom checks (see [[concepts/symptom_checklist]]) to decide whether to continue, slow down, or turn back.

## Example 4‑Week Plan (from [[summaries/session-36_2024-06-15]])
| Week | Distance | Elevation Gain | Effort (RPE) | Key Focus |
|------|----------|----------------|--------------|-----------|
| 1 | 3–3.5 mi | 700–900 ft | 4/10 | Establish steady breathing; use [[concepts/breathing_technique]]; short rests |
| 2 | 4 mi | 1,000–1,200 ft | 5/10 | Introduce modest cadence changes; optional pole use [[concepts/trekking_pole_technique]] |
| 3 | 5 mi | 1,300–1,600 ft | 6/10 | Add mid‑week short walk; monitor hydration and nutrition |
| 4 | 6 mi | 1,600–1,900 ft | 6–7/10 | Test “full‑day” stamina; ensure symptom checklist passes before extending further |

### Extending to Goal
After week 4, the hiker can aim for 6–7 mi and 1,800–2,200 ft, following the same incremental logic.

## Supporting Techniques
- **Breathing**: Inhale for two steps, exhale for two‑three; keeps oxygen intake steady ([[concepts/breathing_technique]]).
- **Trekking Poles**: Opposite‑foot planting, slight backward angle; reduces leg load and helps maintain cadence ([[concepts/trekking_pole_technique]]).
- **If‑Then Planning**: Pre‑define actions for symptom thresholds (e.g., *if headache persists >10 min → turn back*) (see [[concepts/if_then_planning]]).

## Monitoring & Adjustment
1. **Heart‑Rate Check**: Use watch data as trend, not absolute, because altitude elevates HR.
2. **Talk Test**: Ability to speak in short sentences indicates appropriate effort level.
3. **Symptom Checklist**: Follow the decision tree in [[concepts/symptom_checklist]] to decide continuation vs. descent.

## Benefits
- Improves aerobic capacity and muscular endurance gradually.
- Reduces risk of altitude‑related illness.
- Builds confidence through measurable, attainable milestones.
