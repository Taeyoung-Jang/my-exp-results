# Altitude Symptom Checklist

**Purpose** – Provide hikers with a concise, on‑trail decision tree to differentiate normal altitude discomfort from warning signs that require an immediate turn‑around. The checklist is designed for gradual altitude exposure and can be integrated into a broader [[concepts/gradual_hike_progression]] plan.

## Decision Tree (time‑based)
1. **Stop & Rest (0‑5 min)**
   - Drop your pack if possible, sit or stand still.
   - Breathe slowly: inhale through the nose, exhale through pursed lips.
   - Sip a few sips of water and eat a quick snack (e.g., a granola bar or nuts).
2. **First Re‑assessment (after 5 min)**
   - **Improving headache / mild breathlessness** → Continue at a slower pace.
   - **No change** → Rest another 5 min, repeat breathing and hydration.
3. **Second Re‑assessment (after 10 min total)**
   - **Improvement** (even slight) → Proceed cautiously, keep monitoring.
   - **No improvement or new symptoms** (dizziness, nausea, chest tightness, confusion) → **Turn around immediately**.

## Symptom Categories
| Category | Typical Signs | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal altitude discomfort** | Light headache that eases with slower pace, mild shortness of breath, leg heaviness that fades with rest. | Continue slowly; keep using the checklist if symptoms recur. |
| **Warning signs** | Persistent or worsening headache, dizziness, nausea, chest tightness, confusion, inability to speak a full sentence. | Stop, rest, hydrate, and **descend** as soon as possible. |

## Integration with Other Practices
- Pair the checklist with regular **[[concepts/breathing_technique]]** (e.g., inhale 2 steps, exhale 2‑3 steps) to keep respiration steady.
- Maintain adequate fluid and energy intake using [[concepts/hydration_guidelines]] and snack recommendations.
- Use the **[[concepts/rpe_and_heart_rate]]** talk test (ability to speak in short sentences) as a real‑time indicator alongside the checklist.
- Incorporate the checklist into an overall **[[concepts/altitude_acclimatization]]** strategy, advancing weekly as outlined in the [[summaries/session-36_2024-06-15]].

## Practical Tips
- Keep a small printed copy of the checklist in your pocket or on your watch.
- If using a smartwatch, set a silent 5‑minute timer when you first notice symptoms.
- Trekking poles (see [[concepts/trekking_pole_technique]]) can help reduce leg strain, potentially lowering the likelihood of symptom escalation.

**Result** – By following this structured, time‑gated approach, hikers can safely differentiate between benign altitude effects and serious warning signs, ensuring timely descent and reducing the risk of altitude‑related illness.
