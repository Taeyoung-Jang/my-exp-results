# Summary of Session 37 (2024-12-13)

**Main Idea**: A practical budgeting framework that isolates essential recurring bills from optional subscriptions using separate financial buckets, especially when a prepaid card with a hard monthly cap is required.

## Key Concepts
- budgeting – structuring finances to avoid surprise charges.
- recurring charges – automatic drafts that can cause “gotcha” fees.
- dedicated bill account – a checking account used **only** for essential auto‑draft providers (utilities, phone, insurance, etc.).
- virtual cards – disposable or limited‑use cards for trials and non‑essential subscriptions.
- prepaid card cap – a prepaid debit card with a fixed monthly spending limit that blocks any recurring charge.
- monthly review routine – a brief, repeatable checklist to verify that each bucket stayed within its intended purpose.
- essential vs optional spending – the distinction that drives the three‑bucket system.

## Concrete Three‑Bucket Setup
1. **Essentials Account (Account A)**
   - A regular checking account given to all providers that require auto‑draft and do not accept prepaid cards.
   - Funded each month with the exact sum of known essential bills plus a small buffer.
2. **Daily Spending Card (Card B)**
   - Your primary debit/credit card for groceries, gas, and other routine purchases.
   - Not linked to any recurring subscription, so unexpected charges stand out immediately.
3. **Virtual/Trial Card (Card C)**
   - Generated virtual card for any service you are testing or that you suspect may auto‑renew.
   - Can be locked or deleted after the trial period.
4. **Prepaid Cap Card (Card D)**
   - The counselor‑issued prepaid card with a hard monthly cap.
   - Handles all discretionary spending; its cap guarantees no overspend.

## Monthly Routine (≈2‑3 minutes)
1. **Check Essentials Account** – Verify that all expected drafts landed and note any amount changes.
2. **Review Daily Spending Card** – Scan the statement for unfamiliar merchants; flag any accidental subscription attempts.
3. **Audit Virtual Cards** – Close or freeze any that are no longer needed.
4. **Confirm Prepaid Cap** – Ensure the cap aligns with your discretionary budget and that the balance is zeroed out.
5. **Adjust Next Month’s Transfer** – If any essential bill changed, modify the amount transferred to Account A for the following cycle.

## Handling Bills That Reject Prepaid/Virtual Cards
- Route those providers to **Account A** (the dedicated bills account).  Because this account is isolated from all other spending, it satisfies the provider’s requirement while preserving the overall budget guardrails.

## Benefits
- **Zero surprise renewals** on the prepaid card due to its hard cap.
- **Clear separation** of essential vs. optional spending, making monitoring trivial.
- **Scalable**: add more virtual cards for new trials without affecting the core budget.
- **Low maintenance**: a single monthly glance keeps the system on track.
