# Summary of Session 36 (2025‑06‑29)

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/fertility_awareness_methods]]**: cycle tracking, cervical mucus observation, and [[concepts/basal_body_temperature_shift]] monitoring.  
- **Reliability**: Cycle‑tracking alone is least reliable, especially with irregular cycles. Cervical mucus and basal body temperature provide more precise cues but have their own limitations.  
- **Basal body temperature shift**: A sustained temperature rise indicates ovulation has already occurred (typically ~1 day after ovulation) and is confirmed when the rise persists for about three days.  
- **Fertile window**: The days *before* ovulation plus the day of ovulation; a post‑shift basal body temperature means the window is likely closed.  
- **[[entities/opk]]** and mucus tracking give *advance* notice, while basal body temperature offers *confirmation* after the event.

## Findings & Practical Advice
- Combining all three signs (cycle, mucus, basal body temperature) improves accuracy even with irregular cycles.  
- A “steady climb” in basal body temperature usually signals that ovulation has already happened; however, external factors (sleep, illness, environment) can mimic a rise.  
- If basal body temperature is already elevated, trying to conceive that cycle has lower chances but is not impossible.  
- After a clear temperature rise, OPKs and mucus tracking provide little additional information for that cycle.

## Recommended Plan for Irregular Cycles
1. **Start OPK testing early** – around day 8‑10 after period onset (or day 8 for highly variable cycles).  
2. **Test frequency** – once daily is sufficient; switch to twice‑daily once the test line begins darkening.  
3. **Cervical mucus** – observe daily during bathroom visits; look for clear, stretchy, egg‑white mucus as a sign of approaching ovulation.  
4. **Basal body temperature** – take a single temperature each morning before moving; use it to confirm ovulation after the LH surge or mucus peak.  
5. **Integration** – mucus and OPK give advance warning; basal body temperature validates that ovulation occurred, reducing missed [[concepts/fertile_window_timing]].

## Simple Daily Routine (Low Burden)
- **Morning**: Record basal body temperature.  
- **Any bathroom visit**: Quick glance at cervical mucus.  
- **Afternoon/evening**: Perform OPK (if within testing window).  

This lightweight protocol balances thorough monitoring with minimal disruption, helping users with irregular cycles capture the fertile window without overwhelming their mornings.
