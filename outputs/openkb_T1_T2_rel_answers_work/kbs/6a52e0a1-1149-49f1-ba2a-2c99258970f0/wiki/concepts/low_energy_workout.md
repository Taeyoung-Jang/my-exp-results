# Low‑Energy Workout

**Definition**: A set of gentle, low‑intensity movement options intended for days when hormonal fluctuations reduce overall energy, allowing the body to stay active without excessive strain.

## Core Principles
- **Treat energy as a rhythm** – align activity with the natural ebb and flow of the menstrual cycle [[concepts/energy_rhythm]].
- **Maintain rather than push** – focus on keeping the body warm, mobile, and lightly engaged.
- **Flexibility** – provide clear options to scale down (rest, heating pad) or scale up if the day feels better than expected.

## Typical Movement Options
| Duration | Example Activities |
|----------|-------------------|
| **10 min** | Slow walk, gentle stretching, easy mobility flow |
| **20 min** | Relaxed yoga session, slightly longer walk, light bodyweight work at a comfortable pace |
| **30 min** | Longer walk or gentle cycling, mellow yoga, very light strength work with ample rest |

### Upgrade Paths
- If energy improves, extend the chosen duration (e.g., 10 → 20 min) or add a second short movement block.
- Keep upgrades modest; avoid high‑intensity or heavy‑load work.

## Integration with Productivity
Low‑energy workouts pair with short, low‑focus work blocks (20–45 min) to create a balanced day. The workout length often dictates the number and length of work blocks, supporting the concept of **productivity blocks** that match energy levels [[concepts/productivity_blocks]].

## Printable Menu
A concise menu can be created listing the three movement durations and the associated work‑block options, along with a simple rule:
- **Low energy** → 10 min movement + 1–2 × 20 min work blocks.
- **Average** → 20 min movement + 2–3 × 30–45 min work blocks.
- **Better than expected** → 30 min movement + add a longer work block (45–60 min).

This menu enables quick daily decision‑making without over‑thinking, reinforcing the maintenance‑mode approach.
