## Fertile Window Timing

The **fertile window** comprises the days when intercourse can result in conception. It includes the **two to three days before ovulation** plus the **day of ovulation itself**. After ovulation, the chance of fertilization drops sharply because the egg is viable for only about 12‑24 hours.

### How it is identified
- **Cervical mucus**: Clear, stretchy, egg‑white mucus signals that the fertile window is approaching.
- **Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs)**: Detect the luteinizing hormone (LH) surge, which occurs roughly 24‑36 hours before ovulation, giving an advance cue.
- **Basal body temperature (BBT) shift**: A sustained rise in temperature, described in [[concepts/basal_body_temperature_shift]], confirms that ovulation has already occurred, typically about one day after the event.

### Practical guidance from [[summaries/session-36_2025-06-29]]
- For **irregular cycles**, start OPK testing early (around day 8‑10 after period onset) and monitor cervical mucus daily to catch the window.
- Combine **advance indicators** (cervical mucus, OPKs) with **post‑ovulation confirmation** (BBT) to avoid missing fertile days.
- A post‑shift BBT rise indicates the fertile window is likely closed; however, trying to conceive can still be attempted, albeit with reduced odds.

### Integrated approach
Using a blend of [[concepts/fertility_awareness_methods]]—cervical mucus observation, OPK testing (see [[entities/opk]]), and BBT tracking—provides both **early warning** and **confirmation**, making fertile window timing more reliable even when cycles are not perfectly regular.
