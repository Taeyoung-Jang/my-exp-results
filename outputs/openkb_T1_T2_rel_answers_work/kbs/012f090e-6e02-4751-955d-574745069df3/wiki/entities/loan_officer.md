# Loan Officer

**Role**: A loan officer is the primary point of contact between the borrower and the [[entities/lender]] during the mortgage [[concepts/closing_process]]. They manage underwriting, verify source of funds, and ensure the loan meets all requirements for a clear‑to‑close.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-34_2025-02-13]]
- **Fund Verification**: The loan officer must be informed before any large transfers (e.g., moving proceeds from a [[entities/brokerage]] to checking) so they can confirm the source and avoid triggering [[concepts/underwriting_triggers]].
- **Documentation**: Borrowers are asked to submit brokerage statements, trade confirmations, transfer receipts, and bank‑statement screenshots directly to the loan officer.
- **Communication Priority**: The loan officer should be contacted **first** when planning fund movements; once they approve, the borrower can coordinate with the [[entities/title_company]] about payment delivery methods (wire or cashier’s check).
- **Timing Guidance**: The loan officer advises whether to wait for trade settlement (T+1/T+2) before transferring funds, ensuring the money appears as cleared cash in the borrower’s account.

## Relationships
- Works with the [[entities/lender]] to assess credit and loan eligibility.
- Coordinates with the [[entities/title_company]] on closing payment logistics after fund verification is complete.
- Interacts with the borrower to collect required documents and address any last‑minute issues that could affect the [[concepts/closing_process]].

## Relevance to Concepts
- **[[concepts/liquid_funds]]**: The loan officer validates that the borrower’s liquid assets meet the lender’s thresholds.
- **[[concepts/underwriting_triggers]]**: They monitor for red‑flag deposits or gifts that could reopen underwriting.
