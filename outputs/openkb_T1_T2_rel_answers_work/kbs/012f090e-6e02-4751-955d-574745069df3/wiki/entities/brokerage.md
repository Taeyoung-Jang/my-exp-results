## Brokerage

A brokerage is a financial services organization that facilitates the buying and selling of securities such as stocks, bonds, and mutual funds on behalf of its clients. In the context of [[summaries/session-34_2025-02-13]], the buyer plans to liquidate assets through a brokerage to produce the required **liquid funds** for a residential real‑estate closing.

### Key Points from Session 34
- **Purpose**: The buyer intends to move money from the brokerage account to a checking account to meet the lender’s “funds in account” deadline.
- **Documentation**: The lender requires trade confirmations and settlement statements from the brokerage to verify the source of the cash.
- **Timing**: Best practice is to wait until the trade settles (T+1/T+2) before transferring the cash, ensuring the funds appear as cleared in the buyer’s account.
- **Risk Management**: A clear paper trail from the brokerage helps avoid triggering [[concepts/underwriting_triggers]] and keeps the closing process on track.

### Related Entities and Concepts
- **Related Entity**: [[entities/lender]] – the party requiring verification of the liquid funds.
- **Related Entity**: [[entities/loan_officer]] – the point of contact for submitting brokerage documentation.
- **Related Entity**: [[entities/title_company]] – coordinates the final payment method but does not need brokerage details.
- **Concepts**: [[concepts/liquid_funds]], [[concepts/underwriting_triggers]], [[concepts/closing_process]]

### Practical Guidance for Buyers
1. Request a detailed statement and trade confirmations from the brokerage once a sale is executed.
2. Confirm settlement dates and ensure the cash is fully cleared before initiating a transfer.
3. Provide the lender (through the loan officer) with all brokerage documentation promptly to prevent delays.
4. Keep communication clear and documented to maintain a smooth [[concepts/closing_process]].
