## Lender (Organization)

A **lender** is the financial institution that originates the mortgage loan for a home purchase. In the context of the closing process, the lender is responsible for assessing the borrower’s creditworthiness, verifying that sufficient **[[concepts/liquid_funds]]** are available, and issuing the *clear‑to‑close* once all conditions are satisfied.

### Role in the Closing Process
- Reviews the borrower’s financial picture, including recent deposits, job changes, and credit activity.
- Flags any **[[concepts/underwriting_triggers]]** such as large last‑minute deposits, gifts, or unexplained transfers.
- Requires documentation like brokerage statements, trade confirmations, and bank statements to verify source of funds.
- Communicates directly with the **[[entities/loan_officer]]**, who acts as the primary point of contact for the borrower.
- Coordinates with the **[[entities/title_company]]** primarily on the timing and method of delivering the final payment (wire or cashier’s check).

### Interaction with Borrower (as seen in [[summaries/session-34_2025-02-13]])
- Advises the borrower to keep financial activity stable and to avoid opening new credit lines or making large, undocumented deposits.
- Requests clear documentation for any transfer of funds, especially from a brokerage account, to ensure the money is fully settled before the Friday “funds in account” deadline.
- Provides guidance on whether a transfer should wait for settlement (T+1/T+2) or can be initiated earlier, depending on the lender’s internal policies.
- Confirms that the presence of the funds in the borrower’s checking account, along with supporting paperwork, is sufficient; it does **not** replace the need for a final payment method verification with the title company.

### Documentation Requirements
- Recent brokerage statements and specific trade confirmations for any asset sales.
- Transfer confirmations showing the movement of cash into a checking or savings account.
- Updated bank statements or balance screenshots reflecting the deposited funds.
- Any explanations for large deposits, especially if they originate from sources other than the borrower’s own accounts.
