# Closing Process

## Overview
The closing process is the final phase of a residential real‑estate transaction where ownership is transferred, funds are disbursed, and all contractual obligations are satisfied. It involves tight coordination among the buyer's [[entities/lender]], the [[entities/loan_officer]], the [[entities/title_company]], and other parties.

## Common Last‑Minute Deal‑Breakers
- Sudden changes in the buyer’s financial picture (new credit lines, job change, large deposits).
- Title report issues such as unpaid liens or missing signatures.
- Lender delays or missing documentation.
- Property problems uncovered during the final walk‑through.

These risks align with the broader [[concepts/underwriting_triggers]] that can delay or derail a closing.

## Liquid Funds Requirements
Lenders require **liquid funds**—money readily available in checking, savings, or money‑market accounts. Assets that need to be sold or are otherwise illiquid generally do not qualify. Large, late‑stage deposits, especially from new sources or gifts, trigger additional verification.

Relevant concept: [[concepts/liquid_funds]].

## Fund Transfer Verification
Moving proceeds from a brokerage account to checking is acceptable if the borrower provides:
1. Trade confirmation showing the sale.
2. Settlement statement confirming the funds are cleared (preferably after T+1/T+2).
3. Transfer receipt and a bank‑statement screenshot of the post‑transfer balance.

A clear paper trail usually prevents a full re‑underwrite, though the lender may request a quick verification.

## Coordination Hierarchy
1. **First:** Notify the loan officer of any planned fund movements. The loan officer (underwriting) is the gatekeeper for any changes that could affect approval.
2. **Second:** After lender approval, inform the title company about the payment method (wire or cashier’s check) and timing.

This workflow reflects best practices for [[concepts/closing_process]] coordination.

## Practical Checklist (48‑Hour Window)
- Gather brokerage statements, trade confirmations, and pending transfer receipts.
- Obtain recent bank statements and a balance screenshot after the transfer posts.
- Email the loan officer with a note of intent and all supporting documents.
- Ask the title company:
  - Preferred payment method (wire vs. cashier’s check).
  - Deadline for funds to arrive.
  - Final amount required for closing.

## Impact on Underwriting
Large inbound transfers from the borrower’s own accounts typically trigger only a brief verification, not a full re‑underwrite. Gifts or unexplained deposits near closing are the most likely to restart underwriting.
