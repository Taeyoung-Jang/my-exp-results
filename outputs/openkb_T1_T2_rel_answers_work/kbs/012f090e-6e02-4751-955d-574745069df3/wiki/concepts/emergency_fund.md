# Emergency Fund

An **emergency fund** is a readily accessible cash reserve intended to cover essential living expenses during unforeseen circumstances such as job loss, medical emergencies, or major repairs. The primary purpose is to provide financial security without forcing the liquidation of long‑term investments.

## Guiding principles from session‑06

- **Size of the fund**: Aim for 3‑6 months of essential expenses; extend to 6‑9 months if income is unstable.
- **Liquidity**: Keep the money in a high‑yield savings account or other cash‑equivalent vehicle that can be accessed instantly and without penalty.
- **Interaction with investing**: Treat the emergency fund as separate from the [[concepts/asset_allocation]] strategy. Once the fund reaches the target, excess cash can be allocated to [[concepts/long_term_investing]].

### Example

For someone with $6,000 monthly essential costs and $60,000 total savings:
- 3‑6 months of expenses → $18,000‑$36,000 as cash.
- A typical split might be $24,000 in a high‑yield savings account and $36,000 invested long‑term.
- If income is irregular, increase the cash target to 6‑9 months ($36,000‑$54,000), reducing the amount invested.

## Practical steps

1. Calculate monthly essential expenses.
2. Multiply by the desired months of coverage.
3. Store that amount in an instantly accessible, low‑risk account.
4. Re‑evaluate periodically, especially after changes in income stability or expense levels.
