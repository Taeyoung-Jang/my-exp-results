# Long-Term Investing

**Definition**: Long‑term investing involves allocating capital to assets that are intended to be held for several years—typically five years or more—to benefit from compound growth and to ride out short‑term market fluctuations.

## Core Principles
- **Time Horizon**: A longer horizon reduces the impact of market volatility and allows investments to recover from downturns.
- **Diversification**: Spreading money across broad, low‑cost index funds or diversified portfolios mitigates risk.
- **Asset Allocation**: Determining the mix of stocks, bonds, and other assets based on risk tolerance and goals; see [[concepts/asset_allocation]].
- **Separation from Cash Needs**: Funds earmarked for long‑term growth should be distinct from the emergency cash buffer, which is covered under [[concepts/emergency_fund]].

## Practical Steps
1. **Establish an Emergency Fund**: Ensure you have enough liquid cash (see [[concepts/emergency_fund]]) before committing additional money to long‑term assets.
2. **Choose Low‑Cost Index Funds**: Opt for broad market index funds to achieve diversification without high fees.
3. **Set a Consistent Contribution Schedule**: Automate regular contributions to benefit from dollar‑cost averaging.
4. **Monitor Allocation Periodically**: Re‑balance the portfolio as needed to maintain the desired asset mix.
