# Liquid Funds

**Definition**

Liquid funds are cash or cash‑equivalents that can be accessed immediately without the need to sell or liquidate assets. In mortgage lending they typically include balances in checking, savings, and money‑market accounts. Funds tied up in investments, retirement accounts, or assets that require a sale are generally *not* considered liquid for closing purposes.

**Why It Matters in the [[concepts/closing_process]]**

During the final stages of a home purchase, lenders must verify that the borrower has sufficient liquid resources to cover the down‑payment, closing costs, and any required reserves. The verification is part of [[concepts/underwriting_triggers]]; unexpected or unexplained deposits can trigger additional underwriting review and potentially delay the clear‑to‑close.

**Typical Sources of Liquid Funds**

- Personal checking or savings account balances.
- Money‑market accounts.
- Recently settled proceeds from a brokerage account (e.g., [[entities/brokerage]] trade confirmations).

Funds must be *documented*: recent bank statements, transaction receipts, and, when applicable, brokerage trade confirmations showing the settlement date.

**Common Pitfalls**

| Pitfall | Impact |
| --- | --- |
| Large, unexplained cash deposits | May trigger additional verification or a re‑underwrite. |
| Gifts received late in the process | Often require a Gift Letter and can delay closing. |
| Transfers that have not settled (e.g., pending T+1/T+2 trades) | Lender may request proof of settlement before counting them as liquid. |

**Best Practices**

1. **Maintain a clear paper trail** – keep statements, trade confirmations, and transfer receipts.
2. **Notify the [[entities/loan_officer]] before moving large sums** – a short note describing the source and timing helps prevent surprises.
3. **Prefer settled proceeds** – wait until brokerage sales settle before transferring to your checking account, or obtain lender approval to count pending transfers.
4. **Coordinate with the [[entities/lender]] and [[entities/title_company]]** – ensure they understand the timing and method (wire or cashier’s check) for delivering the final payment.

**Documentation Checklist**
- Recent bank statements showing the final balance.
- Brokerage statements and trade confirmations for any sales used to generate cash.
- Transfer confirmations (ACH, wire, or internal transfer) showing the funds in the checking account.
