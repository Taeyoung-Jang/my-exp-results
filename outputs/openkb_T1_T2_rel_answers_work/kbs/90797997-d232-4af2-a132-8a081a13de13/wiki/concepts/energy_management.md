## Energy Management

**Definition** – Energy Management is the practice of aligning daily and weekend activities with one's current physical and mental energy levels, allowing deliberate choices between low‑energy (restorative) and higher‑energy (social) modes.

**Why it matters** – In [[summaries/session-37_2024-09-15]] the user seeks a system that prevents weekend burnout while still permitting fun and productivity. By monitoring how they feel at the end of the week, they can pick a template that respects their capacity.

### Link to Intentional Weekend Planning

Energy Management works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/intentional_weekend_planning]]. The latter supplies the idea of an "anchor" activity; Energy Management decides *how much* additional activity to layer around that anchor based on current stamina.

### Energy‑Based Weekend Templates

- **Minimum‑viable (low‑energy) version** – Keeps the schedule light: short errands, ample downtime, and only one optional social block.
- **Higher‑energy version** – Adds extra errands, a longer social hang‑out, or a hobby session while still preserving a core rest period.

Both formats are built on the [[concepts/weekend_template]] structure; the only difference is the amount of time allocated to each block, governed by the user’s energy state.

### Decision Rule (Friday Check‑in)

A concise three‑question rule helps decide which template to adopt:

1. **How does my body feel after the week?** (energized vs. drained) – ties to the energy assessment.
2. **Do I crave connection or solitude?** – informs the social intensity.
3. **Would an extra plan feel exciting or heavy?** – gauges capacity.

Answering these mirrors the pattern described in [[concepts/decision_rule]] and quickly surfaces the appropriate energy level.

### Boundary Setting for Low‑Energy Weeks

When the decision leans toward low‑energy, clear communication is essential. A simple script such as:

> "I’m taking it slow this weekend so I don’t run myself down, but I’d love to find another time soon."

demonstrates the overlap with [[concepts/boundary_setting]], ensuring the user maintains relationships without over‑committing.

### Practical Tips
- **Morning check‑in** – Spend 5‑10 minutes on Friday evening noting physical fatigue, mental clarity, and social desire.
- **Tag activities** – Mark each planned item as *restorative* or *activating* to visualize the energy balance.
- **Built‑in buffer** – Preserve at least one open hour each day for spontaneous rest or low‑key enjoyment.
- **Re‑evaluate** – If energy shifts mid‑weekend, feel free to downgrade or upgrade the template.

By consistently applying Energy Management, weekend plans become adaptable, preventing overwhelm while still leaving space for intentional fun.
