# Interruptible Work

**Definition**: Work that can be stopped and later resumed without loss of progress, additional communication, or penalty. It is especially valuable for people with highly variable availability, such as caregivers.

## Core Characteristics
- **Self‑contained tasks**: Each task has a clear, finite deliverable (e.g., "clean this spreadsheet" or "dedupe this CSV").
- **No scheduled meetings or calls**: Communication is limited to asynchronous messages.
- **Short, modular time blocks**: Designed to fit into 30–60 minute windows.
- **Predictable effort**: The total time required can be reasonably estimated (often ≤ 3 hours total).

## Why it matters for caregivers
In [[summaries/session-07_2022-09-15]], the user is the sole caregiver for a young daughter and can only work in brief evening slots, with some weeks offering zero time. Interruptible work allows them to earn income and build skills without the risk of over‑commitment or missed meetings.

## Practical Application
1. **Identify task type** – Prefer spreadsheet cleanup, data entry, or micro‑gigs that are file‑based.
2. **Use a vetting checklist** – Apply the [[concepts/task_vetting_checklist]] to ensure tasks are truly interruptible.
3. **Plan in micro sessions** – Align with [[concepts/micro_planning]]: each session targets a single sub‑task (e.g., learn a function, apply it, submit).
