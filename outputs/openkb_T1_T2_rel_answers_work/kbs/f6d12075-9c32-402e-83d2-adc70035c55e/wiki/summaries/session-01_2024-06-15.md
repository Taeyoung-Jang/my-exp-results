## Summary

The document offers practical advice for planning outings that minimize standing fatigue and physical strain. It emphasizes **pre‑visit accessibility research**—checking venue websites, FAQs, and photos for seating and mobility‑aid policies—without needing to disclose personal medical details. It recommends building in **planned rest stops**, choosing off‑peak times, and using on‑site transportation options.

### Portable Seating & Mobility Aids
- **Collapsible tripod stool** – highlighted as the most discreet option for airports and museums. Important features include a weight rating of ≥ 225 lb, seat height of 16–18 in, and a folded length comparable to a water bottle (1–1.5 lb). Usage tips: set up on the edge of lines, position legs away from foot traffic, and angle the stool sideways.
- **Cane‑seat** – suitable when only brief rests are needed and no additional walking support is required.
- **Compact rollator** – ideal when both stability while walking and a built‑in seat are needed, despite being more visible.
- Additional low‑key aids: lightweight leaning stools, folding cane‑seats, and travel rollators that blend in with bags.

### Finding Venue Seating Info
- Look for sections titled “Visitor Info,” “Accessibility,” or “FAQs” on official sites.
- Send a brief, neutral email inquiry (e.g., “What seating is available throughout the museum?”).
- Use Google Maps or social‑media photos to spot benches and rest areas.

### Decision Framework
1. Identify **where fatigue occurs** – sitting vs. walking.
2. Choose a **cane‑seat** for brief rests without walking support.
3. Opt for a **tripod stool** for compact, low‑profile sitting.
4. Select a **rollator** when stability and extended walking support are needed.
