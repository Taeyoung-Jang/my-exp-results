# Progressive Overload

**Definition**: Progressive overload is the systematic, incremental increase of training stress (volume, intensity, or frequency) to provoke physiological adaptation and improve performance.

**Why it matters**: Without gradually raising the load, the body quickly plateaus; with too rapid an increase, injury risk rises. The principle works in conjunction with consistency and appropriate recovery.

**Application in distance running (session‑38)**:
- The advice to “increase one thing at a time” and add “a few extra minutes each week” exemplifies progressive overload applied to **long‑run duration**.
- The run‑walk interval approach encourages small, manageable steps in both **volume** (total time) and **intensity** (run vs. walk), aligning with the principle.
- The guideline to stop increasing when soreness lasts more than a day reflects a feedback loop that keeps overload safe.

**Practical guidelines**
1. Choose a single training variable (e.g., run time, distance, or pace).
2. Increase it by a modest amount (commonly 5–10 % per week or a few minutes).
3. Monitor recovery; if fatigue persists, maintain the current level before the next step.
4. Pair overload with **[[concepts/consistency]]** to ensure regular stimulus.
