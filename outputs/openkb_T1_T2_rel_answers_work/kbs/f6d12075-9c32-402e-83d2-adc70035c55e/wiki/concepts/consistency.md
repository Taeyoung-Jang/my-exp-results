# Consistency (running)

**Definition**: Consistency in running means establishing a regular schedule of low‑intensity workouts that become a dependable habit before focusing on speed or mileage.

## Why consistency matters
- **Foundation building**: Regular movement trains the body to handle the mechanical stress of running without injury.
- **Progressive adaptation**: When runs are predictable, you can apply [[concepts/progressive-overload]] safely—adding a small amount of volume or intensity each week.
- **Psychological reward**: Sticking to a routine reinforces motivation and reduces the mental barrier to getting out the door.

## Key practices from [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-15]]
1. **Run‑walk intervals**: Start with 1‑2 min of running followed by 1‑2 min of walking, totaling 20‑30 min every other day. This keeps the effort easy and promotes adherence.
2. **Simple weekly layout**: With three runs per week, designate one as an easy run, one as a quality run, and one as a long run—all anchored around a consistent habit of showing up.
3. **Gradual increase**: Add only a few minutes to a run at a time, monitoring recovery; if soreness lasts more than a day, hold steady.

## Related concepts

- [[concepts/progressive-overload]] – the principle of incremental load increase that relies on a consistent base.

## Practical tip
Treat each scheduled run as a non‑negotiable appointment. Even on days when motivation wanes, completing a short run‑walk session preserves the habit and sets the stage for future performance gains.
