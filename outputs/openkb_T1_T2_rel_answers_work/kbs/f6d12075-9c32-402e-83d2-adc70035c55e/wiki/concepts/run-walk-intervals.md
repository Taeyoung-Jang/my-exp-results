# Run‑Walk Intervals

Run‑walk intervals are a beginner‑friendly training method that alternates brief periods of running with short walking recoveries. The approach is designed to develop **consistency** while gradually exposing the body to higher loads, aligning with the principle of **progressive overload**.

## Core Structure (as described in [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-15]])
- **Run phase**: 1–2 minutes of easy running (a conversational effort).
- **Walk phase**: 1–2 minutes of brisk walking to recover.
- **Total session length**: 20–30 minutes initially.
- **Frequency**: Every other day (e.g., Mon, Wed, Fri) to allow recovery.

## Progression Guidelines
1. **Maintain the alternating pattern** while increasing *one* variable at a time – either the duration of the run segment, the overall session length, or the number of repetitions.
2. **Monitor how you feel** after each session; if soreness persists beyond a day, hold steady rather than adding more volume.
3. **Transition to longer continuous runs** only after comfortably handling several weeks of run‑walk intervals.

## Benefits
- Low injury risk due to built‑in active recovery.
- Reinforces the habit of regular movement, supporting [[concepts/consistency]].
- Provides a structured yet flexible way to apply [[concepts/progressive-overload]] without overwhelming the beginner.

## When to Move Beyond Run‑Walk Intervals
- Once you can comfortably complete 30‑45 minutes of continuous running or have built a solid weekly routine, you can start introducing **quality** or **long** runs as outlined in the same session summary.
