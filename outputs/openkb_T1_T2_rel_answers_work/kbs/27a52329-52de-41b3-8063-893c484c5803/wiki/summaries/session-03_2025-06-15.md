# Summary of session-03_2025-06-15

## Key concepts
- **Chore roles** – Assign recurring chores as stable, time‑bound roles (e.g., kitchen cleanup vs. laundry) to reduce daily negotiation. [[concepts/chore-roles]]
- **Clear expectations** – Define what “done” looks like for each chore in a shared note to avoid disputes. [[concepts/clear-expectations]]
- **Effort balancing** – Roughly track time spent on chores for a week or two to ensure each role represents a comparable workload.
- **Sanity‑check check‑ins** – Brief “how’s this feeling?” conversations every 2 weeks to gauge perceived load without formal score‑keeping.
- **Monthly reset** – A lightweight 10–15 minute agenda that reviews feelings, identifies pain points, applies a simple decision rule, and records a one‑sentence plan for the next month. [[concepts/monthly-reset]]
- **Decision rule** – Swap a chore if one partner wants to hand it off and the other is neutral/okay; split disliked chores into smaller pieces; otherwise keep the status quo.

## Practical steps
1. **Set up role clusters** – Agree on a rotation period (e.g., one month) and assign each partner a small set of chores.
2. **Document expectations** – Create a shared note listing each chore and its completion criteria.
3. **Periodic feel‑check** – Every couple of weeks, each partner answers three prompts:
   - How did chores feel this period?
   - What felt heavier or more annoying?
   - Any schedule or energy changes?
4. **Monthly reset script** – Use the checklist below:
   - Prompt 1: “How did my chores feel this month overall?”
   - Prompt 2: “Did anything feel heavier or more annoying?”
   - Prompt 3: “Has anything in my schedule changed affecting chore bandwidth?”
   - Prompt 4: “If we changed one small thing for next month, what would make it smoother?”
   - Apply the **decision rule** to swap/add/drop a chore.
   - End with a one‑sentence summary: “For next month, I’m doing ___, and you’re doing ___.”

## Outcome
The approach aims to keep chore division **fair**, **transparent**, and **low‑maintenance**, allowing partners to adjust quickly without turning the process into a formal negotiation or score‑keeping exercise.
