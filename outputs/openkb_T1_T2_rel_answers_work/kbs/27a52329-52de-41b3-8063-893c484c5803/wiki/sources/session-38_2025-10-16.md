**User:** I’ve been trying to get better at eating well when my schedule is all over the place—what are some realistic strategies for putting together decent meals when you don’t have the time or energy to cook much?

**Assistant:** One of the easiest ways to stay on track is to think in terms of “components” rather than full recipes. If you can keep a few ready-to-use pieces on hand—like prewashed greens, precut veggies, cooked grains, rotisserie chicken, tofu, or canned beans—you can mix and match them into simple meals in just a few minutes. Even microwavable grain packs and frozen vegetables can make a big difference when you’re tired.

It also helps to cook a little extra whenever you *do* have the energy. Making a double batch of something like soup, roasted vegetables, or a protein you like means you’ve got ready-made building blocks for the next few days. You don’t need to meal-prep an entire week; even preparing one or two things reduces the pressure on busy days.

And when you’re really out of steam, lean on “assembly-only” meals: things like a good-quality frozen entrée paired with a handful of baby spinach, or a yogurt bowl with fruit and nuts, or a wrap using whatever leftovers are in the fridge. These don’t feel like cooking, but they’ll still give you a balanced meal without much effort.

Finally, make it easy to choose well by keeping a few go-to options visible and accessible. If the simplest choice is also a reasonably healthy one, you’re much more likely to stick with it even when your schedule is chaotic.
