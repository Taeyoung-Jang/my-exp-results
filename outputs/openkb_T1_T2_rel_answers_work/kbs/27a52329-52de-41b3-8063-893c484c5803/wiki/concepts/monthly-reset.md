# Monthly Reset

**Definition**: A lightweight, structured check‑in held once a month to evaluate how household chore roles feel, identify any imbalances, and make quick adjustments without turning the process into a formal negotiation.

## Purpose
- Keeps chore distribution **fair** and **transparent** over time.
- Provides a regular, low‑stakes moment to surface hidden workload shifts.
- Enables small, temporary swaps or tweaks instead of large renegotiations.

## When to Run
- Allocate 10–15 minutes, ideally at the start of the first weekend of the month.
- Treat it as a brief conversation, not a meeting.

## Agenda (Script)
1. **Feel‑check prompt**: “How did my chores feel this month overall?” – each partner answers briefly.
2. **Pain‑point prompt**: “Did anything feel heavier, more annoying, or more time‑consuming than usual?”
3. **Context prompt**: “Has anything in my schedule or energy changed that affects chore bandwidth?”
4. **Improvement prompt**: “If we changed one small thing for next month, what would make things feel smoother for each of us?”
5. **Decision rule** (see below) to decide whether to swap, add, or drop a chore.
6. **One‑sentence summary**: “For next month, I’m doing ____, and you’re doing ____.”

## Decision Rule
- **Swap**: If one partner wants to hand off a chore and the other feels neutral or okay taking it, swap it for the month.
- **Split**: If both partners dislike the same chore, break it into smaller parts and trade those pieces.
- **No change**: If neither partner wants any adjustments, keep the current allocation.

## Connection to Other Concepts
- Works best when chore responsibilities are organized as stable [[concepts/chore-roles]].
- Relies on clear criteria for completion, which are captured in [[concepts/clear-expectations]].
