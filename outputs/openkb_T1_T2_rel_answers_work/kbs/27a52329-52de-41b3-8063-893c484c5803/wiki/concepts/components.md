# Components

**Definition**: A practical approach to meal planning that treats individual foods—proteins, grains, vegetables, fats, and extras—as interchangeable building blocks rather than fixed recipes.

## Why it matters
When time, energy, or kitchen access is limited, thinking in terms of components lets you assemble nutritious meals quickly. By stocking a small set of versatile items, you can mix and match to meet your nutritional goals without the overhead of traditional meal preparation.

## Core components
| Category | Examples (from the café) |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Protein  | grilled chicken, shredded chicken, tofu, hard‑boiled eggs, tuna salad |
| Grain / Starch | quinoa (occasionally), microwavable rice or quinoa cups, roasted potatoes |
| Veggies  | mixed greens, spinach, romaine, cucumbers, tomatoes, carrots, bell peppers, roasted broccoli/cauliflower |
| Legumes / Fiber | chickpeas, black beans, lentils (in soup) |
| Healthy fats | avocado, nuts (sunflower seeds, almonds), feta, shredded cheddar |
| Extras / Flavor | vinaigrette, olive oil, hummus |

## Applying components in the real world
The concept is illustrated in the **session‑38** summary, where several "default" meals are built by selecting one item from each category:

* **Hearty salad + soup** – mixed greens (veg) + grilled chicken (protein) + chickpeas (legume) + quinoa (grain) + feta/avocado (fat) + vinaigrette (extra) paired with lentil or vegetable soup.
* **Sandwich/wrap + soup** – turkey sandwich or chicken Caesar wrap (protein + grain) paired with lentil soup or chili (legume + veg).
* **Grain‑anchored bowl + soup** – quinoa or brown‑rice base (grain) + mixed greens (veg) + chickpeas (legume) + grilled chicken (protein) paired with chicken‑noodle or tomato‑basil soup.

These examples show how you can keep a **portable pantry** of components and use the café’s available items to assemble balanced meals on the fly.

## Benefits
- **Speed** – Assembling a meal takes minutes once the components are on hand.
- **Flexibility** – If one component is unavailable (e.g., no quinoa that day), you can substitute another from the same category.
- **Nutrition** – By intentionally choosing a protein, a fiber source, a healthy fat, and a whole‑grain or starch, you hit the key macronutrient balance for satiety and steady energy.
- **Reduced waste** – Extra portions of any component can be stored for later meals, minimizing leftover spoilage.
