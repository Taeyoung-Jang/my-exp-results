# Low‑Effort Attention

**Definition**: A bartending technique that delivers a quick, genuine signal of recognition to a guest—such as a brief eye contact, a short line, or a small smile—while keeping the bartender’s primary focus on service tasks.

**Why it matters**
- Maintains the overall [[concepts/vibe-management]] by keeping the bar atmosphere calm and efficient.
- Supports [[concepts/room-reading]] by allowing the bartender to assess a guest’s desire for interaction without a prolonged dialogue.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with a [[concepts/baseline-persona]] to provide consistent, low‑stakes engagement.

**Core practices**
1. **Eye contact & smile** – A quick glance and a warm smile signals you’ve noticed the guest.
2. **Short acknowledgement phrase** – Lines like “I’ll be right back,” “Thanks for your patience,” or “Did I miss the good part of the story?” acknowledge the guest while indicating you’re busy.
3. **Brief follow‑up** – When you return, a simple “What were we talking about?” re‑engages without making a scene.

**Relationship to other concepts**
- [[concepts/pattern-memory]]: By categorizing guests, you can apply the same low‑effort signals to repeat patrons who expect a certain level of acknowledgment.
- [[concepts/boundary-framing]]: The brief phrases act as soft boundaries, letting guests know you’re attentive yet occupied.
- [[concepts/energy-awareness]] (implicitly): Using low‑effort attention helps regulate the bar’s energy flow, preventing over‑stimulation.

**Example from [[summaries/session-32_2025-02-15]]**
> “A short moment of eye contact, a warm line like ‘I want to hear the rest of that—let me knock these out and I’ll be right back,’ and then you move on.”

**Practical tip**: Keep a mental list of 2‑3 go‑to phrases. Deploy them automatically when you notice a guest looking for interaction; this reduces the cognitive load of “performing” the entire shift.
