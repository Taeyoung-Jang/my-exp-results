# Baseline Persona

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A steady, polite, slightly upbeat default self that bartenders maintain to provide consistent, low‑effort service without constantly shifting roles.

---

## Overview

The *baseline persona* is a deliberate, minimal‑effort identity that a bartender adopts at the start of a shift.  Instead of trying to tailor a unique personality for each guest, the bartender chooses one reliable, friendly demeanor—polite, steady, and a touch upbeat—and uses it as a stable foundation for all interactions.

This approach:
- Reduces mental load by avoiding constant persona‑switching.
- Allows guests to read the bartender’s cues more easily.
- Provides a clear baseline from which low‑effort attention and boundary framing can be applied.

---

## How It Works

1. **Define the Core Traits**
   - **Polite**: Use courteous language, smile, and acknowledge guests.
   - **Steady**: Keep a calm rhythm in service, even under pressure.
   - **Slightly Upbeat**: Offer a light‑hearted tone without being overly energetic.

2. **Maintain Consistency**
   - Keep these traits visible in body language and speech throughout the shift.
   - When a guest asks for more attention, apply *low‑effort attention* (quick eye contact, short acknowledging line) while staying within the baseline demeanor.

3. **Deploy Boundaries Effectively**
   - Use *boundary framing* phrases (e.g., "I’ll be right back") that respect the baseline tone.
   - This signals busyness without breaking the friendly vibe.

## Benefits

- **Reduced Cognitive Load** – Fewer mental switches, less fatigue.
- **Predictable Guest Experience** – Guests know what to expect, building trust.
- **Scalable Interaction** – Allows the bartender to handle busy periods while staying personable.

---

## Practical Tips for Bartenders

- **Write a One‑Line Personal Mantra** (e.g., "Polite, steady, upbeat") and keep it visible.
- **Practice the Core Traits** during off‑hours or slower shifts.
- **Pair with Quick Signals**: Use eye contact and short phrases as part of the *low‑effort attention* toolkit.
- **Review After Shifts**: Note moments when you slipped from the baseline and adjust for next time.
