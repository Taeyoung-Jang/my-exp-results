# Friendship Cadence

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A balanced rhythm of interaction that sustains friendships without burnout.

## Overview
Friendship cadence refers to the regularity and pattern of contact between friends that feels sustainable for all parties involved. It is not a strict schedule but a flexible rhythm that adapts to individual energy levels, life demands, and the depth of the relationship.

## Key Elements
- **Energy Awareness** – Monitoring personal signals such as dread, fatigue, or a sense of disconnection to adjust contact frequency.  [[concepts/energy-awareness]]
- **Low‑Pressure Touchpoints** – Brief, context‑free messages (e.g., a quick photo, a meme, a short check‑in) that keep the connection warm without creating obligations.  [[concepts/friendship-cadence]]
- **Present Presence** – Being fully engaged during in‑person meetings so the time spent together feels valuable, reducing the need for constant communication.
- **Boundary Framing** – Communicating limits as personal energy needs rather than a judgment of the other person, paired with reassurance.  [[concepts/boundary-framing]]

## How It Emerges
In the conversation summarized in [[summaries/session-10_2024-06-15]], the assistant outlines a process for discovering a healthy cadence:
1. **Notice personal energy patterns** – If plans feel draining, slow down; if feeling disconnected, reach out more.
2. **Implement light touchpoints** – Use brief messages or reactions to stay connected.
3. **Set clear, compassionate boundaries** – Phrase limits in terms of personal needs and offer reassurance.
4. **Maintain quality during meetings** – Focus on being present to reinforce the bond.

## Benefits
- Prevents friendship burnout and resentment.
- Supports both introverted and extroverted preferences through adaptable pacing.
- Encourages mutual respect for personal time and wellbeing.

## Practical Tips
- Aim for a baseline of **in‑person meet‑ups roughly twice a month** if that feels sustainable.
- Between meet‑ups, send a short, non‑urgent message once a week or when something reminds you of the friend.
- When boundaries are needed, use language that frames the limit as a personal energy requirement and includes a warm reassurance.
