# Revision Passes

**Revision Passes** are a disciplined approach to manuscript editing that breaks the overall revision process into separate, purpose‑driven stages. Rather than attempting to improve every aspect of a document at once, the writer focuses on one dimension per pass—such as overall structure, argumentative clarity, or surface‑level polishing—before moving on to the next.

## Why Use Revision Passes?
- **Prevent endless loops**: By limiting each session to a single objective, you avoid the temptation to constantly revisit earlier changes.
- **Increase efficiency**: Targeted work reduces cognitive load and makes it easier to gauge progress against a [[concepts/definition-of-done]] checklist.
- **Facilitate planning**: Passes can be mapped onto a [[concepts/weekly-rhythm]] or a sprint‑style schedule, ensuring that each week includes a balanced mix of deep editing, admin, and defense preparation.

## Typical Passes (as described in [[summaries/session-36_2024-06-15]])
1. **Structural Pass** – Verify the logical flow of chapters, sections, and arguments; ensure each part supports the central thesis.
2. **Clarity Pass** – Refine language for readability, tighten sentences, and resolve ambiguous statements.
3. **Citation & Evidence Pass** – Check that all sources are correctly cited, data are accurately presented, and figures/tables are properly referenced.
4. **Formatting & Compliance Pass** – Confirm adherence to graduate‑school style guidelines and formatting requirements.
5. **Surface Polish Pass** – Hunt for typos, grammatical errors, and minor stylistic tweaks.

## Integration with Other Concepts
- **Definition of Done**: The completion of each pass brings the manuscript closer to the criteria outlined in the [[concepts/definition-of-done]] checklist.
- **Weekly Rhythm**: A weekly schedule can allocate distinct time blocks for each pass, allowing the writer to alternate between deep work and lighter tasks while maintaining momentum.
- **Milestone‑Based Sprint Planning**: Passes can be bundled into short sprints (e.g., a two‑day structural sprint followed by a one‑day clarity sprint) to create clear, achievable milestones.

## Practical Tips
- **Set explicit goals** for each pass before you begin (e.g., "Finish structural pass for Chapter 3").
- **Time‑box** the work (e.g., two‑hour blocks) and move on once the goal is met.
- **Document outcomes** after each pass to track progress and inform the next pass.
- **Limit overlap**: Avoid making large structural changes during a clarity or surface pass.

By applying Revision Passes, writers can achieve a sense of closure on each aspect of their dissertation, making the overall "done" state more attainable and less overwhelming.
