## Summary

The document provides a practical framework for recognizing when a dissertation draft is “done” and offers concrete tools to maintain momentum without burnout.

**Key concepts**  
- [[concepts/definition-of-done]]: a checklist‑style threshold covering argument coherence, chapter alignment, committee revisions, citation completeness, figure/table finalisation, formatting compliance, and typo‑free text.  
- [[concepts/revision-passes]]: separating structural, clarity, and surface edit passes to avoid endless loops.  
- [[concepts/weekly-rhythm]]: a templated weekly schedule (Monday–Sunday) that balances dissertation editing, admin/committee coordination, and brief defense‑preparation slots.  
- defense readiness checklist (slide deck, talk outline, anticipated questions, contribution summary, logistical confirmations).  
- energy‑scaled weekly templates (high‑energy and low‑energy variants) that adjust block lengths while preserving the three pillars (edits, admin, defense prep).

**Artifacts provided**  
- A printable “definition of done” checkbox list.  
- A “defense readiness” mini‑checklist.  
- Two complete weekly schedules with explicit time blocks (e.g., 9–11 am editing, 2–3 pm admin) for high‑energy and low‑energy weeks.

**Practical advice**  
- Set time‑bound goals and stop when the checklist is satisfied.  
- Use short, dedicated defense‑prep sessions (≈30 min on Wed, 20 min on Sat) to integrate preparation without overtaking editing work.  
- Reserve full rest days to prevent fatigue before the defense.

These resources aim to help graduate students finalize their dissertations efficiently while preparing for a smooth defense.
