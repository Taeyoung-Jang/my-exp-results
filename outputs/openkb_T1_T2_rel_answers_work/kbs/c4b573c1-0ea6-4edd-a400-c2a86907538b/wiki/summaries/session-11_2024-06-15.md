# Summary of session-11_2024-06-15

## Core Question
- How to decide between a **technical** career track and a **leadership** track in STEM/engineering, and what self‑assessment questions help avoid being boxed into the wrong path.

## Decision Framework [[concepts/technical-vs-leadership-paths]]
- **Technical track**: thrives on deep problem‑solving, expertise, and hands‑on work.  
- **Leadership track**: thrives on shaping direction, coordinating people, and decision‑making with incomplete information.  
- **Self‑assessment questions**:
  1. Do I prefer long stretches of individual problem‑solving or guiding others?  
  2. Do I want to deepen expertise in one domain or move across domains managing projects/teams?  
  3. How much of my day should be spent communicating, negotiating, and making strategic decisions?  
  4. How do I feel when I watch people a few levels ahead – inspired or drained?  
  5. Can I experiment with a small leadership task or a deep technical task to gauge fit?

## Signaling a Technical Preference [[concepts/signaling-technical-track]]
- Frame the preference around **impact** (solving complex problems, owning technical depth) rather than avoidance of teamwork.  
- Emphasize willingness to **collaborate, mentor, and communicate** while keeping the core focus on engineering.  
- Suggested titles that signal hands‑on work: *Engineer, Scientist, Specialist, Architect* (Senior, Staff, or Principal versions such as [[entities/senior-engineer]], [[entities/staff-engineer]], [[entities/principal-engineer]]).  
- Titles to be cautious about: *Project Manager* ([[entities/project-manager]]), *Program Manager* ([[entities/program-manager]]), or any role where deliverables are primarily schedules, budgets, or personnel decisions.

## Crafting 1:1 Scripts
### Short (≈30 seconds)
> "I’ve realized my strongest impact comes from deep technical work in the lab. I’d like to keep growing on the technical track and take on increasingly complex problems so I can become a go‑to expert for the team."

### Longer (2‑3 minutes)
> "I’ve been paying attention to the parts of my work where I’m both most effective and most energized, and it’s the hands‑on technical side—designing experiments, troubleshooting complex issues, and driving technical decisions. I’d like to keep developing along the technical track and grow toward being the person people rely on for depth and execution. I’m happy to take on more responsibility, but I want that responsibility to be in the form of technical ownership, higher‑impact projects, and mentoring others on the technical side rather than management duties."

### Manager Push‑back: “Leadership is the next step”
> "I definitely want to grow, and I see leadership as part of that—but leadership through expertise. I’d rather lead by driving technical decisions, mentoring juniors on the technical side, and owning complex problem areas. That’s where I can contribute the most right now."

## Measurable Growth Goals [[concepts/growth-goal-setting]]
1. **Technical ownership** of a more complex subsystem or research area.  
2. **End‑to‑end project delivery**: take a concept from design through lab validation.  
3. **Become the in‑house expert** on a specific technique/tool and document best practices.  
4. **Design and execute** a challenging experimental campaign that unlocks a team objective.  
5. **Mentor** a junior engineer on lab methods or data analysis (e.g., monthly coaching sessions).  
6. **Submit a patent or internal invention disclosure** related to your work.  
7. **Present a technical deep‑dive** to the group or at an internal symposium.  
8. **Publish** a paper or white‑paper (if applicable) showcasing a novel methodology.
