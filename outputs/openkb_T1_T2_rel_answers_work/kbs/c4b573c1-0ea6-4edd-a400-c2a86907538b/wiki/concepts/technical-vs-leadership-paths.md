# Technical vs. Leadership Career Paths

**Source:** Derived from [[summaries/session-11_2024-06-15]].

## Overview

The concept describes two primary career trajectories for engineers and other STEM professionals:

* **Technical track** – focus on deep problem‑solving, hands‑on work, and building subject‑matter expertise.
* **Leadership track** – focus on shaping direction, coordinating teams, and making strategic decisions.

Choosing a path early can help avoid being steered into a role that feels limiting or misaligned with one’s strengths.

## Decision Factors

| Factor | Technical Track | Leadership Track |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| Core activity | Individual problem‑solving, experiment design, code/hardware development | Defining vision, managing people, allocating resources |
| Motivation | Enjoys deep dives, mastering a domain, being the go‑to expert | Enjoys influencing outcomes, mentoring at scale, building teams |
| Typical titles (examples) | [[entities/senior-engineer]], [[entities/staff-engineer]], [[entities/principal-engineer]] | [[entities/project-manager]], [[entities/program-manager]] |
| Success metric | Technical depth, patents, publications, system ownership | Team performance, project delivery, organizational impact |

## Self‑Assessment Questions

1. Do I prefer long stretches of individual problem‑solving or guiding others?
2. Do I want to deepen expertise in one domain or move across domains managing projects?
3. How much of my day should be spent communicating, negotiating, and making decisions with incomplete information?
4. When I observe people a few levels above me, do I feel inspired or drained?
5. Can I experiment with a small leadership task or a deep technical task to gauge fit?

## Signaling a Technical Preference

When you decide the technical track is right for you, frame the conversation around **impact** rather than avoidance of teamwork. For guidance on phrasing and tactics, see [[concepts/signaling-technical-track]].

## Experimentation

Try taking on brief responsibilities that sit on the border of the two paths – e.g., lead a small technical project, mentor a junior on a lab technique, or sit in on a planning meeting. Observe where you feel most energized and where you add the most value.
