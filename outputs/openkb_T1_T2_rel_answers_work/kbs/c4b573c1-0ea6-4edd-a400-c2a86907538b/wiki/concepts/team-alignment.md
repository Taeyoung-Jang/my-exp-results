# Team Alignment

**Definition**: The practice of establishing a common understanding of current work, priorities, and upcoming handoffs so that everyone moves in the same direction without unnecessary friction.

## Why it matters
In fast‑moving teams, misaligned priorities and unclear transitions can cause bottlenecks, duplicated effort, and morale loss. The conversation in [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-16]] highlights that early, low‑pressure conversations and a single, visual artifact are effective ways to surface misalignment quickly.

## Core practices (from session‑38)
1. **Conversational discovery** – Spend the first weeks holding informal chats with contributors to learn what they are doing, what blocks them, and what they wish were clearer.
2. **Lightweight visual board** – Deploy a one‑page [[concepts/lightweight-dashboard]] that lists each active piece of work with owner, priority, phase, next handoff, date, and a simple health signal.
3. **Weekly async check‑in** – Have owners update their row before a set time each week and post a brief written summary of any red or overlapping high‑priority items.
4. **Objective health signaling** – Use traffic‑light colors tied to timing and blockage rather than subjective feeling (see [[concepts/handoff-risk-management]]).

## Relationship to other concepts
- **[[concepts/lightweight-dashboard]]** provides the concrete artifact that makes alignment visible.
- **[[concepts/handoff-risk-management]]** is a subset of alignment focused on the moments where work moves between people.
- **[[concepts/growth-goal-setting]]** can feed into alignment by clarifying how individual growth objectives map to team priorities.

## Steps to introduce alignment in a chaotic team
1. **Listen first** – Conduct brief one‑on‑ones to map current work and pain points.
2. **Create the board** – Use a spreadsheet or Notion table with columns: Project, Owner, Collaborator, Priority, Phase, Next Handoff, Handoff Date, What’s Needed Next, Health.
3. **Define health rules** – Green = realistic date, no block; Yellow = at‑risk; Red = likely miss or blocked.
4. **Set the cadence** – Weekly async updates; no extra synchronous meeting unless a red flag appears.
5. **Iterate** – After a few weeks, trim any columns that are rarely touched and reinforce the habit of quick, transparent updates.

## Benefits
- Immediate visibility of overlapping high‑priority work.
- Early detection of handoff risk and stalled items.
- Reduced need for lengthy status meetings.
- Builds trust through a shared, low‑overhead source of truth.
