# Short Exercise, Long Rest Pattern

**Definition**: A care approach that emphasizes brief, low‑intensity exercise sessions combined with ample, uninterrupted rest time.  It is especially important for breeds such as greyhounds that are built for short bursts of speed rather than endurance.

## Core Elements
- **Exercise Length**: 10–20 minutes of walking or gentle sprinting, enough to stretch the muscles and satisfy a dog’s need for movement without causing fatigue.
- **Rest Duration**: After each exercise bout, the dog should have access to a comfortable, quiet space where it can nap for the majority of the day.
- **Routine Predictability**: Consistent timing of walks and rest periods helps the dog feel secure and reduces anxiety.
- **Enrichment Integration**: Short, mentally stimulating activities (e.g., puzzle feeders, snuffle mats) can be offered during rest periods to keep the mind engaged without increasing physical exertion.

## Application to Greyhounds
The pattern is highlighted in [[summaries/session-39_2024-06-15]], where the assistant advises Luma, a three‑year‑old greyhound, to receive two short walks per day (morning and evening) and to spend the majority of the time resting in a calm, gated room. The guidance notes that greyhounds are “sprinters, not endurance dogs,” making the short‑exercise‑long‑rest model a natural fit.

## Benefits
- **Physical Health**: Prevents over‑exertion in breeds prone to muscle strain.
- **Mental Well‑Being**: Predictable schedules and adequate rest reduce stress and anxiety.
- **Owner Convenience**: Short walks fit easily into typical workday schedules while still meeting the dog’s exercise needs.
