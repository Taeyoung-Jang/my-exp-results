# Breed‑Specific Care (Greyhound Example)

Greyhounds have unique needs that differ from many other breeds. Understanding these requirements helps owners provide a comfortable, healthy environment.

## Core Principles
- **Short bursts of exercise**: 10‑20 minute walks or sprint sessions are sufficient; they are sprinters, not endurance dogs. [[concepts/short_exercise_long_rest]]
- **Long periods of rest**: After activity, greyhounds love to lounge for hours. Provide a quiet, temperature‑controlled space.
- **Predictable routine**: Consistent meal, walk, and rest times reduce anxiety and promote confidence.
- **Tolerance for alone time**: With proper setup, they can handle 8‑hour absences, but need reassurance and enrichment.

## Daily Routine (Based on Luma)
| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| **Morning** | Calm wake‑up, quick bathroom break, breakfast, 10‑20 min walk, safe enrichment (e.g., treat puzzle). |
| **Midday** | If possible, a brief dog‑walker visit; otherwise, a gated indoor area with bed, water, and low‑volume background noise. |
| **Evening** | Longer walk (up to 20 min), gentle play or sniff‑scavenger game, dinner, then settle near owner for winding down. |

## Indoor Dog Zone
- Prefer a **gated room** over a crate for long stretches; greyhounds like to stretch out.
- Include multiple bedding options and a **non‑slip rug** for comfort.
- Use soft music, a low‑volume TV channel, or a white‑noise machine to create a soothing ambience. [[concepts/indoor_dog_zone]]

## Safe Enrichment
- Durable rubber toys or frozen treat toys that cannot be torn apart.
- **Snuffle mat** for gentle foraging (if the dog is not destructive). [[entities/snuffle_mat]]
- Simple **puzzle feeders** or treat‑stuffed toys for mental stimulation. [[concepts/puzzle_feeders]]

## Signs of Stress When Alone
- Persistent vocalizing that does not subside.
- Accidents occurring only during owner‑away periods.
- Pacing, restlessness, or destructive chewing near doors.
- Over‑clinginess upon return.

If these behaviors appear, consider adjusting the routine, adding a midday check‑in, or consulting a trainer/vet.
