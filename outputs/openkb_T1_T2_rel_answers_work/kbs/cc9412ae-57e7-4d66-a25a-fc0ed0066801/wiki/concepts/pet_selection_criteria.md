# Pet Selection Criteria

Choosing the right pet starts with an honest assessment of your daily life and environment. The core questions revolve around five key dimensions:

1. **Time Availability** – How many hours each day can you dedicate to hands‑on care, walks, training, and play?  Consider both weekday work hours and weekend flexibility.
2. **Living Space** – Do you have sufficient indoor room for the animal to move comfortably?  Are there noise restrictions, building rules, or roommates that limit certain species or sizes?
3. **Budget** – Beyond the initial adoption fee, factor in ongoing costs such as food, veterinary care, grooming, and emergency funds.
4. **Energy Level & Activity** – Match your own activity level with the pet’s needs.  High‑energy owners may thrive with a dog that enjoys long runs, while lower‑energy households benefit from a more low‑maintenance companion.
5. **Desired Interaction** – Decide how social and affectionate you want the animal to be.  Some owners prefer a constant lap‑dog presence, while others enjoy an independent pet that is comfortable with alone time.

These criteria help narrow down species, breeds, and temperaments that fit your lifestyle.  For example, the user in [[summaries/session-39_2024-06-15]] chose a greyhound because they valued short bursts of exercise and long rest periods, aligning with a predictable, low‑noise routine.  The same framework can be applied to any potential pet, ensuring that the chosen animal’s needs align with the owner’s capacity.
