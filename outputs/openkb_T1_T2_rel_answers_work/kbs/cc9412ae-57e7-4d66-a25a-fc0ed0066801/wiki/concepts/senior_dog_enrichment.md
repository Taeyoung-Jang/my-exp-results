# Senior Dog Enrichment

**Definition**: A set of low‑impact, safety‑focused activities and environmental adjustments that support the physical comfort and mental stimulation of older dogs.

---

## Core Principles
1. **Predictable environment** – Keep essential items (bed, water, toys) in a consistent location to reduce confusion and anxiety.  
2. **Safe surfaces** – Use non‑slip rugs or runners to provide traction on hardwood or tiled floors, preventing slips and joint strain.  
3. **Supportive resting space** – Provide a low‑side, cushiony bed that is easy to climb in and out of.  
4. **Gentle mental challenges** – Offer puzzle feeders that match the dog's natural instincts (e.g., scent work for beagles) while ensuring early successes to build confidence.  
5. **Minimal clutter** – Limit the zone to a few high‑value items and rotate them sparingly to keep the area calm but interesting.

---

## Practical Applications (from [[summaries/session-08_2022-06-15]])
- **Indoor zone setup**: Designate a quiet corner of the living room and place a supportive bed, water bowl, and a favorite toy there.  
- **Floor traction**: Lay a medium‑sized, low‑pile rug under the bed and add a narrow runner (or a second stepping‑stone rug) leading to the water source.  
- **Puzzle feeders**: Use a **[[entities/snuffle_mat]]** for nose‑work and a **[[entities/wobble_feeder]]** for gentle food dispensing.  
- **Behavior shaping**: Start each feeder with easy, high‑value rewards and gradually increase difficulty only after the dog experiences quick wins.
