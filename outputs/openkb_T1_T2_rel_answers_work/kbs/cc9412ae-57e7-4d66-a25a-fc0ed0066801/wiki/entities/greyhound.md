# Greyhound (Dog Breed)

Greyhounds are a sighthound breed known for their incredible speed, slender build, and gentle temperament. They are often described as "sprinters, not endurance dogs," which shapes their daily care needs.

## Key Characteristics
- **Exercise pattern**: Require brief, high‑intensity walks (10‑20 minutes) rather than long, sustained outings. This aligns with the [[concepts/short_exercise_long_rest]] principle.
- **Rest needs**: After a short burst of activity, they spend many hours lounging and sleeping. A comfortable, quiet space is essential.
- **Routine‑oriented**: Thrive on predictable daily schedules for meals, walks, and rest periods.
- **Alone time**: Generally tolerant of being alone for several hours if the environment is calm and enrichment is provided.
- **Sensitivity**: Can be shy or anxious around sudden noises; a low‑key departure and return routine helps them stay relaxed.

## Home Environment Recommendations
- **Indoor dog zone**: A gated room (rather than a crate) works best for long stretches, allowing the dog to stretch out and choose a resting spot. See [[concepts/indoor_dog_zone]].
- **Bedding**: Provide multiple soft beds or blankets so the dog can select its preferred spot.
- **Background noise**: Soft music, a TV channel, or a white‑noise machine can make the space feel less empty.
- **Safe enrichment**: Use durable rubber toys, frozen treat toys, or puzzle feeders that cannot be torn apart. The [[concepts/puzzle_feeders]] concept covers suitable options.

## Care Example from [[summaries/session-39_2024-06-15]]
The session outlines a sample weekday routine for **Luma**, a three‑year‑old greyhound:
- **Morning**: Quick bathroom break, breakfast, 10‑20 minute walk, then a treat puzzle before the owner leaves.
- **Midday**: If a walker is unavailable, Luma stays in a gated room with a bed, water, and background noise.
- **Evening**: Slightly longer walk, gentle play or a sniff‑scavenger game, dinner, and quiet time near the owner.

## Signs of Stress When Alone
- Persistent vocalizing after departure.
- Accidents occurring only during the owner's absence.
- Pacing, restlessness, or destructive chewing near doors.
- Over‑clinginess when the owner returns.

If these behaviors appear, consider adjusting the routine, adding more enrichment, or consulting a trainer/vet.
