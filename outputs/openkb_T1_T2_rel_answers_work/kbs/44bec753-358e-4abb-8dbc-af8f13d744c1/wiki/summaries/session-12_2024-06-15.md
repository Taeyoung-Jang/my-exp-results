# Summary of session-12_2024-06-15

This document provides practical advice and ready‑to‑use email templates for negotiating with vendors (printing, catering, venue, etc.) in a small community fundraising drive.  The key points are:

- **Negotiation approach** – Treat the interaction as a collaborative conversation rather than a hard‑ball negotiation.  Be clear about needs, timeline, and budget range, ask open‑ended questions, and restate agreed terms to avoid misunderstandings.  vendor negotiation
- **Core contract terms to watch** – For printing: turnaround time, proofing steps, re‑print policy, rush‑order fees.  For catering: headcount policy, cancellation/reduction deadlines, service fees, and what is included per‑person (staff, equipment, setup).  contract terms
- **Email scripts** – Templates for:
  1. Initial quote request that signals authority and the need for documented quotes.  
  2. Follow‑up when a price is higher than expected, asking for flexibility or cost‑saving tweaks.  
  3. Award email confirming scope, total cost, deadlines, and next steps.  
  4. Polite decline email for vendors not selected.  All scripts emphasize warm, transparent language while maintaining clear documentation for the committee.  email scripts
- **Authority and documentation** – Emphasize that the organizer is the point person with decision‑making power, but must provide written details for committee records.  This balances autonomy with accountability.  authority and decision‑making clear documentation
- **Budget awareness** – Highlight the importance of aligning vendor proposals with the allocated budget and requesting alternatives when costs exceed limits.

These guidelines can be reused across future fundraising events and adapted for other small‑group projects that involve vendor management.
