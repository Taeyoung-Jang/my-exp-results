# Summary of Session 11 (2024-06-15)

## Core Idea
A practical framework for deciding when to upgrade a phone or laptop, how to assess repair vs. replacement, and what questions to ask before buying.

## Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/upgrade_decision_criteria]]** – focus on whether the current device is *actively* hindering everyday tasks rather than on appealing new features.  
- **[[concepts/real_problem_triggers]]** – specific symptoms that signal a genuine need to upgrade:  
  - **Phone**: battery fails a full day, apps freeze or lag, camera no longer meets needs, missing security/software updates.  
  - **Laptop**: slowdowns on core work tasks, fans loud under light load, storage constantly full, essential software incompatibility, poor video‑call performance.  
- Decision tree – a simple fork: *Is the device limiting what you need to do?* → **Yes** = upgrade; **No** = wait.  
- Exception cases – situations where earlier upgrades make sense:  
  1. A new feature will markedly improve your workflow (e.g., faster compile/export times).  
  2. Device becomes a security risk due to end‑of‑updates.  
  3. Repair costs approach the price of a new device, making replacement more economical.  
- **[[concepts/repair_vs_replacement]]** – treat repairs as “buying time.”  
- Cost thresholds – rule of thumb: a repair is worthwhile if its cost is **< ≈ 33 %** of a new device and extends usable life by at least **1–2 years**.  
  - Phones: battery swap usually adds ~1 year+.  
  - Laptops: storage or battery upgrades can add a couple of years if CPU/RAM remain adequate.

## Practical Checklist
1. Identify any **real‑problem trigger** for your device.  
2. Ask: *Will the upgrade solve this problem or merely add nice‑to‑have features?*  
3. If yes, proceed; if no, stay put.  
4. For borderline cases, compare **repair cost** vs. **one‑third of new‑device price** and estimate the extra lifespan you’d gain.  
5. Consider **exception cases** before dismissing an early upgrade.

## Decision Guidance
- **Phone thresholds**: battery < 1 day, frequent app freezes, no security updates.  
- **Laptop thresholds**: tasks > 2 × normal time, constant storage pressure, essential apps refuse to run.  
- **When to repair**: cost < ⅓ of new device AND expected life extension ≥ 1 year.  
- **When to replace**: repair cost > ⅓ new price, or device already hits software/security limits.

## Takeaway
Use the decision tree and cost‑threshold rule to move from impulse‑driven upgrades to data‑driven choices, ensuring upgrades are justified by real functional benefits rather than marketing hype.
