## Real Problem Triggers

**Definition**: Observable performance or functional issues that signal a device (phone or laptop) is no longer adequately supporting a user's everyday tasks, prompting consideration of an upgrade.

### Why it matters
Real Problem Triggers are the primary criterion in [[concepts/upgrade_decision_criteria]]: they distinguish genuine needs from mere desire for new features. When any trigger is present, the device is actively limiting productivity or convenience, making an upgrade or repair justified.

### Phone Triggers
- **Battery life**: Cannot sustain a normal day even after standard power‑saving steps.
- **App performance**: Frequent freezes, noticeable lag, or slow launches during routine use.
- **Camera adequacy**: Image quality or functionality no longer meets the user’s needs.
- **Software updates**: Loss of security patches or incompatibility with required apps.
- **Workarounds**: Regularly needing to charge midday, avoid certain apps, or adjust usage patterns.

### Laptop Triggers
- **Task slowdown**: Core work applications take significantly longer (e.g., double the usual time).
- **Noise & heat**: Fans run loudly under light loads, indicating inefficiency.
- **Storage pressure**: Persistent near‑full storage requiring constant file off‑loading.
- **Software compatibility**: Essential programs refuse to run or receive updates.
- **Performance during calls**: Video calls degrade, drops, or audio issues.

### Connection to Other Concepts
- These triggers feed into the decision tree described in [[concepts/upgrade_decision_criteria]].
- When evaluating repair versus replacement, the severity of a trigger helps assess whether a repair (e.g., battery swap) can extend useful life, linking to [[concepts/repair_vs_replacement]].
