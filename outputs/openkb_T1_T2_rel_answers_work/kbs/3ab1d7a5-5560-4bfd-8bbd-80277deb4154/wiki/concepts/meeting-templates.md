# Meeting Templates

**Definition**: Pre‑written messages that convey availability, translate between time zones, and provide concrete slot examples, making scheduling faster and less error‑prone.

## Core Elements
- **Reference to a safe meeting window** – typically a personal rule such as 8 am–6 pm Pacific Time, which maps to 11 am–9 pm for clients three hours ahead. See [[concepts/safe-meeting-window]].
- **Time‑zone conversion** – explicitly state both the sender’s zone and the recipient’s zone to avoid confusion. This is a key practice in [[concepts/time-zone-management]].
- **Concrete example slots** – include 2–3 specific times that fall within the allowed window, showing both PT and the client’s time.
- **Format variations** – a longer explanatory version, a concise email one‑liner, and an ultra‑casual text‑message version.

## Example Templates from [[summaries/session-15_2024-06-15]]
### General Availability Template
> "I’m available between 8am and 6pm Pacific Time, which is 11am to 9pm your time. For example, I could meet at 9am PT (noon your time), 1pm PT (4pm your time), or 4:30pm PT (7:30pm your time). Let me know if one of those works, or if there’s another time in that window you prefer."

### Specific Slot Template
> "I can do something in the morning or mid‑afternoon Pacific Time. For instance, 8:30am PT (11:30am your time), 11am PT (2pm your time), or 3pm PT (6pm your time). Happy to adjust within my 8am–6pm PT window if another slot is better for you."

### Email One‑Liner
> "I’m free between 8am–6pm PT (11am–9pm your time); for example, 9am PT / noon your time works if that’s good for you."

### Casual Text Message
> "I’m good anytime 8–6 PT—want to do like 9 PT (noon for you)?"

## Practical Tips
- Store these templates in a note‑taking app for quick copy‑paste.
- Adjust the client‑zone reference when dealing with locations other than [[entities/new-york-city]] (e.g., Europe or Asia).
- Pair templates with calendar tools like [[entities/google-calendar]] or [[entities/calendly]] that can auto‑populate time‑zone conversions.
