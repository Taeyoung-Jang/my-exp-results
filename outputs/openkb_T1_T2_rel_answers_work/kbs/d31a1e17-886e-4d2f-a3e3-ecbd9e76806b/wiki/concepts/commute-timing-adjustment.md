# Commute Timing Adjustment

**Definition**: A low‑effort strategy that shifts the start or end of a daily commute by a small amount (typically 5–15 minutes) to avoid the heaviest traffic periods, thereby lowering stress, fuel consumption, and overall travel time.

## Key Details from [[summaries/session-14_2024-06-15]]
- **Impact on Stress**: Leaving even 10–15 minutes earlier or later can turn a stop‑and‑go slog into a smoother drive, reducing tension and mental fatigue.
- **Fuel Savings**: Smoother traffic flow means fewer accelerations and decelerations, which directly cuts per‑mile fuel burn.
- **Simple Experiment**: Test a new departure window for three consecutive days, record travel time, fuel used, and a quick stress rating (tense/neutral/relaxed). Compare against the baseline schedule.
- **No Major Lifestyle Change**: This adjustment requires only a minor tweak to daily timing, making it accessible for most commuters.

## How to Implement
1. **Identify Peak Windows**: Use a traffic app or personal observation to pinpoint the most congested 15‑minute blocks.
2. **Shift Incrementally**: Adjust your departure time in 5‑minute steps until you notice a consistent reduction in stop‑and‑go traffic.
3. **Track Metrics**: Over a week, log:
   - Door‑to‑door travel time
   - Fuel used (fill up before each test block)
   - Subjective stress level
4. **Evaluate**: Choose the timing that yields the lowest combined stress and fuel cost.
