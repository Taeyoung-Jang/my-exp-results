# Route Optimization

**Definition**: Evaluating and selecting commuting paths that trade off mileage against traffic smoothness, fuel consumption, and personal stress levels.

## Core ideas
- **Smooth vs. shortest**: A longer route that maintains steady speed can consume less fuel and reduce driver fatigue compared to the shortest, stop‑and‑go‑heavy route.
- **A/B testing**: Try each candidate route for three consecutive workdays, logging travel time, fuel used, and a quick stress rating (tense/neutral/relaxed). Compare averages to identify the optimal path.
- **Metrics**:
  - Door‑to‑door travel time
  - Fuel volume per test block (fill tank before each 3‑day trial)
  - Subjective stress level after each drive
- **Iterative refinement**: Adjust timing and experiment with alternate roads or multimodal legs (e.g., park‑and‑ride, biking to a transit hub) based on test outcomes.

## Application in session‑14_2024‑06‑15
The conversation highlighted a commuter traveling ~30 miles each way who experiences stress from stop‑and‑go traffic. The assistant suggested:
1. **Timing shift** first, then
2. **Route optimization** as a second priority, emphasizing that a smoother, slightly longer path may lower fuel costs and improve comfort.
3. Conduct a week‑long experiment using the metrics above to decide between the current shortest route and a smoother alternative.
