# Menu Planning for Small Food Ventures

**Definition**: Menu planning is the process of selecting a compact, cohesive set of food items that match the baker’s capacity, equipment, market constraints, and branding goals.

---

## Why It Matters
- Determines the **equipment** you need (e.g., a single home oven) and the **permits** required (cottage‑food regulations).
- Drives the **production schedule** – a well‑chosen menu lets you batch‑produce efficiently.
- Influences **pricing**, **branding**, and the overall customer experience at the market stall.

---

## Core Principles (derived from [[summaries/session-33_2025-08-12]])
1. **Keep the menu narrow** – aim for 3–4 items that can be produced with the same base dough or batter.
2. **Share components** – use a single enriched dough for both cinnamon rolls and cardamom buns, and a single cookie base for chocolate‑chip and oatmeal cookies. This reduces ingredient inventory and prep time.
3. **Match product shelf‑life to market hours** – choose items that stay fresh through a Saturday market (e.g., baked goods that can be refrigerated the day before and finished on‑site).
4. **Consider your production windows** – with ~2 hours on weekday evenings and a 6‑hour block on Friday, the menu must be producible within those limits.
5. **Align with branding** – a concise menu makes packaging, signage, and visual identity clearer and more trustworthy.

---

## Step‑by‑Step Menu Planning Process
1. **Identify your target customer** (e.g., weekend market shoppers looking for sweet treats).
2. **Brainstorm product families** that share a base:
   - Enriched dough → cinnamon rolls, cardamom buns.
   - Cookie dough → chocolate‑chip cookies, oatmeal cookies.
3. **Validate feasibility** against your resources:
   - Does [[entities/home_oven]] accommodate the batch sizes? (≈1 kg flour per batch).
   - Are the items permitted under your local [[entities/cottage_food_permit]]?
4. **Set initial inventory targets** (e.g., 24 rolls, 24 buns, 48 cookies) to avoid over‑production.
5. **Sketch a rough price point** using ingredient cost + labor + margin.
6. **Create a simple name and visual style** that can be applied consistently across all four items.
