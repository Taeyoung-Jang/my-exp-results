# Cottage Food Permit

A **Cottage Food Permit** is a regulatory authorization that permits individuals to prepare and sell certain low‑risk foods from a home kitchen. It is typically required for anyone planning to operate a small‑scale food business, such as a weekend market stall, and ensures compliance with local health and labeling rules.

## Key Facts from Session 33
- The assistant advises checking *local cottage‑food* rules early to determine if production can be done at home and what permits are needed. [[summaries/session-33_2025-08-12]]
- The permit may dictate requirements for labeling, handling, and storage of baked goods sold at the [[entities/saturday-market]].
- Possession of the permit is part of the broader **menu planning** and **production schedule** workflow, as it impacts where and how the dough and cookies can be prepared. [[concepts/menu_planning]] [[concepts/production_schedule]]
- Food safety practices such as sanitizing surfaces, using a thermometer, and proper packaging are tied to permit compliance. [[concepts/stall_setup]]

## Related Entities & Concepts
- **Home Oven**: The primary equipment used for baking under the permit’s home‑kitchen allowance. [[entities/home_oven]]
- **Menu Planning**: Selecting a limited menu that conforms to the types of foods allowed by the cottage‑food regulations. [[concepts/menu_planning]]
- **Production Schedule**: Timing of prep and baking activities must respect the permit’s storage and handling limits. [[concepts/production_schedule]]
- **Stall Setup**: The final presentation at the market must include proper labeling as required by the permit. [[concepts/stall_setup]]

## Practical Guidance
1. **Research Local Requirements** – Contact the county or city health department to obtain the specific cottage‑food permit application.
2. **Determine Eligible Foods** – Verify that items like cinnamon rolls, cardamom buns, and cookies are permitted under the low‑risk category.
3. **Labeling & Packaging** – Include ingredient lists, allergen warnings, and the permit number on all packaged goods.
4. **Maintain Records** – Keep a log of production batches, temperatures, and cleaning routines for inspection readiness.
5. **Renewal & Inspection** – Stay aware of renewal dates and any required kitchen inspections to keep the permit active.
