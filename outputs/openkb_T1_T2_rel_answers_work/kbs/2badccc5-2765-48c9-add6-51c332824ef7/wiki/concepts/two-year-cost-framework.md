# Two‑Year Cost Framework

The **Two‑Year Cost Framework** is a practical tool for evaluating whether to continue using an existing vehicle or to replace it, by estimating the total out‑of‑pocket expense for each option over the next two years.  It was articulated in the conversation documented in [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]].

## Goal
- Provide a side‑by‑side financial picture of the **keep** versus **replace** paths so a driver can make an informed decision.

## Core Input Elements
1. **Resale value** – Expected private‑party price of the current car today.
2. **Repair & maintenance estimate** – Projected cost of all foreseeable repairs and routine upkeep for the next two years.
3. **Replacement purchase price** – All‑in price (including taxes and fees) of the vehicle being considered.
4. **Down‑payment amount** – Cash you plan to put toward the new purchase.
5. **Financing terms** – Loan term, interest rate, or an estimated monthly payment.
6. **Insurance cost difference** – Current insurance premium versus the anticipated premium for the replacement.
7. **Fuel usage** – MPG for each vehicle and your expected annual mileage.

## Calculation Steps
### Keep Scenario
1. **Repair & maintenance** – Add the 2‑year repair estimate.
2. **Depreciation** – Estimate the drop in resale value over two years (see [[concepts/vehicle-depreciation]]).  Subtract the projected future resale value from the current resale value.
3. **Insurance** – Assume the current premium stays roughly constant.
4. **Fuel** – Compute yearly fuel cost: `miles ÷ MPG × fuel price`; double for two years.
5. **Total Keep Cost** = Repairs + Depreciation loss + Insurance + Fuel.

### Replace Scenario
1. **Financing cost** – Multiply the monthly payment by 24 (or calculate total interest over the chosen term).
2. **Depreciation** – Estimate loss of value on the new vehicle over two years (again using [[concepts/vehicle-depreciation]]).
3. **Insurance** – Add the higher premium for the newer car.
4. **Fuel** – Same method as above, using the replacement’s MPG.
5. **Down‑payment offset** – Subtract the resale cash received from the current car.
6. **Total Replace Cost** = Financing + Depreciation + Insurance + Fuel – Down‑payment offset.

## Assumptions Often Used
- **Depreciation rates**: older cars lose 10‑15 % over two years, while a newer used car can lose 15‑25 %.
- **Insurance**: newer models typically cost $200‑$300 more per year.
- **Fuel price**: a constant per‑gallon price is assumed for simplicity.
- **Maintenance**: older cars have higher projected maintenance; newer cars assume a modest $300‑$500 over two years.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- This framework feeds directly into the broader decision‑making process captured in [[concepts/keep-vs-replace-decision]].
- It relies on realistic depreciation estimates, which are explored in [[concepts/vehicle-depreciation]].

By applying the **Two‑Year Cost Framework**, users can quantify the financial impact of each path and choose the option that aligns best with their budget and lifestyle.
