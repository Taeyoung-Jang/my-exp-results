# Keep vs. Replace Decision

The **Keep vs. Replace Decision** framework helps car owners compare the costs and benefits of staying with their current vehicle versus purchasing a newer one. It originates from the discussion in [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]], where a practical 2‑year cost comparison was outlined.

## When to Keep
- The car remains **reliable** and meets daily needs.
- Ongoing **maintenance and repair costs** are modest and predictable.
- The vehicle is **paid off**, reducing monthly cash‑flow pressure.
- Depreciation is low because the car is already an older model.

## When to Replace
- **Frequent breakdowns** or costly repairs that approach or exceed the car’s resale value.
- Changing lifestyle needs (e.g., larger family, different climate, desire for better fuel efficiency or safety features).
- Higher **insurance premiums** or anticipated fuel costs for a newer vehicle.
- Opportunity to upgrade to a vehicle with lower long‑term operating costs.

## The 2‑Year Cost Framework
A structured way to quantify the decision uses the [[concepts/two-year-cost-framework]]:
1. **Resale value** of the current car.
2. **Projected repairs & maintenance** for the next two years.
3. **Purchase price** (including taxes/fees) of the replacement.
4. **Down‑payment** and loan terms (interest rate, monthly payment).
5. **Insurance cost difference** between the two cars.
6. **Fuel cost comparison** based on MPG and annual mileage.
7. **Depreciation** impact for both the current and replacement vehicles, linked to [[concepts/vehicle-depreciation]].

By plugging in these seven numbers, owners can calculate total two‑year out‑flows for each option and see which path is cheaper.

## Practical Steps
1. List the seven data points above (the user in the source document provided a template).
2. Apply realistic assumptions for each (e.g., modest depreciation on an older car, higher insurance on a newer model).
3. Sum the costs for the **Keep** scenario: repairs, maintenance, depreciation, fuel, insurance.
4. Sum the costs for the **Replace** scenario: loan payments, depreciation, higher insurance, fuel, minimal maintenance.
5. Compare the two totals; the lower figure indicates the financially preferable choice.
