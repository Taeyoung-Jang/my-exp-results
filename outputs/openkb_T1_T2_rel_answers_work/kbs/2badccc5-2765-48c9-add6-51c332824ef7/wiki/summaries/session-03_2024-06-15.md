## Summary of session-03_2024-06-15

This conversation walks through how to decide whether to keep a current vehicle or replace it, focusing on a practical **2‑year cost comparison** framework. The assistant outlines the major **signs to keep** a car (reliability, low maintenance costs, paid‑off status) and the typical **push factors for upgrading** (frequent breakdowns, rising repair costs, changing lifestyle needs).  

The user then requests a concrete numeric analysis and provides a list of **seven key data points** needed for the comparison:

1. Expected resale value of the current car.  
2. Estimated 2‑year repair and maintenance costs.  
3. All‑in purchase price of the replacement vehicle (taxes/fees included).  
4. Cash down‑payment amount.  
5. Loan term and interest rate (or monthly payment).  
6. Current vs. replacement insurance costs.  
7. Fuel efficiency (MPG) and annual mileage for both cars.  

The assistant explains how each input feeds into total cost calculations covering **repairs**, [[concepts/vehicle-depreciation]], **loan payments**, **insurance**, **maintenance**, and **fuel**. It also notes the assumptions typically made (e.g., modest depreciation on older cars, higher insurance on newer models). The goal is to produce side‑by‑side 2‑year totals so the user can see which option is cheaper.
