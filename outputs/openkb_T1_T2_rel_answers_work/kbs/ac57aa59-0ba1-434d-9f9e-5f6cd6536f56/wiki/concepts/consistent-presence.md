# Consistent Presence in Friendships

**Definition**: Consistent presence refers to the practice of maintaining a steady, predictable connection with a friend through low‑frequency, low‑pressure interactions and scheduled anchor points.

**Key Elements**
- **Scheduled anchors**: Setting recurring, modest‑frequency activities (e.g., a monthly call, a quarterly meetup) that create predictability and signal ongoing interest. [[concepts/consistent-presence]]
- **Low‑pressure check‑ins**: Brief, context‑driven messages that show you’re thinking of the person without demanding an immediate, deep response. [[concepts/low-pressure-checkins]]
- **Mutual rhythm**: Both parties notice and respond to these cues, fostering a balance where neither feels overwhelmed or neglected. [[concepts/balanced-initiation]]

**Why It Matters**
Consistent presence helps friendships survive life’s ebbs and flows. By providing reliable yet unobtrusive contact, friends feel valued even during busy periods, reducing the risk of gradual disengagement.

**Evidence from Source**
The conversation in [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]] highlights that friendships “stick when both people show up regularly in each other’s lives” and that “predictability matters more than frequency.” It recommends marking calendar anchors and using brief, genuine messages as practical ways to embody consistent presence.

**Practical Tips**
1. **Set a recurring reminder** (e.g., first Thursday of each month) for a short catch‑up call.
2. **Send contextual notes** like “Saw this article and thought of you” to keep the thread alive.
3. **Adjust cadence** if you notice you’re the only one initiating; gently reduce frequency and observe if the friend steps in.
4. **Combine with balanced initiation** by occasionally inviting the friend to suggest the next meeting time.
