# Low‑Pressure Check‑ins

**Definition**: Low‑pressure check‑ins are short, context‑driven messages that maintain a sense of presence in a friendship without requiring a lengthy response or creating social pressure.

**Key Characteristics**
- **Brevity** – A single sentence or phrase that conveys care.
- **Relevance** – Tied to something that reminded you of the friend (e.g., an article, event, or shared interest).
- **No urgency** – Explicitly signals that a reply is not required immediately, keeping the interaction low‑stakes.

**Why It Works**
Low‑pressure check‑ins support the [[concepts/consistent-presence]] pillar of adult friendships by providing regular, lightweight contact. They help avoid the pitfalls of [[concepts/balanced-initiation]] where one party may dominate outreach, offering a gentle way to stay on each other's radar.

**Examples from [[summaries/session-12_2024-06-15]]**
- “Hey, I saw something today that reminded me of you—hope you’re doing okay.”
- “How did that thing at work go? No rush to reply, just thinking of you.”

**Practical Tips**
1. **Anchor to a trigger** – Use an event, article, or memory as a natural reason to reach out.
2. **Add a soft opt‑out** – Phrases like “no rush to reply” signal low pressure.
3. **Keep it occasional** – Pair with other habits such as scheduled monthly calls for [[concepts/consistent-presence]].

**Related Concepts**
- [[concepts/consistent-presence]] – the broader habit of maintaining predictable, low‑frequency contact.
- [[concepts/balanced-initiation]] – ensuring outreach effort is shared.
