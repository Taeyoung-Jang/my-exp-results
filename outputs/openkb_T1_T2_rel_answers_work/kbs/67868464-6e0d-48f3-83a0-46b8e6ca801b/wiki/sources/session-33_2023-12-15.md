**User:** Hey—quick question. I’m registering to vote for the first time and the online portal keeps asking for the number from the letter they mailed after my ceremony—where exactly is that printed? I’ve got the letter in front of me but I’m not sure which number they mean (there are a few different ones on the page).

**Assistant:** It’s usually the number that’s explicitly labeled as a “reference,” “application,” or “case” number on the notice they sent you. On most post‑ceremony letters, it’s printed near the top of the page, often close to your name and the date, and it’s typically the only long, clearly labeled ID number on the letter.

If you see multiple numbers, choose the one that’s actually described on the page as the reference or document number. If nothing is labeled clearly, the safest option is to check the portal’s help text or contact their support, because different jurisdictions format their letters differently.

**User:** On my letter, I see an “A-Number” (starts with A and 8 digits), a “USCIS #,” and then a “Receipt Number” that starts with three letters and a bunch of numbers. The portal just says “enter the number from your naturalization letter.” Which one do they usually want?

**Assistant:** Different offices use these numbers for different things, so there isn’t a single universal answer, but here’s the general rule of thumb:

If the portal really means “the number from the letter we mailed you,” they usually want the receipt number, because that’s the number tied to the specific notice they sent. The A‑Number and the USCIS# both identify you as a person, while the receipt number identifies the case or notice.

That said, voter‑registration systems often want the number from your actual Certificate of Naturalization, not from the USCIS notice. If the portal isn’t mentioning “certificate number,” and is truly asking for something from the letter, the safest move is to check the portal’s help section or contact their support, because different states word this differently.

**User:** I’m in California—this is the CA online voter registration site. It specifically says “Naturalization number (from your naturalization document/letter)” and I’m stuck because my certificate has one number and this USCIS letter has the A-number/USCIS#/receipt number. Which one should I enter for CA?

**Assistant:** For California, the number they want is the one printed on your actual Certificate of Naturalization, not any of the numbers on your USCIS notice. California uses the certificate’s unique “Certificate Number” as the naturalization number for voter registration.

It’s usually on the top right of the certificate and looks different from the A‑Number. The A‑Number, USCIS#, and receipt number from the letter won’t work in the CA voter registration form.

Use the certificate’s own certificate number, and you should be good.

**User:** Okay, that helps—on my certificate I see a “Certificate Number” in the top right, and separately my A-number. The CA form says the naturalization number should be 7–8 digits and my certificate number is longer (it has letters). Should I enter it exactly as printed (including letters), or just the digits?

**Assistant:** Enter the certificate number exactly as it appears on your Certificate of Naturalization, including the letters. California’s form sometimes mentions “7–8 digits,” but the state accepts the full certificate number with its letters. The A‑number isn’t the right one, so just type the certificate number exactly as printed.

**User:** I tried entering the certificate number exactly as printed (letters + digits), but the CA form throws an error saying it must be 7–8 digits. If I remove the letters it accepts it, but I’m worried that’s wrong. What should I do here?

**Assistant:** California’s online form is a bit behind on how certificate numbers are formatted now, so the system often forces you to enter only the digits even though the printed number includes letters. When this happens, the state’s elections offices advise people to enter just the digits so the form will submit.

You aren’t doing anything wrong by leaving the letters off. Your registration will still process because the rest of your information—name, date of naturalization, last four of SSN or ID number—lets them verify you.

If you want absolute confirmation, you can call your county elections office, but in practice, entering only the digits is what people end up doing when the CA form rejects the letters.

**Assistant:** Keep all the digits exactly as they appear, including any leading zeros. The form will accept it, and elections staff can still verify you with the full set of information you provide.
