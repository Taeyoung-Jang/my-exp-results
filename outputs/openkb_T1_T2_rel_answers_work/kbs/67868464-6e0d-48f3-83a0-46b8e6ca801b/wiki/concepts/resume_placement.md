# Resume Placement of Supplemental Information

**Definition**: The practice of locating brief, optional lines—such as work‑authorization status—on a resume so they remain visible to recruiters without detracting from core qualifications.

## Core Guidance from [[summaries/session-13_2022-06-15]]
- **Bottom‑section placement** – Insert the line in an **Additional Information** or **Work Authorization** section placed after the **Skills** and **Education** blocks. This keeps the focus on experience and achievements while still surfacing the detail.
- **One‑page constraint** – On a single‑page resume, use a concise sentence (e.g., “U.S. permanent resident, fully authorized to work without sponsorship”) to fit within the limited space.
- **Avoid headline and summary** – Do not embed immigration status in the headline or main "About Me" summary; those areas should highlight professional branding rather than legal status.

## Rationale
- Recruiters scan headlines and experience first; placing supplemental info at the bottom ensures it is seen after the primary content.
- Highlights eligibility early enough for compliance checks without cluttering the narrative of the candidate’s skills.

## Practical Example
```
Additional Information
   • U.S. permanent resident, fully authorized to work without sponsorship
```

This format follows the guidance from the source session and can be adapted for other brief, supplemental statements (e.g., language proficiency, security clearance).
