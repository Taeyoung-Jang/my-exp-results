# Immigration Status

**Scope** – This concept covers how to correctly state a non‑citizen, permanent resident (green‑card) status in résumés, LinkedIn profiles, and job application forms, ensuring clarity for recruiters and compliance with legal terminology.

---

## Overview

A *permanent resident* in the **[[entities/united-states]]** is legally authorized to work without needing visa sponsorship. The proper way to convey this status is through concise, professional wording that distinguishes it from citizenship while highlighting work eligibility.

---

## Recommended Phrasing

- Short: “U.S. permanent resident, fully authorized to work without sponsorship.”
- Slightly longer: “I am a U.S. lawful permanent resident and do not require visa sponsorship for employment.”
- Alternative: “Authorized to work in the United States as a U.S. permanent resident.”

These phrasings align with the guidance in **[[summaries/session-13_2022-06-15]]** and can be reused across multiple application contexts.

---

## Placement in Job‑Search Documents

### Résumé

- Do **not** place the status in the headline or main summary.
- Add a dedicated line in the **Additional Information** or **Work Authorization** section at the bottom of a one‑page résumé, typically after the skills or education block. This follows the best practices outlined in **[[concepts/resume_placement]]**.

### LinkedIn

- Insert the sentence at the end of the **About** section (the profile’s summary), not in the headline. This keeps the information visible to recruiters while maintaining a professional profile focus.

---

## Application Forms & Dropdowns

When an application presents a dropdown such as “Citizen / Permanent Resident / Visa,” select **Permanent Resident**. Permanent residents do not need sponsorship, so answer “No” to any sponsorship‑required questions unless the form asks for additional detail.

---

## Terminology Choices

- Preferred: “U.S. permanent resident” or “lawful permanent resident.”
- Acceptable but informal: “green card holder” (refer to the entity **[[entities/green-card]]** for the formal term). Use the formal phrasing in professional documents to maintain a polished tone.
