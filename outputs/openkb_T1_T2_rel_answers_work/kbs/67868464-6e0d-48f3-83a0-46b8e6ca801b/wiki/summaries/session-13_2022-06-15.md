# Summary of session-13_2022-06-15

**Topic:** How to accurately and discreetly present permanent resident (non‑citizen) status on resumes, LinkedIn profiles, and job application forms for U.S. software engineering roles.

## Key Points

- **Immigration/Citizenship wording** – Use a clear one‑liner such as:
  - “U.S. permanent resident, fully authorized to work without sponsorship.”
  - Alternative phrasing: “Authorized to work in the United States as a U.S. permanent resident.”
- **Placement on resume** – Keep the status out of the headline and main summary. Add a short line in the **Additional Information** or **Work Authorization** section at the bottom of a one‑page resume, after skills or education. See [[concepts/resume_placement]].
- **LinkedIn positioning** – Insert the sentence at the end of the **About** section (not the headline) so recruiters see it without it dominating the profile. Refer to [[entities/linkedin]].
- **Application dropdowns** – When presented with options like “Citizen / Permanent Resident / Visa,” select **Permanent Resident**. Permanent residents do not need visa sponsorship, so answer “No” to sponsorship questions unless explicitly asked otherwise. This relates to [[concepts/work_authorization]].
- **Terminology choice** – Prefer “U.S. permanent resident” or “lawful permanent resident” for a professional tone. “Green card holder” is accurate but considered informal for formal documents; see [[entities/green-card]].
- **Wording variations** – Provide short vs. longer options for a resume line:
  - Short: “Lawful permanent resident, fully authorized to work in the U.S.”
  - Longer: “I am a U.S. lawful permanent resident and do not require visa sponsorship for employment.”
