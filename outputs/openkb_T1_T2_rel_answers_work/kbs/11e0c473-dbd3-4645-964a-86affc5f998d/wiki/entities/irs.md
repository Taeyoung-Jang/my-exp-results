# Internal Revenue Service (IRS)

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) is the United States federal agency responsible for tax collection and tax law enforcement.  In the context of moving, the IRS is mentioned as one of the government entities whose address may need to be updated to ensure tax correspondence reaches the taxpayer.

## Key Facts from [[summaries/session-32_2025-02-15]]
- **Address update timing**: The checklist suggests updating the IRS address *after settling* unless an urgent change is required.  This can be done by filing Form 8822 (Change of Address) with the agency.
- **Relation to other address tasks**: The IRS update is part of the broader [[concepts/address-update-workflow]] that includes USPS forwarding, DMV registration, and voter registration.
- **Placement in the moving timeline**: It falls under the "After you’re settled" phase of the [[concepts/moving-checklist-timeline]], after more immediate billing and shipping address changes.

## Related Entities and Concepts
- **[[entities/usps]]** – Handles mail forwarding, which can serve as a safety net while the IRS address is being processed.
- **[[entities/dmv]]** – Another government agency requiring a timely address change during a move.
- **[[concepts/address-update-workflow]]** – The overall process of updating home, billing, and shipping addresses across services.
- **[[concepts/moving-checklist-timeline]]** – The timeline that categorizes when to address each update, including the IRS.

## Practical Tips
- Submit Form 8822 online or by mail as soon as you have a confirmed new residence.
- Keep a copy of the confirmation for your records and to reference in case of any correspondence delays.
- Verify that the IRS has the correct address by checking your online account or contacting the agency after a few weeks.
