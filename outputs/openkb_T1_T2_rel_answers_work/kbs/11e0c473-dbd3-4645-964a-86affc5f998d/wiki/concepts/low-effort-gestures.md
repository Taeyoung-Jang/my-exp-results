# Low‑Effort Gestures

**Definition**: Small, easy-to‑perform actions that help neighbors feel cared for and connected while respecting personal privacy.

## Core Idea
Low‑effort gestures are a key component of a healthy [[concepts/neighborhood-vibe]]. They enable [[concepts/casual-neighbor-interaction]] by providing natural, low‑stakes opportunities for contact.

## Typical Examples (from [[summaries/session-04_2024-06-15]])
- **Sharing excess produce or herbs** – offering a handful of home‑grown vegetables when you have more than you need.
- **Baking and gifting treats** – leaving a small batch of cookies or bread for a neighbor.
- **Offering tools or small favors** – lending a ladder, screwdriver, or helping with a quick fix.
- **Brief check‑in messages** – a short text after a storm: “Hey, just making sure you weathered that okay. Need anything?”
- **Simple greetings tied to chores** – waving while taking out the trash or grabbing mail.

## Why They Work
1. **Low Commitment** – Requires little time or planning, so neighbors can participate without feeling obligated.
2. **Contextual Relevance** – Tied to everyday activities (cooking, gardening, chores), making the gesture feel natural.
3. **Trust Building** – Repeated small acts signal reliability and care, strengthening the sense of community.
4. **Privacy‑Respectful** – Provides a way to show concern without demanding personal details or prolonged conversation.

## Practical Tips for Implementing
- Keep the gesture **brief** and **purpose‑driven** (e.g., “I have extra basil, want some?”).
- Align gestures with **shared spaces** or **common routines** (e.g., leaving a note on the community bulletin board).
- Vary the gestures to match the **level of closeness**: more personal for close neighbors, very brief for those who value privacy.
