**Assistant:** A simple way to get through it in two to three weeks is to divide it by Parts rather than exact page counts. If you read roughly half a Part per day, you’ll fall into a rhythm that keeps the plot fresh without feeling rushed. Most editions make that something like 25–35 pages a day, which is enough to stay engaged but not enough to feel punishing.

A natural checkpoint is the end of Part One. If the mood, the tension, and Raskolnikov’s unraveling haven’t started to hook you by then, the book may not click right now. If they have, the rest unfolds more quickly than you’d expect.

Reading *Rebecca* alongside it won’t ruin the momentum. In fact, having something atmospheric and easy to sink into can make the heavier stretches of *Crime and Punishment* feel less daunting. Just make sure you treat *Crime and Punishment* as the main track and dip into *Rebecca* when you want a breather rather than alternating mechanically chapter for chapter.

**User:** Could you map that plan a bit more concretely by Parts/chapters over 21 days (I don’t need exact page counts since editions vary)? And what’s a good “minimum” daily reading to keep the thread of the story without burning out?

**Assistant:** You can treat the 21 days as three loose “weeks,” each tied to a couple of Parts. The novel has six Parts plus the Epilogue, and they’re uneven, so it works better to cluster them rather than force perfect daily quotas.

A simple rhythm is this: spend the first seven days on Parts One and Two, the next seven on Parts Three and Four, and the final seven on Parts Five, Six, and the short Epilogue. If you follow that, you’ll usually read a chapter or two per day, depending on chapter length. Most people find that one solid chapter a day is the minimum needed to keep the thread of the story; two chapters a day is ideal because the plot tends to move in pairs of beats.

If you want a clear day-by-day sense of where you should be: aim to finish Part One by day three or four, Part Two by day seven, Part Three by around day ten or eleven, Part Four by day fourteen, Part Five by day seventeen or eighteen, and then wrap up Part Six and the Epilogue in the last few days. Nothing here needs to be exact, but this pacing keeps the narrative alive and gives you space to absorb the heavier sections without losing momentum.

As long as you hit roughly one chapter per day, you won’t feel like you’re starting over every time you sit down. Two chapters is the sweet spot if you want the book to carry you along.
