# Checkpoint Evaluation

**Definition** – A *checkpoint evaluation* is a brief, purposeful pause at a natural narrative milestone (e.g., the end of a Part or major chapter) to gauge whether the reader is still engaged and should continue the work.

## Why It Matters

- Provides a low‑stakes way to decide if a book is “clicking” without committing to a full read.
- Helps maintain momentum by preventing prolonged slog on material that isn’t resonating.
- Works well with structured [[concepts/reading_plan]]s that break a work into manageable segments.

## How It Was Applied in Session‑05

In the conversation on **[[entities/crime_and_punishment]]**, the assistant suggested using the end of *Part One* as the primary checkpoint. The user was advised to assess whether the tension around Raskolnikov’s arc had begun to hook them. If not, they could reconsider continuing; if yes, the rest of the novel tends to accelerate.

## Practical Steps

1. **Identify a natural break** – a Part, a major chapter, or a clear plot turning point.
2. **Set a short evaluation window** – ask yourself if the story’s stakes, tone, and pacing feel compelling.
3. **Decide** – continue, adjust reading speed, or pause for a different book.
