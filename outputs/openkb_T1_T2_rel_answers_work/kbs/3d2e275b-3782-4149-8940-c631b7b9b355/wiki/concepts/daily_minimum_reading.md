# Daily Minimum Reading

**Definition**: The smallest amount of reading (or equivalent audio listening) a reader should do each day to keep the story thread alive while avoiding fatigue.

## Core Idea
In the context of classic literature, especially when tackling dense works like *Crime and Punishment*, a daily minimum ensures the reader stays engaged with the plot and characters. The source conversation recommends **one chapter per day** as the absolute minimum, with **two chapters per day** being the optimal target for steady progress.

When the medium shifts to audiobooks, the same principle applies: a short, consistent listening chunk (e.g., 20–30 minutes on weeknights) serves as the daily minimum. This time‑based minimum aligns with the [[concepts/chunked_listening_schedule]] and helps maintain continuity without overwhelming the listener.

## Why It Matters
- **Narrative Continuity**: Reading or listening at least a chapter (or a 20‑minute audio segment) each day prevents the feeling of starting over each session, preserving momentum.
- **Burnout Prevention**: A modest, consistent chunk avoids the exhaustion that can come from long marathon sessions, whether on page or via speaker.
- **Checkpoint Alignment**: The daily minimum supports natural evaluation points, such as the end of Part 1 in *Crime and Punishment*, which serves as a checkpoint to decide if the book is clicking. This mirrors the [[concepts/checkpoint_evaluation]] used for audiobooks when deciding whether to stay with the main title or switch to a backup.
- **Narrator Impact**: Choosing a narrator who makes the material feel effortless reduces cognitive load, making it easier to meet the daily minimum for audiobooks [[concepts/narrator_impact]].

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Integrated into a broader reading plan that structures a 21‑day schedule across the novel’s parts (plain text, not a wikilink).
- Complements balanced reading habits by allowing a lighter companion book (e.g., *Rebecca*) without derailing the main track (plain text).
- Aligns with [[concepts/reading_intentionality]] by encouraging purposeful, distraction‑free daily reading or listening habits.
- Works hand‑in‑hand with [[concepts/audiobook_habit_formation]] and [[concepts/main_backup_pairing]]: the main book gets the daily minimum chunk, while a backup provides a flexible alternative on low‑energy evenings.

## Practical Example
From the session‑05 discussion, the suggested plan breaks the novel into three weekly blocks. By committing to at least one chapter each day, the reader meets the daily minimum, reaches the Part 1 checkpoint by day 3‑4, and can comfortably increase to two chapters per day for faster progress.

Applying the same logic to audiobooks (session‑36), a listener might:
1. **Weeknights (20‑30 min)**: Treat this as a "daily minimum" for the main audiobook (e.g., *The Seven Husbands of Evelyn Hugo*). Listen to a single chapter or a fixed time segment.
2. **Weekend (1‑2 h+)**: Use the longer block to dive deeper, covering multiple chapters and building noticeable momentum.
3. **Backup Use**: When the main feels too taxing, switch to a backup title (e.g., *Born a Crime*) while still honoring the daily minimum time commitment.

This blended approach ensures steady progress across both reading and listening mediums, preventing drift and supporting completion.
