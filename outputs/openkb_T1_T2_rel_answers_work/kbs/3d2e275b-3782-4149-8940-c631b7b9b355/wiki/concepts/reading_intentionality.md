# Reading Intentionality

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** Focused, purposeful selection and scheduling of reading activities to match personal intentions, mood, and desired outcomes.

---

## Overview
Reading intentionality is the practice of approaching books with clear objectives rather than accidental or habit‑driven consumption. It involves:
- Defining what you want to get out of a reading session (e.g., entertainment, insight, mood regulation).
- Choosing titles that fit those goals, often using criteria such as timeless relevance, readability, and emotional resonance.
- Structuring a schedule that sustains engagement without burnout.

The concept was highlighted in [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]], where the user sought “timeless but entertaining” classics and a concrete plan for *Crime and Punishment*.

---

## Key Elements

1. **Goal Clarification** – Identifying whether the reading aim is comfort, challenge, escapism, or intellectual growth.
2. **Curated Selection** – Picking works that satisfy the goal, as demonstrated by the recommendation of classics like *Rebecca* and *A Tree Grows in Brooklyn* for timeless entertainment.
3. **Reading Plan Design** – Translating goals into a concrete schedule, linked to [[concepts/reading_plan]], ensuring regular progress.
4. **Translation Choice** – Selecting a version that supports the intended experience, connected to [[concepts/translation_selection]] (e.g., the Michael Katz translation for smoother reading).
5. **Balancing Materials** – Pairing heavier texts with lighter ones to maintain momentum, tied to [[concepts/balanced_reading]].
6. **Minimum Daily Commitment** – Establishing a baseline to keep the narrative thread alive, referenced by [[concepts/daily_minimum_reading]].
7. **Checkpoint Evaluation** – Using logical milestones (e.g., end of Part 1) to assess whether the reading continues to meet the original intent, linked to [[concepts/checkpoint_evaluation]].
