# Sleep Hygiene

**Definition**: A set of routine habits and environmental adjustments that support the body’s natural sleep‑wake cycle, making it easier to fall asleep and stay asleep.

## Core Elements
1. **Consistent bedtime** – Going to sleep around the same time each night (e.g., 10:30 PM) stabilises the internal clock.
2. **Wind‑down window** – Starting a calming routine about 30 minutes before bed, as outlined in [[summaries/session-07_2025-06-15]].
3. **Soft lighting** – Dimming lights an hour before sleep signals the brain that night is approaching, reducing alertness.
4. **Screen avoidance** – Replacing electronic devices with low‑stimulation activities prevents blue‑light disruption.
5. **Mind‑offload** – Brief journaling or a body‑scan helps clear mental chatter.
6. **Physical relaxation** – Gentle stretching, warm showers, or self‑massage eases muscular tension.
7. **Quiet activity** – Engaging in non‑screen, low‑arousal tasks such as calm reading or listening to soft instrumental music (see [[concepts/quiet-activity]]).
8. **Shifted sleep window** – For evening or night shift workers, protect a regular sleep period even if it falls later (e.g., 2 a.m.–10 a.m.) and treat it as non‑negotiable [[concepts/shifted-sleep-window]].
9. **Evening wind‑down routine** – After a late shift, use a short (15‑30 minute) unwind ritual (change, light snack, dim lights) to transition smoothly to sleep [[concepts/wind-down-routine]].
10. **Last‑bus buffer** – Stop non‑essential closing tasks ~25 minutes before the final bus to ensure a calm, timely commute and reduce bedtime stress [[concepts/last-bus-buffer]].
11. **Commute safety checklist** – Simple pre‑departure checks (phone charged, someone notified, keys ready, well‑lit route) lower anxiety, supporting better sleep [[concepts/commute-safety-checklist]].
12. **Meal timing for night shift** – Align meals (late breakfast, mid‑afternoon main meal, light pre‑work dinner) to sustain energy and avoid late‑night heaviness, which can impair sleep [[concepts/meal-timing-night-shift]].
13. **Social synchronization** – Schedule occasional daytime touchpoints (e.g., late‑morning coffee) to stay connected and maintain mental wellbeing, aiding sleep quality [[concepts/social-sync]].

## How it appears in the source documents
- The session‑07 conversation recommends three 20‑30 minute wind‑down templates that embody sleep‑hygiene principles:
  - **Regular start time (10:00 PM)** and consistent lights‑out (10:30 PM) create a predictable schedule.
  - **Lighting adjustments** and **screen‑free activities** reduce physiological arousal.
  - **Journaling, breathing exercises, and stretching** target both mental and physical sources of sleeplessness.
  - Each template ends with a **quiet activity** that is deliberately low‑stimulus, reinforcing the overall hygiene strategy.
- The session‑31 discussion expands hygiene to night‑shift contexts, adding:
  - The importance of a **shifted sleep window** that stays constant even when bedtime moves to the early morning hours.
  - A concise **wind‑down routine** after a late commute to protect the sleep window.
  - A **last‑bus buffer** strategy to prevent rushed departures and stress.
  - A **commute safety checklist** as part of the pre‑sleep routine to reduce anxiety.
  - Guidance on **meal timing for night shifts** and **social sync** activities that keep the sleeper’s circadian rhythm anchored.
