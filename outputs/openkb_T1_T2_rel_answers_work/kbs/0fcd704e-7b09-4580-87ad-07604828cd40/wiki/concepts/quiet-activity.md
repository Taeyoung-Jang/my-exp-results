## Quiet Activity

**Quiet activity** refers to any low‑stimulus, non‑screen pursuit performed during the evening wind‑down period to help the mind and body transition toward sleep. The goal is to maintain relaxation without introducing new mental or sensory arousal that could delay sleep onset.

### Core characteristics
- **Non‑screen**: Avoids blue‑light emission and interactive content that can keep the brain alert.
- **Low‑arousal**: Engages the senses gently (e.g., soft auditory input, tactile comfort) without demanding intense focus.
- **Brief**: Typically 5–15 minutes, fitting within a 20–30 minute wind‑down window.

### Examples from [[summaries/session-07_2025-06-15]]
- Reading a calm, low‑plot book
- Listening to soft instrumental or ambient music
- Performing a silent body‑scan meditation
- Holding a warm mug of herbal tea while breathing slowly

These activities are deliberately chosen to complement other components of a bedtime routine, such as soft lighting, slow breathing, and gentle stretching. When paired with a consistent [[concepts/wind-down-window]] and broader [[concepts/sleep-hygiene]] practices, quiet activities help signal the nervous system that it is time to unwind, facilitating a smoother transition to sleep.
