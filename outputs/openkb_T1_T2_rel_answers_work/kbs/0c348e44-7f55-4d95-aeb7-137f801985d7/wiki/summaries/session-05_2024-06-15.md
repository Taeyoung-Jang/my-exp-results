# Summary of session-05_2024-06-15

## Main Points
- **Pros of streaming:** instant access, device‑agnostic convenience, discovery opportunities.  
- **Cons of streaming:** lack of true ownership, catalog volatility, subscription cost creep, loss of tangible collection value.  
- **Physical media (Blu‑ray/CD) remains the most reliable form of ownership** because it is not tied to licenses or servers.  
- **Digital downloads** are valuable only when they are DRM‑free (e.g., [[entities/bandcamp]], niche film stores); most mainstream purchases are merely licensed.  
- **Libraries** provide a low‑cost, reliable supplement for items you don’t need to own permanently.

## Balancing Convenience with Ownership
- Keep buying Blu‑rays and CDs, then **rip** them for personal use to enable streaming‑like access at home.  
- Use **DRM‑free digital purchases** sparingly to fill gaps without resorting to subscriptions.  
- Ensure legal compliance when ripping for private use.  

## Practical Ripping & Serving Setup
1. **Hardware:** low‑power mini‑PC/[[entities/intel_nuc]] or a NAS; external Blu‑ray drive.  
2. **Software:**  
   - **Ripping:** [[entities/makemkv]] (Blu‑rays) + [[entities/handbrake]] (encoding).  
   - **Audio:** any standard CD‑ripper.  
   - **Media Server:** [[entities/plex]] or [[entities/jellyfin]] to stream to TVs, phones, tablets.  
3. **Backup Plan:**  
   - Primary library on the media box/NAS.  
   - Mirror to a rotating external hard‑drive (plugged in periodically).  
   - Optional second backup stored in a different location in the home.  
   - Refresh drives every few years and verify integrity to avoid bit‑rot.

## Pitfalls & Best Practices
- Follow local copyright law regarding personal ripping.  
- Keep at least **two independent backups** and store them separately.  
- Periodically **migrate data** to newer storage media.  
- Monitor drive health; replace failing drives promptly.
