# iPhone (product)

The iPhone is a line of smartphones produced by Apple, widely used for everyday tasks including photography. In the context of [[summaries/session-36_2025-06-15]], the iPhone serves as the **capture tool** for photographing items during a decluttering and downsizing process.

## Role in the Decluttering System
- **Capture phase**: Users take photos of items they keep with the iPhone's built‑in camera, leveraging its convenience for quick, on‑the‑spot documentation.
- **Digital workflow**: After shooting, the images are **uploaded to [[entities/google_drive]]**, which acts as the single source of truth for the digital home inventory. The iPhone’s photo library is then cleared to avoid duplicate storage.
- **Support for [[concepts/digital_inventory_management]]**: This approach aligns with the digital inventory management concept by keeping a lean, centralized repository of item photos, preventing photo clutter across devices.

## Interaction with Other Concepts
- The iPhone’s use embodies the **“one in, one gone before it enters”** principle (see [[concepts/one_in_one_out_rule]]) by providing a rapid visual record that helps decide what stays and what is removed.
- It fits into the broader **[[concepts/decluttering_loop]]**, where noticing, choosing, and resetting are supported by quick visual documentation.

## Practical Tips from the Session
1. **Snap only what you keep** – avoid photographing items destined for donation.
2. **Weekly transfer** – move new photos from the iPhone to the designated Google Drive folder each week.
3. **Immediate deletion** – delete transferred images from the iPhone to keep the device uncluttered.

By integrating the iPhone into a disciplined photo‑management routine, users can maintain minimal digital overhead while supporting their physical decluttering goals.
