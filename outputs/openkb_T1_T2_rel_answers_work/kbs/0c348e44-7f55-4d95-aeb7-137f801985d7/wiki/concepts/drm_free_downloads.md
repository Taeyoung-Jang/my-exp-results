# DRM‑Free Downloads

**Definition**: A DRM‑free download is a digital file that is sold without any digital rights management restrictions, meaning the buyer can keep, copy, and use the file indefinitely without needing a license server or subscription.

## Why It Matters
- Provides the **convenience** of digital access while preserving true **ownership** of the media.
- Avoids the pitfalls of streaming services where catalogs can disappear or licenses can be revoked.
- Enables users to back up files and integrate them into personal media libraries, such as a home‑served collection built with [[concepts/disc_ripping]].

## Sources and Examples
- The conversation in [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]] highlights **Bandcamp** as a reputable platform offering DRM‑free music downloads, allowing collectors to keep files forever.
- Niche film distributors also sell DRM‑free video files that can be stored locally and served via media‑server software like [[entities/plex]] or [[entities/jellyfin]].

## Practical Use Cases
1. **Supplementing Physical Media** – Purchase a DRM‑free version of a title you already own on Blu‑ray to have a digital copy for on‑the‑go streaming.
2. **Building a Personal Library** – Store DRM‑free files alongside ripped media, then serve them through a home server for seamless playback on TVs, phones, and tablets.
3. **Long‑Term Preservation** – Keep multiple backups of DRM‑free files, ensuring access even if the original vendor ceases operations.

## Considerations & Pitfalls
- Verify that the download is truly DRM‑free; some services market “download” options that are still tied to a user account.
- Maintain a backup strategy (e.g., rotating external drives) to protect against hardware failure or bit‑rot.
- Be aware of local copyright laws regarding personal use and copying.
