# Decluttering Loop

**Definition**: A repeatable habit cycle that helps maintain a minimalist environment by continuously cycling through three stages – **notice**, **choose**, and **reset**.

---

## Core Stages

1. **Notice** – Actively observe the current state of your belongings. Identify items that feel out‑of‑place, unused, or are contributing to visual clutter.
2. **Choose** – Make a concrete decision for each observed item: keep it (and assign a clear home), donate it, or discard it. The decision is guided by whether the item is used regularly or adds genuine value.
3. **Reset** – Perform a short, scheduled sweep (often 2‑3 minutes at the end of the day) to put items back in their designated places and to ensure that new items have not slipped back in unnoticed.

---

## How It Was Applied in Session‑36 (2025‑06‑15)

- The assistant framed decluttering as a **loop rather than a one‑time purge**, encouraging the user to treat it as an ongoing process.
- The user was advised to work through **small categories** (e.g., jackets, mugs) one at a time, applying the notice‑choose‑reset cycle to each item.
- A **daily 2‑3 minute reset** was recommended to stop the “slow creep into chaos” that often follows ad‑hoc decluttering.
- The loop supports the **"one in, one gone before it enters"** rule by ensuring that the *choose* step always involves a clear swap decision before a new item is allowed in.

## Benefits

- **Sustainable minimalism** – By making decluttering a habit rather than a one‑off event, users avoid relapse.
- **Reduced decision fatigue** – The loop’s structure provides a repeatable decision‑making framework.
- **Scalability** – Works for both physical items and digital records (photos, inventories).
