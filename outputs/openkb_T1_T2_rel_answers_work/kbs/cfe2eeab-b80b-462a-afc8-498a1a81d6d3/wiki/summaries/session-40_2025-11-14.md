# Summary of Session 40 (2025-11-14)

## Key Beginner Mistakes in Bouldering bouldering beginner mistakes
- Over‑relying on arm strength; neglecting body positioning and leg work.
- Rushing to higher grades instead of repeating easy problems for fluidity.
- Hanging with straight, locked‑out fingers, causing tendon strain.  A relaxed, slightly curved grip is safer.

## Simple Grip‑Strength & Confidence Routine grip strength routine
1. **Easy Traverse**: 2‑3 times a week, low‑angle wall, focus on technique.
2. **Post‑climb Hangs**: 5–10 seconds per hang, 2‑3 sets, plenty of rest. Use a pull‑up bar or a big jug hold.
3. **Home Exercise**: Squeeze a soft stress ball for brief forearm activation.
- Emphasize consistency, keep load below discomfort threshold.

## Distinguishing Normal Pump from Tendon Discomfort forearm pump vs tendon pain
- **Normal Pump**: Swelling, heat, dull tightness; gradual onset; fades after a few minutes of rest.
- **Problem Discomfort**: Sharp, localized pain on finger sides or joints; “pinchy” or “zingy” sensations; stop immediately.

## Recommended Hanging Frequency training frequency
- **Initial Phase**: 2‑3 hang days per week on non‑climbing days.
- **When Climbing 2×/week**: Add one hang day between sessions (total 3 finger‑load days).
- Skip hangs on climbing days unless the session was very light.

## Progression Timeline progression plan
- Stay in the “super‑easy” hang zone for **3‑4 weeks** until:
  - No lingering joint tightness.
  - Hangs feel trivially easy.
- Then gradually increase hang duration (add seconds) or add a set.
- Aim for slow, steady gains to avoid finger tweaks and maintain climbing time.

## Practical Takeaways
- Prioritize footwork and relaxed grip over brute arm power.
- Use short, comfortable hangs (5‑10 s) on easy holds.
- Monitor pain type to prevent tendon injuries.
- Limit hang training to 2‑3 days weekly, complementing 2 climbing sessions.
- Remain in the easy phase for at least a month before progressing.
