# Neutral wrist posture

Neutral wrist posture means keeping the wrists in a straight, relaxed line with the forearms, avoiding any upward or inward bend. This alignment reduces strain on tendons, ligaments, and nerves, which is especially important for people recovering from injuries or who spend many hours typing, using a mouse, or handling devices.

## Key elements
- **Forearm‑to‑wrist alignment**: Sit so that your elbows stay close to your body and your forearms are parallel to the floor. The wrists should hover just above the keyboard or mouse surface rather than resting heavily on it.
- **Floating wrists**: Allow the wrists to “float” in the air, using a light touch only when needed. If a palm rest is used, it should be engaged only during brief pauses, not while actively typing.
- **Elbow positioning**: Keep elbows at roughly a 90‑degree angle; this naturally positions the wrists in a neutral line.
- **Desk height**: Adjust desk or keyboard height so that shoulders stay relaxed and the forearms do not have to lift or drop to reach the work surface.

## Why it matters
Maintaining neutral wrist posture helps prevent:
- Gradual forearm ache and stiffness
- Tingling or numbness in the fingers
- Overuse injuries such as carpal tunnel syndrome or ligament strain

## Practical tips from [[summaries/session-07_2025-07-15]]
- Take frequent [[concepts/micro-breaks]] (every 15‑20 minutes) to shake out the hands and reset posture.
- Use ergonomic input devices (e.g., a split or low‑profile keyboard) that encourage straight wrist alignment.
- When using a trackball, keep the hand open and relaxed, which supports neutral wrist posture and ties into [[concepts/trackball_ergonomics]].
