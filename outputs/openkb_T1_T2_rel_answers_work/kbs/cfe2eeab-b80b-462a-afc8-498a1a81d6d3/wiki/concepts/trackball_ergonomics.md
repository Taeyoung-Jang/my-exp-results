# Trackball ergonomics for low‑grip interaction

**Definition**: Design and use of a trackball that allows the hand to rest open, minimizing wrist flexion, thumb pressure, and overall grip strength while maintaining precise cursor control.

## Why a trackball?
- For users recovering from wrist injuries or with ligament issues, a trackball reduces the need to grip or rotate an entire mouse, keeping the wrist in a neutral position. This aligns with [[concepts/wrist_neutrality]].
- The hand can stay relaxed on the device, and only the fingers (or thumb) move a small ball, which lowers muscular load.

## Preferred design features
| Feature | ergonomic benefit |
|---|---|
| **Finger‑ball layout** | Allows the index or middle finger to roll the ball while the palm rests flat, avoiding thumb tension. |
| **Broad, supportive housing** | Provides a stable platform for the hand, reducing the urge to clamp down. |
| **Smooth, low‑resistance ball** | Prevents the need for extra force to move the cursor, decreasing grip strength. |
| **Large, easy‑click buttons** | Reduces finger strain when clicking; stiff buttons can provoke clenching. |

## Configuration tips
1. **Pointer speed**: Start with a moderate speed (Windows ~5/11, macOS ~4‑5) to avoid overshooting, then fine‑tune. This balances effort and precision without forcing rapid, forceful finger strokes. See [[summaries/session-07_2025-07-15]].
2. **Acceleration**: Turn off or lower acceleration; it creates unpredictable jumps that can make users over‑compensate with extra grip.
3. **Cleaning**: Keep the ball and bearings clean to maintain low resistance; a gritty ball increases the force needed to move it.
4. **Micro‑breaks**: Incorporate brief hand‑shake or finger‑stretch pauses every 15‑20 minutes to reset tension. [[concepts/micro-breaks]]

## Red‑flag signs of poor ergonomics
- Tingling, aching, or fatigue in the fingers, palm, or forearm after short periods of use.
- Unintended clenching or tension when trying to move the ball.
- Need to constantly “re‑center” the cursor, indicating speed or acceleration is mis‑set.

If any of these appear, re‑evaluate the device fit, ball resistance, or pointer settings.
