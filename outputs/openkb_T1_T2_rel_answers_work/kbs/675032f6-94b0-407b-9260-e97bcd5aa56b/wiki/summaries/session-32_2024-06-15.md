## Summary  
The document provides concrete, low‑budget recommendations for making community‑center announcements, flyers, signage, and group activities more inclusive across ages, languages, and accessibility needs. It includes a reusable flyer template, a bilingual example (Roman‑letter script first), and volunteer speaking tips to improve live caption accuracy.

## Key Concepts  
- **Inclusive communication** – using high‑contrast visuals, plain language, and universal icons [[concepts/inclusive-communication]].  
- **Low‑budget accessibility** – leveraging existing tools (phone recordings, free design software) to enhance reach without cost [[concepts/low-budget-accessibility]].  
- **High‑contrast design** – large text (36‑48 pt for titles, 24‑28 pt for details) on light backgrounds for readability.  
- **Plain language** – short sentences, consistent phrasing, and clear pauses to aid captions and translation [[concepts/plain-language]].  
- **Bilingual announcements** – presenting a Roman‑letter version first, followed by English, with simple translations.  
- **Live captions & translation‑friendly formatting** – structuring scripts for optimal automatic captioning and machine translation.  
- **Volunteer speaking tips** – steady pace, brief pauses, avoid overlapping speech, and articulate tricky words for cleaner captions.  
- **Template design** – a copy‑ready flyer layout and spoken script that can be adapted to any event.  
- **Signage best practices** – uncluttered, eye‑level placement, arrows, and universal symbols for wayfinding.

## Practical Templates  
1. **Flyer template** (title 36‑48 pt, details 24‑28 pt, high contrast, white space).  
2. **Spoken script** – 10‑15 seconds, short sentences, pause between each line.  
3. **Bilingual example** – Roman‑letter version (e.g., Spanish in Roman script) placed first and larger, followed by English, ending with a plain‑English summary line.

## Volunteer Speaking Tips  
- Speak in short, clear sentences.  
- Pause 0.5‑1 second between sentences.  
- Face the microphone; avoid turning away.  
- Maintain a steady, moderate pace.  
- If a name or term is uncommon, say it slightly slower.  
- Coordinate turn‑taking; avoid overlapping speech.

## Recommendations  
- Adopt the provided flyer and script templates for all events.  
- Solicit feedback from community members to refine signage and announcements.  
- Train volunteers using the speaking tips to improve caption quality.  
- Keep a library of high‑contrast icons and universal symbols for quick reuse.  
- Regularly review and update bilingual text to ensure accuracy and cultural relevance.
