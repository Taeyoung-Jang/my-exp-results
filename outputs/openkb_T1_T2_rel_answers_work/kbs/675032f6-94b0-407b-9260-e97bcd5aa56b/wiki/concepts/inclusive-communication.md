# Inclusive Communication

**Definition**: Inclusive communication is the practice of designing and delivering messages so that people of varying ages, language backgrounds, and accessibility needs can understand and engage with the content.

## Core Principles
- **High‑contrast visual design** – large text (36‑48 pt for titles, 24‑28 pt for details) on a light background for readability.
- **Plain language** – short sentences, consistent phrasing, and clear pauses to aid comprehension and automatic captioning. See [[concepts/plain-language]].
- **Multiple modalities** – combining written notices with audio/video recordings and visual icons.
- **Bilingual presentation** – placing the Roman‑letter version (e.g., Spanish in Roman script) first and larger, followed by English, then a very simple summary line.
- **Universal symbols** – arrows, icons, and color cues that convey meaning without relying on text.

## Low‑Budget Strategies (from [[summaries/session-32_2024-06-15]])
- Use free design tools (Canva, Google Slides) to apply high‑contrast templates.
- Record short announcements on a smartphone and share via community social media or messaging groups.
- Solicit feedback from community members to identify confusing signage and improve it.
- Create a reusable flyer template that includes placeholders for title, date, time, location, and contact info.

## Volunteer Speaking Tips for Cleaner Live Captions
- Speak in short, clear sentences with a 0.5‑1 second pause between them.
- Face the microphone and maintain a steady, moderate pace.
- Avoid overlapping speech; take turns.
- Articulate uncommon names or terms slightly slower.

## Related Concepts

- [[concepts/plain-language]] – using simple, direct language to improve understanding.

## Example Application
A community center implements the inclusive communication framework for a free dinner event:
1. **Flyer**: Roman‑letter Spanish version first, large title (48 pt), high contrast.
2. **Audio**: 10‑15 second script recorded on a phone.
3. **Signage**: Eye‑level, uncluttered signs with arrows and icons.
4. **Volunteer training**: Follow the speaking tips to ensure live captions are accurate.

By adopting these practices, announcements become clearer, more welcoming, and accessible to all community members.
