# Plain Language

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** Using simple, clear phrasing to make communication easier to understand for diverse audiences.

## Overview
Plain language is a communication style that prioritizes clarity, brevity, and readability. It avoids complex vocabulary, long sentences, and jargon, focusing instead on short sentences, consistent phrasing, and logical structure. The goal is to ensure that information is quickly and accurately comprehended by people of varying ages, language backgrounds, and accessibility needs.

## Key Practices
- **Short sentences** – Keep statements concise, typically 15 words or fewer.  
- **Consistent phrasing** – Use the same wording for recurring information (e.g., dates, times, locations).  
- **Clear pauses** – When speaking, pause 0.5‑1 second between sentences to aid live captioning.  
- **Avoid jargon** – Replace technical terms with everyday language unless they are essential and defined.  
- **Logical ordering** – Present details in a predictable sequence: what, when, where, why, how.

## Benefits Highlighted in [[summaries/session-32_2024-06-15]]
- **Improved live captions** – Clear, short sentences and consistent phrasing produce more accurate automatic captions and translations.
- **Better accessibility** – Plain language pairs well with high‑contrast visual design and bilingual announcements, supporting readers with limited literacy or non‑native speakers.
- **Low‑budget implementation** – No extra cost; simply revising wording and pacing yields significant inclusion gains.

## Practical Example from the Source
The session‑32 document provides a flyer and spoken script template that applies plain‑language principles:

**Flyer template** – Title (36‑48 pt), details (24‑28 pt), each item on its own line, high contrast.

**Spoken script** – Ten‑to‑fifteen seconds, short sentences, pause between each line, e.g.,
```
You’re invited to our upcoming event.
It takes place on [date] at [time] at [location].
We’ll be [brief description].
If you need more information, contact us at [phone/email].
We hope to see you there.
```
This script follows plain‑language guidelines, making it easier for speech‑to‑text engines to generate accurate captions.

## Implementation Tips for Community Centers
1. **Draft first, then simplify** – Write the full message, then edit to remove unnecessary words.
2. **Read aloud** – Ensure each sentence sounds natural and includes a pause for captioning.
3. **Test with a captioning tool** – Run a quick recording to see how the text is rendered and adjust as needed.
4. **Create templates** – Save plain‑language flyer and script templates for repeated use.
