**User:** I’ve been thinking about how community centers can make their events and announcements easier for everyone to follow—especially when there’s a mix of ages, languages, and different accessibility needs. If you were advising a community center, what are some practical, low-budget ways to make announcements, signage, and group activities more inclusive and understandable?

**Assistant:** You can make things much clearer without spending much money by focusing on consistency, simplicity, and multiple ways of sharing information.

One helpful step is to use the same visual style on all announcements: large, high‑contrast text, simple language, and clear icons that people recognize at a glance. Posting information in more than one language—even if it’s just a short summary—can make a big difference. When possible, pair written notices with a quick audio or video version recorded on a phone and shared on social media or messaging apps.

For signs inside the building, keeping them uncluttered and placing them at eye level helps people of all ages. Adding arrows, universally understood symbols, and color cues makes directions easier to follow, even for those with limited reading skills.

During group activities, it helps when facilitators explain tasks in plain language, then demonstrate them briefly. Having printed or visual step‑by‑step instructions available can support people who process information differently. Creating a predictable structure—starting each event the same way, using the same visual markers for transitions—also helps everyone feel more comfortable.

And finally, asking community members what they find confusing and letting them suggest improvements usually leads to the most effective, low‑cost changes.

**User:** Could you give a simple “template” we could copy for a typical announcement (like a one-page flyer and a short spoken script), including what to put in the top lines, how big the text should be, and how to format it so live captions and translations work better?

**Assistant:** Here’s a simple, copy‑friendly template you can use for a one‑page flyer and a matching short spoken script. I’ll keep it conversational and straightforward so it works well with captions, screen readers, and quick translation tools.

For the flyer, start with a clear title at the top in very large text—something around 36 to 48 points. Put the key details directly under it in slightly smaller but still large text—around 24 to 28 points. Keep each detail on its own short line: the event name, date, time, location, and one short sentence about what to expect. Use high contrast, like dark text on a light background. Leave plenty of white space so the page doesn’t feel crowded. End with a brief “Who to contact” line and a phone number or email in large, easy‑to‑read text.

A basic flyer might look like this:

Event Title (very large text)  
Date and Time (large text)  
Location (large text)  
One clear sentence about what will happen  
Contact information at the bottom

For the spoken version, keep it to about ten to fifteen seconds so captions stay clean. Speak in short sentences with a small pause between them. A simple script might be: “You’re invited to our upcoming event. It takes place on [date] at [time], at [location]. We’ll be [brief description]. If you need more information, please contact us at [phone or email]. We hope to see you there.”

Using short sentences, consistent phrasing, and clear pauses helps automatic captions and translation apps produce much cleaner results.

Tips for volunteers speaking on a microphone so captions stay clean:  
Use short sentences and pause briefly between them. Avoid talking while turning your head away from the mic. Keep your pace steady rather than fast. If two people are presenting, take turns instead of overlapping. And if a word is unusual or a name is tricky, say it a little slower so the captioning software has a better chance of getting it right.
