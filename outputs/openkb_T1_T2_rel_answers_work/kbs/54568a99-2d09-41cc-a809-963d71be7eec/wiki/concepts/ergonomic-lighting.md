# Ergonomic Lighting

Ergonomic lighting refers to the intentional arrangement of light sources in a workspace so that visual comfort, eye health, and body posture are optimized. In pottery studios, where artists spend long periods at a wheel, the quality, direction, and color temperature of illumination can significantly affect fatigue, headaches, and overall productivity.

## Core Principles
- **Soft, even illumination**: Diffused light that avoids harsh hotspots reduces strain on the eyes. Warm or neutral‑tone bulbs are gentler over multi‑hour sessions, aligning with the findings in [[summaries/session-38_2025-07-22]].
- **Lamp angle and distance**: Position task lamps so the beam skims across the clay rather than shining directly at the artist. Adjusting angle and moving the lamp farther away spreads the light and mitigates glare.
- **Balanced lighting**: Adding a secondary side lamp helps equalize shadows and prevents rapid contrast shifts that can cause dizziness.
- **Color temperature**: Neutral to warm LEDs (around 3000 K) are easier on the eyes, whereas overly cool (bluish) light can feel “sharp” and exacerbate [[concepts/eye-strain]].
- **Micro‑breaks**: Brief pauses to look away from the work surface give eye muscles a rest and are a key ergonomic strategy (see [[concepts/micro-breaks]]). 

## Typical Warning Signs
| Symptom | Likely Lighting Issue |
|---|---|
| Dull forehead ache | Light too bright or too direct |
| Frequent blinking, dry eyes | Glare from reflected light |
| Clay appears overly contrasty or color‑shifted | Inappropriate bulb color temperature |
| Light‑headedness when looking up | Sudden contrast between work area and skylight |

When any of these appear, the first step is a low‑risk adjustment: re‑angle the lamp, shift the stool, or add a gentle side light.

## Safe Adjustments
1. **Re‑angle task lamp** to side‑wash the clay.
2. **Raise or lower lamp head** to broaden the light spread.
3. **Move stool a few inches** to avoid glare hotspots.
4. **Introduce a low‑watt side lamp** for balanced illumination.
5. **Use built‑in diffusers or warm‑tone LEDs** instead of fabric covers (see safety guidance in the source).
