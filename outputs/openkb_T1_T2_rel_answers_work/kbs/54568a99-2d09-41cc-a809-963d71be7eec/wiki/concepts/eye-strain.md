# Eye Strain in Craft Environments

## Definition
Eye strain in craft environments refers to visual fatigue and discomfort that arises from prolonged exposure to studio lighting, glare, contrast shifts, and suboptimal posture while working with hands‑on media such as clay.

## Common Symptoms
- Dull forehead ache or pressure after a session
- Dry eyes, frequent blinking, or a feeling of gritty eyes
- Light‑headedness or slight dizziness when looking up from the wheel
- Perception that the material looks unusually bright or dim
- General tiredness that feels out of proportion to the time spent

## Primary Causes in Studios
- **Lighting intensity and direction** – overly bright or directly pointed task lamps create harsh glare.
- **Glare from wet clay or water buckets** – reflected light increases eye workload.
- **Bulb color temperature** – cool, blue‑heavy light can feel “sharp”; warm or neutral light is gentler.
- **Contrast between skylight and artificial lamps** – sudden shifts force the eyes to constantly re‑adjust.
- **Posture and wheel height** – craning the neck or hunching can compound visual strain.

## Mitigation Strategies
- Implement [[concepts/ergonomic-lighting]]: use soft, even light from side‑angled lamps to reduce direct glare.
- Choose warm‑tone LEDs or bulbs (see [[concepts/warm-lighting-for-migraines]]) to avoid the harshness of cool light.
- Adjust lamp distance or raise the lamp head so the beam spreads more gently across the work surface.
- Add a second low‑watt side lamp to balance shadows without increasing heat.
- Incorporate frequent [[concepts/micro-breaks]]: brief glances away, a quick stretch, or a half‑hour full break to let the eyes relax.
- Avoid covering lamps with fabric or parchment; use built‑in diffusers or lower‑brightness bulbs for safer light softening.

## Warning‑Sign Checklist (from [[summaries/session-38_2025-07-22]])
- **Forehead ache** → light too bright or too direct.
- **Dry, blinking eyes** → glare from wet surfaces.
- **Off‑color clay appearance** → bulb temperature mismatch.
- **Dizziness when looking up** → contrast between skylight and task lamp.
- **Overall fatigue** → cumulative eye strain; increase micro‑break frequency.

## Safety Note
Never drape fabric, parchment, or other materials over a lamp; high‑watt bulbs can overheat and pose a fire risk. Favor lamps with built‑in diffusers or replace bulbs with lower‑intensity, warm‑white LEDs.
