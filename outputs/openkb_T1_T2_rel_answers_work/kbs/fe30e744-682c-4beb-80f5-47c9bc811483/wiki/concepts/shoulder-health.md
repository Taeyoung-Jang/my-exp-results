## Shoulder Health

**Definition**: Shoulder health refers to maintaining functional, pain‑free range of motion and joint stability while performing strength‑training activities.

### Core Elements
1. **Movement Selection** – Choose exercises that keep the shoulder joint within comfortable ranges and avoid overhead positions when they aggravate the tissue. The tendonitis‑friendly plan from [[summaries/session-07_2024-07-15]] emphasizes neutral‑grip presses, low‑angle landmine work, and supported rows to reduce stress on the rotator cuff.
2. **Grip & Angle** – Neutral or slightly underhand grips keep the elbows closer to the body, limiting harmful shear forces. Low‑incline or decline angles further limit shoulder compression.
3. **Volume & Load Management** – Use moderate loads (5‑8 reps for main presses, 6‑10 reps for rows) with sufficient rest (90 s‑2 min). Progression follows a gentle increase in reps before weight, as described in [[concepts/progression-strategies]].
4. **Accessory Work** – Targeted shoulder‑stability drills (cable lateral raises, rear‑deltoid pulls, external rotations) are performed with light loads and controlled tempo to reinforce the surrounding musculature without over‑loading the tendons.
5. **Pain‑First Rules** – Mild, transient discomfort is acceptable, but any sharp or lingering pain requires immediate reduction of range, load, or swapping to an alternative exercise. This aligns with the [[concepts/tendonitis-friendly-training]] approach.

### Practical Guidelines from the Source
- **Day‑by‑Day Structure** – Each workout pairs a main press (neutral‑grip, low‑angle) with a complementary pull and a shoulder‑finisher. Swaps are provided for each movement to accommodate flare‑ups.
- **Progression Strategy** – Add reps within the prescribed range first; once the top is reached for two weeks, increase weight modestly and revert to the lower rep range. During flare‑up weeks, halve the load and slow tempo, then resume normal progression once pain subsides.
- **External Rotation & Rotator‑Cuff Care** – Include low‑resistance external‑rotation drills with elbows tucked, using cables or bands, to strengthen the rotator cuff without overstretching.

- Maintaining shoulder health supports broader athletic performance and injury prevention.
