## Finger Injury Prevention in Climbing

**What it is** – A set of techniques, training structures, and warning signs aimed at protecting the fingers and tendons of climbers, especially beginners who are just starting bouldering.

### Why it matters
- Beginners often over‑grip, pulling with their hands instead of using their legs, which quickly loads the finger flexor tendons.
- Hanging full body weight on small holds before the tendons are conditioned can trigger micro‑tears and lead to early finger tendinitis.
- Long, unstructured sessions increase fatigue, causing form breakdown and additional strain.

These issues were highlighted in [[summaries/session-40_2025-01-15]], where the assistant emphasized that "overgripping" and "full‑weight hangs on tiny holds" are the most common pitfalls.

### Core prevention strategies
1. **Leg‑first movement** – Prioritize foot placement and push with the legs; keep the arms supporting, not pulling.
2. **Relaxed grip** – Hold onto holds just firmly enough to stay attached; release tension between moves.
3. **Controlled warm‑up** – Spend 8–10 minutes on large holds, easy traverses, and gentle shoulder rolls before attempting any problems.
4. **Session length** – Limit active climbing to 30–45 minutes for standard workouts or ~20 minutes for lunch‑break sessions; stop as soon as grip feels sloppy.
5. **Rest and recovery** – Include at least one rest day between climbs; on off‑days do light finger shakes, gentle stretching, and avoid other gripping activities.
6. **Progressive loading** – Follow a tendonitis‑friendly training progression (see [[concepts/tendonitis-friendly-training]]) that ramps up problem attempts gradually week by week.
7. **Technique cues** – Keep shoulders engaged (see [[concepts/shoulder-health]]) and avoid locking off on a single arm; use smooth, dynamic movement.

### Warning signs to stop climbing
- Sharp or pinching sensations in any finger.
- Persistent ache on the inner or outer side of the elbow (often linked to finger strain).
- Grip feels weak or unstable even after a brief warm‑up.
- Dull, lingering fatigue that does not dissipate after a minute of movement.

If any of these appear, skip the next session and allow extra recovery time.
