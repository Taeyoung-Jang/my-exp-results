## Tendonitis‑Friendly Training

**Definition**: A set of exercise selections, loading principles, and progression rules designed to build upper‑body strength while minimizing irritation of inflamed shoulder tendons.

### Core Principles
1. **Avoid overhead loading** – Eliminate true vertical presses that place the tendon under high tensile stress.
2. **Neutral‑grip and low‑angle movements** – Use grips that keep the elbows close to the body and angles that stay within a comfortable range of motion.
3. **Pain‑first rule** – Mild discomfort is permissible, but any sharp or lingering pain triggers an immediate reduction in range, load, or a swap to an alternative exercise.
4. **Gradual progression** – Prioritize adding repetitions before increasing load; weight jumps are small (2‑5 %) and only after two weeks of pain‑free performance.
5. **Flare‑up management** – In weeks with tendon flare, cut load to ~50 % and slow tempo rather than stopping training entirely.

### Program Structure (from [[summaries/session-07_2024-07-15]])
- **Three‑day weekly split** focusing on a main press, a main pull, and a shoulder‑health finisher each session.
- **Day 1**: Neutral‑grip dumbbell press (flat/decline) → chest‑supported row → cable lateral raise.
- **Day 2**: Low‑incline or landmine press → neutral‑grip pull‑down → rear‑deltoid cable work.
- **Day 3**: Close‑grip neutral bench press or neutral‑grip push‑ups → single‑arm row → external‑rotation cable.
- Each exercise includes 1‑2 alternative swaps that are more joint‑friendly.
- Rest intervals: 90 s–2 min between main lifts, 60 s for accessories.

### Integration with Related Concepts
- Works closely with [[concepts/shoulder-health]] by emphasizing joint‑stable movement patterns.
- Utilizes the [[concepts/progression-strategies]] outlined for safe, incremental load increases.
- Forms a subset of broader [[concepts/tendonitis-friendly-training]] approaches that can be applied to other body regions.

### Benefits
- Builds functional upper‑body strength without aggravating tendonitis.
- Allows consistent training even during mild flare‑ups.
- Encourages long‑term joint health through controlled load and movement variety.
