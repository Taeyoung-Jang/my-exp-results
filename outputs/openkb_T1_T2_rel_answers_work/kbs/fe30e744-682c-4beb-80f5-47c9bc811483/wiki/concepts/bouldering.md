# Bouldering

**Type:** Concept

## Overview
Bouldering involves climbing short walls without ropes, focusing on solving "problems" that require a blend of strength, balance, and problem‑solving. It is often recommended as an entry point to climbing because sessions can be brief and the activity emphasizes technique over sheer endurance.

## Benefits
- Provides an active hobby that doubles as a mental puzzle.
- Can be practiced in short time blocks (e.g., 30‑45 min), making it suitable for lunch‑break workouts.
- Develops body awareness, footwork, and core stability.

## Common Beginner Injuries
- **Finger strain** from over‑gripping or hanging on tiny holds before tendons adapt. See [[concepts/finger-injury-prevention]].
- **Elbow soreness** caused by locking off with straight arms or excessive pulling. See [[concepts/elbow-strain-prevention]].
- General tendon irritation mitigated by [[concepts/tendonitis-friendly-training]] strategies.

## Beginner Guidelines (from [[summaries/session-40_2025-01-15]])
1. **Prioritize legs** – use your feet to push rather than pulling with your hands.
2. **Warm‑up** – start with easy, large holds and light shoulder mobility (see [[concepts/shoulder-health]]).
3. **Limit session length** – aim for 20‑30 min of actual climbing in a 30‑35 min lunch break, or 40‑45 min in a longer gym visit.
4. **Rest between attempts** – take generous breaks to keep grip quality high.
5. **Stop early** – end the session as soon as grip feels sloppy or form degrades.

## Sample Training Schedules
### Standard 45‑minute sessions (3×/week)
- Week 1: 6‑8 easy problems.
- Week 2: 8‑10 easy problems.
- Week 3: Add 1‑2 slightly harder problems.
- Week 4: 10‑12 attempts, still stop early if fatigued.

### Lunch‑break 30‑35 minute sessions (2‑3×/week)
- Week 1: 3‑4 easy problems.
- Subsequent weeks: optionally add one extra easy problem or a single harder problem if form remains smooth.

## Technique Cues for Elbow Health
- **Legs first** – place your feet before pulling.
- **Shoulder engagement** – keep shoulders down and back to offload the elbows.
- **Smooth movement** – avoid lock‑offs; transition fluidly between holds.
- **Relaxed grip** – release tension whenever possible, reducing finger strain.

## Warning Signs to Rest
- Sharp or pinching sensations in fingers.
- Persistent ache on the inner or outer elbow.
- Weak or unreliable grip after warm‑up.
- Dull, lingering fatigue that does not dissipate quickly.
