# Delay‑Cause Logging

**Definition**: The practice of recording the reasons behind commute delays (e.g., traffic, parking, weather) to help fine‑tune buffer times and improve overall travel efficiency.

## Why It Matters
- Provides insight into recurring bottlenecks that simple timestamp logging may miss.
- Helps decide whether a larger buffer is needed for specific patterns.
- Can inform adjustments to route choice or departure time.

## How to Implement with Minimal Friction
1. **Primary tracking**: Continue logging only departure and arrival times as described in [[concepts/timestamp-logging]] and [[concepts/low-effort-tracking]].
2. **Optional note**: After safely arriving (e.g., once parked), add a brief comment on the main cause of any delay, such as “traffic jam” or “searching for parking.”
3. **Frequency**: Do this only when you have a spare moment; otherwise, rely on the timestamps alone.

## When to Use It
- When you notice frequent fluctuations in travel time that buffer adjustments alone don’t resolve.
- When planning a more precise [[concepts/buffer-time]] for high‑stakes mornings (important meetings, childcare pickups, etc.).

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Works alongside [[concepts/low-effort-tracking]] to keep the overall tracking burden low.
- Complements [[concepts/timestamp-logging]] by adding qualitative context.
- Can feed into a refined [[concepts/buffer-time]] calculation.
