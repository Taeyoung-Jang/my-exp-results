# Timestamp Logging for Commutes

**Definition**: Timestamp logging is the practice of recording only the departure and arrival times of a daily commute to generate a clear, low‑effort data set for evaluating travel duration and variability.

## Why Use Timestamp Logging?
- Provides a **simple, low‑friction** method for tracking real commute times without requiring detailed notes on each segment. [[concepts/low-effort-tracking]]
- Generates enough data to calculate an average travel time and its standard fluctuation, which directly informs how much **buffer time** to add to your schedule. [[concepts/buffer-time]]
- Supports the development of a stable **commute routine**, making the daily journey feel more automatic and less stressful. [[concepts/commute-routine]]

## How to Implement
1. **Choose a capture tool** – phone notes, calendar entry, or a quick screenshot of the lock‑screen clock when you leave and when you arrive.
2. **Record only two timestamps** per day: 
   - *Departure time* (when you start the commute)
   - *Arrival time* (when you reach your destination)
3. **Repeat for 7‑14 days** to gather a representative sample.
4. **Review the data** by calculating the mean travel time and noting the range of variation.
5. **Adjust your buffer** based on the observed average plus a small safety margin (e.g., 10‑15 minutes). [[concepts/buffer-time]]

## Optional: Logging Delay Causes
- Adding cause notes (traffic, parking, weather) can be useful, but only if it can be done safely **after** you finish the commute. For most users, the pure timestamps are sufficient. [[concepts/delay-cause-logging]]

## Benefits
- **Minimal effort**: No need for detailed logs or extra apps.
- **Actionable insight**: Directly informs the size of your daily buffer and helps you plan a more relaxed start to the day.
- **Scalable**: The same method can be applied to any regular travel routine, not just morning commutes.
