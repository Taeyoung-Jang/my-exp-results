# Commute Pre‑Planning

**Definition**: The practice of organizing key elements of a morning trip the evening before, including route selection, traffic checks, and setting a realistic departure time with a small buffer.

## Core Elements
- **Item preparation**: Lay out needed items (keys, bag, work materials) the night before.
- **Route check**: Review traffic or transit conditions for the planned path and choose a predictable route rather than one that is only theoretically faster.
- **Departure time**: Pick a realistic time that includes a modest buffer (typically 10‑15 minutes) to accommodate common delays.
- **Buffer planning**: Relates to [[concepts/buffer-time]] – a buffer should be based on observed typical travel times, not worst‑case scenarios.
- **Consistency**: Aligns with [[concepts/commute-routine]]; repeating the same preparation steps each evening builds an automatic habit.

## Benefits
- Reduces morning decision fatigue.
- Lowers stress by eliminating last‑minute scrambling.
- Improves punctuality, allowing optional early‑arrival activities such as checking messages or having a coffee, as described in [[concepts/early-arrival-productivity]].

## Related Practices
- **Low‑effort tracking**: Simple logging of departure and arrival timestamps supports fine‑tuning of the buffer; see [[concepts/low-effort-tracking]] and [[concepts/timestamp-logging]].
- **Stress management**: A well‑planned commute provides mental space for calming techniques (music, podcasts) covered in [[concepts/commute-stress-management]].
