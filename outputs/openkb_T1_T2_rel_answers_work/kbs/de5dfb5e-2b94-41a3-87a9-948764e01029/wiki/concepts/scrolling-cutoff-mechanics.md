# Scrolling Cutoff Mechanics

**Type:** Concept

## Definition
A set of low‑effort, mechanical strategies that create a clear boundary for phone scrolling in the evening, relying on physical cues and preset timers rather than willpower.

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-10-17]])
- **Phone placement:** Put the phone across the room as soon as the main evening activity begins. This physical distance makes reaching for it less automatic.
- **Timed window:** Start a single 10‑minute timer labelled “scroll time.” When the timer expires, the phone stays where it is and you transition to bedtime routines.
- **No‑willpower reliance:** The rule removes the need for self‑control; the environment and timer enforce the limit.

## How It Connects to Other Concepts
- It supports [[concepts/evening-routine-design]] by providing a concrete boundary that keeps the routine from slipping into mindless scrolling.
- It complements [[concepts/flexible-blocks]] by offering a flexible end‑point that can adapt to varying energy levels.
- The **transition ritual** (see [[concepts/transition-ritual]]) can incorporate the phone‑placement step, turning it into a cue that signals the start of the intentional block.
- When users want a quick list of actionable steps, the approach can be framed as a simple **micro‑task menu** (see [[concepts/micro-task-menu]]).

## Practical Implementation
1. **Prepare the environment:** As you begin your chosen evening activity (reading, cooking, etc.), place your phone on a nightstand or shelf opposite you.
2. **Set the timer:** Use a physical kitchen timer, a smart‑speaker timer, or any dedicated device (e.g., a [[entities/phone-timer]]) set for ten minutes.
3. **Engage in the activity:** Focus on the main task without the phone in reach.
4. **Timer rings:** When the timer finishes, treat the phone as “off‑limits” and move on to your wind‑down routine (e.g., washing face, dimming lights).

## Benefits
- **Predictable limit:** The scrolling window is clearly defined and repeatable.
- **Reduced decision fatigue:** No need to repeatedly decide whether to scroll; the rule handles it.
- **Improved sleep hygiene:** Less exposure to blue light and mental stimulation before bed.
