# Flexible Blocks

**Definition**: Flexible Blocks are short, adaptable periods (typically 10–15 minutes or 30–60 minutes) allocated for tasks that can shift around core commitments, or *anchors*, allowing a day to stay productive despite frequent interruptions.

## Core Characteristics
- **Size matters**: 
  - *10–15 minute blocks* are ideal for quick, low‑friction actions such as replying to emails, sending a brief message, tidying a workspace, or making a small decision.
  - *30–60 minute blocks* suit deeper work that incurs a higher switching cost, like writing, coding, planning, or reading.
- **Placement**: Blocks are positioned between anchors, filling the gaps that naturally arise in a fragmented schedule.
- **Adaptability**: Because the blocks are not tied to a fixed clock time, they can be re‑assigned on the fly when a gap of the appropriate length appears.
- **Energy‑aware**: Blocks can be chosen based on current energy levels, echoing the *flexible‑energy‑based* approach used in evening routines where a more demanding block follows a period of higher stamina and a restorative block is chosen when fatigue is higher.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Flexible Blocks work hand‑in‑hand with anchors, which provide the stable reference points around which blocks are arranged. *(anchors is plain text as it is not a whitelisted concept)*
- They are a central component of the [[concepts/micro-task-menu]] approach: the menu supplies the specific items that can be slotted into each block.
- In evening planning, they complement [[concepts/evening-routine-design]] by providing the scaffolding for a transition ritual, a meaningful activity block, and a calming wind‑down block.
- The notion of a mechanical scrolling cutoff (see [[concepts/scrolling-cutoff-mechanics]]) can be scheduled as a short, predefined block at the end of an evening routine, often triggered by a [[entities/phone-timer]].
- A brief [[concepts/transition-ritual]] (e.g., 10 minutes to change clothes, brew tea) can itself be treated as a 10‑minute Flexible Block that signals the start of the evening block sequence.

## Practical Application (from [[summaries/session-15_2025-06-16]] and [[summaries/session-37_2025-10-17]])
1. **Morning planning**: Identify one or two anchors (e.g., a brief setup task). The remaining time is divided into flexible blocks.
2. **Midday reset**: Review what blocks have been completed and re‑allocate any remaining gaps.
3. **Evening routine integration**: 
   - Begin with a 10‑minute transition ritual (a Flexible Block) to shift out of work mode.
   - Allocate a 30‑40 minute block for a meaningful activity (reading, sketching, hobby, or a short workout) based on current energy.
   - Reserve a final 15‑20 minute block for a calming wind‑down, possibly followed by a 10‑minute scrolling‑cutoff block managed by a [[entities/phone-timer]].
4. **Avoiding reshuffling**: Follow the “two‑update rule” – only adjust the schedule in the morning and at the midday reset. Outside those windows, work within the current block allocation.
5. **Template snapshot**:
   ```
   Morning Anchor (5 min): Quick priority scan
   Flexible Block 1 (10‑15 min): Micro‑task (e.g., send email)
   Flexible Block 2 (30‑60 min): Deep work (e.g., draft report)
   Midday Reset – update plan
   Evening Anchor (5 min): End‑of‑day wrap‑up
   Flexible Block 3 (10 min): Transition ritual (change clothes, tea)
   Flexible Block 4 (30‑40 min): Meaningful activity (read, sketch, hobby)
   Flexible Block 5 (15‑20 min): Calm unwind (music, light reading)
   Flexible Block 6 (10 min): Scrolling cutoff (phone‑timer triggers end)
   ```

## Benefits
- **Structure without rigidity** – Provides a framework that bends with the day’s realities.
- **Reduced decision fatigue** – Pre‑defined block sizes simplify the choice of what to do next.
- **Improved focus** – Longer blocks protect tasks that need sustained attention, while short blocks keep momentum during brief gaps.
- **Energy alignment** – By choosing block length and type based on current stamina, the schedule remains sustainable throughout the day and into the evening.
- **Controlled media use** – Embedding a mechanical scrolling cutoff as a block removes reliance on willpower.
