# Transition Ritual

**Definition**: A short, intentional activity that marks the boundary between work mode and personal time, helping the brain reset and prepare for evening activities.

## Key Elements (from [[summaries/session-37_2025-10-17]])
- **Duration**: Typically 5–15 minutes.
- **Typical actions**: Changing clothes, making a drink (tea/coffee), a quick stretch, or a brief breathing exercise.
- **Purpose**: Signals the end of work, reduces mental residue, and creates a mental cue that the evening block is starting.

## How it Supports Evening Routines
- Serves as the first step in the **[[concepts/evening-routine-design]]** pattern of "reset → meaningful activity → calming wind‑down."
- Provides a consistent anchor that makes flexible time blocks easier to enter, aligning with the principles of **[[concepts/flexible-blocks]]**.
- By establishing a clear start, it reduces the temptation for unstructured scrolling, complementing the **[[concepts/scrolling-cutoff-mechanics]]** strategy.

## Practical Tips
1. **Pick a repeatable set of actions** – e.g., change into comfortable clothes, brew tea, and do a 2‑minute stretch.
2. **Keep it low‑effort** – the goal is transition, not achievement.
3. **Place cues nearby** – a water bottle, a stretch mat, or a favorite mug can cue the ritual.
4. **Pair with a mechanical cue** – for example, plug your phone across the room (see **[[entities/phone-timer]]**) to reinforce the start of the ritual.

## Variations
- **Micro‑task menu**: Choose one of a small set of 3‑minute tasks (e.g., journal line, quick tidy, or breathing exercise). This links to **[[concepts/micro-task-menu]]** for those who prefer variety.
- **Energy‑based tweak**: If feeling high‑energy, add a brief walk; if low, emphasize a calming activity like tea‑making.
