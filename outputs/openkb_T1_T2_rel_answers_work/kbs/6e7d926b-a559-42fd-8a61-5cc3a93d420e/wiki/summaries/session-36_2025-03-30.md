# Summary of Session 36 – Closing an LLC  

**Date:** 2025‑03‑30  

This conversation outlines the practical and emotional steps involved in winding down a small business, specifically an LLC, and provides a detailed 0‑30 / 31‑60 / 61‑90‑day checklist.

## Key Concepts  
- [[concepts/business-closure]] – Recognizing the “point of no return” and differentiating a rough patch from a sustainable termination.  
- [[concepts/llc-dissolution]] – Filing dissolution with the state, obtaining confirmation, and handling post‑filing status.  
- [[concepts/emotional-impact]] – Identity shift, grief, guilt, and relief that owners experience.  

Other relevant ideas (plain text): checklist timeline, common gotchas, record retention, state confirmation, final tax filings.

## Practical Steps (High‑Level)  
1. **File dissolution** with the state (or start the clearance process).  
2. **Notify accountant** to prepare final tax returns.  
3. **Stop new obligations** – no new contracts, projects, or purchases.  
4. **Close tax accounts** – payroll (using [[entities/eftps]] and other IRS/ state withholding) and sales‑tax accounts.  
5. **Settle liabilities** – pay vendors, collect receivables, pay off loans/credit cards.  
6. **Cancel recurring services** – software, domain renewals, PO boxes, etc.  
7. **Back up records** and plan for retention.  
8. **Maintain essential accounts** until the very end (bank, merchant, accounting software, tax IDs, insurance, business email).

## 0‑30 Day Checklist  
- File LLC dissolution.  
- Alert accountant for final returns.  
- Halt new business activities.  
- Close payroll and sales‑tax accounts (if applicable).  
- Notify vendors, contractors, customers.  
- Compile list of all subscriptions, licenses, permits, insurance, bank and merchant accounts.  
- Cancel non‑financial recurring services.  
- Back up all business records and financial data.

## 31‑60 Day Checklist  
- Pay outstanding bills and collect remaining receivables.  
- Cancel unneeded licenses/permits.  
- Cancel insurance policies except those needed for ongoing liabilities (e.g., workers‑comp tail).  
- Ensure accountant is preparing final returns and any required final payroll/sales‑tax filings.  
- Review business bank account and merchant processors for pending charges/refunds.  
- Update the [[entities/irs]] and state address if mail will change.  
- Shut down website or replace with a “closed” notice.

## 61‑90 Day Checklist  
- File final federal and state tax returns.  
- Obtain official state dissolution confirmation.  
- Close IRS payroll accounts (final Form 941/940) and state tax accounts.  
- Close business bank account after all transactions settle.  
- Close merchant accounts (e.g., [[entities/stripe]], [[entities/paypal]], [[entities/square]]) once charge‑back windows are safe.  
- Cancel any remaining subscriptions tied to banking/tax workflows.  
- Close remaining insurance policies.  
- Archive and store business records for the legally required retention period (typically 3–7 years).

## What to Keep Open Until the End  
- Business bank account – to receive refunds or settle late charges.  
- Merchant account – for late customer payments or chargebacks.  
- Accounting software – needed for final filings and record‑keeping.  
- Tax accounts still pending final filings – payroll, sales tax, state IDs.  
- Insurance – until all liabilities are closed.  
- Business email – important notices will still arrive there.

## Immediate Double‑Check After Filing Dissolution  
1. Verify the state’s official confirmation (online entity search or receipt).  
2. Confirm federal and state tax accounts show no pending filings and that the accountant is aware of the final‑return requirement.  
3. Ensure a valid mailing address is on file for the [[entities/irs]] and state agencies.

## Most Common Missed Item in the First Week  
Assuming the state dissolution automatically closes tax accounts, licenses, and subscriptions. Those must be manually terminated; otherwise, fees, notices, or automatic renewals continue.
