# LLC Dissolution

**Definition** – LLC dissolution is the legal procedure by which a limited liability company terminates its existence, ceases operations, and is removed from the state’s roster of active entities.

## Core Steps (as outlined in [[summaries/session-36_2025-03-30]])
1. **File dissolution paperwork** with the state where the LLC was formed. Some states require a tax‑clearance certificate before the filing is accepted.
2. **Notify the accountant** so a final federal and state tax return can be prepared.
3. **Close tax accounts** – for payroll (via [[entities/eftps]]) and sales‑tax, and inform the [[entities/irs]] of the final filing status.
4. **Settle liabilities** – pay outstanding bills, collect receivables, and resolve loans or credit‑card balances.
5. **Cancel licenses, permits, and insurance** that are no longer needed, keeping only coverage required for any lingering obligations (e.g., workers‑comp tail coverage).
6. **Terminate recurring services** – software subscriptions, domain renewals, PO boxes, and merchant processors such as [[entities/paypal]], [[entities/stripe]], [[entities/square]].
7. **Back up and archive records** for the legally required retention period (typically 3‑7 years).
8. **Obtain official state confirmation** that the dissolution is complete; verify via the state’s online entity search.

## Timeline (0‑30, 31‑60, 61‑90 days)
- **0–30 days**: File dissolution, stop new business activity, close payroll and sales‑tax accounts, compile a master list of all subscriptions and accounts, begin record backup.
- **31–60 days**: Pay off remaining debts, cancel non‑financial licenses and insurance, ensure final tax returns are being prepared, review bank and merchant accounts for pending items.
- **61–90 days**: File final federal and state returns, receive state dissolution approval, close bank and merchant accounts, cancel any remaining subscriptions, and archive records.

## Common Gotchas
- Assuming the state filing automatically closes tax accounts, licenses, or subscriptions – these must be cancelled manually.
- Forgetting to keep the business bank account open long enough for delayed refunds or late charges.
- Overlooking autopay subscriptions (domain, software, mailing services) that continue to bill after the LLC is inactive.

## Emotional Dimension
The procedural tasks often trigger an identity shift and feelings of loss or failure. This emotional response is captured by the related concept [[concepts/emotional-impact]]. Recognizing that the closure is a transition rather than an ending can mitigate the psychological burden.

## Relationship to Broader Topics
- LLC dissolution is a sub‑process of the overall [[concepts/business-closure]] lifecycle.
- It interacts with tax compliance, financial wind‑down, and post‑closure record‑keeping.
