## Business Closure

**Business closure** is the systematic winding down of a company’s operations, legal existence, and financial obligations. It involves recognizing when a business has passed the "point of no return," executing formal dissolution steps, handling outstanding liabilities, and coping with the emotional impact on owners.

### Core Elements
- **Decision point** – Identification of sustained cash‑flow problems, unresolvable debts, or an unsustainable personal cost that signals it is time to cease operations.
- **Legal termination** – Filing the appropriate dissolution paperwork with the state, often referred to as **[[concepts/llc-dissolution]]** when the entity is a Limited Liability Company.
- **Financial wrap‑up** – Settling debts, collecting receivables, filing final federal and state tax returns, and closing payroll or sales‑tax accounts (e.g., via **[[entities/eftps]]** for payroll).
- **Administrative closure** – Cancelling licenses, permits, insurance policies, subscriptions, and merchant accounts such as **[[entities/paypal]]**, **[[entities/square]]**, and **[[entities/stripe]]**.
- **Record retention** – Archiving essential documents for the legally required period (typically 3–7 years).
- **Emotional impact** – Owners often experience grief, guilt, identity shift, and relief; this is captured under **[[concepts/emotional-impact]]**.

### Typical Timeline (adapted from [[summaries/session-36_2025-03-30]])
| Phase | Days | Key Actions |
|------|------|--------------|
| **0‑30** | Decision & initial wind‑down | File dissolution, notify accountant, stop new obligations, list subscriptions, back up records |
| **31‑60** | Settle liabilities | Pay outstanding bills, collect receivables, cancel non‑essential licenses/insurance, prepare final tax filings |
| **61‑90** | Final confirmations | File final tax returns, obtain state confirmation, close bank and merchant accounts, archive records |

### Common Gotchas
- Assuming state dissolution automatically closes tax accounts, licenses, or recurring services.
- Forgetting to cancel autopay subscriptions (domains, software, PO boxes).
- Leaving the business bank account open too early, which can cause bounced refunds.
- Not maintaining a valid mailing address for the **IRS** and state agencies, leading to missed notices.

### Checklist Highlights
- **State confirmation** – Verify the dissolution status on the state’s entity search.
- **Final tax filings** – Ensure the accountant files final federal and state returns and that payroll/sales‑tax accounts are marked "final."
- **Open‑until‑the‑end accounts** – Keep the business bank account, merchant processors, accounting software, and relevant tax IDs active until all transactions settle.
