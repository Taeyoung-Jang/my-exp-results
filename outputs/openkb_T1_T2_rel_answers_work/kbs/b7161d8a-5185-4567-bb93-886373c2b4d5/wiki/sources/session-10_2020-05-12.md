**User:** I’m trying to streamline how I handle invoicing and getting paid for freelance projects—what are some modern, low-friction ways to set up invoicing, payment links, and basic bookkeeping without turning it into a whole second job?

**Assistant:** You can keep things surprisingly simple these days without committing to full accounting software.

A lot of freelancers get the most mileage from using an invoicing tool that doubles as a payment processor. Services like Stripe Invoicing, Square, or PayPal let you create clean invoices, send them with a link, and get paid by card or bank transfer without much setup. You can usually save client info, duplicate past invoices, and let the system send reminders so you’re not chasing people manually.

If you want something that also handles light bookkeeping, tools like Wave or FreshBooks keep things streamlined. Wave is free for invoicing and bookkeeping, and you only pay the card-processing fees. FreshBooks is a bit more polished and automates more categorization, but it’s still simple to run day to day.

A lot of freelancers pair one of these with a separate business bank account so transactions stay clean. Most modern banks, like Novo, Mercury, or even some traditional ones, auto-categorize income and expenses enough to make tax time far less painful.

In short: pick one tool that sends invoices and collects payments, connect it to a business bank account, and let the automated categorization handle most of the bookkeeping in the background. That’s usually enough to avoid turning it into a second job.

It automatically pulls in all your transactions, auto‑categorizes most of them, and produces the basic reports you need for taxes. That combo gives you fast invoicing, frictionless payments, and automatic bookkeeping without feeling like you’re running an accounting department.

**User:** Okay—if I go with Stripe Invoicing + a separate business bank + Wave, how much setup am I realistically looking at in the first week, and what are the few key things I should configure so it mostly runs on autopilot (recurring invoices, reminders, categories, etc.)?

**Assistant:** The initial setup is pretty quick. Realistically, you’re looking at maybe an hour or two spread over a couple of days, not a whole week.

In Stripe, you just create your account, add your business details, connect your bank, and make one invoice template so you’re not rebuilding things each time. It’s worth turning on automatic reminders, saving your common line items, and setting up recurring invoices if you have ongoing clients.

For the business bank account, it’s usually just an application, identity check, and waiting a day or two for approval. Once it’s open, connect it to both Stripe and Wave.

Wave is mostly just linking the bank and Stripe so the transactions sync. After that, it helps to go through one month of your past spending and assign categories so Wave learns your patterns. Once it knows what’s what, it handles the bulk of categorization on its own.

After this, most of it runs automatically: clients get emailed when invoices are due, payments flow into your bank, and Wave keeps your books updated without you babysitting it. You’ll probably just check in once every week or two to tidy up anything Wave didn’t categorize correctly.

**User:** Can you give me a concrete first-week checklist (day-by-day is fine) for setting up Stripe Invoicing + a business bank + Wave, including the exact settings to flip on (auto-reminders, recurring invoices, payment methods, invoice terms, categories), and a “weekly 10-minute maintenance” routine so it stays mostly autopilot?

**Assistant:** Here’s a simple day‑by‑day walkthrough that stays easy to follow without turning into a project plan.

**Day 1: Stripe setup**  
Create your Stripe account, add your business details, and connect your new or existing business bank account. Go into your Invoicing settings and turn on automatic payment reminders, allow both card and bank‑transfer payments, set your default invoice terms to whatever you normally use, and save a basic invoice template with your logo, business info, and one or two common line items. If you have clients you bill every month, set up a recurring invoice schedule for them now.

**Day 2: Business bank account**  
If you haven’t opened the business account yet, apply today. Once it’s active, make it the payout account in Stripe. If the bank offers automatic categorization, turn that on, but you don’t need to customize much here yet.

**Day 3: Wave setup**  
Create your Wave account and link both your business bank and Stripe so transactions sync automatically. Review Wave’s default income and expense categories and tweak anything that feels off, but keep it simple. You don’t need to create a giant custom chart of accounts.

**Day 4: Train Wave a little**  
Take ten minutes to categorize a small batch of recent transactions. Wave learns quickly from your choices, so this early pass will make future automation much smoother.

**Day 5: Test your workflow**  
Create one test invoice in Stripe and send it to yourself or a friend just to confirm the branding, payment link, and reminders look right. When it “pays,” make sure the transaction shows up correctly in Wave.

**Day 6: Light polish**  
Check your Stripe items list, your saved clients, and your invoice template one more time. In Wave, tweak any categories that didn’t auto‑apply correctly.

**Day 7: Hands off**  
At this point everything is connected and automated. Nothing else to do unless you want to add more saved line items or adjust your invoice terms.

For your ongoing maintenance, a weekly ten‑minute check‑in is plenty. Open Wave, look at the last week of transactions, fix any that were miscategorized, and confirm Stripe payouts have synced. That’s usually all you need to keep the whole setup on autopilot.
