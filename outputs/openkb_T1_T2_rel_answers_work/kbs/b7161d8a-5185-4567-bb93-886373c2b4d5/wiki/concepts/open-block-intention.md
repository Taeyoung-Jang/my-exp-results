# Open Block Intention

**Definition**: An *open block intention* is a brief, pre‑chosen prompt used during unstructured periods to quickly decide on a small, satisfying activity. It turns vague free time into intentional, low‑effort engagement without creating a rigid schedule.

**Purpose**
- Provides direction while preserving flexibility.
- Reduces the anxiety of “empty time” that can arise after retirement or schedule changes.
- Supports the balance advocated in [[concepts/lightly-structured-routine]] by offering a lightweight decision‑making tool.

**How to Use**
1. **Create a short menu** of concrete options (e.g., walk, read a chapter, plant care, call a friend).  
2. At the start of an open block, ask a simple question such as *“What would feel good for the next hour?”* or *“What tiny thing would make the day more satisfying?”*
3. Pick the first option that resonates and commit to it for the chosen time slice.
4. Return to open space afterward, ready for the next intention.

**Key Elements**
- **Simplicity**: One question, one answer.
- **Variety**: Include items from categories like Home, Social, Outdoors, Learning, and Relaxation (as demonstrated in [[summaries/session-32_2025-05-12]]).
- **Minimal tracking**: Optional tick boxes (“Anchor done?” / “Day felt okay?”) keep the process frictionless.

**Relation to Other Concepts**
- Works alongside [[concepts/daily-ritual-anchor]] to provide both fixed and flexible structure.
- Complements the overall design of a [[concepts/lightly-structured-routine]] by filling the “open space” component.
- Can be incorporated into a broader [[concepts/weekly-maintenance-routine]] when weekly planning includes designated open‑block periods.

**Example from Session 32**
In the retired‑person week template, the open‑block menu lists 10–15 concrete actions (e.g., short walk, coffee with a friend, reading a chapter). The intention question “What would feel good for the next hour?” guides the choice, ensuring the open time feels purposeful without becoming a new to‑do list.
