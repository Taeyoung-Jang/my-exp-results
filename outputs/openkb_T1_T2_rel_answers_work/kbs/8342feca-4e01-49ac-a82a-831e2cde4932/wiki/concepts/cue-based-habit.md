# Cue‑Based Habit

**Definition**: A cue‑based habit is a behavior that is automatically triggered by a concrete, pre‑arranged cue in the environment, reducing the need for deliberative decision‑making.

---

## Core Elements
1. **Concrete Cue** – A physical object or signal that reliably prompts the next action (e.g., salsa bag by the door, pre‑packed snack in the fridge).
2. **Immediate Follow‑Through** – The habit includes a short, prescribed sequence that is executed as soon as the cue is noticed (e.g., change into class clothes, grab the bag, head out).
3. **Minimal Friction** – The cue and the action are placed together to eliminate effort or choice points.
4. **Fallback Option** – A low‑effort, “minimum viable” version that still counts toward the habit when energy is low.

---

## How It Appears in Session 37
The document **[[summaries/session-37_2025-01-11]]** provides a concrete illustration of cue‑based habit design for attending a weekday salsa class:

- **Night‑Before Cue Prep**: The salsa bag, keys, class clothes, and a grab‑and‑go snack are arranged at the front of the house. These objects serve as the *cue* that triggers the after‑work routine.
- **If‑Then Planning**: "If it’s salsa night, then I will send myself a reminder before leaving work." This creates a mental cue that keeps the intention active.
- **Physical Cue at Arrival**: Upon entering the home at 6:00 PM, the user **does not sit**; instead they immediately change clothes, pick up the snack, and grab the bag – the cue (bag + snack) launches the next steps.
- **Phone Reminder Wording**: Short, non‑cheesy nudges such as “Change clothes first, decide after” act as *digital cues* that reinforce the routine.
- **Minimum‑Viable Fallback**: If exhaustion hits, the habit still counts by driving to the studio parking lot or doing a five‑minute dance warm‑up at home. This fallback preserves the cue‑action loop while lowering effort.

## Practical Steps to Build a Cue‑Based Habit
1. **Identify the Desired Action** – e.g., attend salsa class.
2. **Select a Reliable Cue** – place the bag, keys, and snack where you’ll see them first.
3. **Create a Tiny Immediate Follow‑Through** – change clothes, grab snack, play one song.
4. **Add a Digital Cue** – set a brief phone reminder with a clear prompt.
5. **Design a Minimum‑Viable Fallback** – define the smallest version that still counts (e.g., drive to the studio lot).
6. **Test and Iterate** – observe if the cue consistently triggers the action; adjust placement or wording as needed.

---

## Benefits
- Reduces decision fatigue by eliminating the “sit‑or‑go” choice.
- Aligns physical environment with behavioral goals.
- Offers flexibility through a fallback that respects current energy levels.
