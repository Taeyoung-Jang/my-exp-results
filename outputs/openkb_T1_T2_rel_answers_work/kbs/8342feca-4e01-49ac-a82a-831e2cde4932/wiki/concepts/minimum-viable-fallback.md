# Minimum‑Viable Fallback

**Type:** Concept

**Description:** A low‑effort habit variant that still counts toward the goal when energy is low.

---

## Overview
The *Minimum‑Viable Fallback* (MVF) is a streamlined version of an after‑work routine that preserves the habit loop even on days when motivation or energy is depleted. Instead of requiring the full sequence of actions, MVF defines the smallest set of cues and actions that still qualify as a successful attempt, keeping the behavior chain intact and preventing a break in consistency.

## Key Elements
1. **Core Cue** – The earliest physical trigger that initiates the routine (e.g., the salsa bag by the door).
2. **Essential Action** – The minimal movement that moves the user out of a sit‑down context, such as getting into the car or stepping onto the driveway.
3. **Fallback Decision Point** – A low‑stakes option that allows the user to stop at a later, still‑productive location (e.g., the studio parking lot) without fully abandoning the habit.
4. **Micro‑Reward** – A tiny sense of completion, like completing a brief warm‑up in salsa clothes, that reinforces the habit loop.

## How It Works in Practice
In the *session‑37* routine, the MVF is applied after the user arrives home at 6:00 PM:

1. **Follow the standard script up to the car.** Change clothes, grab snack, pick up the salsa bag.
2. **If exhaustion hits**, the user:
   - Drives directly to the studio parking lot and decides there whether to attend the class. The act of arriving at the venue counts as a successful habit execution.
   - If even the drive feels too much, the user does a five‑minute dance warm‑up at home while still dressed in salsa attire. This keeps the cue‑action link alive.

Both pathways preserve the habit loop (cue → action → reward) while lowering the energy cost.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- Part of a broader **[[concepts/after-work-routine]]** strategy that uses cues and scripts to bridge the work‑home gap.
- Relies on **[[concepts/cue-based-habit]]** principles: the bag, keys, and pre‑packed snack act as automatic triggers.
- Connects to **[[concepts/energy-management]]** by offering a scalable response to low‑energy states.
- Complements **[[concepts/emotional-checkpoints]]** by providing a gentle, non‑punitive decision point ("if‑then" style) that reduces guilt.

## Benefits
- **Consistency:** Prevents streak breaks by ensuring at least a minimal version of the habit is performed.
- **Flexibility:** Adapts to fluctuating energy levels without requiring rigid adherence.
- **Psychological Safety:** Reduces all‑or‑nothing thinking, lowering the risk of habit abandonment.
