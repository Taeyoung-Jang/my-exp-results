# Social Security Card

**Type:** Product

The Social Security Card is an official United States document issued by the Social Security Administration that contains a unique nine‑digit number used for tax reporting, benefit eligibility, and identity verification.

## Role in immigration compliance
- For permanent residents (green card holders) in the United States, the Social Security Card is a key piece of personal identification that does **not** expire and does not require renewal. It is often presented alongside the [[entities/green_card]] and [[entities/passport]] for employment verification and other official purposes.
- While the card itself does not need to be updated when moving states or changing jobs, it should be kept in a safe place and referenced when completing forms for the [[entities/uscis]] or other government agencies.

## Relationship to other documents and concepts
- The Social Security Card complements the [[entities/green_card]] and [[entities/passport]] as part of the core set of documents a permanent resident should retain, as highlighted in the [[summaries/session-13_2021-07-14]].
- It is unrelated to the address‑update requirement, which is covered by the [[concepts/address_update_ar11]] and the filing of the AR‑11 form (see [[entities/ar_11_form]]).
- The card does not play a direct role in the conditional‑green‑card removal process (see [[entities/form_i_751]]), but it may be needed for identity verification when filing such petitions.

## Practical checklist (from the session summary)
1. Keep the Social Security Card with the green card, passport, and original approval notice.
2. No renewal needed; retain the card indefinitely.
3. Use it for employer verification (I‑9) and any USCIS‑related forms that request a Social Security Number.
4. Store a digital copy of the card in a secure location for backup, especially when updating address via [[concepts/address_update_ar11]].
