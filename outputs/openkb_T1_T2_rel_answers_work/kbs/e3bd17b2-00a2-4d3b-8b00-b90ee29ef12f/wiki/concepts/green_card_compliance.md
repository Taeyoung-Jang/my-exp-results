## Green Card Compliance

**Overview**

Green Card Compliance refers to the set of responsibilities that a U.S. permanent resident must follow to maintain lawful status. It covers tracking document expirations, updating personal information with immigration authorities, and meeting travel and residency requirements.

**Key Elements**

1. **Document Tracking**
   - **Physical green card**: Ten‑year card must be renewed before expiration; two‑year conditional card requires filing [[entities/form_i_751]] to remove conditions.
   - **Core identification**: Keep a copy of your [[entities/passport]], original approval notice, and [[entities/social_security_card]] readily available.
   - **Employer verification**: Provide the green card or passport for I‑9 verification; this does not affect immigration status.

2. **Address Update**
   - Permanent residents must file an AR‑11 change‑of‑address form with [[entities/uscis]] within **10 days** of moving. This is known as the [[concepts/address_update_ar11]] requirement.
   - Save the confirmation receipt (PDF, screenshot, or email) as proof of filing.

3. **Travel Considerations**
   - Short trips abroad are permissible, but extended absences can raise questions about continuous residence. No specific paperwork is needed, but be aware of residency‑continuity rules if you plan to naturalize later.

4. **State‑Level Updates** (non‑immigration):
   - Update driver’s license, vehicle registration, and mailing address with the new state. These are administrative but ensure you receive immigration‑related mail.

5. **Future Planning**
   - When considering naturalization, meet the residency‑time requirements (e.g., 5 years of continuous residence) and maintain a clean compliance record.

**Practical Checklist**
- Renew green card (or file I‑751) before expiration.
- Keep copies of [[entities/green_card]], [[entities/passport]], and approval notice.
- File [[entities/ar_11_form]] (AR‑11) within ten days of any address change.
- Preserve the AR‑11 confirmation receipt.
- Update state driver’s license and vehicle registration.
- Monitor travel duration to avoid jeopardizing residency.
