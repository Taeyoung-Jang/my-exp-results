# Immigration Status Categories

**Related summary:** [[summaries/session-13_2021-07-14]]

In the United States, most people encounter four broad immigration status groups. Each group has its own typical purposes, required documents, and compliance considerations.

---

## 1. Temporary Visitor Status
- **Purpose:** Tourism, short‑term business trips, or short academic programs that do not grant residency.
- **Typical paperwork:** Visa or electronic travel authorization, passport stamp, and sometimes proof of funds or travel itinerary.
- **Key point:** The status has a fixed end date; the holder must leave the U.S. or change status before it expires.

---

## 2. Work‑Authorized Non‑Immigrant Status
- **Purpose:** Employment for a specific employer or job category (e.g., H‑1B, L‑1, O‑1).
- **Typical paperwork:** Government approval notice, work authorization card (if applicable), and a visa stamp for entry.
- **Key point:** Staying in the U.S. is tied to the authorized job; extensions must be filed before the current period ends.

---

## 3. Permanent Residency (Green Card)
- **Purpose:** Allows the holder to live and work in the U.S. indefinitely.
- **Typical paperwork:** The physical **[[entities/green_card]]**, original approval notice, and supporting identity documents such as a **[[entities/passport]]**.
- **Key point:** The status itself does not expire, but the card must be renewed (10‑year card) or, for a conditional 2‑year card, a petition (**[[entities/form_i_751]]**) must be filed before expiration. Compliance details are covered in [[concepts/green_card_compliance]].

---

## 4. Humanitarian or Family‑Based Status
- **Purpose:** Protect individuals based on asylum, refugee claims, or family sponsorship.
- **Typical paperwork:** Application notices, possible work authorization cards, and renewal notices specific to the category.
- **Key point:** Each sub‑category has its own timeline and documentation requirements.
