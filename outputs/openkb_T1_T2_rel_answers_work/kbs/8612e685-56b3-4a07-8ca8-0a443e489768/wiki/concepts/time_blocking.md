# Time Blocking

**Definition**: Time blocking is a planning technique that divides the day into dedicated blocks for specific activities, reducing decision fatigue and protecting focus.

## Core Elements
- **Fixed start and end times** for each activity (e.g., wake‑up, commute, work, errands, workouts, wind‑down).
- **Buffers** before and after high‑energy tasks such as workouts to ease transitions.
- **Consistent sleep window** to achieve target sleep duration (7.5–8 hours).
- **Dedicated prep block** (e.g., a 15‑20 minute "Pack for Tomorrow" slot) to keep mornings smooth.
- **Low‑effort fallback routines** for days when energy is low (quick dinner, earlier bedtime).

## How It’s Applied in Session‑35 (2025‑02‑12)
The session provides two daily templates (normal and errand days) that illustrate time blocking:
1. **Morning block** – 6:00‑6:45 am for waking, breakfast, and quick preparation.
2. **Commute block** – 6:45‑8:15 am outbound, 5:00‑6:30 pm inbound.
3. **Evening block** – includes a decompression period, dinner, a 10‑15 min transition, a capped 45‑min workout, cool‑down buffer, and a final wind‑down leading to a 9:45‑10:00 pm bedtime window.
4. **Errand block** – 6:30‑7:30 pm dedicated to errands, followed by a quick‑dinner slot.
5. **Weekly block** – workouts are scheduled on Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday, with Friday reserved for a recovery routine.
