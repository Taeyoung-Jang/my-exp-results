# Permanent Resident Card

The **Permanent Resident Card** (often called the PR card) is an official identity document issued by **[[entities/immigration-refugees-and-citizenship-canada]]** to individuals who have been granted permanent resident status in **[[entities/canada]]**.  It serves as proof of status for domestic identification, re‑entry into Canada, and access to certain provincial services.

## Key Facts (from [[summaries/session-05_2023-06-15]])
- **Validity & Renewal**: The card has an expiration date (usually five years).  It must be renewed **before it expires**; renewal from abroad is difficult or impossible for most applicants.
- **Travel Requirement**: A valid PR card is required to board commercial airlines when returning to Canada.  Without it, a permanent resident may need a temporary travel document.
- **Residency Obligation**: Holding a PR card does not itself guarantee compliance with the **[[concepts/residency_obligation]]** (730 days of physical presence in any rolling five‑year window).  Card holders must still track their time in Canada.
- **Physical Presence Tracking**: Maintaining accurate records of entry and exit dates supports both compliance with the residency obligation and smooth renewal.  See [[concepts/physical_presence_tracking]] for suggested methods.
- **Link to Citizenship Decision**: While the PR card confirms permanent residency, it does not grant the political rights covered by Canadian citizenship (e.g., voting).  The distinction is described in [[concepts/citizenship_vs_permanent_residency]].

## Practical Considerations
- **Documentation**: Keep the PR card up to date and store supporting evidence of Canadian ties (lease, bank accounts, tax filings) alongside it.
- **Renewal Timing**: Apply for renewal at least six months before expiry to avoid being stuck abroad without a valid card.
- **Lost or Stolen Card**: Report immediately to **[[entities/immigration-refugees-and-citizenship-canada]]** and request a replacement; a replacement can take several weeks.
