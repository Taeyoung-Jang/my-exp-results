# Birth Certificate

**Type:** product  

**Description:** A government‑issued record of birth used for identity and citizenship verification.

## Role in Government Onboarding

In the context of federal compliance hiring (see [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-16]]), a birth certificate is one of the primary documents accepted for **eligibility verification**. It can be paired with a driver’s license or state ID to satisfy the identity and citizenship proof required before a start date is set.

## Accepted Identity/Citizenship Combinations

- **Driver’s license or state ID + original birth certificate** – satisfies the requirement for proof of U.S. citizenship.  
- The birth certificate may also be used with a **passport** or **passport‑card**, but the driver’s license + birth certificate pairing is the most common.

## Original vs. Copy Requirements

- The onboarding process typically requires the **original** birth certificate or a **certified copy**. Simple photocopies are generally not accepted unless HR explicitly permits scanned versions.

## Related Entities and Concepts

- [[entities/drivers-license]] – used together with the birth certificate.  
- [[entities/passport]] and [[entities/passport-card]] – alternative citizenship documents.  
- [[entities/enhanced-drivers-license]] – can serve as identity proof but still needs a citizenship document.  
- [[concepts/eligibility-verification-documents]] – outlines the overall document set needed for clearance eligibility.  
- [[concepts/background-check-process]] – the broader screening that includes document verification.  
- [[concepts/security-clearance-levels]] – determines how extensive the documentation may need to be.

## Practical Tips

- Keep the original certificate in a safe place; bring a certified copy if the agency allows it.  
- If you have undergone a name change, have the associated legal documents ready alongside the birth certificate.
