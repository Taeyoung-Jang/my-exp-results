# Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC)

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) is the federal department responsible for immigration policy, processing permanent residency applications, issuing PR cards, and overseeing citizenship matters in Canada.

## Role in Permanent Residency
- IRCC sets and enforces the **[[concepts/residency_obligation]]** for permanent residents, including the 730‑day physical presence rule within any rolling five‑year period.
- It issues and renews the **[[entities/pr-card]]**, which serves as proof of status and is required for re‑entry.
- IRCC provides guidance on how temporary work assignments abroad may count toward physical presence when the employer qualifies under its rules.

## Interaction with Key Concepts
- **Physical presence tracking**: IRCC expects permanent residents to keep accurate records of entry and exit dates, often recommending a simple spreadsheet or calendar log. This aligns with the [[concepts/physical_presence_tracking]] concept.
- **Citizenship vs. permanent residency**: IRCC outlines the differences in rights, obligations, and benefits between becoming a Canadian citizen and maintaining permanent residency, a topic covered in [[concepts/citizenship_vs_permanent_residency]].

## Relevant Documentation
- The department publishes the **Residency Obligation Guide**, which details the 730‑day rule, acceptable exceptions, and required documentation for proof of ties to Canada.
- For PR card renewal, IRCC reviews the applicant’s travel history over the preceding five years, as highlighted in the discussion in [[summaries/session-05_2023-06-15]].

## Related Entities
- **[[entities/canada]]** – the country whose immigration system IRCC administers.
- **[[entities/pr-card]]** – the official document issued by IRCC to permanent residents.
