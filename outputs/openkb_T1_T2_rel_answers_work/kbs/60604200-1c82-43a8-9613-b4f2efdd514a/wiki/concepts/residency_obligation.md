# Residency Obligation

The **Residency Obligation** is the rule that permanent residents of Canada must be physically present in the country for at least **730 days within any rolling five‑year period**.  Failing to meet this threshold can lead to loss of PR status, refusal of re‑entry, or problems when renewing a PR card.

## Core Requirement
- **730 days** (roughly two years) of physical presence must be accumulated in the **most recent five‑year window** at the time of entry, PR‑card renewal, or any IRCC assessment.
- The window is **rolling**, meaning officials look back five years from the date of the relevant event (e.g., re‑entry, renewal), not from a fixed calendar period.

## Common Pitfalls
1. **Extended abroad assignments** – Long work trips (12‑18 months) can consume the buffer of days, especially if the resident does not have enough prior presence.
2. **Expired PR card or outdated address** – Administrative lapses can be interpreted as abandoning residence.
3. **Tax non‑filing** – Even if no tax is owed, not filing a return may suggest loss of ties.
4. **Misunderstanding the rolling window** – Days that were counted earlier can fall out of the five‑year window as time passes, reducing the available total.

## Exceptions & Credits
- **Canadian‑employer posting**: If a resident is sent abroad by a qualifying Canadian employer, the days abroad may still count as Canadian residence, provided the employer meets IRCC criteria and formal documentation is obtained.

## Documentation to Preserve
- Lease or mortgage statements, utility bills, and bank records showing ongoing ties to Canada.
- Employment contract or posting letter confirming the temporary nature of the overseas assignment.
- Detailed travel log (airline itineraries, passport stamps) for every entry and exit.
- Annual tax filings, even when no tax is due.

## Practical Compliance Steps
1. **Calculate days before departure** – Verify that you have at least 730 days in the upcoming five‑year window.
2. **Maintain a travel spreadsheet** – Track every entry and exit to stay aware of your running total; see [[concepts/physical_presence_tracking]] for methods.
3. **Renew PR card before expiry** – Prefer renewal while in Canada; renewing from abroad can be costly and slow.
4. **Keep address and contact info current with IRCC**.
5. **Gather evidence of Canadian ties** – Store the documents listed above in a single, easily accessible folder.
