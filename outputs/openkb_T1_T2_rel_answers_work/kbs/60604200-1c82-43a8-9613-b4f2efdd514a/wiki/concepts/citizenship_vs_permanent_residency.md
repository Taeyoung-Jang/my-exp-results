# Citizenship vs Permanent Residency

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** Comparison of rights, obligations, and practical impacts of Canadian citizenship and permanent residency.

---

## Overview
Canadian **citizenship** and **permanent residency (PR)** both allow an individual to live and work in Canada indefinitely, but they differ substantially in terms of political rights, travel flexibility, tax obligations, and the security of status.  The differences become most relevant in day‑to‑day situations such as voting, holding government jobs, obtaining a passport, and maintaining status during long periods abroad.

---

## Core Differences
| Aspect | Citizenship | Permanent Residency |
|--------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Political Rights** | Can vote in federal, provincial, and municipal elections; may run for elected office; eligible for many public sector jobs. | No voting rights; cannot hold most positions that require Canadian citizenship. |
| **Travel & Re‑entry** | Holds a Canadian passport, generally enjoys visa‑free travel to many countries and streamlined re‑entry to Canada. | Uses a PR card; must present the card and may be subject to additional questioning at the border; must maintain **physical presence** to keep status. |
| **Status Security** | Status cannot be revoked for failure to meet residency requirements (except for very serious criminal reasons). | Status can be lost if the holder fails to meet the **residency obligation** (730 days in a rolling five‑year window) or lets the PR card lapse while abroad. |
| **Taxation** | Taxed on worldwide income; cannot avoid Canadian tax obligations by moving abroad unless citizenship is renounced. | Generally taxed on Canadian‑source income; can avoid worldwide taxation by establishing non‑residency for tax purposes, provided the residency obligation is satisfied. |
| **Path to Full Rights** | Immediate full rights upon grant of citizenship. | Must first satisfy residency obligations and may later apply for citizenship, gaining the additional rights listed above. |

---

## Practical Implications for Long‑Term Travel
The **residency obligation** is a key factor for PR holders.  As explained in [[summaries/session-05_2023-06-15]], Canada requires **730 days of physical presence within any rolling five‑year period**.  A long‑term work assignment abroad (e.g., 12‑18 months) is permissible only if the holder already has sufficient days accumulated or can count the time abroad under a qualifying employer‑posted assignment exception.

Citizenship eliminates this constraint: a citizen can be outside Canada for years without jeopardising status, and re‑entry is uncomplicated with a passport.

---

## When One Status Is Preferable
- **Choose Permanent Residency** when you want long‑term stability without the political commitments of citizenship, or when you wish to avoid worldwide tax obligations and are comfortable managing the physical‑presence requirement.
- **Choose Citizenship** when you need the security of an immutable status, desire full political participation, travel convenience, and do not want to monitor days spent in Canada.
