# Eligibility Verification Documents

**Definition**: The set of identity and citizenship records an individual must present to satisfy federal onboarding and security‑clearance eligibility requirements.

## Core Purpose
Eligibility verification documents are used by Human Resources and security offices to confirm:
1. The applicant’s legal identity.
2. U.S. citizenship or lawful permanent residency status (where applicable).
3. Any name‑change or naturalization history that might affect background investigations.

These documents are typically collected **before a start date** is confirmed, allowing the agency to begin the security‑clearance investigation (e.g., [[entities/sf-86]] or [[entities/sf-85]]) with confidence that the candidate meets the basic legal requirements.

## Standard Accepted Combinations
| Identity Document | Citizenship Document | Comments |
|-------------------|----------------------|----------|
| Driver's license or state ID | Original birth certificate (or certified copy) | Most common pairing |
| Driver's license or state ID | Certificate of Naturalization | For naturalized citizens |
| Driver's license or state ID | Consular Report of Birth Abroad | For citizens born overseas |
| Driver's license or state ID | **Enhanced driver’s license** + any citizenship document | Enhanced ID proves identity only; still needs citizenship proof |
| **Passport (book)** | — (passport itself proves citizenship) | Acceptable as a single document |
| **Passport card** | — (passport card also proves citizenship) | Acceptable for eligibility, though not for international travel |

### Originals vs. Copies
- **Passport (book or card)**: original required.
- **Birth certificate**: original or certified copy; plain photocopies are usually rejected.
- **Naturalization certificate**: original required.
- **Driver’s license / state ID / enhanced driver’s license**: original required.

If the agency permits a secure electronic upload, a scanned copy of an *original* may be accepted, but the original must be available for inspection if requested.

## Additional Documents (Often Requested Later)
- Name‑change paperwork (e.g., marriage certificate, court order).
- Dual‑citizenship or foreign passport information (usually disclosed narratively, not submitted).
- Proof of lawful permanent residency for non‑citizens (not covered here; see [[concepts/citizenship_vs_permanent_residency]]).

## What **Not** to Submit Unprompted
- Tax returns, pay stubs, bank statements, or other financial records. These are private and only submitted if explicitly required by the clearance questionnaire.

## Relation to Clearance Levels
The breadth of documentation does **not** change across clearance tiers, but higher levels (Secret, Top Secret) demand more detailed personal histories (address and employment look‑back periods of up to 10 years). The same eligibility documents are still the foundation.

## Practical Checklist (Derived from [[summaries/session-38_2024-12-16]])
- **Required**: One government‑issued photo ID + one citizenship document (or passport alone).
- **Commonly requested**: Name‑change or naturalization documents, supervisor contact info for the employment history.
- **Nice‑to‑have**: Old leases, W‑2s, offer letters (kept for reference, not submitted).
