# Physical Presence Tracking

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** Methods for recording and verifying days spent in Canada to meet PR residency rules.

---

## Overview
Physical presence tracking is the systematic recording of every entry to and exit from Canada in order to demonstrate compliance with the **730‑day‑in‑five‑years** requirement for permanent residents.  The concept is crucial for anyone who wishes to maintain their status while undertaking long‑term travel, work assignments abroad, or temporary absences.

## Why It Matters
- **Residency obligation**: Canadian permanent residents must accumulate at least 730 days of physical presence within any rolling five‑year window. Failing to meet this threshold can lead to loss of PR status at re‑entry or during a PR‑card renewal.
- **Rolling window nuance**: Officers evaluate the most recent five years at the time of entry or at the time of a renewal application, not a fixed calendar period. This makes continuous tracking essential.
- **Risk reduction**: Accurate logs prevent accidental violations caused by forgetting older days that have “fallen off” the five‑year window.

## Core Components
1. **Entry/Exit Log**
   - Record the exact date (and time, if possible) of each border crossing.
   - Include the mode of travel (air, land, sea) and the destination country.
2. **Supporting Documentation**
   - Airline itineraries, passport stamps, or travel‑history reports from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA).
   - Employment letters or posting agreements that may allow days abroad to count toward presence.
   - Proof of ongoing ties to Canada (lease, mortgage, utility bills, bank statements, tax filings).
3. **Tools and Techniques**
   - **Spreadsheet**: Simple columns for "Date In", "Date Out", and a calculated "Days in Canada" column. A running total can be summed automatically.
   - **Calendar App**: Mark each travel day; many apps can export a CSV for further analysis.
   - **Travel‑history report**: Request a formal report from CBSA as a verification checkpoint.

## Practical Workflow (Based on session‑05_2023‑06‑15)
1. **Pre‑departure audit**
   - Verify you have at least 730 days in the current five‑year window.
   - Obtain documentation from a Canadian employer if the assignment qualifies for the "posted abroad" exception.
2. **During absence**
   - Keep all travel receipts, boarding passes, and passport stamps.
   - Maintain Canadian ties (lease, bank, tax returns) and store copies with your log.
3. **Return and re‑entry**
   - Present the log and supporting documents if questioned at the border.
4. **Renewal preparation**
   - Update the log to cover the entire five‑year period preceding the renewal date.
   - Ensure the total days meet or exceed the 730‑day threshold.

## Example Calculation (from the source summary)
- Landed: **July 1 2022** – stayed continuously until **July 1 2024** → ~730 days.
- Abroad: **July 2 2024 – Jan 1 2026** → ~550 days.
- Re‑entry **Jan 2 2026**: the five‑year window (Jan 2 2021‑Jan 2 2026) still contains the full 730 days, proving compliance.

## Risks of Poor Tracking
- **Unintended loss of status** when older qualifying days drop out of the window.
- **Delays or denial** of PR‑card renewal if the IRCC cannot verify the required presence.
- **Inability to use the posted‑abroad exception** without documented employer support.
