# Background Check Process

## Overview
The background check process is the initial screening performed by federal agencies and contractors to verify a candidate’s identity, employment and education history, criminal record, and overall suitability for a position. It precedes any [[concepts/security-clearance-levels]] investigation and ensures the individual meets basic eligibility before onboarding.

## Primary Stages
1. **Identity Verification** – Confirms the applicant’s legal name and citizenship using documents such as a [[entities/drivers-license]], [[entities/passport]], [[entities/passport-card]], or a [[entities/birth-certificate]].
2. **Employment & Education History** – Checks past employers, dates of service, and educational credentials, often cross‑referencing public records or using verification services.
3. **Criminal Record Check** – Searches national and state databases for convictions, arrests, or ongoing investigations.
4. **Financial & Foreign Influence Screening** – Looks at credit history, bankruptcies, and any foreign contacts or travel that could pose a security risk.
5. **Eligibility Verification** – Determines if the applicant can proceed to a clearance investigation, aligning with the requirements described in [[concepts/eligibility-verification-documents]].

## Typical Documents Collected
- Government‑issued photo ID (e.g., driver’s license or state ID).
- Proof of U.S. citizenship (passport, passport‑card, or original birth certificate).
- Name‑change or naturalization paperwork when applicable.
- Address history (5–10 years depending on clearance level).
- Employment timeline (matching the same look‑back period).

## Relationship to Clearance Levels
If the role requires a **public trust**, **Secret**, or **Top Secret** clearance, the background check feeds into a deeper suitability review. Higher clearance levels expand the look‑back period (e.g., 10 years for Top Secret) and may trigger additional forms such as the [[entities/sf-85]] or [[entities/sf-86]].

## Best Practices
- Provide accurate, gap‑free timelines; label any unemployment or travel periods clearly.
- Submit originals for citizenship documents; certified copies are acceptable for birth certificates.
- Avoid sending unnecessary financial records (tax returns, pay stubs) unless explicitly requested.
