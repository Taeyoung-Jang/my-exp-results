# Summary of session-05_2023-06-15

**Topic:** Practical trade‑offs between Canadian permanent residency (PR) and citizenship, with a focus on maintaining PR status during long‑term travel or work assignments abroad.

### Key Points
- **Residency requirement** – Canada mandates **730 days of physical presence within any rolling five‑year window** for PR holders.  
- **Impact of long trips** – An 18‑month abroad stint is permissible if you have already accumulated roughly two years (≈730 days) of presence before leaving, but those days will gradually fall out of the five‑year window.
- **Rolling window nuance** – Officers evaluate the most recent five‑year period *at the time of entry or at PR‑card renewal*, not a fixed calendar window.  
- **Employer‑posted abroad exception** – Days abroad may count if you are on a valid assignment for a Canadian employer that qualifies under IRCC rules; obtain formal documentation.
- **Risk factors**  
  1. Exceeding the allowed absence and dropping below 730 days.  
  2. Letting the PR card or address lapse while overseas.  
  3. Failing to file tax returns or maintain other ties that demonstrate residence.  
- **Documentation to keep**  
  - Lease/mortgage, utility bills, bank statements showing ongoing Canadian ties.  
  - Employment contract or posting letter confirming temporary assignment.  
  - Travel records (airline itineraries, passport stamps) for entry/exit dates.  
  - Annual tax filings, even if no tax is owed.  
- **Practical compliance steps**  
  1. Calculate days before departure; ensure at least 730 days remain within the upcoming 5‑year window.  
  2. Keep a simple spreadsheet or calendar log of every entry/exit.  
  3. Renew PR card before it expires; avoid renewing from abroad when possible.  
  4. Maintain a Canadian mailing address and update IRCC of any changes.  
- **Example timeline** (based on user’s dates)  
  - Landed: **July 1 2022** → stayed until **July 1 2024** (≈730 days).  
  - Abroad: **July 2 2024 – Jan 1 2026** (≈550 days).  
  - Re‑entry: **Jan 2 2026** – Five‑year window (Jan 2 2021‑Jan 2 2026) includes the full 730 days, so entry is compliant.  
  - Latest safe departure before re‑entry becomes risky: roughly **2 years** of continuous absence would drop you below the 730‑day threshold.  
  - Mid‑2027 PR‑card renewal: IRCC looks at the five years preceding the application (e.g., Jun 2022‑Jun 2027). Days accumulated after Jan 2026 must offset the earlier absence to stay above the requirement.

### Actionable Checklist
1. **Pre‑departure**: Verify you have ≥730 days in the rolling window; obtain employer posting letter if applicable.  
2. **During travel**: Keep all proof of ties to Canada and a detailed travel log.  
3. **Return**: Be ready to present entry/exit records and supporting documents at the border.  
4. **Renewal**: Ensure you have at least 730 days of presence in the five years before the renewal date; keep PR card current.
