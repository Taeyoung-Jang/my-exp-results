# Summary of session-10_2025-06-15

**Main focus:** Practical advice for building a daily schedule around a fixed safety‑compliance block, managing interruptions, and using polite deferment phrases.

## Key Concepts
- **Anchor block** – Keep the first hour of the shift dedicated to safety checks; treat it as a stable anchor for the day. [[concepts/time-blocking]]
- **Flexible micro‑blocks** – Add short, protected work periods (20‑30 min) after the anchor to ensure progress without over‑structuring. [[concepts/time-blocking]]
- **Interruption rule** – Handle questions < 1 min immediately; defer longer items to designated check‑in windows. [[concepts/interruption-management]]
- **Check‑in windows** – Two brief windows (mid‑morning, mid‑afternoon) of 10‑15 min for batching follow‑ups. [[concepts/check-in-windows]]
- **Urgent vs. non‑urgent** – Define true urgency (e.g., safety incidents, production stoppages) and communicate the distinction.
- **Communication scripts** – Polite, specific defer phrases for walk‑ups and digital messages; variations that set expectations without naming exact times.

## Advice Summary
1. **Treat the safety hour as an anchor** and build lighter blocks around it rather than scheduling the whole day rigidly.
2. **Create short protected work periods** (20‑30 min) after the anchor to maintain momentum.
3. **Adopt a simple interruption rule**: answer sub‑minute queries now; schedule longer ones.
4. **Implement two daily check‑in windows** (e.g., 10 am & 2 pm) for clearing backlogged items; keep them brief.
5. **When faced with “urgent but not urgent” requests**, respond with a concrete time (“I can look at this at 11 am”) and ask for clarification if the request truly blocks safety or production.
6. **Use ready‑made defer phrases**:
   - Walk‑up: “I’m in the middle of a task, but I can take a look at that at 11. Will that work?”
   - Teams/email: “Thanks for reaching out. I’m tied up now, but I’ll circle back around 11.”
   - Urgency qualifier: add “unless it’s a safety issue or production stoppage.”
   - No exact time: “I’ll handle this in my next check‑in window; flag it if it’s urgent on the safety side.”
7. **End each shift with a quick note** of top priorities for the next day to ease the transition back into blocks.

## Takeaway
By anchoring a critical task, using micro‑blocks, defining clear interruption handling rules, and employing concise defer scripts, you can maintain focus, reduce backlog, and set expectations for colleagues without appearing unhelpful.
