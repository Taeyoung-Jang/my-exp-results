# Burnout Prevention

**Definition**: Burnout prevention encompasses proactive habits and organizational practices that reduce chronic stress, maintain mental health, and sustain performance for individuals on on‑call rotations.

## Core Elements (derived from [[summaries/session-40_2025-10-15]])

1. **Track page frequency and repeat incidents**
   - Monitor how often you are paged and whether the same issue recurs. Frequent repeats signal a need for reliability work or automation.
2. **Ensure dedicated recovery time**
   - Schedule at least one quiet day after a shift ends to reset mental load.
3. **Sleep hygiene**
   - Go to bed earlier during rotation weeks, return to sleep immediately after a page, and use short naps the next day.
4. **Boundary setting**
   - Define what activities (e.g., alcohol, long drives, extra project work) are off‑limits while on‑call and communicate these limits.
5. **Settle‑in window**
   - Introduce a 5–10 minute period at the start of a shift to log in, review dashboards, and gain context before taking alerts. See [[concepts/settle-in-window]].
6. **Interruption management**
   - Treat alerts as signals for systemic fixes rather than routine firefighting. Use structured runbooks to reduce cognitive load. See [[concepts/interruption-management]].
7. **Check‑in windows & time‑blocking**
   - Periodically check metrics or personal well‑being during a shift (e.g., brief mid‑shift check‑ins). Block time for focused work and rest. See [[concepts/check-in-windows]] and [[concepts/time-blocking]].

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/on-call-sustainability]]**: Burnout prevention is a key pillar of sustainable on‑call practices.
- **[[concepts/settle-in-window]]**: A structured start‑of‑shift routine reduces immediate stress and improves response quality.
- **[[concepts/interruption-management]]**: Managing how interruptions are handled helps keep mental load manageable.
- **[[concepts/check-in-windows]]** and **[[concepts/time-blocking]]**: Regular self‑checks and dedicated focus blocks support long‑term resilience.

## Practical Tips
- Keep a simple log of page counts and repeat incidents.
- Communicate any patterns to the team to trigger reliability initiatives.
- Use a brief “settle‑in” ritual to transition into on‑call mode.
- Schedule a post‑shift “recovery day” with no on‑call responsibilities.
- Review personal well‑being during scheduled check‑in windows.
