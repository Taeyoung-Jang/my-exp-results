# Check‑In Windows

**Definition**: Check‑In Windows are short, recurring time slots (typically 10–15 minutes) built into a workday to collect and process deferred tasks, questions, and follow‑ups.

## Purpose
- Provide a predictable rhythm for handling interruptions without derailing larger work blocks.
- Reinforce the structure created by [[concepts/time-blocking]] by offering designated catch‑up periods.
- Support the [[concepts/interruption-management]] rule that immediate answers are reserved for sub‑minute queries, while longer items are deferred.

## Typical Implementation (as described in [[summaries/session-10_2025-06-15]])
1. **Timing**: Choose natural lull points, often a mid‑morning slot (around 10 am) and a mid‑afternoon slot (around 2 pm).
2. **Duration**: Keep each window brief—10 to 15 minutes is sufficient to clear short follow‑ups.
3. **Scope**: Review items deferred from earlier in the day; if any task requires more effort, schedule it into the next open work block rather than extending the check‑in.
4. **Urgency Filter**: During a window, prioritize items flagged as truly urgent (e.g., safety incidents or production stoppages); otherwise, keep the focus on routine follow‑ups.

## Benefits
- **Reduced context switching**: By batching interruptions, you minimize frequent task switching.
- **Predictability for colleagues**: People learn when they can expect a response, leading to fewer ad‑hoc interruptions.
- **Clarity on urgency**: The windows reinforce a clear boundary between urgent and non‑urgent matters.

## Tips for Adoption
- Communicate the schedule to your team so they know when to bring non‑urgent queries.
- Use polite defer scripts (see the conversation in [[summaries/session-10_2025-06-15]]) to direct requests to the next window.
- Adjust the exact times and frequency to match your workflow and natural workflow dips.
