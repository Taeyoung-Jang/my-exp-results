# Time Blocking

**Definition**: Time Blocking is a planning technique where the workday is divided into distinct, pre‑allocated periods—or *blocks*—each dedicated to a specific type of activity. By assigning tasks to fixed time slots, individuals reduce context‑switching and create predictable rhythms that support deeper focus.

## Core Elements
- **Anchor Block**: A non‑negotiable block that protects a high‑priority activity. In [[summaries/session-10_2025-06-15]] the author uses the first hour of the shift for safety‑compliance checks.
- **Micro‑Blocks**: Short, protected intervals (20–30 minutes) placed after the anchor to make incremental progress on other tasks without over‑scheduling the day.
- **Flexibility**: Rather than mapping the entire day rigidly, the approach balances structure (the blocks) with adaptability for interruptions.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/check-in-windows]]** – Complementary brief windows (e.g., mid‑morning, mid‑afternoon) where deferred items are batched and addressed. These windows fit naturally between or after time blocks.
- **[[concepts/interruption-management]]** – A set of rules for handling unexpected requests (e.g., answer sub‑minute queries instantly, defer longer ones to the next check‑in window). Effective interruption management reinforces the integrity of time blocks.

## Practical Guidance from Session 10
1. **Establish a fixed anchor** for critical work (e.g., safety checks) to serve as the day’s foundation.
2. **Add flexible micro‑blocks** after the anchor to maintain momentum on other responsibilities.
3. **Use brief check‑in windows** to clear back‑logged items, ensuring they don’t erode the dedicated blocks.
4. **Apply a simple interruption rule**: immediate handling for < 1 minute queries; defer longer items to the next window.
5. **End the shift with a short priority note** to smoothly transition back into the next day’s blocks.

By combining an anchor block, micro‑blocks, and structured interruption handling, Time Blocking helps individuals stay focused, manage workload variability, and prevent the day from fragmenting into endless ad‑hoc tasks.
