## Interruption Management

**Definition**: A set of practices for handling unexpected queries and requests during work, balancing immediate responsiveness with protected focus time.

### Core Principles (from [[summaries/session-10_2025-06-15]])
1. **One‑minute rule** – If a question can be answered in less than a minute, address it immediately; otherwise, defer it.
2. **Designated check‑in windows** – Brief (10‑15 min) periods, typically mid‑morning and mid‑afternoon, for batching and clearing deferred items.
3. **Urgency filter** – Only truly urgent matters (e.g., safety incidents or production stoppages) bypass the defer process.
4. **Clear communication** – Use scripted defer phrases to set expectations without seeming dismissive.

### How it fits with other concepts
- **[[concepts/time-blocking]]** – Interruption management supplies the rules that protect the blocks you create.
- **[[concepts/check-in-windows]]** – The windows are the primary mechanism for processing deferred interruptions.

### Practical Steps
- **Set a rule**: "If it takes > 1 min, I’ll note it and handle it later."
- **Create two daily windows**: 10 am and 2 pm, each 10‑15 min, dedicated to reviewing the list of deferred items.
- **Define urgency**: Communicate that safety issues or production stops are the only exceptions.
- **Use scripts**: For walk‑ups – "I’m in the middle of a task, but I can look at that at 11. Does that work?"  For digital messages – "I’m tied up now, I’ll circle back during my next check‑in window."

### Benefits
- Reduces task‑switching cost.
- Keeps focus blocks intact.
- Provides transparent expectations for coworkers.
- Prevents a hidden backlog of deferred items.
