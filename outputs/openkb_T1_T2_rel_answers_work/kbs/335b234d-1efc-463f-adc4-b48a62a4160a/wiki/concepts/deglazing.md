# Deglazing

Deglazing is a cooking technique that involves adding a small amount of liquid to a hot pan after sautéing or searing, in order to dissolve the caramelized bits (fond) stuck to the bottom. This not only prevents those bits from burning but also incorporates their deep, savory flavor into a sauce or base.

## Why It Matters
- **Flavor extraction**: The fond contains concentrated Maillard‑derived flavors; deglazing releases them into the dish.
- **Temperature control**: The added liquid quickly lowers the pan temperature, protecting delicate ingredients like [[entities/garlic]] from over‑cooking.
- **Versatility**: Any neutral or mildly acidic liquid can be used, allowing the cook to tailor the final taste.

## Common Liquids
- **[[entities/broth]]** – adds savory depth without added acidity.
- **Water** – a neutral option when only the fond’s flavor is desired.
- **[[entities/lemon]]** or a splash of [[entities/vinegar]] – provides a bright finish without overwhelming sourness.
- **[[entities/wine]]** – contributes both acidity and complexity.

## Technique (as described in [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]])
1. **Build the base**: Sauté [[entities/onion]] and [[entities/garlic]] (and optional aromatics) until softened.
2. **Add spices** and briefly [[concepts/blooming-spices]] them (20‑40 seconds).
3. **Deglaze**: Pour a small amount of broth, water, or a mild acid‑balanced liquid into the pan. The liquid instantly lifts the browned bits, preventing the garlic from turning bitter.
4. **Finish**: Stir the deglazed mixture, allowing it to reduce slightly if a thicker sauce is desired.

## Practical Tips
- Use just enough liquid to cover the fond; excess can dilute the flavor.
- Add the liquid **immediately** after the spices have bloomed to keep the pan temperature from climbing too high.
- For a subtle brightness, add a tiny squeeze of [[entities/lemon]] after deglazing, or a dash of [[entities/vinegar]] for a sharper note.
- When using [[entities/soy-sauce]] or [[entities/tomato-paste]] in a recipe, deglaze **after** they have cooked briefly to avoid burning.
