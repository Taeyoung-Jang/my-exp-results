# Garlic

Garlic is a widely used aromatic [[entities/garlic]] that provides a distinctive savory depth in many cuisines. In the context of the cooking guidance from [[summaries/session-05_2024-06-15]], garlic plays a central role in the **foundation aromatics** approach.

## Role in Foundation Aromatics
- Combined with [[entities/onion]] and optional carrot, celery, or ginger to create a flavor base. 
- Added **after** the onions have softened, typically **30–60 seconds** of cooking to become fragrant without turning bitter.
- Acts as a timing cue for when to introduce [[concepts/blooming-spices]]; spices are added immediately after the garlic aromas appear.

## Techniques to Prevent Burning
- Add garlic a little later than onions, letting the onions act as a heat buffer.
- If garlic begins to brown, a splash of liquid (e.g., [[entities/broth]] or water) can be added to lower the pan temperature.
- This practice ties into the **deglazing** step, where a small amount of broth, water, or a mild acid (like [[entities/lemon]] or [[entities/vinegar]]) is poured in to lift browned bits and keep the garlic from over‑cooking.

## Common Pairings Mentioned
- **Tomato paste**: garlic is added before stirring in [[entities/tomato-paste]] for a quick sauté.
- **Soy sauce**: after garlic and spices bloom, a splash of [[entities/soy-sauce]] is added before deglazing.
- **Wine** or a tiny squeeze of [[entities/lemon]] may be used in the deglaze for subtle brightness.

## Summary of Key Points
- **Timing**: onions first (5‑7 min), then garlic (30‑60 sec), then spices (20‑40 sec bloom).
- **Flavor protection**: deglazing with broth or water prevents garlic bitterness.
- **Flexibility**: works with variations like tomato paste or soy sauce to adapt to different dishes.
