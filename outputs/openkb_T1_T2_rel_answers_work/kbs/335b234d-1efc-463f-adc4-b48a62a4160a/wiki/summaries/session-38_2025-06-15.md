## Overview
The document compares several dietary philosophies—including vegetarianism, veganism, kosher, halal, and Jain dietary rules—and offers a practical 5‑day weeknight dinner plan that can be adapted for each.

## Dietary philosophies
- **Vegetarianism** – excludes meat but permits dairy and eggs. vegetarianism
- **Veganism** – excludes all animal products. veganism
- **Kosher** – requires certified ingredients and separation of meat and dairy. kosher
- **Halal** – permits only allowed animals and specific slaughter methods. halal
- **Jain diet** – avoids all root and underground vegetables (e.g., onions, garlic, carrots, potatoes). Jain diet

## Detailed Shopping List (for 2 people)
**Grains**
- Rice: 450 g
- Pasta: 500 g
- Quinoa: 170 g (1 cup dry)

**Proteins**
- Red lentils: 170 g (1 cup)
- Chickpeas: 2 cans (400 g each)

**Vegetables (non‑root)**

- Cabbage: 1 small head (or equivalent shredded)
- Yellow squash: 1 medium
- Fresh herbs (basil or cilantro): 1 bunch
- Ginger: a thumb‑sized knob

**Pantry / Flavorings**
- Coconut milk: 2 cans (400 ml each)
- Olives or capers: 1 small jar
- Soy sauce: 1 bottle
- Olive oil (or other cooking oil): as needed
- Lemon or lime: 4 units
- Spices: cumin 50 g, turmeric 50 g, coriander 50 g, paprika 50 g (optional), chili powder small jar, asafoetida/hing pure small pack (Jain‑safe)

## Label‑checking guidance
Packaged items can hide onion or garlic in powders, “natural flavors,” or spice blends. When following Jain or other onion‑free constraints, inspect the ingredient list of:
- Soy sauce
- Pre‑mixed spice blends
- Asafoetida mixes (many contain onion/garlic)
- Canned tomato sauces or stews
- Coconut milk with added seasoning
- Jarred olives, pickles, or sauces
- Bouillon cubes or broth powders

Look for terms like “onion powder,” “garlic powder,” “dehydrated onion,” “dehydrated garlic,” and vague “natural flavor” statements.
