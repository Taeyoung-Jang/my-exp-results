## Summary of session‑33 (2025‑06‑01)

This document captures a concise framework for running a small research team’s weekly check‑ins without devolving into endless status meetings. The core contribution is a **lightweight async pre‑read** and a **tight 30‑minute agenda**, coupled with a **single running tracker** that records open actions, completed items, budget asks, and hiring pipeline status.

### Key Concepts
- **[[concepts/weekly-check-in-structure]]** – A short, predictable 20‑30 minute alignment huddle focused on changes, decisions, and blockers.
- **[[concepts/async-pre-read]]** – A Slack or doc template where each team member provides:
  1. Focus for the week (one sentence)  
  2. What changed since last week  
  3. Blockers/decisions needed  
  4. **Resourcing notes** – early signals about budget or hiring.
- **[[concepts/action-tracking-doc]]** – A running document with sections for *Open actions*, *Done*, *Budget notes*, and *Hiring notes* that is carried forward week to week.
- **Resourcing notes** – A dedicated line in the pre‑read to surface budget or hiring implications without derailing the meeting.
- **Meeting agenda** – Structured as:
  1. Rapid pass on discussion‑worthy pre‑read items  
  2. Team review of shared risks/decisions  
  3. Confirmation of actions & owners  
  4. Brief meeting snapshot for historical context.

### Findings & Recommendations
1. **Pre‑read first, discussion second** – Collect status asynchronously; the live meeting only addresses items that truly need group input.
2. **Limit meeting length** – 20–30 minutes signals that the structure is working; if there’s nothing to discuss, the meeting can be skipped.
3. **Inline action assignment** – When a decision is made, immediately note the owner and next step in the pre‑read thread, keeping the action list centralized.
4. **Running tracker** – One shared document holds:
   - **Open actions** – Owner, next step, target date.  
   - **Done** – Completed items for audit.  
   - **Budget notes** – Small purchases, upcoming requests, approval status.  
   - **Hiring notes** – Candidate pipeline, open roles, next steps.  
   - **Meeting snapshot** – One‑sentence log per week (e.g., "Mar 18 – decision on equipment purchase; risk of delayed data delivery.")
5. **Resourcing early** – By flagging budget/hiring needs in the pre‑read, the team can decide quickly whether to allocate meeting time or handle it in a focused follow‑up.

### Practical Templates
- **Async pre‑read (Slack paste)**:
  ```
  ————————
  Weekly check‑in pre‑read
  Focus for the week:
  (One sentence on your top priority.)

  What changed since last week:
  (Anything others should know that might affect plans.)

  Blockers or decisions needed:
  (Only list items that actually require input.)

  Resourcing notes:
  (Any early signals about budget needs or possible hiring implications.)
  ————————
  ```
  *(Replace “Slack” with a reference to [[entities/slack]] if needed.)*

- **Running tracker (one‑page doc)**:
  ```
  Weekly team tracker

  Open actions
  Owner – Next step – Target date

  Done
  (Completed actions move here so there’s a record.)

  Budget notes
  (Log of small purchases, upcoming requests, pending approvals.)

  Hiring notes
  (Current candidates, status of open roles, next steps.)

  Meeting snapshot
  (Timestamped one‑sentence summary of decisions, risks, or shifts.)
  ```

This approach ensures the team stays aligned, decisions are surfaced early, and follow‑through is transparent and repeatable week after week.
