# Technical vs Managerial Career Paths

**Definition**: The set of contrasting career trajectories in conservation/restoration that separate deep, hands‑on technical work from leadership and managerial responsibilities.

## Overview
Conservators often face a choice between two broad paths:

| Dimension | Technical (hands‑on) | Managerial (leadership) |
|-----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| **Primary focus** | Direct treatment of objects, material analysis, developing specialized techniques | Strategic planning, team coordination, budgeting, policy development |
| **Typical titles** | Conservator, Senior Conservator, Principal Conservator, Specialist | Lab Manager, Director, Conservation Department Head |
| **Key motivations** | Love of solving material puzzles, tactile interaction, direct impact on artifacts | Desire for broader influence, mentorship, shaping institutional direction |
| **Performance metrics** | Treatment success, technical publications, workshops, specialist expertise | Project delivery, staff performance, budget adherence, program growth |
| **Career progression** | Senior technical tracks that reward expertise without supervising staff | Management ladders that increase supervisory scope and administrative duties |
| **Compensation drivers** | Scarcity of specialized skills, grant‑funded research, high‑impact projects | Scope of responsibility, size of budget/team, organizational impact |

The **session‑14_2023‑06‑01** conversation outlines how professionals can evaluate which path aligns with their preferences and outlines trade‑offs such as bench time, impact, and salary potential.

## Decision‑Making Guidance (from [[summaries/session-14_2023-06-01]])
1. **Self‑assessment** – Notice which activities energize you: solving technical puzzles vs. strategizing and mentoring.
2. **Trial** – Shadow both roles or take on small leadership tasks while remaining technical to see where you thrive.
3. **Trade‑offs** – Hands‑on offers deeper material connection but slower promotion; managerial offers broader impact and higher pay but reduces bench time.
