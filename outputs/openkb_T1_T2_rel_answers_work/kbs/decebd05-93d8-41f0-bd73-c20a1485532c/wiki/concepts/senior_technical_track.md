# Senior Technical Track

**Definition**: A career ladder in conservation/restoration organizations where advancement, status, and compensation are based on depth of technical expertise rather than supervisory responsibilities.

## Core Characteristics
- **Expertise‑based promotion**: Raises are tied to mastery of materials, techniques, or analytical methods, not the size of a team.
- **Protected bench time**: Roles explicitly safeguard a substantial portion of work for hands‑on treatment, often documented in job descriptions.
- **Separate from managerial track**: Institutions offer parallel paths – one for managers, one for senior specialists.
- **Recognition mechanisms**: Titles such as *Senior Conservator*, *Principal Conservator*, or *Technical Lead* signal seniority without managing staff.

## How It Appears in Practice
- Larger museums, well‑funded conservation labs, federal or state agencies, and some private studios structure their staff with a distinct senior technical track.
- Performance evaluations focus on **research output, specialized project completion, publication of case studies, and training activities** rather than budgeting or personnel oversight.
- Access to specialized equipment, analytical collaborations, and opportunities for advanced training are common signals of a robust senior technical track.

## Benefits for Hands‑On Professionals
- **Continued bench work**: Enables deep engagement with objects while growing influence.
- **Higher compensation**: Pay scales reflect scarcity of specialized knowledge; compensation can be negotiated by demonstrating unique skill sets.
- **Professional visibility**: Opportunities to lead complex treatments, develop new methods, and mentor without formal staff responsibilities.

## Identifying a Senior Technical Track (Interview Checklist)
- Ask if the institution has a formal senior or principal conservator track that does **not** require supervising staff.
- Inquire how performance is evaluated: look for criteria linked to technical mastery, research, and project outcomes.
- Check for language about protected bench time or limited administrative duties.
- Verify the percentage of time senior technical staff spend on treatment work.
- Look for support for advanced training, workshops, and conference attendance.
