# Friction Reduction

**Description:** Identifying and removing obstacles that cause morning chaos for parents of young children.

---

## Overview

Friction reduction is the process of pinpointing and eliminating the specific pain points that make a routine—especially a **weekday morning** with young children—inefficient, stressful, or prone to conflict. By removing these sources of friction, parents can create smoother, more predictable mornings that require less negotiation and mental overload.

## Key Elements

1. **Identify high‑impact friction points** – In the context of [[summaries/session-39_2025-03-16]], the biggest sources of chaos were:
   - **Shoes**: children often can’t find them quickly.
   - **Permission slips**: forgotten or misplaced paperwork.
   - **Lunch packing**: last‑minute assembly leads to stress.
2. **Targeted, minimal effort fixes** – The solution focuses on a short, ten‑minute **evening reset** that directly addresses each friction point without adding steps. This aligns with the concept of [[concepts/evening-reset]].
3. **Consistent placement** – Shoes and daycare bags are placed in a dedicated spot each night, ensuring they’re always where they need to be.
4. **Quick verification** – A brief visual check of the “big three” (slip, lunch, shoes) at the entryway prevents last‑minute discovery.
5. **Good‑enough preparation** – Rather than trying for perfection, the approach emphasizes completing just enough tasks to clear the major roadblocks.

## Benefits

- **Reduced negotiation**: Children know where items are, lowering the chance of arguments.
- **Time savings**: Eliminates time spent searching for shoes or paperwork in the morning.
- **Lower mental load**: Parents can start the day with a clear plan, decreasing stress.
- **Scalable habit**: The process is short enough to become a habit without feeling like a second shift.
