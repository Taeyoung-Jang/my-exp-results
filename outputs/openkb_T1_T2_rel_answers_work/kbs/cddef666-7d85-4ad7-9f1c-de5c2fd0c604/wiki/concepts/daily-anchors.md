## Daily Anchors

**Definition**: Small, repeatable outdoor moments placed at key times of the day (morning, midday, evening) that act as gentle “hooks” to get a person out of the house without pressure.

**Origin**: Introduced in the advice given in [[summaries/session-03_2024-06-15]], where a simple routine was proposed to combat a sedentary slump.

**Key Characteristics**
- **Brief**: Each anchor lasts only a few minutes (2–10 min) to keep the effort low.
- **Timed**: Typically scheduled for a morning start, a midday errand, and an evening wind‑down.
- **Flexible**: Can be scaled down to a *minimum version* (30 seconds outside) or expanded to longer walks when motivation is high.
- **Purpose‑Driven**: Serves to provide fresh air, sunlight, and a sense of movement, which collectively raise energy levels.

**Practical Implementation**
1. **Morning Anchor** – Step outside right after waking (e.g., 8:30 am) for a quick breath of fresh air while drinking water or coffee.
2. **Midday Anchor** – Perform a tiny errand or walk (e.g., 12:30 pm) such as mailing a letter, grabbing a drink, or a short block loop.
3. **Evening Anchor** – End the day with a short stroll or sit outdoors before dinner (e.g., 6:00 pm).

These anchors can be recorded in a simple habit tracker using the codes O (basic), M (minimum), and E (expanded) and a one‑word feeling note.
