# Simple Outdoor Routine

A **Simple Outdoor Routine** is a lightweight, repeatable set of brief outdoor moments designed to break sedentary patterns, increase exposure to fresh air and light, and gently raise energy levels. The approach emphasizes consistency over intensity, using small “anchors” throughout the day that require minimal planning or motivation.

## Core Structure
- **Daily anchors**: Three short outdoor slots – morning, midday, and evening – that act as routine touch‑points. See [[concepts/daily-anchors]] for the underlying pattern.
- **Versioning**:
  - **Minimum version** – a 30‑second step outside or opening a window for deep breaths on low‑motivation days.
  - **Expanded version** – a 15‑20 minute walk or a pleasant outing when energy is higher.
- **Templates**:
  - **Weekday template** (e.g., 8:30 am, 12:30 pm, 6:00 pm).
  - **Weekend template** (e.g., 9:30 am, 2:00 pm, 7:00 pm).
- **Errand rotation**: Simple, no‑cost tasks such as mailing a letter, returning a library book, or picking up a single grocery item to give purpose to the outing.
- **Go‑to places**: A curated list of ten crowd‑free, nature‑leaning spots (parks, trails, quiet streets, covered shelters) that can be used in most towns, even in bad weather.

## Tracking Progress
The routine pairs with a **habit tracker** that uses a single‑letter code (O = basic, M = minimum, E = expanded) plus a one‑word feeling note. This ultra‑simple system avoids the feeling of a chore while still providing feedback. See [[concepts/habit-tracker]] for the tracking method.

## Benefits
- Low barrier to entry; no special equipment or planning required.
- Flexible: can be scaled down on “bad” days or expanded on “good” days.
- Encourages regular exposure to daylight and fresh air, which can improve mood and energy.
