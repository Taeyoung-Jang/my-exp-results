# Open Invite Text

**Definition**: A set of low‑pressure, optional‑tone text templates that let you invite friends, acquaintances, or small groups to join you spontaneously, without creating expectations.

## Overview
The concept originates from the conversation in [[summaries/session-32_2025-02-13]], where the assistant provided concrete wording for different relationship contexts. The goal is to convey a friendly "FYI" vibe, keeping the invitation casual and pressure‑free.

## Key Templates
- **Close friend**: "I’m heading to the park to read for a bit. If you feel like hanging out, come join me. No biggie if not!"
- **Acquaintance**: "I’ll be at Oak Street Café this afternoon working. If you’re nearby and want to say hi, I’ll be around."
- **Small group**: "Grab a table at Luna Café around 6 tonight. Anyone free is welcome, but no pressure."

These examples illustrate how to adjust the level of familiarity while maintaining the same open‑invite structure.

## Tone & Boundaries
Open invite texts rely on **neutral, low‑stakes language** and an explicit "no worries" clause to set clear communication boundaries. This aligns with [[concepts/communication_boundaries]] and helps prevent the perception of pressure.

## Frequency Guidance
To avoid sounding spammy, the source recommends sending an open invite **once every two weeks** per individual, or **no more than once per week** overall if varied across contacts. This ties into managing **[[concepts/spontaneous_vs_planned_interaction]]** by keeping spontaneous outreach modest.

## Integration with Weekly Rhythm
The concept fits neatly into a **[[concepts/weekly_social_rhythm]]** strategy: designate a specific day (e.g., Wednesday) as the "open‑invite day" where you send these casual texts, while other days are reserved for planned meet‑ups.
