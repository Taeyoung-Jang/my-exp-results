# Communication Boundaries

**Definition**: Strategies for expressing personal limits on social interaction (frequency, mode, and intensity) in a way that maintains a positive relational vibe.

## Core Elements
1. **Clarity without rigidity** – State preferences (e.g., "I prefer a slower pace") without imposing hard numeric rules.
2. **Tone alignment** – Match the phrasing style (casual, thoughtful, flirty) to the context and relationship stage.
3. **Reassurance** – Offer brief explanations to separate interest from availability, preventing misinterpretation.
4. **Specific next steps** – Pair boundary statements with a concrete plan to show continued interest.
5. **Adaptation to partner style** – Acknowledge spontaneous or high‑frequency communicators and frame limits as personal pacing needs.

## Illustrative Examples from [[summaries/session-07_2024-06-15]]
- **Casual text**: "Hey, just so you know my social battery is limited, so I usually hang out about twice a month…"
- **Flirty, non‑numeric**: "I love our vibe and prefer a slow‑burn pace; it makes each meetup feel special."
- **Spontaneous‑planner response**: "I love your spontaneous energy; if I’m free I’m happy to say yes, just need a little more space between hangouts."
- **Reassuring after hurt**: "It’s not that I don’t want to see you – I just know my limits, and spacing out helps me be fully present."
- **Low‑key insert**: "By the way, I’m kind of a slow‑pace person with hangouts. I like having some space between plans—it just works better for my brain."
- **Text‑frequency boundary**: "I’m not the best all‑day texter, but I love hearing from you and will get back when I can."
