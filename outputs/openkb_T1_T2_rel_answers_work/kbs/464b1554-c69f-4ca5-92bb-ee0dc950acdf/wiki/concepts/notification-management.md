# Notification Management

Notification Management is the practice of configuring app alerts so that only important information reaches the user, while unnecessary interruptions are suppressed. Effective notification settings contribute to better battery performance, lower background activity, and a cleaner user experience.

## Key Settings from Session 02 (2024‑06‑15)

### iPhone (iOS)
- **Banners & Badges**: Keep banners and badges enabled for work and chat apps (e.g., messaging, email) to stay informed in real time. Disable sounds for non‑critical apps to avoid constant audio alerts.
- **Badge‑Only Mode**: Use badges only for social, shopping, and finance apps; this lets you see pending items without visual pop‑ups.
- **Scheduled Summary**: Add non‑urgent apps (social, shopping, media, most finance) to the Scheduled Summary so they deliver notifications at set times rather than throughout the day.
- **Focus Modes**: Create a Work focus that allows only chat, mail, calendar, and navigation notifications, blocking everything else.

### Android
- **Notification Channels**: Turn on the main message channel for work and chat apps. Disable promotional, suggestion, or status channels for social, shopping, and finance apps.
- **Channel Granularity**: Keep transaction‑related channels for finance apps active, but mute marketing channels.
- **Battery/Background Interaction**: Restrict background activity for apps with disabled notification channels to further reduce wake‑ups.

## New Insights from Session 33 (2025‑02‑13)

- **Minimal‑but‑Reachable Setup**: Emphasizes keeping only essential communication channels (calls, texts, a single group‑chat app) active on the primary device, while muting all other notifications. This aligns with a “low‑friction” approach where urgent messages come through via direct mentions or a short‑list of priority contacts.
- **Mentions‑Only Alerts**: For group chat apps, configure notifications to fire **only** on direct mentions or messages from designated priority contacts. This reduces constant checking while still guaranteeing receipt of time‑sensitive updates.
- **Scheduled Check‑In Windows**: Establish personal rules to open the chat app only at set times (e.g., morning and after dinner). Pair this with OS‑level scheduling or Do Not Disturb periods to enforce the routine.
- **Device Separation for Chat**: Suggest using a secondary device (old smartphone, tablet, or computer) dedicated to the group chat, accessed only at home. This physically separates optional social interaction from the primary device used for essential communication, reinforcing the notification discipline.
- **Maps as Low‑Risk App**: Keeping a basic navigation app on the primary phone is considered safe because it does not encourage scrolling. If the primary phone lacks navigation, a dedicated GPS device can be used instead, further isolating optional functionality.
- **Rule‑of‑Thumb**: Separate “must‑reach‑me” channels (calls, texts) from “nice‑to‑check” channels (social/chat apps) by device or strict notification settings, preventing the slippery‑slope back into constant alerts.
