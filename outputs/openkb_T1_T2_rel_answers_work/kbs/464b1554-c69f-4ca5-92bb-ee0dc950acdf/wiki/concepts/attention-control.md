# Attention Control

**Definition**: The practice of deliberately shaping digital environments and habits to keep mental focus on what matters, while minimizing disruptive stimuli.

## Core Elements
- **Notification filtering** – Turning off all non‑essential alerts and allowing only direct mentions or messages from a short list of priority contacts. This is a key tactic in [[concepts/notification-management]].
- **Device separation** – Assigning “must‑reach‑me” channels (calls, texts) to a primary device (e.g., a flip phone) and relegating optional, potentially distracting apps to a secondary device or a laptop. See [[concepts/device-separation]].
- **Scheduled check‑ins** – Defining specific times (morning, after dinner) to open chat apps, preventing constant, reflexive checking.
- **Minimalist toolset** – Keeping only essential utilities such as a basic map app for navigation, which does not encourage endless scrolling. This aligns with [[concepts/tech-simplification]].

## How It Appears in Practice
In the conversation from [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-13]], the user seeks a “least slippery‑slope” approach to stay reachable for group chats and maps while preserving evening quiet. The recommended strategies illustrate attention control:
1. **Mute all group‑chat notifications except for direct mentions** – ensures urgent information surfaces without the noise of every message.
2. **Use a secondary device for chat** – isolates the chat app from the pocket, reducing impulse checks.
3. **Keep navigation on the primary phone or a dedicated GPS device** – provides necessary functionality without opening a gateway to other apps.
4. **Communicate new habits to contacts** – sets expectations so that urgent matters are conveyed via calls or texts, the channels that remain fully active.

## Benefits
- Reduced mental clutter and scattered focus.
- More intentional use of time, leading to calmer evenings.
- Easier identification of truly important information.

## Risks if Ignored
- Feeling of disconnection or anxiety about missing updates.
- Gradual drift back into habitual scrolling and constant phone checking.
