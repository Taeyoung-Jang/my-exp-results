# Device Separation

**Type:** Concept
**Description:** Using separate devices for essential and optional digital tasks to limit distraction.

## Overview
Device separation is a strategy for [[concepts/tech-simplification]] that involves assigning different categories of digital activity to distinct physical devices. By keeping *must‑reach‑me* channels (calls, texts, urgent group chats) on a primary device—often a minimalist **[[entities/flip-phone]]**—and relegating *nice‑to‑check* or recreational services (social media, news feeds, optional messaging) to a secondary device such as a **[[entities/laptop]]** or an older **[[entities/android]]** phone, users create a natural barrier against mindless scrolling.

## Why It Works
- **Attention Control:** Physical separation reduces the temptation to switch contexts, supporting [[concepts/attention-control]].
- **Notification Management:** With fewer apps on the primary device, notification settings become simpler, enhancing the effectiveness of [[concepts/notification-management]].
- **Battery Optimization:** Fewer background processes on the main phone improve battery life, linking to [[concepts/battery-optimization]] (though not a primary focus here).

## Key Practices (from [[summaries/session-33_2025-02-13]])
1. **Primary Device Minimalism** – Keep only calls, texts, and a single essential chat app on the flip phone. Mute all other notifications.
2. **Secondary Device for Optional Apps** – Use a laptop or an old smartphone (e.g., an Android device) for social apps, news, and hobby‑related tools. Access them only during designated “check‑in” periods.
3. **Dedicated Navigation** – If the primary phone lacks reliable mapping, supplement with a **[[entities/dedicated-gps-device]]** rather than loading a full‑featured phone app that could become a new distraction source.
4. **Clear Rules & Communication** – Tell friends and family that urgent matters should be sent via call or text, reserving the chat app for non‑urgent group conversations.
5. **Physical Placement** – Store the secondary device out of reach (e.g., in a drawer) when not in use, reinforcing the habit of not reaching for it automatically.

## Benefits
- **Reduced Cognitive Load:** Fewer apps mean fewer mental switches.
- **Predictable Availability:** Contacts know which channel to use for urgent communication.
- **Improved Well‑Being:** Evening routines become quieter, supporting better sleep and lower stress.
