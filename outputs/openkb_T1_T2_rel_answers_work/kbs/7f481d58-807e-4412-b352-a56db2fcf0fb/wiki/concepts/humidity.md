# Humidity

Humidity refers to the concentration of water vapor in the atmosphere. It influences how light interacts with the air, affecting visibility and the appearance of scenes, especially during early morning hours.

## Role in Morning Haze

- **Moisture and Light Scattering**: Higher humidity increases the number of tiny water droplets or aerosols that can scatter sunlight, leading to a soft, diffused look often described as a "soft focus" effect. This is a direct example of [[concepts/light-scattering]].
- **Temperature Interaction**: When the ground and near‑surface air are cool, they cannot hold as much moisture, allowing droplets to linger close to the surface. As the sun warms the air, humidity can decrease, causing the haze to dissipate.
- **Distinguishing Haze Types**: Persistent haze that feels damp and settles in valleys is typically driven by moisture (high humidity). In contrast, a dry‑to‑the‑touch veil that remains despite warming may indicate a [[concepts/temperature-inversion]] trapping aerosols rather than moisture.

## Practical Observations

1. **Feel**: A humid haze often feels moist on the skin and may leave surfaces damp.
2. **Behavior with Sun and Wind**: As sunlight heats the air, humidity‑driven haze thins and light breezes can disperse it quickly.
3. **Visual Cues**: Near‑by objects retain contrast when humidity is the primary cause, while distant objects appear washed out due to scattering.
