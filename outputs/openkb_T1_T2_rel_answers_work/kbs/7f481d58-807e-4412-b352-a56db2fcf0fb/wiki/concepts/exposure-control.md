# Exposure Control

**Definition**: Exposure control encompasses the methods and adjustments photographers use to balance the brightness of the sky and foreground, preventing washed‑out highlights while retaining detail in darker areas.

## Core Principles
1. **Tap‑to‑Expose** – On iPhone cameras, tap the brightest part of the sky to set the focus and metering point.
2. **Exposure Slider** – Drag the exposure slider down (approximately one‑third of the default) to recover sky texture and color.
3. **Iterative Balance** – If the foreground becomes too dark after lowering exposure, keep the sky tapped and raise the slider slightly until foreground shapes are visible without losing sky detail.
4. **HDR Usage** – Keep High Dynamic Range (HDR) enabled to blend multiple exposures automatically, giving a smoother transition between sky and ground.
5. **RAW/ProRAW Advantage** – Shooting in [[entities/proraw]] (preferably 48 MP) retains more highlight information, allowing greater latitude in post‑processing.

## Practical Guide from [[summaries/session-35_2025-03-16]]
- **If the sky looks blown out**: tap the sky and lower exposure until you see gradients and texture.
- **If the foreground is too dark**: maintain the sky tap, then raise exposure just enough to reveal foreground shapes.
- **If the image appears flat**: lower exposure a bit more; the increased dynamic range of ProRAW lets you lift shadows later.
- **Rule of thumb**: start with a one‑third reduction of exposure; adjust in small increments based on real‑time preview.

## Workflow for Sunrise Photography
1. **Preparation** – Ensure [[entities/iphone]] is set to ProRAW mode (48 MP for maximum detail).
2. **Initial Frame** – Position the camera, tap the bright sky, and apply the exposure reduction.
3. **Composition Check** – Verify that foreground subjects are still recognizable; fine‑tune exposure if needed.
4. **Capture** – Take the shot; HDR should remain on, Night mode off.
5. **Post‑Processing** – In editing software, increase shadow exposure if desired; the ProRAW file preserves highlight detail.
