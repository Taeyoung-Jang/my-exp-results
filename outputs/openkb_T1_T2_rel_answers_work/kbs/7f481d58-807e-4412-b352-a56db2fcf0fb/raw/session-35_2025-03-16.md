**Assistant:** You could keep it really simple: step outside just before sunrise, take a minute for a slow stretch of your neck, shoulders, and back, then start an easy walk around your block or along a route with a clear view of the horizon. Aim to start when the sky is just beginning to brighten so you can catch the transition from cool blue tones to warm light. During the walk, pause once or twice to photograph whatever catches your eye—clean skies work well for silhouettes, so try framing a tree, building edge, or lamppost against the brightening sky. If you’re using a phone, tap to expose for the brightest part of the sky so it doesn’t blow out; you can lift the shadows later. If your camera has it, use a lower ISO and a slightly higher shutter speed to keep everything crisp in the clear morning air.

When you get back home, do a quick one‑minute forward fold or gentle side stretch to settle your breathing, and you’re done. The whole thing can fit easily into 30 to 45 minutes, and because it’s simple and repeatable, it’s much easier to stick with.

On an iPhone, keep HDR on because it helps balance the bright sky and darker foreground. When you compose, tap the brightest part of the sky and gently drag the exposure slider downward to protect the highlights—this alone prevents that washed‑out look. If Night mode appears, turn it off at sunrise since it can make things look muddy. If you have ProRAW or RAW, turn it on for sunrise shots; it gives you more room to recover highlights later. Otherwise, the regular camera mode works fine as long as you expose for the sky. Keep your stance steady, hold the phone with both hands, and let the crisp air and clean light do most of the work.
