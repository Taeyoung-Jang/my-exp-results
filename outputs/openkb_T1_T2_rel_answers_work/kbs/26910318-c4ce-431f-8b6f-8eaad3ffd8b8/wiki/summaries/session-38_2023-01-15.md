# Summary of Session 38 (2023‑01‑15)

**Topic:** Winter maintenance for first‑year rosemary and lavender beds.

## Key Recommendations
- **Mulch:** Apply a light layer (straw, shredded leaves, pine needles, or pea gravel) but keep it 1–2 in away from stems to avoid moisture buildup. [[concepts/mulch_strategy]] [[entities/straw]] [[entities/shredded_leaves]] [[entities/pine_needles]] [[entities/pea_gravel]]
- **Spacing & Airflow:** Ensure at least a 6‑inch ring of mulch/gravel around each plant to promote airflow and prevent mildew.  
- **Watering:** Continue normal fall watering, then reduce as temperatures drop; give a moderate watering the day before an expected freeze to prevent root desiccation. [[concepts/herb_winter_care]]
- **Pruning:** Light shaping only; defer heavy pruning until spring when new growth appears.  
- **Frost Cloth:** Use breathable frost cloth during cold snaps, loosely draped over plants; remove during warm daytime periods to let foliage dry. [[entities/frost_cloth]] [[concepts/frost_protection]]
- **Gravel Mulch:** Pea gravel works; keep a gap from stems and use a ring rather than covering the whole bed. [[entities/pea_gravel]]
- **Cold‑Snap Warning Signs:**  
  - Lavender: drooping, darkened, or mushy stems.  
  - Rosemary: brittle, brown tips extending downward.  
- **Damage Management:** Brush off wet mulch, allow drying, and only trim clearly dead tissue. Wait until spring to prune browned tips unless tissue is mushy.  

## Practical Dimensions
- **Gravel Ring Width:** ~6 in for first‑year plants; can be widened as plants mature.  
- **Frost Cloth Application:** Can be draped directly; optional stakes for lift.

## Summary Insight
Proper drainage, minimal moisture retention, and breathable protection are the core strategies for helping rosemary and lavender survive winter without the need for potting or intensive intervention. [[concepts/herb_winter_care]]
