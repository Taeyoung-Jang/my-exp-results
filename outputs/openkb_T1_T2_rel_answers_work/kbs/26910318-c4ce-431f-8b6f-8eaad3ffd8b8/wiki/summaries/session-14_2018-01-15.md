# Summary of session‑14_2018-01-15

**Topic:** Planning outdoor projects and home‑maintenance for winter conditions.

**Key ideas**  
- **Flexibility & preparation** – build plans around a range of possible weather outcomes; keep backup routes and a go/no‑go threshold for activities. [[concepts/flexibility_in_winter_planning]]  
- **Pre‑winter (late fall) priorities** – clear gutters, inspect roof, seal drafts, insulate exposed plumbing, service furnace/boiler, ready outdoor tools. [[concepts/pre_winter_home_maintenance]]  
- **Right‑before‑first‑snow tasks** – final gutter/de‑spout check, store vulnerable items, ensure exterior drains are clear, close & drain outdoor water supplies, verify basement window‑well cleanliness.  
- **Winter‑time monitoring** – watch for ice dams, roof‑load stress, basement moisture, blocked vents, snow piling against siding or heat‑pump units. [[concepts/ice_dam_prevention]]  
- **Season‑specific timeline**  
  - *Late November*: finish all clearance, sealing, insulation, heating‑system service, gear prep.  
  - *Early December*: double‑check vents, drains, window wells; inspect roof after first snows for ice formation; set indoor humidity.  
  - *Monthly (Dec‑Mar)*: inspect for ice dams, assess snow load, check basement for dampness, ensure vents/sides aren’t obstructed.

**Suggested checklist format** – a concise, non‑rigid plan: prep window, button‑up window, then monitor throughout winter.

**Takeaway:** A structured timeline—late‑fall prep, early‑December final checks, and simple monthly monitoring—helps avoid costly winter damage and keeps outdoor hobbies safe.
