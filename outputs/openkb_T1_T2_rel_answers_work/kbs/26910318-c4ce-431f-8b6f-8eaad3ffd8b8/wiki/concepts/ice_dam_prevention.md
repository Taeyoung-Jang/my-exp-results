# Ice Dam Prevention

**Definition**: Strategies to stop the formation of ice dams on roofs by controlling heat loss, snow melt, and water runoff.

## Why Ice Dams Matter
Ice dams form when warm air from the attic melts snow on the roof, the water runs down the slope, refreezes at the eaves, and creates a ridge of ice. The ridge can block further runoff, forcing water under shingles and into the home, which can cause leaks, interior water damage, and structural issues.

## Key Prevention Steps (from [[summaries/session-14_2018-01-15]])
1. **Seal Drafts & Insulate** – Ensure attic and roof spaces are well‑sealed and insulated to keep heat from escaping into the roof deck. This limits snow‑melt.
2. **Check Roof Condition** – Before heavy snow, inspect for missing shingles, damaged vent chutes, or any areas where warm air could escape.
3. **Maintain Proper Ventilation** – Adequate intake and exhaust vents keep the roof surface close to outside temperature, reducing melt.
4. **Clear Gutters Early** – Clean gutters in late fall so melt water can flow freely and not back up under the eaves.
5. **Monitor Early Snowfall** – After the first substantial snow, watch roof edges for ice formation; early detection lets you address heat issues before a large dam develops.

## Ongoing Winter Monitoring
- **Monthly Checks** (Dec‑Mar): Look for ice buildup after each storm.
- **Snow Load Awareness**: Heavy, deep snow can increase melt pressure; ensure roof load isn’t excessive.
- **Attic Temperature**: Keep attic temperature close to outside temps to reduce melting.
