# Frost Protection

**Definition**: Frost protection comprises techniques that reduce plant exposure to damaging low temperatures, preventing tissue damage while avoiding excess moisture buildup.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Breathable Coverings**
   - Use a lightweight, breathable material such as a frost cloth. The cloth should be draped loosely over the plants, allowing air movement but cutting wind chill. [[entities/frost_cloth]]
   - Keep the covering off the ground when possible, but direct contact is acceptable if the material is not airtight.
   - Remove the cloth during daytime periods when temperatures rise above freezing and the sun is out, allowing foliage to dry and receive light.

2. **Mulch Management**
   - Apply a light mulch layer (e.g., pine needles, shredded leaves, straw, or pea gravel) *away* from the plant stems (1‑2 in gap) to avoid trapping moisture against woody tissue. [[concepts/mulch_strategy]]
   - Mulch helps moderate soil temperature swings but should not become a water‑logged barrier.

3. **Airflow and Spacing**
   - Ensure adequate spacing between plants (≈6 in for first‑year rosemary and lavender) to promote airflow, reducing the risk of frost‑induced dampness and mildew. [[concepts/herb_winter_care]]

---

## Practical Application for Rosemary and Lavender

- **Covering**: Drape a frost cloth directly over rosemary and lavender during a predicted frost week, especially when wind speeds are high. Lift the cloth slightly with stakes if convenient, but tight sealing is unnecessary.
- **Timing**: If daytime temperatures exceed 32 °F (0 °C) and sunlight is present, uncover the plants to let them dry. Keep the cloth on through prolonged cold, cloudy periods.
- **Mulch Choice**: Pine needles or shredded leaves are ideal for quick drying; pea gravel is also suitable and provides a dry surface without insulating the soil excessively.
- **Warning Signs**: After a cold snap, check for drooping or mushy lavender stems and brittle brown tips on rosemary. If these appear, clear wet mulch and trim only clearly dead tissue.
