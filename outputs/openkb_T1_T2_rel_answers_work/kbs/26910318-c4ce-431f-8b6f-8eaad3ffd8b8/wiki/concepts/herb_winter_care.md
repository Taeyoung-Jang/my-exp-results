# Herb Winter Care

**Definition**: A set of horticultural practices aimed at ensuring the health and survival of herb plants—particularly first‑year [[entities/rosemary]] and [[entities/lavender]]—through the colder months.

## Core Elements

### Mulch Strategy
- Use a **light** mulch layer of [[entities/straw]], [[entities/shredded_leaves]], or [[entities/pine_needles]] placed **1–2 in** away from the plant stems to prevent moisture accumulation.
- [[entities/pea_gravel]] can serve as an alternative or supplemental mulch; apply it as a **ring** around each herb, keeping a small gap from the woody base.
- The goal is to moderate soil temperature while maintaining good drainage, aligning with the broader [[concepts/mulch_strategy]].

### Spacing & Airflow
- Maintain at least a **6‑inch** mulch or gravel ring around each plant to promote airflow and reduce the risk of mildew.
- Adequate spacing between plants improves winter [[concepts/frost_protection]] by allowing wind to circulate and preventing damp micro‑environments.

### Watering
- Continue regular fall watering, then **reduce** as temperatures drop.
- Provide a moderate watering the day **before an anticipated freeze** to protect roots from desiccation.

### Pruning
- Perform only **light shaping** during winter; defer heavier pruning until spring when new growth begins.
- This practice minimizes stress and follows the principle of delayed [[concepts/frost_protection]].

### Frost Cloth Use
- Deploy a breathable [[entities/frost_cloth]] during cold snaps. Drape it loosely over the herbs, optionally lifting it with stakes for better air flow.
- Remove the cloth during warm daytime periods to allow foliage to dry and receive light.
- This approach balances protection against wind‑driven chill with moisture management, a core aspect of [[concepts/frost_protection]].

### Warning Signs & Damage Management
- **Lavender**: drooping, darkened, or mushy stems.
- **Rosemary**: brittle, brown tips that extend downward.
- If mushy tissue appears, trim it out promptly; otherwise, **wait until spring** to prune brown tips, allowing the plant a chance to recover.
