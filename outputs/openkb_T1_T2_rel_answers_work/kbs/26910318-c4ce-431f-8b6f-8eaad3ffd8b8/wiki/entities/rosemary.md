# Rosemary

Rosemary is a woody, aromatic herb native to the Mediterranean region. It is commonly grown in herb beds for culinary and ornamental purposes.

## Winter Care Recommendations

- **Drainage & Soil**: Rosemary tolerates cold but dislikes sitting in soggy ground. Ensure the soil remains well‑drained throughout winter. [[concepts/herb_winter_care]]
- **Mulch**: Apply a light layer of mulch (e.g., [[entities/pine_needles]], [[entities/shredded_leaves]], or [[entities/straw]]) but keep it 1–2 in away from the stems to prevent moisture buildup. [[concepts/mulch_strategy]]
- **Gravel Mulch**: A ring of [[entities/pea_gravel]] about six inches from the stem works well for first‑year plants, providing good airflow while keeping the base dry.
- **Watering**: Continue normal fall watering, then reduce as temperatures drop. Give a moderate watering the day before an expected freeze to avoid root desiccation.
- **Frost Protection**: Use a breathable [[entities/frost_cloth]] during cold snaps. Drape it loosely over the plants and remove it during warm daytime periods. [[concepts/frost_protection]]

## Warning Signs of Cold Damage

- **Brittle, brown tips** extending downward on rosemary stems.
- **Mushy or collapsed tissue** (requires immediate removal).

If brown tips appear, wait until spring to prune, as some tissue may recover with new growth.

## Pruning

- Perform only light shaping in winter if needed.
- Defer heavy pruning until spring when new growth begins.

## Companion Plants

Rosemary is often planted alongside [[entities/lavender]]; both share similar soil and winter‑care requirements.
