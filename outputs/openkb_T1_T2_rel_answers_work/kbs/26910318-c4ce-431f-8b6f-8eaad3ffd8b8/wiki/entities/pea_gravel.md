# Pea Gravel

Pea gravel is a rounded, small‑sized stone (typically ¼–⅜ inch) often used as a decorative and functional ground cover. In the context of winter herb care, it serves as a **mulch** option that promotes drainage while providing a clean, low‑maintenance surface.

## Key Points from [[summaries/session-38_2023-01-15]]
- Recommended for first‑year [[entities/rosemary]] and [[entities/lavender]] beds as an alternative to organic mulches.
- Apply as a **ring** about six inches out from the stem, leaving a 1–2 inch gap between the gravel and the plant base to avoid moisture trapping.
- Pea gravel does not compact heavily, allowing water to move through easily, which helps prevent root rot in cold, wet conditions.
- No need to cover the entire bed; a localized ring is sufficient unless the whole area needs improved drainage.

## Practical Tips
- Ensure the gravel layer is **light**; excessive depth can reduce soil temperature buffering.
- Pair with breathable frost protection (e.g., [[entities/frost_cloth]]) during severe cold snaps if wind exposure is high.
- Monitor for any signs of moisture buildup against stems and adjust the gap as needed.
