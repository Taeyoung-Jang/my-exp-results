### Summary

This document provides practical advice for designing a nighttime routine that sticks, focusing on:

- **Anchor habit** – a single, repeatable cue (e.g., “lights down and phone parked at 9 PM”) that marks the transition to wind‑down. [[concepts/anchor_habit]]
- **Screen and caffeine cut‑offs** – recommending a ~1 hour screen shutdown before bed and a 2 PM caffeine limit to protect sleep quality. 
- **Wind‑down routine** – a short, flexible set of calming activities (shower, stretch, reading) that can be compressed on late‑work or stressful evenings. [[concepts/wind_down_routine]]
- **Habit stacking** – building the routine around the anchor by adding one small change at a time, rather than overhauling everything at once. [[concepts/habit_stacking]]
- **Flexible scheduling** – sample timelines for a *normal night* (7:30 PM – 10 PM) and a *late‑work/stressed night*, showing how the same anchor can be retained while compressing other steps. 
- **Alarm and emergency handling** – keeping the phone on a charger in a set spot, using Do‑Not‑Disturb with exceptions, so the anchor works even when the phone serves as an alarm. 
- **Alternative anchors** – options like “wash face” or “dim lights” that can serve as the cue when a fixed time isn’t feasible, ensuring the routine remains adaptable. 

**Key takeaways**

1. Start with one clear anchor habit; everything else revolves around it.
2. Make changes incremental and realistic.
3. Preserve the anchor even on irregular evenings to maintain routine continuity.
4. Adjust screen and caffeine exposure to support the anchor and sleep timing.

These principles aim to create a sustainable evening flow that accommodates occasional deviations without derailing overall sleep health.
