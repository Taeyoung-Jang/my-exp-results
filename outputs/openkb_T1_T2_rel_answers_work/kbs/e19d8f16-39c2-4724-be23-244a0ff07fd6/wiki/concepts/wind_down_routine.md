# Wind‑Down Routine

A **wind‑down routine** is a short, adaptable sequence of low‑stimulus activities performed shortly before bedtime to signal the body and mind that it is time to transition to sleep. It is designed to be simple enough to maintain consistently, even on nights when schedules shift.

---

## Core Elements (from [[summaries/session-06_2024-09-21]])
- **Timing:** Typically occupies the last 30‑45 minutes before lights‑out, but can be compressed to as little as five minutes on stressful or late‑work evenings.
- **Activities:** Examples include a warm shower or rinse, light stretching, reading a few pages, or brief deep‑breathing exercises. The key is that the activity is calming and can be done anywhere.
- **Flexibility:** The routine can be shortened or modified without breaking the overall habit, preserving the anchor cue.

---

## Relationship to Other Concepts
- **[[concepts/anchor_habit]]** – The wind‑down routine builds around a single, repeatable anchor (e.g., “lights down and phone parked at 9 PM”). The anchor marks the start of the wind‑down phase.
- **[[concepts/habit_stacking]]** – The routine exemplifies habit stacking: the anchor habit is stacked with one or more calming actions, added incrementally to avoid overwhelming change.

---

## Typical Schedule (Normal Night)
| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| 7:30 – 8:00 | Finish dinner / wrap up evening tasks |
| 8:00 – 9:00 | Light leisure (chat, low‑stimulus media) |
| **9:00** | **Anchor:** lights dim, phone placed on charger (anchor habit) |
| 9:00 – 9:45 | Wind‑down routine: shower → stretch → read |
| 9:45 – 10:00 | In‑bed quiet time, lights out |

---

## Compressed Schedule (Late‑Work / Stressed Night)
When time is limited, retain the anchor at 9 PM, then opt for a brief version of the routine (e.g., a five‑minute warm rinse or slow breathing) before heading to bed.

---

## Implementation Tips
1. **Choose a simple anchor** that you can trigger reliably each evening.
2. **Select 1‑2 calming activities** that you enjoy and can perform in any environment.
3. **Start with a short duration** (e.g., 5‑10 minutes) and gradually extend as the habit solidifies.
4. **Allow flexibility**: on nights when you work later, keep the anchor but truncate the routine.
5. **Consistency over length** – the routine’s power lies in its regular occurrence, not its duration.

---

## Benefits
- Reduces physiological arousal, easing the transition to sleep.
- Strengthens the cue‑response loop of the anchor habit, improving overall routine adherence.
- Provides a mental cue that signals the end of the day, helping manage stress and screen exposure.
