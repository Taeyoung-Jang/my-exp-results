# Anchor Habit

**Definition**: An *anchor habit* is a simple, consistent action performed at roughly the same time each evening that signals the brain to shift into a wind‑down mode. It serves as the cornerstone of a nighttime routine, allowing other habits to be built around it.

## Core Characteristics
- **Simplicity**: Must be easy to remember and execute (e.g., dimming lights, placing the phone on a charger, washing the face).
- **Consistency**: Performed at the same moment each night, even on irregular evenings.
- **Signal Function**: Acts as a mental cue that the day is ending, prompting the body to prepare for sleep.

## Example from [[summaries/session-06_2024-09-21]]
The source conversation proposes "lights down and phone parked at 9 PM" as an anchor habit. This cue combines two actions:
1. **Lighting cue** – dimming lights reduces blue‑light exposure and signals relaxation.
2. **Phone parking** – placing the phone on a charger in a designated spot, switching it to Do‑Not‑Disturb, while still allowing alarms and emergency calls.

## Building a Routine Around the Anchor
- **Habit stacking**: Once the anchor is set, additional small actions can be stacked before or after it (e.g., a quick stretch, a brief reading session). See [[concepts/habit_stacking]] for the underlying principle.
- **Wind‑down routine**: The anchor anchors the broader wind‑down sequence, which may include showering, stretching, or reading. See [[concepts/wind_down_routine]] for how these activities fit together.

## Flexible Anchors
If a fixed time isn’t feasible, the anchor can be *event‑based* (e.g., washing the face as soon as you feel ready to stop working) or *environment‑based* (e.g., dimming lights whenever you decide to enter evening mode). The key is that the same cue consistently marks the transition, regardless of the clock time.

## Benefits
- **Reliability**: A single, clear cue is easier to maintain than a long checklist.
- **Scalability**: New habits can be added incrementally without overhauling the whole routine.
- **Resilience**: Even on late‑work or stressful nights, preserving the anchor keeps the routine from collapsing.
