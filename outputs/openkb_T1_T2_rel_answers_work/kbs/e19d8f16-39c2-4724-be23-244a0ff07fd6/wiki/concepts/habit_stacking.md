# Habit Stacking

**Definition**: Habit stacking is the practice of attaching a new, desired behavior to an already‑established routine cue, creating a chain of actions that reinforce each other.

## Core Idea
The concept hinges on leveraging an automatic, reliable trigger (the *anchor habit*) to cue a secondary activity. By doing so, the new behavior inherits the stability of the existing habit, making it easier to adopt and sustain.

## How It Appears in Session 06
In the conversation from [[summaries/session-06_2024-09-21]], the assistant advises starting with a single anchor habit—"lights down and phone parked at 9 PM"—and then layering additional steps (e.g., shower, stretch, reading) around that cue. This incremental approach exemplifies habit stacking: rather than overhauling the entire evening at once, each new element is added one at a time, anchored to the same nighttime trigger.

## Practical Steps
1. **Identify a stable cue** – choose an action you already perform consistently (e.g., brushing teeth, turning off lights). See [[concepts/anchor_habit]] for examples.
2. **Select a tiny new habit** – keep the addition simple and brief (e.g., five minutes of deep breathing).
3. **Pair them** – perform the new habit immediately after the cue each night.
4. **Iterate** – once the new habit feels automatic, add another, always linking it to the same anchor.

## Benefits
- **Reduced friction**: The cue removes decision‑making effort for the new behavior.
- **Higher adherence**: The existing habit’s reliability boosts the likelihood of repeat performance.
- **Scalable**: Multiple layers can be built over time without overwhelming the person.

## Relation to Other Concepts
- The **anchor habit** serves as the foundational cue for stacking (see [[concepts/anchor_habit]]).
- The stacked habits often form a **wind‑down routine**, a series of calming actions before sleep (see [[concepts/wind_down_routine]]).
