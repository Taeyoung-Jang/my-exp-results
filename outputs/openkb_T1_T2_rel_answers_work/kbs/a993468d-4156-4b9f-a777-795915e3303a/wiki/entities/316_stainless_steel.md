# 316 Stainless Steel

**Type:** product

## Overview
316 stainless steel is an austenitic alloy containing molybdenum, which gives it superior resistance to chloride‑induced corrosion compared with the more common 304 grade. Because of this property it is the preferred material for fasteners, fittings, and other hardware exposed to salty, damp environments.

## Relevance to the coastal bungalow checklist
In the *session‑38_2024‑06‑15* checklist, 316 stainless steel is recommended for all exterior fasteners and hardware to mitigate the effects of **[[concepts/coastal_corrosion]]**. Using this alloy helps keep doors, hinges, railings, and gutter brackets from seizing or rusting, which in turn reduces the frequency of **[[concepts/maintenance_budgeting]]** expenses related to corrosion repair.

## Practical guidance from the document
- **First‑30‑day priority:** Rinse existing hardware and replace any non‑stainless fasteners with 316 stainless components. This aligns with the checklist’s “top 5 immediate items” for moisture and salt control. 
- **DIY vs professional cost:** Swapping a set of exterior screws with 316 stainless equivalents typically falls under the **[[concepts/DIY_vs_professional_costs]]** range of $20–$40 for materials, while hiring a contractor may add $100–$150 for labor.
- **Preventative maintenance:** Regularly inspect 316 stainless hardware for mechanical wear, but corrosion should remain negligible when combined with periodic salt‑water rinses and a corrosion‑resistant lubricant such as **[[entities/boeshield_t9]]**.
