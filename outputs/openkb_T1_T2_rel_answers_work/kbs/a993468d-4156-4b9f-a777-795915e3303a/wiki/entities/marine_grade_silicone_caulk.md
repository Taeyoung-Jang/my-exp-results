## Marine‑Grade Silicone Caulk

Marine‑grade silicone caulk is a high‑performance, flexible sealant designed to withstand the harsh conditions of salt‑air, damp coastal environments. It remains elastic despite temperature swings and UV exposure, creating a watertight barrier that resists mold, mildew, and salt corrosion.

### Key Facts from Session 38 (2024‑06‑15)
- **Primary use:** Sealing exterior joints, window and door frames, and any area where moisture intrusion is a risk in a coastal bungalow.
- **Recommended type:** Polyurethane or marine‑grade silicone (the document refers to it simply as “marine‑grade silicone caulk”).
- **Performance needs:** Must tolerate constant humidity, occasional salt spray, and wood swelling without cracking or losing adhesion.
- **Cost/DIY considerations:** Typically applied as a DIY task; a standard 10‑12 oz tube costs $5‑$10, with professional application adding $50‑$100 per linear foot if preferred.
- **Related maintenance concepts:** It is a core material for [[concepts/moisture_control]] and [[concepts/seasonal_maintenance]], especially in the first‑year checklist for coastal properties.
- **Synergy with other products:** Works alongside [[entities/316_stainless_steel]] fasteners and [[entities/boeshield_t9]] lubricants to form a comprehensive corrosion‑prevention system.

### Practical Guidance
1. **Surface preparation:** Clean the area of old caulk, salt residue, and loose paint. Allow the surface to dry fully before application.
2. **Application:** Use a caulking gun, applying a continuous bead. Smooth with a caulk‑shaping tool or a wet finger to ensure good contact.
3. **Curing:** Allow 24‑48 hours for full cure, keeping the sealed area dry and out of direct rain.
4. **Maintenance:** Inspect annually during seasonal maintenance; reapply where the caulk shows cracks, peeling, or mold growth.
