# Preventative Maintenance Checklist

A **preventative maintenance checklist** is a structured, recurring set of inspections, services, and minor repairs aimed at catching issues early, extending the life of building systems, and avoiding costly emergency fixes. The concept is detailed in [[summaries/session-38_2024-06-15]] and connects to budgeting, DIY decision‑making, moisture control, and seasonal planning.

## Core Elements

1. **Timing** – Split the first year into monthly focus blocks, then roll those tasks into a seasonal rhythm (spring, summer, fall, winter). This aligns with [[concepts/seasonal_maintenance]].
2. **Scope** – Target the major systems first (roof, gutters, drainage, HVAC, plumbing, electrical) before moving to routine upkeep. Early inspection of these systems is the backbone of [[concepts/preventative_maintenance]].
3. **Budgeting** – Allocate **1‑2 % of the home’s value per year** for maintenance, with higher percentages for older homes. This ties to [[concepts/maintenance_budgeting]].
4. **Cost Decision** – Distinguish tasks that are feasible as DIY (often free or low‑cost) from those that merit a professional (higher reliability, code compliance). See [[concepts/DIY_vs_professional_costs]] for cost ranges.
5. **Tools & Spares** – Keep a core toolkit (adjustable wrench, screwdriver set, plunger, flashlight, step stool, spare bulbs, furnace filters, exterior‑grade caulk, plumber’s tape) on hand to handle most minor issues.

## Example Month‑by‑Month Outline (from Session 38)

- **Month 1** – Roof/gutter/drainage inspection, salt‑rinse exterior hardware, oil hinges, dehumidifier setup, HVAC service, mold check. DIY cost ≤ $20; professional $150‑$300.
- **Month 2** – Plumbing inspection for corrosion and sweating pipes; replace corroded lines if needed.
- **Month 3** – Electrical panel and exterior fixture audit; replace rusted outlets.
- **Months 4‑5** – Gutter cleaning after storms, HVAC filter change, caulk inspection and touch‑up, dehumidifier operation.
- **Months 6‑8** – Crawlspace/basement moisture check, under‑sink inspections, exterior hardware rinse, paint touch‑ups.
- **Months 9‑12** – Pre‑winter gutter clear, attic damp‑spot inspection, door/window swelling assessment, final hardware rinse.

## Coastal Adaptations

For properties like a [[entities/coastal_bungalow]] exposed to salt air and high humidity, the checklist adds emphasis on:
- **Corrosion mitigation** – Use [[entities/316_stainless_steel]] fasteners and [[entities/marine_grade_silicone_caulk]] for exterior sealing. Relates to [[concepts/coastal_corrosion]].
- **Moisture control** – Deploy a [[entities/dehumidifier_35_50_pt]] sized unit (35‑50 pint/day) and monitor indoor humidity (45‑55 %). Links to [[concepts/moisture_control]].
- **Lubrication** – Apply marine‑grade silicone spray or [[entities/boeshield_t9]] on hinges and latches.

## Benefits
- **Risk reduction** – Early detection of roof leaks, pipe corrosion, or electrical rust prevents cascade failures.
- **Cost control** – By following the DIY vs professional guidance, owners keep annual spend within the 1‑2 % budget.
- **Longevity** – Regular upkeep of critical systems and protective finishes extends the useful life of the home.

## Implementation Tips
- **Create a calendar** – Mark the monthly tasks and seasonal reminders.
- **Track expenses** – Log actual costs to refine future budgeting.
- **Adjust for climate** – In damp, salty areas, increase frequency of hardware rinsing and caulk inspection.

The preventative maintenance checklist thus serves as a practical framework for homeowners to transition from reactive repairs to proactive care, integrating financial planning, technical knowledge, and climate‑specific considerations.
