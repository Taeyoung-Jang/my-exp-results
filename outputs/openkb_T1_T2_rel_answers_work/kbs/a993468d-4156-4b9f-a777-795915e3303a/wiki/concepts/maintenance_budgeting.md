# Maintenance Budgeting for Older Homes

**Definition**: A budgeting approach that allocates a predictable share of a home's value each year to cover routine upkeep, repairs, and preventive measures, especially for older properties that are prone to aging systems and hidden issues.

## Core Principle
- **Percentage Rule**: Allocate **1 %–2 % of the home’s current market value per year**. Older houses typically sit at the higher end (≈ 2 %) because systems such as roof, HVAC, plumbing, and electrical are more likely to need early attention.

## Why It Matters
- Provides a realistic financial cushion beyond mortgage payments.
- Reduces the risk of costly emergency repairs by funding **preventative maintenance**.
- Aligns with a practical rent‑vs‑own decision framework that weighs ongoing responsibility against lifestyle preferences.

## Key Elements from [[summaries/session-38_2024-06-15]]
1. **Annual Range** – The session recommends the 1‑2 % rule, noting that actual spend can vary year‑to‑year (some years low, others high after a roof or HVAC issue).
2. **First‑Year Focus** – Front‑load inspections of roof/gutters, HVAC, plumbing, and electrical systems to identify hidden problems before they cascade.
3. **Cost Categorization** – Distinguish between **DIY** tasks (e.g., gutter cleaning, caulking) and **professional** services (e.g., HVAC tune‑up, electrical safety inspection). This aligns with the [[concepts/DIY_vs_professional_costs]] concept.
4. **Tool Kit** – A small set of tools (adjustable wrench, screwdriver set, plunger, flashlight, step stool, spare bulbs, furnace filters, exterior‑grade caulk, plumber’s tape) enables many low‑cost fixes.

## Practical Steps to Apply the Budget
| Step | Action | Typical Cost (DIY) | Typical Cost (Pro) |
|------|--------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | Estimate home value (online tools, appraisal) | $0 | $100‑$300 |
| 2 | Calculate 1‑2 % of that value | – | – |
| 3 | Allocate funds into categories: **Preventive** (filters, caulk), **Routine** (gutter cleaning, minor repairs), **Reserve** (major unexpected repairs). | – | – |
| 4 | Review annually; adjust based on actual spend and any major upgrades.

## Interaction with Related Concepts
- **[[concepts/preventative_maintenance]]**: The budgeting framework funds early inspections and minor repairs that keep larger systems healthy.
- **[[concepts/DIY_vs_professional_costs]]**: Understanding when to DIY vs hire a pro helps stretch the budget.
- **[[concepts/moisture_control]]**: In damp or coastal environments, extra allocation may be needed for dehumidifiers, corrosion‑resistant hardware, and marine‑grade caulk.
- **[[concepts/seasonal_maintenance]]**: Seasonal task lists (spring gutter cleaning, fall HVAC checks) should be budgeted within the annual allocation.

## Example Calculation
- Home value: $250,000
- Budget range: $2,500 – $5,000 per year
- Suggested split: 40 % Preventive ($1,000‑$2,000), 30 % Routine ($750‑$1,500), 30 % Reserve ($750‑$1,500)

## Takeaway
A disciplined **1 %‑2 % budgeting rule** paired with a proactive maintenance calendar helps owners of older homes avoid surprise expenses, maintain property value, and enjoy the stability that home ownership promises.
