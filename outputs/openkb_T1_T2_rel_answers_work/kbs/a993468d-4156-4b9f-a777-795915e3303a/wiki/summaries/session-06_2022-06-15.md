## Overview
The conversation outlines how living at higher elevations affects everyday activities such as **breathing**, **exercise**, **sleep**, **cooking**, and **hydration**, and provides practical advice for short‑term exposure and acclimatization. See the full source in [[summaries/session-06_2022-06-15]].

## Noticeable Elevation Effects
- Effects become perceptible for most people around **1500–2000 m** above sea level. [[concepts/elevation_effects]]
- Immediate symptoms include faster breathing, higher heart rate, and reduced exercise tolerance.

## Adaptation Timeline
- **Initial adjustment**: a few days as ventilation and heart output increase.
- **Physiological adaptation** (e.g., increased red blood cell production) takes **2–3 weeks**. [[concepts/acclimatization]]

## Signs of Poor Adaptation vs Normal Adjustment
- **Normal**: mild shortness of breath on exertion, slight fatigue, light headache that eases with rest.
- **Warning signs** (potential altitude sickness): persistent headache, nausea, dizziness, severe fatigue, or breathlessness at rest. [[concepts/altitude_sickness]]

## Practical Strategies for Short Trips (2000–3000 m)
- **Take it easy on arrival**: limit intense activity for the first afternoon.
- **Hydration**: drink water steadily; dehydration worsens symptoms.
- **Sleep**: secure a good night’s rest before strenuous activity.
- **Caffeine**: generally acceptable; may increase perception of breathing but does not raise sickness risk.
- **Alcohol**: avoid or limit the first night; it dehydrates and can aggravate headaches and sleep disturbance.

## Pacing Advice from 600 m to 2500–3000 m
1. **Arrival day**: move normally but avoid activities that spike heart rate; eat light, stay hydrated, and prioritize sleep.
2. **Next‑morning hike**: start slower than usual, warm up gradually, monitor breathing and heart effort.
3. **Monitor symptoms**: distinguish mild adjustment from red‑flag signs.

## Red‑Flag Symptoms to Stop or Descend
- Strong, persistent headache  
- Nausea or vomiting  
- Dizziness or unsteady gait  
- Marked weakness or fatigue at rest  
- Worsening shortness of breath  

Recognizing these symptoms and adjusting activity level promptly can prevent progression to more severe altitude illness.
