**User:** I’m trying to get more intentional about how I use caffeine and alcohol week-to-week—less on autopilot, more based on what actually helps me sleep and feel good. If you were helping someone “audit” their habits without going extreme, what would you ask them to track for a couple weeks, and what patterns would you look for?

**Assistant:** I’d keep it simple and focus on just a few things that meaningfully shift how someone feels. For a couple of weeks, I’d have them note roughly how much caffeine they had, when they had their last cup, how their energy felt through the day, and how easy it was to fall asleep at night. Same idea for alcohol: what they drank, how much, timing, how they felt the next morning, and whether sleep felt restful or fragmented.

I’d also have them jot down anything unusual about their day that might influence things, like stress, exercise, or a late meal, just so they don’t give caffeine or alcohol credit (or blame) for something unrelated.

After two weeks, I’d look for patterns like whether caffeine after a certain hour reliably leads to trouble falling asleep, whether alcohol changes sleep quality even if it doesn’t change sleep duration, whether “just one drink” has a noticeable next‑day mood or energy effect, or whether they actually feel better with a bit less overall than they assumed.

The goal isn’t strict rules—just noticing the few levers that clearly move the needle so they can use them intentionally rather than out of habit.

The idea isn’t to overhaul anything—just nudge the timing or amount and see if your sleep and energy respond.
