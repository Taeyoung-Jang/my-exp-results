**User:** I’m trying to redesign my workout routine to be more joint-friendly while I still build strength—what are some good low-impact exercises and weekly structure ideas that won’t wreck my knees?

**Assistant:** You can get a very solid strength‑building routine without beating up your knees by focusing on controlled, low‑impact movements and a schedule that spreads out stress through the week.

For strength work, think about exercises that load the muscles without sharp compression forces on the joints. Squats to a box or chair, hip hinges like Romanian deadlifts with light to moderate weight, glute bridges or hip thrusts, step‑ups to a low platform, and slow, controlled lunges or split‑squats with a shorter stride all tend to be much friendlier on the knees. Machines like leg presses with a comfortable range of motion, cable work, and any upper‑body strength training are also easy to keep low impact. For conditioning, rowing, cycling, elliptical work, swimming, and incline walking are great options.

A simple weekly structure could look like alternating days of full‑body strength training and low‑impact cardio. For example, you might strength‑train on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday using moderate weights and slow, controlled reps, then use Tuesday and Thursday for low‑impact cardio or mobility work. Weekends can be lighter—maybe an easy bike ride, a swim, or a yoga session to keep things moving without overloading anything.

As long as you keep the volume manageable, move through pain‑free ranges, and focus on smooth, controlled tempo, you can keep building strength while giving your knees a break.

**User:** My knee hasn't fully stabilized since the tear, so I'm avoiding any activities that put heavy pressure on the joint for the time being. With that in mind, could you suggest a specific 3-day strength plan (exercise list + sets/reps) plus 2 low-impact cardio days that emphasize hips/glutes and upper body, and include knee-friendly substitutions if something flares up?

**Assistant:** Here’s a simple, knee‑friendly structure you can follow without putting heavy pressure on the joint. I’ll keep everything in plain sentences so it stays easy to read.

For Day 1 strength, focus on hips and upper body. You can do hip thrusts for three sets of eight to twelve reps, supported Romanian deadlifts with dumbbells for three sets of eight to ten, seated cable rows for three sets of ten to twelve, chest presses for three sets of eight to twelve, and side‑lying hip abductions for two or three sets of twelve to fifteen. If your knee feels touchy, you can swap the RDLs for back‑supported glute bridges to reduce balance demands.

For Day 2 strength, keep it mostly upper body with light posterior‑chain work. You can do lat pulldowns for three sets of ten to twelve, dumbbell overhead presses for three sets of eight to ten, incline dumbbell rows for three sets of ten to twelve, band pull‑aparts for two or three sets of fifteen, and reverse hypers or gentle back extensions for two sets of ten to twelve. If anything stresses the knee during setup, switch to machines that let you stay seated.

For Day 3 strength, return to hips and glutes with minimal knee load. You can do single‑leg glute bridges for three sets of eight to twelve per side, cable kickbacks for three sets of twelve to fifteen, lying hamstring curls for three sets of eight to twelve, chest‑supported rows for three sets of ten to twelve, and biceps or triceps work for two sets of ten to twelve. If hamstring curls bother the knee, switch to slow, controlled stability‑ball curls where you can limit the range.

For your two low‑impact cardio days, choose options that keep the knee quiet. A twenty to thirty minute session on a stationary bike with light resistance usually works well, or you can do the same duration on a rowing machine with a smooth, moderate pace. If the knee complains with either, swimming or deep‑water running are safe substitutes.

All of this should stay pain‑free. If anything flares up, shorten the range of motion or switch to a floor‑based or machine‑based version that doesn’t require knee stabilization.

**User:** This looks workable—can you lay it out as a weekly schedule (Mon–Sun) with suggested rest days, and include simple progression rules for 4–6 weeks (how to add reps/weight without aggravating the knee)?

**Assistant:** You can follow a simple Monday‑through‑Sunday flow without turning it into a rigid checklist. Here’s a smooth, conversational version that still gives you clear structure.

Start your week on Monday with your Day 1 strength session. Tuesday becomes your first low‑impact cardio day. Wednesday is your Day 2 strength session. Thursday is an easy rest or mobility day. Friday is your Day 3 strength session. Saturday works well for your second low‑impact cardio session. Sunday can be full rest or just gentle stretching if you like something light.

Progression can stay very simple so you don’t irritate the knee. For the first week or two, keep all weights on the lighter side and aim to perform the upper end of each rep range only if it feels smooth and stable. Once an exercise feels consistently easy for two sessions in a row and the knee stays calm, you can add a small amount of weight, usually something like a one to five pound bump for dumbbells or the next small plate on a machine. If adding weight feels off, increase reps instead by just one or two per set. Keep changes small so you never feel like you’re suddenly forcing the joint to handle something new.

If anything in a given week makes the knee tighten up or feel unstable, drop back to the last comfortable version you used, shorten the range of motion for a few days, or reduce the load slightly. As long as each progression step feels steady and you’re never rushing to make jumps, you can build strength consistently over four to six weeks without stirring up the joint.
