**User:** I could use some help thinking through what to prioritize when apartment hunting. I spent the afternoon on the phone with the estate agent, comparing listings for a one-bedroom—it's strange measuring rooms by how quiet they feel instead of how much space everyone needs. Can you help me make a shortlist of questions to ask on viewings (noise, neighbors, building stuff, commute, hidden costs), and maybe a simple way to score places so I don’t get swept up by a nice kitchen and forget the basics?

**Assistant:** You can think of the questions as a handful of themes you want to touch on during each viewing, then phrase them naturally as you walk around. For noise, you might ask the agent when the building is usually quiet, whether there are shared walls with bedrooms on the other side, and if there have been any past complaints about sound. For neighbors, it helps to ask who lives above and below, how long they’ve been there, and whether the building tends to turn over quickly. For the building itself, you can ask about recent maintenance, upcoming work, how responsive management is, and whether things like heating, insulation, or water pressure ever cause trouble. For the commute, it’s useful to describe your schedule and ask how reliable local transport is at those times. For hidden costs, you can ask what the average monthly utilities look like, whether there are service charges or move‑in fees, and what’s covered in the rent.

To keep yourself grounded, try a simple ten‑point score for each place. Before you go, choose the five things that matter most to you and assign each of them two possible points. After every viewing, give an honest gut‑level score for each category, add them up, and write a one‑sentence note about how the place felt. It’s quick, consistent, and stops a sleek kitchen from outweighing constant traffic noise or a long commute.
