**Assistant:** A quick way to vet a first‑date spot is to run through a few questions in your head. Ask yourself if the lighting feels friendly rather than dim or isolating, whether you can actually hear someone without shouting, and if it’s easy to get to by either parking or transit so no one feels stranded. Think about whether the space has natural “exit points,” like a place where a short meetup makes sense or where you could politely wrap up without awkwardness. And always choose somewhere public enough that you’d feel just as comfortable being there alone.

If you want a few messages you can send, you could say something like, “If you’re up for a quick meet, there’s a good bakery near me that’s easy to dip in and out of—want to try that?” Or, “We could grab a drink at a cozy spot in the arts district and see how we vibe—totally fine to keep it short.” Or, “There’s a boba place that’s usually pretty chill; happy to meet for 30 minutes and play it by ear.” Or even, “If you’d prefer something low‑key, I’m good with a short walk and a coffee—super easy to call it whenever.”

If someone pushes for coming over, a secluded hike, or anything that skips the public‑first step, the cleanest move is to stay calm and firm without being confrontational. You can say something like, “I’d rather meet somewhere public for a first hang—keeps things easy for both of us,” and then offer an alternative. If they keep pushing past that, it’s usually a sign you don’t need to meet them at all. Your boundary is normal, and anyone worth seeing will treat it that way.
