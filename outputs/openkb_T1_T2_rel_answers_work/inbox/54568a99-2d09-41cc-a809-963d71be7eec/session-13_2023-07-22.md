**User:** I’m redoing the lighting in my apartment and could use some advice on what to buy/setup. I have to stick to dimmer switches because bright lights consistently trigger my migraines. Any tips on the best types of bulbs/fixtures for a living room and home office (warm vs cool, lumens to aim for, smart dimmers vs regular), and how to avoid flicker or harsh glare?

**Assistant:** If migraines are a concern, the two biggest things to focus on are flicker‑free dimming and gentle, warm light. Most people with light sensitivity do better with warmer color temperatures, around 2700K to 3000K. That range feels calm and reduces the sharp, icy quality you get from cooler bulbs. In a living room, leaning toward 2700K usually feels cozy. In a home office, 3000K keeps things a bit crisper without getting harsh.

For brightness, think in general ranges rather than exact numbers since dimmers give you flexibility. Something around 700 to 900 lumens per fixture is usually plenty for a living room if you have more than one light source, and around 800 to 1100 lumens for a desk or overhead light in an office. The key is that you can turn it down easily when needed.

To avoid flicker, choose LEDs specifically labeled as flicker‑free or compatible with dimmers. Not every LED works well with every dimmer, so pairing matters. Lutron’s higher‑end dimmers (like Caseta for smart or Diva for regular) tend to be reliable with most LED bulbs, but it’s good to check the manufacturer’s compatibility list when possible.

Smart dimmers are great if you like presets, very smooth dimming curves, or voice or app control. They usually perform better with LEDs than old-school dimmers. If you prefer to keep things simple, a modern, LED‑rated regular dimmer is totally fine as long as the bulbs match it.

For fixtures, anything that diffuses the light is your friend. Shades, frosted glass, and indirect lighting all soften glare. Avoid exposed LED diodes, very shiny reflectors, or very small point-source bulbs.
