**User:** I’m updating a bunch of official profiles and forms and I keep getting tripped up by the “citizenship/immigration status” section—what’s the safest way to answer those questions consistently across job applications, school records, and government forms without over- or under-sharing?

**Assistant:** A good rule of thumb is to match your answer to exactly what the form is asking for and not volunteer anything extra. Each form usually has its own defined categories, so the safest approach is to choose the option that best matches your legal status as it appears on your official documents and use that same wording everywhere.

If a form asks for “citizenship,” use the citizenship shown on your passport or birth documents. If it asks for “immigration status,” use the status printed on your immigration paperwork or approval notice. If it gives multiple choices, pick the one that corresponds to your current, official status rather than trying to explain your situation in detail. When a form offers an open text box, keep it short and stick to the terminology used by the agency that issued your documents.

If you’re ever unsure which term applies to you, checking your most recent official document or asking the issuing agency for clarification is the safest way to stay consistent without accidentally misreporting anything.

**User:** Okay—so for most places I should just select “U.S. citizen” and leave it at that, and only provide proof (like a passport or naturalization certificate) if they specifically request documentation, right?

**Assistant:** Yes—if “U.S. citizen” is accurate for you, selecting that option is normally all you need to do. Most applications don’t require you to upload or show proof unless they explicitly ask for it, and you don’t need to offer documents on your own.

As long as you answer truthfully and consistently, you’re doing it right. If a specific agency or employer needs verification, they’ll tell you exactly what to provide and when.

**User:** One more thing: when a form asks “Are you a U.S. citizen or national?” should I just answer “Yes,” or is there any situation where I should specify “citizen” vs “national”?

**Assistant:** You can simply answer “Yes” if the question is phrased that way. Forms that ask “Are you a U.S. citizen or national?” are treating those two categories together, so they aren’t asking you to choose between them.

In practice, almost everyone who is eligible to say “Yes” is a U.S. citizen. U.S. nationals who are not citizens are a very small, specific group, and they usually already know their status because their documents say “U.S. national” instead of “U.S. citizen.”

So if your documents say you are a U.S. citizen, just answer “Yes” and leave it at that—no need to specify further unless a form separately asks you to.

**User:** You can list my status as naturalized citizen now that the process is complete. For forms that only offer a checkbox for “U.S. citizen” (and don’t mention “naturalized”), is it best to just select “U.S. citizen,” and only specify “naturalized citizen” if there’s a separate option or a free-text field asking how I obtained citizenship?

**Assistant:** Yes. If the only option is “U.S. citizen,” that’s the correct and sufficient choice for you now. Naturalized citizens and citizens by birth are treated the same for those purposes, so there’s no need to clarify unless the form specifically asks how you obtained citizenship or gives a separate field for it.

If a form does ask for the method of citizenship, then you can state “naturalized.” Otherwise, just selecting “U.S. citizen” is exactly what you’re expected to do.
