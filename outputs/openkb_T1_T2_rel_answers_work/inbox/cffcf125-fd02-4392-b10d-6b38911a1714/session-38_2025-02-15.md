**Assistant:** Using preservative‑free drops a few times a day is generally fine long‑term. They’re designed for repeated use and don’t carry the same risks that preserved drops do. The main “downside” is simply that if you suddenly need them far more often than usual, that can be a sign something else is going on and worth checking.

For nighttime lubricants, gels are lighter and cause mild blur for a short time, while ointments are thicker, last longer, and can blur vision into the morning. If you only need a little extra moisture, a gel is usually enough. If you routinely wake up very dry or your lids don’t fully close, an ointment tends to work better. If you’re unsure, starting with a gel is a safe middle ground.

As for warning signs, normal dryness feels irritating or gritty but not truly painful. If you get sharp pain, worsening redness, light sensitivity, a sudden drop in vision, or one eye acting very differently from the other, it’s better to get checked sooner rather than wait for your next routine visit.
