# Conversation session 7

Date: 2024-07-14 14:30

**User:** I’ve been thinking about how much our phones and apps track us these days—especially with location and all that. If I want to tighten up my privacy without breaking everyday stuff like maps, rideshare, or weather, what are the first settings or habits you’d recommend?

**Assistant:** A good starting point is to look at which apps actually need your precise location and which can work with a more limited version. Most phones let you switch an app from precise to approximate location, which still keeps things like weather and local search useful without giving away your exact movements. It also helps to set location access to “only while using the app” for anything that doesn’t require constant background tracking, like rideshare or maps.

Next, review your phone’s main privacy dashboard. Both iOS and Android have a section that shows which apps recently accessed your location, microphone, camera, and other sensitive data. If something surprises you, turn it off or limit it.

It’s also worth checking the permissions of apps you installed a long time ago. A lot of them request more access than they truly need simply because you tapped “allow” during setup.

Finally, audit background activity. Many apps quietly run in the background, collecting data even when you’re not actively using them. Disabling background refresh for apps that don’t need it will reduce tracking and often improve battery life too.

None of these changes should break the usual services you rely on, but they’ll cut down a lot of unnecessary data leak without making your phone hard to use.

**User:** One constraint: I keep my location services turned off for most apps by default. Given that, what should I set for the handful I actually rely on (maps, rideshare, weather), and are there any other high-impact privacy settings I should prioritize first?

**Assistant:** If you already keep location off by default, you’re most of the way there. For the apps you rely on, you can safely give them limited, situational access. Maps and rideshare work well with “allow while using the app,” and maps doesn’t need precise location unless you’re actively navigating, so you can toggle precision on only when needed. Weather apps usually work fine with approximate location, or you can simply set your city manually and leave location access off entirely.

Beyond that, the next settings that usually make the biggest difference are restricting background app refresh to just the essentials, turning off ad personalization on your phone and in your Google/Apple account, and reviewing which apps have access to your microphone, camera, photos, and contacts. Checking your phone’s privacy dashboard once in a while will catch anything trying to do more than it should without you noticing.

**User:** Can you be more specific per app type—like what exact permission combo you’d pick for Maps vs Uber/Lyft vs a weather app (precise vs approximate, while-using vs always, background refresh on/off)? Also, on iPhone vs Android, what are the top 3 non-location settings you’d change first to reduce tracking?

**Assistant:** For maps, I’d use “while using the app” and keep precise location on only when you’re actively navigating. The rest of the time, approximate location is usually enough for searching or browsing. Background refresh isn’t needed unless you rely on real‑time traffic alerts without opening the app.

For Uber or Lyft, “while using the app” is the safest default. They generally work fine with precise location only during an active ride or pickup. You don’t need “always” unless you use features that track your trip when the app is closed, which most people don’t. Background refresh can stay off unless you notice it slowing down the pickup process.

For weather apps, approximate location is usually more than enough, and many people just enter their city manually and turn location off entirely. They don’t need background refresh unless you want constant push alerts for severe weather.

On iPhone, the first three non‑location settings I’d tighten are turning off ad personalization in Settings → Privacy & Security → Apple Advertising, restricting tracking under “Allow Apps to Request to Track,” and limiting what apps can access your photos by switching them to “selected photos” instead of full library access.

On Android, I’d turn off ad personalization in the Google settings, limit background activity for apps that don’t need to run constantly, and review the permissions manager to cut back microphone, camera, and contacts access to only the apps that truly need them.

**User:** On Android specifically, where do I find the “limit background activity” controls you mentioned (is that per-app battery settings, background data, or something else)? And for iPhone, can you point me to the exact path for “selected photos” and any other permission that’s worth switching from “all the time” to “ask/while using”?

**Assistant:** On Android, the most useful “limit background activity” control is in each app’s battery settings. You open the app’s info page, tap Battery, and switch it to Restricted. That stops the app from running in the background unless you’re actively using it. Background data is separate and lives under Mobile Data; turning that off blocks an app’s mobile‑data use when it’s not open, but most people only need the battery restriction.

On iPhone, the “selected photos” option is in Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Photos. Choose an app and switch its access from All Photos to Selected Photos, then pick the images it can see. For permissions that are safer on “while using,” the typical ones to check are Location, Camera, Microphone, and Bluetooth. You can find all of them in Settings under Privacy & Security, open each category, pick an app, and change the setting to Ask or While Using the App if it doesn’t need full‑time access.
