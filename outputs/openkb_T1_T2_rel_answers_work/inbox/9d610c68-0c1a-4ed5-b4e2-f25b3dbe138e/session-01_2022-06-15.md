**User:** I’m trying to get better at planning a sprint when there’s a big “moving parts” initiative in the mix—what’s your approach for turning a messy migration-style project into a clear, trackable plan without it taking over the whole sprint?

**Assistant:** I usually start by treating the messy initiative as a separate “track” that runs alongside normal sprint work instead of trying to cram it into the sprint like a regular story.

First, I carve the big migration into the smallest meaningful milestones that actually produce a check‑in point: things like “environment ready,” “data model aligned,” “API contract validated,” not every tiny task. Then I make sure only the next milestone is pulled into the sprint, not the whole project. That keeps it visible without letting it dominate.

During the sprint, I keep the migration work time‑boxed and capacity‑limited. If the team has 60 hours of capacity, maybe only 15–20 hours go to the migration track. That prevents it from eating everything else.

The last piece is making progress extremely visible. A simple “current milestone → next milestone → blocked?” check during stand‑ups stops the work from drifting.

So the key moves are: define real milestones, only commit to one at a time, cap the capacity it can consume, and review it like its own mini‑project within the sprint. That usually keeps it clear and trackable without letting it take over.

**User:** That makes sense—can you help me translate that into something concrete for this sprint? We’re migrating a client integration: there’s a new API contract, data mapping changes, and a cutover plan. How would you structure the milestones and what would you actually put into the sprint backlog vs keep as “next milestone”?

**Assistant:** I’d frame the whole migration as a simple sequence of checkpoints and only pull the very next one into the sprint.

For this sprint, the cleanest “current milestone” is getting the new contract and mappings fully validated in your environment. That means the work you actually put in the sprint backlog is something like: implement the new contract in a stub or sandbox, run through the revised data mapping end‑to‑end, and confirm that both sides line up. That’s small enough to finish, visible enough to track, and unblocks everything that comes after. Any detailed cutover planning or actual migration steps stay parked as the next milestone. They exist, they’re visible, but they aren’t promises for this sprint.

Once the contract and mapping validation is done, the next milestone becomes preparing the cutover plan in detail and testing the migration path. But that only gets pulled in after the current milestone is finished, not at the start of the sprint. This keeps the sprint focused, gives you a clear definition of “done,” and stops the bigger project from swallowing the team’s capacity.
