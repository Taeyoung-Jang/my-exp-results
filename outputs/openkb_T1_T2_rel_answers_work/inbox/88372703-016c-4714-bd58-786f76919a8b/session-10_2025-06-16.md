**User:** Let’s say my main goal is general fitness with a bit of muscle gain (especially legs/back/shoulders) while keeping my swimming performance solid. I’ve got access to a basic gym (barbells, dumbbells, cable machine, squat rack) and I’m comfortable with the main lifts but not trying to max out. I can do ~45–60 minutes on strength days. Could you propose a concrete week plan (day-by-day) with example workouts and intensity guidelines, plus how you’d adjust if I start feeling worn down?

**Assistant:** Here’s a simple week that fits your swim schedule without leaning on lists. I’ll walk through it as a flow rather than bullets.

On Monday you swim in the evening, so keep the day otherwise easy. Treat the swim as your main workout and aim for moderate effort so you’re not starting the week already beat up.

On Tuesday you can do your primary strength session. A full‑body focus works well: something like squats or leg press as your main lower‑body lift, a pull movement such as weighted rows or pull‑ups for your back, and an overhead press for shoulders. Fill the remaining time with two or three accessory moves that support your swim muscles, such as hamstring work, lat pulldowns, or face pulls. Intensity should be moderate, around a weight you can manage for six to ten controlled reps.

On Wednesday you’re back in the pool. Keep it at a steady aerobic effort unless you’re feeling especially fresh.

On Thursday you can do a lighter strength day. Think of it as technique and volume rather than heavy loading. Use variations like split squats, Romanian deadlifts with a lighter barbell, cable rows, and light dumbbell shoulder work. Keep everything in the eight‑to‑twelve‑rep range, leaving a couple of reps in the tank. If you want a bit more cardio, finish with ten to fifteen minutes of easy machine work.

On Friday’s swim, decide based on feel whether it’s a moderate or slightly harder session. Since the next day is strength focused, avoid going all‑out on sprints.

On Saturday you can do your second strong lifting day. Make this one slightly heavier than Thursday but not quite as demanding as Tuesday. A deadlift or hip‑hinge variation works well here, paired with a horizontal push like bench or dumbbell press, and another pull for balance. Add a shoulder accessory and a short core piece if time allows.

On Sunday make it a rest day or just light movement like stretching or an easy walk.

If you start feeling worn down, the first adjustment is to dial back intensity on Tuesday and Saturday by choosing slightly lighter loads or trimming one main lift. You can also turn the Thursday session into pure mobility and very light weights for a week. If swim fatigue is the issue, shift Wednesday or Friday’s pool work to an easier aerobic pace. Over a couple of weeks, the goal is to feel like you’re finishing sessions with something still in reserve rather than grinding through them.
