**User:** I’ve been thinking about daily meal routines lately—how do people usually structure breakfast, lunch, and dinner to keep their energy steady? If someone wants a simple, realistic routine on a busy schedule, what would you suggest?

**Assistant:** Most people stay energized by keeping meals fairly steady and balanced rather than perfect. A simple routine on a busy schedule can be as basic as having a small, consistent breakfast with some protein and carbs—something like yogurt and fruit, eggs and toast, or even a smoothie you can take with you. Lunch is usually a bit heartier, built around a protein and something filling like rice, pasta, or greens so you don’t crash in the afternoon. Dinner can be similar but lighter if you don’t want to feel weighed down at night.

The easiest approach is to repeat a few go‑to meals you like, keep snacks such as nuts or fruit on hand in case your energy dips, and make sure you eat at roughly the same times each day. Consistency tends to matter more than perfect nutrition when you’re busy.

**Assistant:** If you’re eating only breakfast and dinner, the key is making breakfast steady enough to last and giving yourself a small bridge in the afternoon so your energy doesn’t tank.

A breakfast with some protein, some slow‑digesting carbs, and a little fat usually keeps you stable the longest. That could be something like eggs with toast, oatmeal with nuts, or a smoothie that isn’t just fruit but has yogurt or nut butter mixed in. You don’t need anything fancy—just something that digests slowly so you’re not running on sugar alone.

In the afternoon, a tiny snack can make a big difference. Even something quick like a handful of nuts, a piece of fruit, a cheese stick, or a protein bar is enough to smooth out the dip without feeling like a full lunch. Think of it as a “fuel top‑up” rather than a meal.

If you keep breakfast consistent, drink water through the day, and have that small snack before you hit the slump, you can usually avoid the crash even with a two‑meal routine.
