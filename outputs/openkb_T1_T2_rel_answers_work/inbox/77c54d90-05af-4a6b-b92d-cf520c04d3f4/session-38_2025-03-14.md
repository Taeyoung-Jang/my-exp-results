**Assistant:** I can help, as long as this stays general and not jurisdiction‑specific legal advice. Here’s a version you can hand to tenants that keeps things simple and easy to scan.

For the “red flags” checklist, you can format it as a short set of headings with quick explanations rather than full bullet points:

Rent increases: Watch for increases that don’t give clear notice periods, don’t match what was verbally discussed, or allow the landlord to raise rent “at any time” without a timeline. Anything vague or open‑ended is a reason to pause.

Deposits and fees: Look for deposits labeled in ways that allow the landlord to keep them for reasons other than damage or unpaid rent. If the agreement mentions “non‑refundable” deposits without saying what they cover, that’s worth questioning. Also keep an eye out for surprise administrative or processing fees.

Repairs and maintenance: Be careful with clauses that shift all repair responsibilities to the tenant, especially for things that are normally the landlord’s job, like structural issues, electrical problems, plumbing, or appliances that came with the unit. Wording that says repairs “may” be done by the landlord, or only “at landlord discretion,” is another sign to slow down.

Entry and inspection: Any clause that allows the landlord to enter without notice, except for emergencies, should be questioned. Tenants should always have clear notice requirements in writing.

Penalties and termination: Watch for clauses that impose large penalties for ending a lease early or that let the landlord terminate “for any reason” without notice. Clauses that make the tenant responsible for the landlord’s legal fees no matter what the dispute is can also be a concern.

And here are a few short message templates tenants can copy and paste. They’re polite, direct, and avoid sounding confrontational:

“Hi [Name], I noticed that the section on rent increases doesn’t specify the amount of notice I’ll receive. Could you clarify the notice period or adjust the wording so that it’s clearly defined? I just want to be sure we’re on the same page before signing.”

“Hi [Name], I saw that the deposit is listed as non‑refundable, but the agreement doesn’t explain what situations that applies to. Could you clarify what the deposit covers or update the language to reflect that? Thank you.”

“Hi [Name], I wanted to double‑check the repairs clause. As written, it sounds like I’d be responsible for repairs that are normally handled by the landlord. Could we clarify which repairs are the landlord’s responsibility and which are mine?”

“Hi [Name], the entry clause seems a little broad. Could we revise it so that it specifies advance notice for non‑emergency entry? I’d just like to have the expectations clear in writing.”
