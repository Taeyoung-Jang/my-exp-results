**User:** I’m starting a new job soon and I’m curious: what are the most useful things to do in the first week to make a strong impression without coming off as overeager?

**Assistant:** In that first week, the best approach is to be genuinely curious and steady rather than trying too hard to prove yourself. Take time to learn how things actually work day‑to‑day, pay attention to the team’s rhythms, and ask thoughtful questions that show you’re engaged without grilling anyone. Showing up prepared, being reliable, and following through on small things tends to make a bigger impression than big gestures.

It also helps to get to know people one‑on‑one. A quick chat over coffee or after a meeting can build rapport naturally. Showing appreciation when someone helps you goes a long way too.

If you focus on listening well, picking up context quickly, and being pleasant to work with, you’ll come across as confident and capable without looking like you’re trying to stand out.

**User:** Today was basically HR paperwork and compliance videos in a windowless room, so I didn’t really get much time with my actual team. Any tips for how to use the next couple days to start building relationships without interrupting everyone’s work?

**Assistant:** You can ease into things by looking for small, natural openings rather than trying to engineer big introductions. When you cross paths with teammates—in the kitchen, before a meeting, or when you’re settling in—just say hello, mention you’ve just wrapped onboarding, and ask something simple about how they like working on the team. Short, friendly moments add up.

If you’re given any starter tasks, use them as excuses to check in. Asking a quick clarifying question or confirming you’re on the right track gives people a chance to engage without feeling pulled away from their work.

You can also let folks know you’re still getting your bearings and don’t want to disrupt them. That kind of awareness usually puts people at ease and makes them more open to chatting when they have a minute.

Keep it light, brief, and genuine, and the relationships will start forming on their own.

**User:** Could you give me a few concrete scripts I can use—like a Slack message to my manager asking for priorities, a short note to a teammate to set up a 15‑minute intro, and what to say if I catch someone between meetings?

**Assistant:** Here are a few simple, natural‑sounding lines you can reuse without feeling awkward.

For a Slack message to your manager, you could say something like: “Hey! Now that I’m through the HR setup, I’d love a quick sense of what you’d like me to focus on over the next few days. Anything you want me to prioritize while I’m getting settled?”

For reaching out to a teammate, you could send a short note such as: “Hi! I’m getting oriented this week and would love to say hello. If you have a spare 15 minutes sometime, I’d really appreciate a quick intro chat so I can learn how we’ll be working together.”

And if you catch someone between meetings, you can keep it light and quick: “Hey, I’m new—I’m still making my rounds. Just wanted to say hi and that I’m excited to work with you. I won’t keep you, but if you ever have a minute later this week, I’d love to hear what you’re working on.”

All of these keep things easy, respectful of time, and still help you start building connections.
