**User:** Could you give me a couple quick “scripts” for that 5–15 minute grounding block—like exactly what to do and what to say to myself—plus one super-short journaling prompt for the 15–25 minute part that won’t spiral into overthinking?

**Assistant:** Here are two simple grounding scripts you can follow without thinking, written like you’d talk to yourself. They’re meant to feel gentle and not at all like “performance.”

Grounding script one:  
Stand or sit comfortably and let your shoulders drop. Breathe in for a slow count of four, breathe out for a slow count of six. As you breathe, say to yourself, “I don’t have to fix anything right now. I’m just letting my body settle.” Keep your eyes soft and notice one thing you can feel under your feet, one thing you can hear in the room, and one thing you can see. Repeat the same slow breathing until the ten minutes are up.

Grounding script two:  
Sit quietly with your hands resting on your legs. On each exhale, quietly think, “Here.” On each inhale, think, “Now.” Let whatever thoughts show up just drift through without grabbing any of them. Every time your mind jumps ahead, gently bring it back with the same two words: “Here. Now.” Keep the pace relaxed and don’t push for calm—just let it come to you if it wants to.

For the journaling part, keep it extremely short. Use this one question:  
“What’s the one thing on my mind that would feel lighter if I wrote it down?”

Write just a sentence or two, close the notebook, and move on. The goal is release, not analysis.
